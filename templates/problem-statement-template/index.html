How to use the problem statement template

  1. Answer who, what, where and when with facts.
  2. Quantify the impact.
  3. Add evidence and the desired outcome.
  4. Note constraints.
  5. Combine it all into one or two sentences that everyone agrees on.

Tips for getting the most from it

  • Do not include a solution in the statement.
  • Use numbers wherever possible.
  • Test it with stakeholders.
  • Use it to open your business case or BRD.

Frequently asked questions

What is a problem statement template?

A problem statement template is a short structure for describing a problem's who, what, where, when, impact and desired outcome.

Is this problem statement template free?

Yes. The problem statement template is free in Google Sheets, Excel and PDF.

How do I write a problem statement with the problem statement template?

Answer each prompt with facts, quantify the impact, then combine the answers into one or two sentences.

What makes a good problem statement template entry?

It is specific, measurable, solution-free and agreed by stakeholders.

Should a problem statement template include the solution?

No. Keep the problem statement solution-free so you can explore options.

Where is a problem statement template used?

In business cases, project charters, requirements documents, Six Sigma projects and design thinking.

How long should a problem statement template be?

The final statement should be one or two sentences.

What is the 5W1H method in a problem statement template?

Who, what, where, when, why and how: questions that make a problem statement complete.

Can I use the problem statement template in Six Sigma?

Yes. It fits the Define phase of DMAIC; see Six Sigma.

Can I download the problem statement template as a PDF?

Yes. Use the PDF button for a printable problem statement template.
