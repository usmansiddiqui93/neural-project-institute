Project Closure: Steps, Checklist and Report Example

Project closure is the final phase of a project, in which deliverables are formally accepted and handed over, contracts and finances are closed, people are released, lessons are recorded and the sponsor signs the project off. This guide gives you a 10-step closure process, a checklist with owners, a closure report template with a worked example, a closure meeting agenda and a method for closing projects that are cancelled early.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 2 October 2026
  • Last updated 4 November 2026
  • 18 min read

Key takeaways

  • Project closure formally ends the project: the customer accepts the deliverables, ownership passes to operations and the sponsor confirms in writing that the project can stop.
  • Completion and closure are different. Completion means the work is done; closure means acceptance, handover, contracts, finances, people, lessons and records are all dealt with too.
  • Plan closure from the start, with time and budget in the schedule. Closure that is left until the last week is usually rushed or skipped.
  • A closure report compares results with the objectives, schedule and budget, lists every open item with a new owner and records the main lessons.
  • Cancelled projects need formal closure as much as successful ones: record why they stopped, salvage what is useful and close contracts and costs.
  • PMBOK, PRINCE2 and agile approaches all include closure; they differ mainly in who authorises it and how often it happens.

What is project closure?

Project closure is the last phase of the project life cycle, in which the project manager confirms that all work is complete, obtains formal acceptance, hands deliverables to their long-term owners, closes contracts and budgets, releases resources, captures lessons learned and obtains sign-off that the project has ended. It turns a temporary project into business as usual.

Closure ends the project, not the product. The new system, building or process carries on under an operational owner; what stops is the temporary team, budget and governance that were set up to create it. That is why handover is at the heart of closure: someone else must be ready, willing and equipped to take responsibility.

Closure can also happen at the end of each phase or stage, not only at the end of the whole project. A phase closure confirms that the phase's outputs are complete and gives the sponsor a decision point on whether to continue. For the full life cycle, see our guide to the five phases of project management.

Why formal project closure matters

Closure is often cut short because the team is tired and new work is waiting. A few days of disciplined closure protect the value the project has just created.

  • Confirms acceptance. Written sign-off from the sponsor or customer settles the question of whether the work is finished.
  • Transfers ownership. Operations or support teams take responsibility, so the project team can stop answering calls about the deliverable.
  • Stops spending. Cost codes, purchase orders and contracts are closed, so no more charges land on the project.
  • Frees people and equipment for other work, with feedback on each person's contribution.
  • Keeps the learning. Lessons learned are captured while memories are fresh and shared with future projects.
  • Creates a complete record for audits, disputes, warranty claims and similar projects in the future.

Projects that drift to an end leave open purchase orders, support arrangements nobody agreed and customers who keep asking for small changes.

Project closure vs project completion

The two terms are often used interchangeably, but they describe different moments. Completion is about the product; closure is about the project as a whole.

AspectProject completionProject closure
Question answeredIs the work finished?Is the project finished, handed over and signed off?
FocusDeliverables built and testedAcceptance, handover, contracts, finance, people, lessons and records
Main evidenceTest results, inspection or demoSigned acceptance and closure report
Who confirms itProject team and quality checksSponsor or governance board
What happens nextClosure activities startOperations own the product; the project organisation is disbanded

A project can be complete but not closed for weeks if handover drags on, so track closure tasks in the plan.

Types of project closure

How you close depends on why the project or phase is ending. The steps are similar, but the emphasis changes.

TypeWhen it happensWhat is different
Normal closureObjectives achieved and deliverables acceptedFull handover, closure report and celebration
Phase or stage closureEnd of a phase, stage or releaseFormal review of the phase and a decision to continue, change course or stop
Premature closureProject cancelled, for example because the business case no longer holdsRecord the reasons, the work done and what can be salvaged; close costs quickly
Contract closureA supplier contract endsVerify the supplier's deliverables, settle payments and formally close the agreement
SuspensionProject paused, for example for funding or a regulatory decisionDocument status so the work can restart; keep minimum governance in place

When project closure starts

Closure is the last phase, but the preparation starts at initiation. The project charter and project management plan should state the acceptance criteria, who signs off and who will own the deliverables afterwards. If those are vague at the start, closure becomes a negotiation at the end.

Build closure tasks into the schedule as a work package with its own effort and budget. A simple timeline for a medium-sized project looks like this:

WhenClosure activities
At initiationAgree acceptance criteria, sign-off authority and the future operational owner
4–6 weeks before go-liveConfirm the handover plan, training dates and support model; warn suppliers about contract close-out; book the closure meeting
2 weeks beforeDraft the closure report; start reconciling costs; list open issues and risks for transfer
Closure weekFormal acceptance, handover, closure meeting and lessons session
2–6 weeks afterFinal invoices paid, cost codes closed, records archived, closure report signed
LaterBenefits reviewed by the business owner, usually months after closure

How to close a project: 10 steps

The steps below follow the order most projects need. Some run in parallel, but acceptance should come before release of the team, and the closure report should come last because it summarises everything else.

1. Confirm the work is complete

Check every deliverable against the project scope and the acceptance criteria. Use the scope statement or deliverables list as a checklist and record the evidence for each item: test results, inspection reports, user acceptance testing or a demonstration. Include approved changes from the change control log, and mark descoped items explicitly so nobody expects them later.

2. Obtain formal acceptance

Ask the sponsor or customer to sign an acceptance form or approve the acceptance in your governance tool. Acceptance with conditions is common: list each condition, such as a minor defect to be fixed, with an owner and a date. Avoid verbal acceptance. Without a written record, the question of whether the work is finished can reopen months later.

3. Hand over the deliverables

Transfer the deliverables to the people who will run, use or support them, together with documentation, training, access and warranty information. The receiving team should confirm in writing that it accepts ownership. The section on handover below covers what to include.

4. Transfer open issues and risks

Go through the issue log and risk register. Close what is resolved. For anything still open, agree a new owner in operations and record the transfer. Risks that only made sense during the project, such as a supplier delay, can be closed with a note.

5. Close contracts

For each supplier, confirm that the statement of work has been met, resolve any claims or disputes, pay final invoices and send formal notice that the contract is complete. Retention payments and warranty periods may continue after closure, so record their end dates and who will manage them. Our guide to procurement management covers contract close-out in more detail.

6. Close the finances

Reconcile actual cost against the project budget, explain the cost variance, accrue any costs still to be invoiced and ask finance to close the cost codes. Cancel open purchase orders and remove project credit cards or approvals so nothing new can be charged.

7. Release resources

Release people in a planned way, keeping the few needed for handover and the closure report. Give each person written feedback for their line manager and remove temporary access and licences.

8. Capture lessons learned

Run a final lessons session with the team and key stakeholders. Focus on causes and recommendations rather than blame, and give each recommendation an owner. Move the lessons into the organisation's repository or PMO knowledge base. The lessons learned template gives you a ready-made structure.

9. Write the closure report and obtain sign-off

Summarise performance against the objectives, schedule, budget and quality targets; list outstanding items and their new owners; record the main lessons; and ask the sponsor to sign. The template and worked example below show what to include.

10. Archive, communicate and celebrate

Store final versions of key documents in the agreed location, following retention rules. Tell stakeholders the project is closed and who to contact from now on. Then thank the team properly.

Project handover to operations

Handover is where many closures stall, because the receiving team will not inherit something it cannot support. Agree the handover contents early, ideally during planning.

Handover itemTypical contentsReceived by
Deliverable and configurationThe product, final versions, configuration and asset recordsOperations or asset owner
DocumentationUser guides, technical manuals, process maps, as-built drawingsSupport team and users
TrainingCompleted training, materials and a list of trained peopleLine managers
Support modelWho handles questions and faults, service levels and escalation routesService desk or support team
Warranties and contractsWarranty terms, support contracts and supplier contactsProcurement or operations
Open itemsKnown defects, open risks and issues with owners and datesOperations lead
Benefits planBenefit measures, baselines and review datesBusiness owner

For larger changes, agree a short period of extra support after go-live, sometimes called hypercare, with an end date and exit criteria so it does not quietly become permanent.

If you track the project in a RAID log, filter it for open items on the day you start closure. It is the fastest way to find the issues, risks and actions that need a new home.

Project closure report template

The closure report, sometimes called an end project report or project completion report, is the formal record of how the project performed and what has been left for others. Keep it short: two to six pages is enough for most projects, with links to detailed records.

SectionWhat to include
Project summaryPurpose, sponsor, project manager, start and end dates, approach used
Objectives and resultsEach objective, its target and the actual result
ScopeDelivered, changed and descoped items, with approved change requests
Schedule performancePlanned and actual dates for key milestones, with reasons for variance
Cost performanceBudget, actual cost and variance, with an explanation
QualityAcceptance results and outstanding defects
BenefitsBenefits achieved so far and how the rest will be measured, by whom
Outstanding itemsOpen issues, risks and actions, each with a new owner and date
Lessons learnedThe three to five most important lessons and recommendations
ApprovalSponsor sign-off and date

Write for someone who was not involved, such as a future project manager or an auditor. Give numbers, not adjectives. An executive summary template is useful for the first page.

Worked example: a closure report

The example below is illustrative. It shows how the template looks when filled in for a payroll system upgrade at a mid-sized organisation.

Project summary. Upgrade of the payroll system to the supplier's current version, with a new pension interface added by approved change. Sponsor: finance director. Planned dates 6 January to 30 June; actual finish 14 July.

ObjectiveTargetResult
Upgrade payroll to the supported versionLive before support for the old version endsAchieved: live on 1 July
Run two parallel payrolls with no unexplained differencesZero unexplained differencesAchieved: two parallel runs reconciled
Automate the pension interface (approved change)Manual pension uploads stoppedAchieved
Train payroll staffAll 9 payroll staff trainedAchieved
Reduce manual processing timeMeasured after three pay cyclesHanded to the finance director to measure
MeasureBaselineActualVariance
Finish date30 June14 July14 days late
Cost$320,000$334,000$14,000 over (4.4%)

Variance explanation. The pension interface change was approved in March, adding $16,000 and two weeks. Excluding the change, the project finished $2,000 under budget and on schedule.

Outstanding items. Two minor defects in the payslip layout transferred to the payroll support team, fix due 15 August. One supplier invoice of $4,000 accrued, payment due 31 July. Benefit tracking passed to the finance director, first review after three pay cycles.

Lessons. (1) Involve the pension provider at the requirements stage; the interface was identified late. (2) Two parallel runs were enough to build confidence and should become the standard. (3) Book payroll staff for training before the busy year-end period.

Approval. Signed by the sponsor, 4 August.

Every number has a baseline, the variance is explained by an approved change and each open item has an owner and a date. For the arithmetic, see our guides to cost variance and schedule variance.

The project closure meeting

A closure meeting, or closeout meeting, brings the sponsor, key stakeholders and team leads together to confirm acceptance, agree how open items will be handled and mark the end of the project. It is different from a team retrospective, which focuses on how the team worked; many projects hold both, with the retrospective first so its findings can feed the closure report.

A 60-minute agenda that works for most projects:

TimeItemLed by
5 minPurpose of the meeting and what will be decidedProject manager
10 minResults against objectives, schedule and budgetProject manager
10 minAcceptance status and any conditionsSponsor or customer
10 minHandover: confirmation from the receiving ownersOperations lead
10 minOpen issues, risks and actions: agree ownersProject manager
10 minTop lessons and recommendationsTeam leads
5 minFormal closure decision and thanksSponsor

Send the draft closure report beforehand so the meeting is spent on decisions rather than reading. A written agenda and clear minutes, ideally with each action's owner and date, make the outcome easy to record.

How to close a cancelled project

Stopping a project that no longer makes sense, for example after a change of strategy, is a good decision, not a failure. Close it formally so the organisation gets the most from what it has spent.

  1. Confirm the decision in writing. Get the cancellation decision and its reasons from the sponsor or board, with an effective date.
  2. Stop work and spending quickly. Tell the team and suppliers, stop new orders and check the notice periods and termination terms in each contract.
  3. Record the status of the work. List what was completed, what was partly done and what was not started, with the cost to date.
  4. Salvage useful outputs. Requirements, designs, data, code, purchased equipment and supplier relationships may be reused; give each one an owner.
  5. Close contracts and finances. Agree termination costs, pay what is owed and close cost codes.
  6. Release people with care. Explain the reasons openly and recognise the work done; people who feel their effort was wasted are slower to commit next time.
  7. Capture lessons. Focus on what could have shown the problem earlier, such as clearer stage gates or benefits checks.
  8. Write a short closure report that records the reasons, work done, money spent, items salvaged and lessons, and get it signed.
Tip

Avoid the word failure in the communication. Say what changed and why stopping is the right decision. Good project governance makes stopping a normal outcome of a stage gate rather than an embarrassment.

Closure in PMBOK, PRINCE2 and agile

Every major framework includes closure; they differ in terminology and in who authorises it.

ApproachHow closure is handled
PMBOK Guide 6th editionClose Project or Phase, a process in project integration management and the only process in the closing process group. It also covers closing procurements.
PMBOK Guide 8th edition (November 2025)Brings back Closing as one of five focus areas, alongside six principles and seven performance domains, with processes described in a non-prescriptive way.
PRINCE2 7The Closing a project process prepares planned or premature closure, hands over products, evaluates the project and recommends closure. The project board authorises closure through Directing a project.
Agile and ScrumEach sprint ends with a review and retrospective; a release or product effort is closed when the product owner and stakeholders agree the goal is met or funding ends. The product usually continues with a support or product team.

For background on the standards themselves, see our guides to the PMBOK Guide, PRINCE2 and the five process groups. If you are preparing for the PMP exam, expect scenario questions about obtaining acceptance before releasing the team and about closing a project that has been cancelled.

Frequently asked questions

What is project closure?

Project closure is the final phase of a project, in which deliverables are formally accepted and handed over, contracts and finances are closed, resources are released, lessons are recorded and the sponsor signs the project off.

What are the steps to close a project?

Confirm completion, obtain acceptance, hand over, transfer open issues and risks, close contracts and finances, release resources, capture lessons, write and sign the closure report, then archive and communicate.

What is a project closure report?

A short document that records how the project performed against its objectives, schedule, budget and quality targets, lists outstanding items with new owners, summarises the main lessons and carries the sponsor's sign-off. PRINCE2 calls it the end project report.

Who is responsible for project closure?

The project manager is responsible for running closure. The sponsor or governance board is accountable for approving it, and coordinators, finance, procurement and operations each handle part of the work.

Who approves project closure?

Usually the project sponsor or the board that authorised the project. In PRINCE2, the project board authorises closure after the project manager recommends it.

How long does project closure take?

It depends on the size of the project and its contracts. A small internal project may close in a few days; a large project with several suppliers can take weeks, mainly because of final invoices, contract close-out and handover.

What is the difference between project closure and contract closure?

Contract closure ends one agreement with a supplier: deliverables are verified, payments are settled and the contract is formally closed. Project closure ends the whole project, which may include several contracts.

What is administrative closure?

Administrative closure covers the paperwork side of closing a project or phase: collecting records, reconciling costs, closing accounts, archiving documents and recording acceptance. It is part of project closure rather than a separate phase.

How do you close a cancelled project?

Close it formally: confirm the decision in writing, stop spending, record what was done, salvage useful outputs, close contracts, capture lessons and write a short closure report.

What is the difference between phase closure and project closure?

Phase closure is a formal review at the end of a phase or stage, confirming its outputs and deciding whether to continue. Project closure happens once, at the end of the whole project, and includes handover and disbanding the project team.

What happens after project closure?

The operational owner runs and supports the deliverable, the business owner tracks benefits that appear later, and the archived records and lessons are available to future projects.

How does the PMBOK Guide handle project closure?

The sixth edition describes Close Project or Phase as part of integration management and the closing process group. The eighth edition, published in November 2025, brings back Closing as one of five focus areas.

How does PRINCE2 handle project closure?

PRINCE2 has a Closing a project process, in which the project manager prepares planned or premature closure, hands over products, evaluates the project and recommends closure to the project board.

Should benefits be measured at project closure?

Measure the benefits that are already visible, but many appear months later. Hand a benefits measurement plan, with baselines and review dates, to the business owner so tracking continues after the project ends.
