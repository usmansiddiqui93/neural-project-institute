Residual Risk in Project Management: Meaning and Examples

Residual risk is the risk that remains after you have planned and carried out your risk responses. Because few responses remove a risk completely, most projects carry some residual risk that must be accepted, monitored or covered by reserves.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 1 October 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Residual risk is what remains after responses.
  • Re-score risks once responses are in place and compare with tolerances.
  • Watch for secondary risks created by responses.
  • Significant residual risk should be explicitly accepted by the sponsor.

Inherent, residual and secondary risk

TermMeaningExample
Inherent riskThe risk before any responseData loss during migration, scored 15
Residual riskThe risk left after responsesAfter backups and a rehearsal, scored 4
Secondary riskA new risk created by a responseThe rehearsal ties up the test environment, delaying other testing

How to assess residual risk

  1. Score the inherent risk: probability x impact before responses.
  2. Agree the response actions and check they are realistic and funded.
  3. Re-score probability and impact assuming the actions are carried out.
  4. Compare the residual score with the organisation's risk appetite or project tolerances.
  5. If it is still too high, add further responses or escalate.
  6. If acceptable, record it, set a contingency plan if needed, and monitor.

Worked example

A construction project risks a crane failure delaying the steel frame. Inherent probability is 3 and impact is 5, a score of 15.

  • Response 1: weekly inspections by the hire company reduce probability to 2.
  • Response 2: a standby agreement with a second hire company limits a delay to three days, reducing impact to 2.
  • Residual score: 2 x 2 = 4, which is within the project's tolerance of 6.
  • Secondary risk: the standby agreement adds a fee; this is logged as a small cost risk.

In money terms, suppose a three-day delay would cost about £15,000 and the residual probability is judged to be 10%. The expected residual cost is £1,500, which the project could hold in its contingency reserve.

Is residual risk acceptable?

  • Compare it with the agreed risk appetite and tolerances in the project management plan.
  • Check whether further reduction would cost more than the exposure it removes.
  • Make the decision visible: the sponsor or governance body should accept significant residual risks explicitly.
  • Record the acceptance in the risk register.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a response removes the risk entirely.
  • Re-scoring before the response has actually been carried out.
  • Forgetting secondary risks introduced by the response.
  • Not telling the sponsor what risk remains.

Frequently asked questions

What is residual risk?

The risk that remains after risk responses have been planned and implemented.

What is the difference between inherent and residual risk?

Inherent risk is the level before any response; residual risk is the level after responses.

How do you calculate residual risk?

Re-score probability and impact assuming responses are in place, then multiply them, or calculate expected value using the reduced probability and impact.

Can residual risk be zero?

Rarely. Only avoiding a risk completely, for example by removing the activity, brings it close to zero.

What is the difference between residual risk and secondary risk?

Residual risk is what remains after a response has been carried out; a secondary risk is a new risk that arises directly from carrying out a risk response.

Who accepts residual risk?

Usually the sponsor or project board, depending on its size and the organisation's governance.

Where is residual risk recorded?

In the risk register, typically in a residual score column next to the original score.

What do you do if residual risk is too high?

Add further responses, change the plan, escalate to a higher level, or decide not to proceed.

How does residual risk relate to contingency reserve?

Contingency reserve is often sized to cover the expected cost of accepted and residual risks.

Is residual risk used outside projects?

Yes. It is a standard concept in information security, health and safety and enterprise risk management.

How does risk appetite affect residual risk?

Risk appetite is the level of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its goals. Residual risk is compared against it to decide whether it can be accepted.
