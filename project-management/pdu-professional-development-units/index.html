PMI PDUs Explained: How to Earn and Report Them

A PDU (professional development unit) is the unit PMI uses to measure the learning and professional service you complete to keep a certification active, and one PDU equals one hour of qualifying activity. PMP holders need 60 PDUs every three years, CAPM holders need 15, and most other PMI certifications need 30. This guide explains what counts, how to split your PDUs across the categories, how to earn them cheaply and how to report them before your cycle ends.

Key takeaways

  • One PDU equals one hour of qualifying learning or giving back, reported in quarter-hour steps.
  • PMP, PgMP, PfMP and PMI-PBA need 60 PDUs per three-year cycle; CAPM needs 15; PMI-ACP, PMI-RMP, PMI-SP, PMI-CP and PMI-CPMAI need 30.
  • Education PDUs have a minimum and must cover all three Talent Triangle areas; Giving Back PDUs are optional and capped.
  • Report PDUs in PMI's CCRS as you earn them, then pay the renewal fee; keep evidence in case of an audit.
  • If you miss the deadline your certification is suspended for 12 months and then expires.

What is a PDU?

A professional development unit (PDU) is the measuring unit PMI uses to quantify approved learning and professional service activities. One hour of qualifying activity equals one PDU, and you can report in quarter-hour increments (15 minutes = 0.25 PDU). Certification holders must earn a set number of PDUs within each three-year cycle to keep their credential active.

PDUs are part of PMI's Continuing Certification Requirements (CCR) programme. The idea is simple: a certification shows what you knew on exam day, and PDUs show that you have kept your skills current since. The requirement applies to every PMI certification, from the entry-level CAPM to the PMP and the specialist credentials. If you are new to PMI itself, read what PMI is and what it does first.

PDUs vs contact hours

PDUs are often confused with contact hours, but they do different jobs. Contact hours are the formal project management education you need *before* you can sit an exam (35 hours for the PMP). PDUs are what you earn *after* you pass, to maintain the certification. Learning completed before your exam date does not count towards your first renewal cycle.

When earnedBefore the exam applicationDuring each three-year cycle, after you pass

How many PDUs do you need?

The number depends on the certification. The table below follows PMI's published requirements (checked 9 October 2026). Every cycle lasts three years and starts on the day you pass the exam.

CertificationTotal PDUs per 3 yearsEducation (minimum)Giving Back (maximum)Minimum per Talent Triangle area
PMP, PgMP, PfMP, PMI-PBA6035258 each
PMI-ACP, PMI-RMP, PMI-SP, PMI-CP, PMI-CPMAI3018124 each
PMI-PMOCP3020103 Ways of Working, 7 Power Skills, 10 Business Acumen
CAPM15962 each

Three rules sit behind the table. First, the education figure is a minimum: you can earn all your PDUs through education if you prefer. Second, the giving back figure is a maximum: anything above it simply does not count. Third, within education you must hit the minimum in each Talent Triangle area; the remaining education PDUs can come from any area.

If you hold more than one certification

You do not need to earn completely separate PDUs for each credential. According to PMI's CCR Handbook, a PDU can count towards more than one certification when its content is relevant to each. For example, an Agile course can count towards both the PMP and the PMI-ACP, while Power Skills, Business Acumen and Giving Back PDUs generally apply across certifications. Each certification still has its own cycle dates and renewal fee, so track them separately.

Tip

Your cycle start date is the date you passed the exam, not the date your certificate arrived. Check the exact dates in your myPMI dashboard and put the end date in your calendar now.

Education and Giving Back PDUs

Every PDU falls into one of two categories: Education (learning) or Giving Back to the Profession (applying and sharing what you know). PMI's CCR Handbook breaks each category into activity types.

Education PDUs

  • Course or training: classroom or online courses from PMI, a PMI Authorized Training Partner, a university or another provider. One hour of instruction equals one PDU.
  • Organisation meetings: PMI chapter meetings and similar professional events that include an educational element. These are usually worth one or two PDUs each.
  • Online or digital media: webinars, podcasts, e-learning and recorded talks.
  • Read: self-directed reading of books, articles and the PMBOK Guide or other standards.
  • Informal learning: structured learning discussions, such as being mentored or a professional study group.

Giving Back PDUs

  • Working as a practitioner: your day-to-day project work. It is capped at 8 PDUs per cycle for the PMP group, 4 for the 30-PDU certifications and 2 for the CAPM, and you claim it once per cycle.
  • Create content: writing articles, blog posts, white papers, books or training material on project management topics.
  • Give a presentation: speaking at a conference, chapter meeting or internal knowledge session.
  • Share knowledge: teaching or mentoring others in project management.
  • Volunteer: service to an organisation that is not your employer or client, such as a PMI chapter or a non-profit project.

Giving Back is optional. Many certification holders earn their full total through education alone, especially when their employer pays for training. If you do use Giving Back, remember that every Giving Back activity counts towards the same cap, including working as a practitioner.

The PMI Talent Triangle

PMI groups education PDUs into three skill areas known as the Talent Triangle. The names were updated in 2022, so older articles may still use the former labels in brackets below.

Talent Triangle areaWhat it coversExample PDU topics
Ways of Working (formerly Technical Project Management)Methods and techniques for delivering projectsPredictive scheduling, Agile and hybrid delivery, risk, earned value, Scrum, Kanban
Power Skills (formerly Leadership)Interpersonal and leadership skillsCommunication, negotiation, stakeholder engagement, coaching, collaborative leadership
Business Acumen (formerly Strategic and Business Management)Understanding the organisation and its environmentBusiness cases, finance, benefits, strategy alignment, regulation, market awareness

The minimum per area is 8 PDUs for the PMP group, 4 for the 30-PDU certifications and 2 for the CAPM (PMI-PMOCP has its own split, shown above). Choose topics that match the gaps in your skills, not just the cheapest PDUs. Our guides can point you to useful subjects in each area:

  • Ways of Working: Agile methodology, earned value management and project risk management.
  • Power Skills: project management skills and stakeholder management.
  • Business Acumen: cost-benefit analysis and return on investment.

When you claim a course in CCRS, you allocate its hours across the three areas according to its content. A two-day course might be split, for example, into 10 Ways of Working and 4 Power Skills PDUs.

How to earn PDUs, including free options

You can earn every PDU you need without spending much. The trade-off is usually time and planning rather than money.

Free and low-cost ways to earn PDUs

  • Webinars: PMI, PMI chapters, software vendors and training providers run regular free webinars. Keep the attendance confirmation.
  • PMI membership resources: PMI says membership gives access to free and discounted professional education; check what is included in your region before you buy courses elsewhere.
  • Chapter meetings and events: many PMI chapters run evening sessions that are free or low cost for members.
  • Reading: books and articles on project management, leadership and business all count as self-directed learning.
  • Podcasts and recorded talks: these count as online or digital media when the content is relevant.
  • Your job: working as a practitioner gives up to 8 PDUs per PMP cycle.
  • Mentoring and volunteering: both count towards Giving Back.

Paid options worth considering

  • Courses from PMI Authorized Training Partners (ATPs): these usually come with an activity ID, which makes claiming quicker.
  • Courses for another certification: studying for a PMI-ACP or a Scrum qualification earns PDUs and a new credential at the same time. See how to learn Agile and get certified and our guide to Scrum certification and training.
  • Conferences: a single event can provide several PDUs across all three Talent Triangle areas.
  • University modules: individual courses can be claimed; you report each relevant course rather than a whole degree.
Tip

Before you pay for a course, check how many PDUs it offers in each Talent Triangle area. A course that fills your weakest area is worth more to your renewal than one that adds to an area you have already covered.

How to report PDUs in CCRS

PDUs are claimed online in PMI's Continuing Certification Requirements System (CCRS), which you reach from your myPMI account. PMI also lets you track PDUs in its official mobile app.

  1. Log in to CCRS with your PMI account and open the option to report PDUs.
  2. Choose the category (Education or Giving Back) and the activity type, such as course or training, online or digital media, or read.
  3. Enter the provider, activity title, dates and a short description. If the provider is an Authorized Training Partner, search for its activity ID so the details fill in automatically.
  4. Allocate education hours across Ways of Working, Power Skills and Business Acumen.
  5. Confirm that the claim is accurate and submit it. Some claims are approved straight away; others are reviewed first.
  6. Once you reach your total, PMI emails you a link to pay the renewal fee. Pay it to complete renewal.
Tip

Report each activity within a few days of completing it. It takes minutes when the details are fresh, and it avoids a stressful rush at the end of year three.

Carry-over, renewal fee and deadlines

Carrying PDUs into your next cycle

You can carry extra PDUs into your next cycle, but only those earned in the final 12 months of the current cycle. PDUs earned in years one and two do not carry over. According to PMI's CCR Handbook, the limits are:

CertificationMaximum PDUs you can carry over
PMP, PgMP, PfMP, PMI-PBA20
PMI-ACP, PMI-RMP, PMI-SP10
CAPM5

Carry-over limits for newer credentials such as PMI-CP, PMI-CPMAI and PMI-PMOCP are not listed in the handbook, so check CCRS or PMI customer care before relying on them.

The renewal fee

Renewal also needs a fee, which is separate from PMI membership dues. PMI's CCR Handbook lists US$60 for PMI members and US$150 for non-members for the main certifications, but PMI's current renewal pages no longer state an amount, so check the fee shown in your myPMI dashboard before you budget. The handbook says payment is due no later than 90 days after the cycle end date.

Key dates in a cycle

  • Day 1: the date you pass the exam. You can start earning PDUs immediately.
  • Years 1 and 2: earn steadily; these PDUs count only towards the current cycle.
  • Year 3: finish your total; extra PDUs from this year can carry over within the limits above.
  • Cycle end date: all PDUs should be reported and the fee paid.

What happens if you miss the deadline?

PMI's CCR Handbook describes three main statuses:

StatusWhat it meansWhat you can do
ActiveAll PDUs reported and the renewal fee paidUse your credential and appear in PMI's online certification registry
SuspendedRequirements not met by the end of the cycle; lasts 12 monthsYou cannot use the credential, but you can still report the missing PDUs and pay to reinstate it. Your next cycle dates do not move.
ExpiredRequirements still not met at the end of the suspensionYou must reapply, pay the fees and retake the exam

There is also a retired status for people who no longer earn their main income from project management and have held the credential in good standing for at least 10 consecutive years. Retired holders do not need to earn PDUs; you apply to PMI in writing, and the handbook explains how to return to active status later.

If illness or other circumstances prevent you from completing your PDUs, contact PMI customer care before the cycle ends. PMI reviews such cases individually.

PDU audits and record keeping

PMI randomly selects a percentage of PDU claims and renewals for audit. If you are selected, you must provide evidence for the activities you claimed. PMI's handbook asks holders to keep supporting records for at least 18 months after the cycle ends.

  • Courses and webinars: certificates of completion or attendance confirmations showing dates and hours.
  • Reading: a list of titles, authors and the time spent, with brief notes on what you learned.
  • Working as a practitioner: a job description or employer letter showing your project role.
  • Creating content and presentations: copies of the articles, slides or materials.
  • Mentoring and volunteering: evidence of the arrangement, such as emails or a letter from the organisation, with dates.

Claims are also covered by PMI's code of ethics, so only claim what you actually did and allocate hours honestly across the Talent Triangle. A simple folder per cycle, with one file per activity named by date, makes an audit straightforward.

Common PDU mistakes

  • Leaving it to the final months. Courses fill up, and you may find you are short in one Talent Triangle area with no time to fix it.
  • Ignoring the per-area minimums. Sixty Ways of Working PDUs will not renew a PMP; you need at least 8 in each area.
  • Over-claiming Giving Back. Anything above the cap is wasted, and working as a practitioner can only be claimed once per cycle.
  • Counting pre-exam study. Learning you completed before passing the exam counts as contact hours, not PDUs.
  • Assuming early PDUs carry over. Only PDUs earned in the final 12 months can move to the next cycle.
  • Not keeping evidence. An audit without certificates or notes is hard to pass.
  • Forgetting the fee. Reporting 60 PDUs does not renew your credential until the fee is paid.

A short personal development plan prevents most of these. Map the skills you need for your next role, for example a move to senior project manager, and choose PDU activities that build them. A skills matrix template is a quick way to spot your gaps.

Frequently asked questions

What is a PDU in project management?

A PDU (professional development unit) is PMI's measure of continuing education and professional service. One hour of qualifying activity equals one PDU, and certification holders must earn a set number every three years to renew.

How many PDUs do I need to renew my PMP?

60 PDUs every three years, with at least 35 from education (including at least 8 in each Talent Triangle area) and no more than 25 from giving back.

How many PDUs do I need for the CAPM?

15 PDUs every three years, with at least 9 from education (at least 2 in each Talent Triangle area) and no more than 6 from giving back.

How many PDUs do I need for the PMI-ACP?

30 PDUs every three years, with at least 18 from education (at least 4 in each Talent Triangle area) and no more than 12 from giving back.

Can I earn 60 PDUs for free?

Yes, it is possible. Free webinars, chapter meetings, reading, podcasts, working as a practitioner, mentoring and volunteering can cover the full requirement if you plan across the Talent Triangle areas.

What counts as a PDU?

Courses, webinars, chapter meetings, reading, informal learning, working as a practitioner, creating content, presenting, mentoring and volunteering, as long as the content relates to project management and the Talent Triangle.

Do PDUs carry over to the next cycle?

Only PDUs earned in the final 12 months of a cycle can carry over: up to 20 for the PMP, 10 for the PMI-ACP, PMI-RMP and PMI-SP, and 5 for the CAPM.

How much does it cost to renew the PMP?

PMI's CCR Handbook lists US$60 for PMI members and US$150 for non-members. Check the current fee in your myPMI dashboard, because PMI's renewal pages no longer publish an amount.

How do I report PDUs?

Log in to PMI's Continuing Certification Requirements System (CCRS), choose the category and activity type, enter the details and allocate education hours to the Talent Triangle areas.

What happens if I do not earn enough PDUs in time?

Your certification is suspended for 12 months. You can still report PDUs and pay to reinstate it during that time; if you do not, it expires and you must retake the exam.

Can I earn PDUs while my certification is suspended?

You can complete and report the missing PDUs during the suspension period to reinstate your certification, but your next cycle dates do not change.

How long should I keep PDU records?

PMI's handbook asks you to keep supporting documentation for at least 18 months after your cycle ends, in case your claims are audited.

Can one course count towards two certifications?

Often, yes. PMI allows PDUs to count towards more than one certification when the content is relevant to each, for example an Agile course for both the PMP and PMI-ACP.

Are PDUs the same as contact hours?

No. Contact hours are the education you need to qualify for an exam; PDUs are the learning you complete after passing to keep the certification active.
