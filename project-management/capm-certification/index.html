CAPM Certification: Requirements, Exam, Cost and Study Plan

The CAPM certification (Certified Associate in Project Management) is the Project Management Institute's entry-level credential, showing that you understand project management fundamentals, predictive and Agile methods, and business analysis. You need a secondary diploma and 23 hours of project management education, and no project experience. This guide explains the requirements, exam, fees, a step-by-step route to certification, a six-week study plan with worked examples and sample questions, how the CAPM compares with the PMP, and how to keep it active.

Key takeaways

  • The CAPM is PMI's entry-level certification and needs no project experience: a secondary diploma and 23 hours of project management education are enough.
  • The exam has 150 questions (135 scored) in 180 minutes, across four domains: fundamentals 36%, predictive 17%, Agile 20% and business analysis 27%.
  • Exam fees are widely reported as US$225 for PMI members and US$300 for non-members; check PMI's site before you pay.
  • Most candidates prepare in four to eight weeks; practise calculations such as schedule variance and the critical path, not only definitions.
  • An active CAPM meets the PMP training requirement, which makes it a natural first step towards the PMP.
  • Keep it active by earning 15 PDUs every three years.

What is the CAPM certification?

The Certified Associate in Project Management (CAPM) is a professional certification awarded by the Project Management Institute (PMI) to people who pass an exam on project management fundamentals. It shows employers that you understand core terms, processes and approaches, including predictive, Agile and business analysis practices, before you have led projects yourself.

The CAPM sits below the PMP certification in PMI's range. The PMP tests experienced judgement and requires years of project leadership; the CAPM tests knowledge and is open to anyone who meets the education rules.

What the CAPM shows, and what it does not

  • It shows that you know the language of project management: scope, schedules, risk, stakeholders, iterations, backlogs and requirements.
  • It shows commitment to the profession, which helps when you have little experience on your CV.
  • It does not show that you have led projects. Employers will still ask for examples of what you have organised, coordinated or delivered.

If you are still comparing options, our overview of project management certifications sets the CAPM alongside PRINCE2, Scrum and other credentials.

CAPM requirements

According to PMI's CAPM page (checked 18 October 2026), you need:

RequirementDetail
EducationA secondary degree, such as a high school diploma, GED or global equivalent
Project management educationAt least 23 hours, completed before you sit the exam
ExperienceNone required
AgePMI's page lists a minimum age of 18

What counts as the 23 contact hours

A contact hour is one hour of structured learning about project management. PMI lists its own on-demand CAPM Exam Prep Course and instructor-led CAPM courses from training providers as options. University or college modules in project management can also qualify. The learning must cover project management topics, and you should keep the certificate or transcript in case your application is audited.

  • Usually counts: project management courses (online or in person) with a certificate showing the hours, academic project management modules, and courses run by PMI chapters or training providers.
  • Usually does not count: networking meetings, general business or software courses without project management content, and reading a book on your own without a course.
  • Courses you have already done: earlier courses can count if you can prove the hours and the content. Check PMI's current handbook if you are unsure.

The CAPM's 23 hours are separate from the PMP's training requirement. Our guide to 35 contact hours for the PMP explains that rule.

Tip

Choose a course built on PMI's current CAPM Exam Content Outline. Courses written before the 2023 exam update cover an older structure and leave gaps in Agile and business analysis.

CAPM exam format

ItemDetail (PMI, checked 18 October 2026)
Questions150 in total: 135 scored and 15 unscored pretest questions
Time180 minutes
DeliveryPearson VUE test centre (PMI recommends this) or online proctored exam
LanguagesEnglish plus several translations, including Arabic, French, German, Japanese, Spanish and Chinese
AttemptsUp to three attempts within a one-year eligibility period
ReschedulingFree more than 30 days before; US$70 within 30 days; not possible within 48 hours (PMI certification handbook)

Question types

Most questions are multiple choice with one correct answer. Training providers report that the current exam can also include multiple-response, matching and hotspot items, so practise with a simulator that includes these formats. The 15 pretest questions are mixed in and look like the rest, so treat every question as if it counts.

Scoring and results

PMI does not publish a passing percentage. It sets the pass standard through psychometric analysis, and your result report shows pass or fail plus a performance rating for each domain. Many training providers suggest aiming for 75% to 80% or more on full-length practice exams before you book, but that is a rule of thumb, not a PMI figure.

What the CAPM exam covers

The exam follows PMI's CAPM Exam Content Outline (ECO), last updated in 2023. It has four domains. The approximate counts below assume the weights apply to the 135 scored questions.

DomainWeightApproximate scored questions
1. Project management fundamentals and core concepts36%About 49
2. Predictive, plan-based methodologies17%About 23
3. Agile frameworks and methodologies20%About 27
4. Business analysis frameworks27%About 36

Domain 1: fundamentals and core concepts (36%)

  • Project life cycles; projects vs programmes, portfolios and operations; predictive vs adaptive approaches.
  • The difference between issues, risks, assumptions and constraints.
  • The purpose of cost, quality, risk and schedule plans; milestones; using a risk register and stakeholder register.
  • Roles of the project manager, sponsor and team; leadership vs management; emotional intelligence.
  • The PMI Code of Ethics, project initiation, benefits planning, closure and common problem-solving techniques.

Domain 2: predictive, plan-based methodologies (17%)

  • When a plan-based approach suits the organisation and the work.
  • Work breakdown structures and work packages.
  • The critical path method and schedule management.
  • Calculating schedule and cost variance; quality and integration management plans; artefacts used in predictive projects.

Domain 3: Agile frameworks and methodologies (20%)

  • When an adaptive approach suits the project; pros and cons compared with predictive work.
  • Planning iterations, translating a WBS into iterations, and adaptive tracking.
  • Components of methods such as Scrum, XP, SAFe and Kanban.
  • Artefacts used in adaptive projects, success criteria and prioritising tasks.

Domain 4: business analysis frameworks (27%)

  • Business analysis roles such as process owner, product manager and product owner, and stakeholder identification.
  • Choosing communication channels for different audiences; see stakeholder management.
  • Gathering requirements with interviews, workshops, surveys, user stories and use cases.
  • Requirements traceability matrices, product backlogs, product roadmaps and release planning.
  • Acceptance criteria and deciding whether a product is ready for delivery.

Which references to study

PMI's PMBOK Guide is the main reference, but the exam is based on the content outline rather than any single book. The eighth edition of the PMBOK Guide was published in November 2025, while the CAPM outline dates from 2023, so use a prep course aligned to the outline and check PMI's current reference list. Add an Agile primer, because a fifth of the exam is Agile; our guide to Agile project management is a good starting point.

How much the CAPM costs

PMI's CAPM page does not list the fee, and PMI says prices vary by membership status and region. The figures below are those widely reported by training providers and education sites in 2025 and 2026 (checked 18 October 2026). Confirm the current price on pmi.org before you pay.

Cost itemPMI memberNon-member
CAPM examUS$225US$300
Re-examination (each retake)US$150US$200
Rescheduling within 30 daysUS$70US$70
23-hour courseVaries by providerVaries by provider
PMI membershipAnnual fee plus a one-time joining fee (check pmi.org)Not applicable

Worked example: total cost

Suppose you take a low-cost online course for US$100 and buy a practice exam simulator for US$50. As a non-member you pay US$300 for the exam, so your total is US$450. If you fail once and retake, add US$200, giving US$650.

As a member the exam costs US$225, a saving of US$75, and a retake costs US$150, a saving of another US$50. Because a year's membership costs more than the US$75 exam saving on its own, membership pays off mainly if you also use other benefits, such as cheaper PMI courses, member access to PMI standards, local chapter events and lower renewal fees.

Tip

Ask your employer before paying. Many organisations fund entry-level certifications for coordinators and project team members, especially if you link the CAPM to a development plan.

How to get the CAPM certification step by step

  1. Check you are eligible. Confirm you have a secondary diploma or equivalent, and that you meet PMI's other conditions.
  2. Complete 23 hours of project management education. Pick a course aligned to the current CAPM content outline and keep the completion certificate.
  3. Decide on PMI membership. Join before you pay the exam fee if you want the member price; PMI applies the member rate only if your membership is active when you pay.
  4. Create a PMI account and apply online. Enter your education and course details. PMI keeps an application open for 90 days while you complete it.
  5. Wait for review, and respond to any audit. PMI reviews most applications quickly. A percentage are randomly audited; if yours is, send copies of your diploma and course certificate within the time PMI gives you.
  6. Pay the exam fee. Once your application is approved, payment opens your one-year eligibility period.
  7. Book the exam with Pearson VUE. Choose a test centre or online proctoring. For online exams, run the system check on the computer you will use.
  8. Prepare and sit the exam. Follow the study plan below and take full-length practice exams under timed conditions.
  9. Receive your result and certificate. You see pass or fail when you finish. Your three-year certification cycle starts, and you can add the credential to your CV and LinkedIn profile.

PMI's handbook allows up to three attempts within the one-year eligibility period. If you do not pass in three attempts, you must wait a year from your last attempt before you can apply again.

A 6-week CAPM study plan

This plan assumes you have finished your 23-hour course and can study for six to eight hours a week. Stretch it to eight weeks if you are new to project work or studying part-time.

WeekFocusActivities
1Fundamentals (part 1)Life cycles, projects vs operations, predictive vs adaptive, roles, ethics. Make flashcards for key terms.
2Fundamentals (part 2)Planning, risk and stakeholder registers, issues vs risks, closure. Do 50 topic questions and review every mistake.
3Predictive methodsWBS, critical path, schedule and cost variance, quality and integration plans. Practise calculations until they are quick.
4Agile methodsScrum roles, events and artefacts; Kanban; XP; SAFe basics; iteration planning and prioritisation.
5Business analysisRequirements gathering, traceability, backlogs, roadmaps, acceptance criteria. First full-length timed practice exam.
6Review and exam practiceSecond and third full-length exams. Re-study your two weakest domains. Book the exam once you score consistently well.
Tip

Keep an error log: for each practice question you get wrong, write down the concept, why your answer was wrong and why the right answer is right. Re-read it the day before the exam.

Worked examples of CAPM calculations

The content outline asks you to calculate schedule and cost variance and apply the critical path method, so expect some arithmetic. These examples show the level of calculation to expect.

Example 1: earned value, schedule and cost variance

A project has a budget at completion (BAC) of US$100,000. At the end of month 3, the plan says 50% of the work should be done. In fact 40% is complete, and US$45,000 has been spent.

MeasureFormulaCalculationResult
Planned value (PV)Planned % complete × BAC50% × 100,000US$50,000
Earned value (EV)Actual % complete × BAC40% × 100,000US$40,000
Actual cost (AC)Money spent so farGivenUS$45,000
Schedule variance (SV)EV − PV40,000 − 50,000−US$10,000 (behind schedule)
Cost variance (CV)EV − AC40,000 − 45,000−US$5,000 (over budget)
Schedule performance index (SPI)EV ÷ PV40,000 ÷ 50,0000.80
Cost performance index (CPI)EV ÷ AC40,000 ÷ 45,0000.89

Negative variances and indices below 1.0 mean the project is behind schedule and over budget. Our guides to earned value management, schedule variance and cost variance go further.

Example 2: finding the critical path

A small project has five activities. B and C can start when A finishes; D needs both B and C; E follows D.

ActivityDuration (days)Depends on
A3None
B5A
C2A
D4B and C
E1D
  1. List the paths through the network: A-B-D-E and A-C-D-E.
  2. Add the durations: A-B-D-E = 3 + 5 + 4 + 1 = 13 days; A-C-D-E = 3 + 2 + 4 + 1 = 10 days.
  3. The longest path, A-B-D-E, is the critical path, so the project takes 13 days.
  4. Activity C has 13 − 10 = 3 days of float: it can slip by up to three days without delaying the project.

A delay to any activity on the critical path delays the whole project, which is why exam questions often ask which activity a project manager should watch most closely.

Sample CAPM-style questions

These are our own practice questions written in the style of the exam. They are not taken from PMI's question bank.

Question 1

A team is building a new mobile app. Users are unsure what they need and the sponsor wants feedback every two weeks. Which approach is most appropriate? (a) Predictive (b) Adaptive (c) No formal approach (d) Phase-gate with a single final release.

Answer: (b). Uncertain requirements and frequent feedback favour an adaptive approach.

Question 2

A key supplier might go out of business before delivering a component. This is best described as: (a) an issue (b) a risk (c) an assumption (d) a constraint.

Answer: (b). It is an uncertain future event that would affect the project. It becomes an issue only if it actually happens.

Question 3

Which document links each requirement to its source and to the deliverables and tests that satisfy it? (a) Product roadmap (b) Stakeholder register (c) Requirements traceability matrix (d) Burndown chart.

Answer: (c). A traceability matrix tracks requirements from origin to delivery and testing.

Question 4

In Scrum, who is accountable for ordering the product backlog? (a) Scrum master (b) Project sponsor (c) Developers (d) Product owner.

Answer: (d). The Scrum Guide makes the product owner accountable for product backlog management, including ordering items.

Exam-day tips

  • Read the last line of the question first. Questions often ask for the "best", "first" or "most appropriate" action; identify what is asked before reading the scenario.
  • Watch for the approach. Decide whether the scenario is predictive, Agile or hybrid; the right answer often depends on it.
  • Eliminate wrong answers. You can usually remove two options quickly, which turns a guess into a 50% chance.
  • Pace yourself. 150 questions in 180 minutes is 72 seconds each. Flag hard questions and come back to them.
  • Answer every question. There is no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave one blank.
  • Check break rules in advance. Rules differ between test centres and online exams, so read PMI's and Pearson VUE's current instructions before exam day.
  • For online exams, clear your desk, test your webcam and connection, and log in early.

LevelEntry levelExperienced practitioner

Renewal15 PDUs every 3 years60 PDUs every 3 years
Typical holderStudents, coordinators, career changersProject managers and senior project staff

PMI states that CAPM holders already meet the PMP training requirement, so the CAPM can save you from taking a separate 35-hour course later. PMI also changed the PMP exam in July 2026 and is updating the rules for live training hours from 1 December 2026; check PMI's PMP page if you plan to move on soon.

Which should you take?

  • Take the CAPM if you have little or no experience leading projects, or want a recognised credential while you build experience.
  • Go straight for the PMP if you already meet its experience requirement. The CAPM adds little once you are eligible for the PMP.

Is the CAPM worth it?

For most people starting out, yes, provided employers in your market recognise PMI credentials. Check job adverts for coordinator and junior project roles in your area to see how often the CAPM or PMP is mentioned.

Benefits

  • A recognised credential with no experience requirement.
  • A structured way to learn predictive, Agile and business analysis basics in one go.
  • It meets the PMP training requirement for later.
  • It signals commitment when your CV is light on project experience.

Limitations

  • It does not replace experience; you will still need examples of work you have organised.
  • It is better known in markets where PMI is strong; in some UK and European public-sector roles, PRINCE2 is asked for more often.
  • It needs 15 PDUs and a renewal fee every three years.

Jobs that suit CAPM holders

  • Project coordinator
  • Assistant or junior project manager
  • Project administrator or PMO analyst
  • Business analyst or junior product roles
  • Team members who want to move into project roles

PMI's CAPM page cites an average US salary for CAPM-certified project managers of US$100,750, from Forbes. Pay depends far more on role, location and experience than on the certificate alone, so treat any average with care. For the wider career route, read how to become a project manager.

Maintaining your CAPM

The CAPM is valid for three years. To renew, you earn 15 professional development units (PDUs) during each three-year cycle and pay PMI's renewal fee. One PDU equals one hour of learning or giving back to the profession.

CategoryCAPM rule
Total PDUs per cycle15
EducationAt least 9, with at least 2 in each Talent Triangle area (Ways of Working, Power Skills, Business Acumen)
Giving backUp to 6, including up to 2 for working as a practitioner

Example renewal plan

  • A 6-hour Agile course: 6 PDUs (Ways of Working).
  • Two 1.5-hour webinars on communication and negotiation: 3 PDUs (Power Skills).
  • A 3-hour course on project finance: 3 PDUs (Business Acumen).
  • Working as a practitioner: 2 PDUs; volunteering at a PMI chapter event: 1 PDU.

That gives 12 education PDUs and 3 giving-back PDUs, 15 in total. If you earn the PMP during the cycle, the CAPM is no longer needed. Our PDU guide explains how to claim and report PDUs.

Common CAPM mistakes to avoid

  • Studying from out-of-date material. Prep books and courses written before the 2023 update miss much of the Agile and business analysis content.
  • Memorising without practising. The exam asks you to apply concepts to scenarios; use practice questions from week 2 onwards.
  • Skipping the calculations. Variance and critical path questions are easy marks once you have practised them.
  • Under-weighting business analysis. At 27% it is the second-largest domain, larger than predictive methods.
  • Booking too early. Wait until you score consistently well on full-length timed practice exams.
  • Joining PMI only for the exam discount. Work out whether you will use the other benefits first.
  • Losing your course certificate. Keep evidence of your 23 hours in case of an audit, even after you pass.
  • Forgetting renewal. Log PDUs as you earn them rather than at the end of the cycle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the CAPM certification?

The Certified Associate in Project Management is PMI's entry-level certification. It shows you understand project management fundamentals, predictive and Agile approaches, and business analysis, and it needs no project experience.

What are the CAPM requirements?

A secondary diploma or equivalent and at least 23 hours of project management education completed before the exam. PMI's page also lists a minimum age of 18.

Can I take the CAPM without experience?

Yes. The CAPM has no project experience requirement, which is why it suits students, career changers and coordinators.

How much does the CAPM cost?

The exam fee is widely reported as US$225 for PMI members and US$300 for non-members, with retakes at US$150 and US$200. Add the cost of your 23-hour course and check PMI's site for current prices.

How hard is the CAPM exam?

It is easier than the PMP because it tests knowledge rather than experienced judgement, but it covers a broad range of topics, including Agile, business analysis and some calculations. Most candidates who study consistently for four to eight weeks are well prepared.

What is the CAPM passing score?

PMI does not publish a passing percentage. It sets the standard through psychometric analysis and reports pass or fail with a performance rating for each domain.

How long should I study for the CAPM?

Many people prepare in four to eight weeks alongside work, studying six to eight hours a week after their 23-hour course.

Can I take the CAPM exam online?

Yes. You can take it at a Pearson VUE test centre or as an online proctored exam.

How many times can I take the CAPM exam?

Up to three times within your one-year eligibility period. After three unsuccessful attempts you must wait a year before reapplying.

Is the CAPM worth it?

For beginners in markets that value PMI credentials, it is a recognised way to show your knowledge and prepare for the PMP. It does not replace experience, so pair it with practical project work.

Should I take the CAPM or the PMP?

Take the CAPM if you do not yet meet the PMP's experience requirement. If you already have several years of project leadership, go straight for the PMP.

Does the CAPM count towards the PMP?

Yes. PMI states that CAPM holders already meet the PMP training requirement, so you do not need a separate 35-hour course. You still need the PMP's project experience.

Does the CAPM expire?

It lasts for three years. To renew, earn 15 PDUs in each cycle and pay the renewal fee.

Which PMBOK Guide edition should I study for the CAPM?

The exam follows PMI's CAPM Exam Content Outline, not a single edition of the PMBOK Guide. The eighth edition was published in November 2025; use a prep course aligned to the current outline and check PMI's reference list.

Should I take the CAPM or PRINCE2 Foundation?

The CAPM suits markets where PMI credentials are common and gives a broad grounding, including Agile and business analysis. PRINCE2 Foundation is more often requested in the UK, Europe and the public sector.
