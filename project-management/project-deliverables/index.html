Project Deliverables: Definition, Types and Examples

A deliverable is any unique, verifiable product, result or capability produced to complete a process, phase or project. Deliverables are what the project hands over.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Deliverables are verifiable outputs of a project.
  • They can be internal or external, tangible or intangible.
  • Define acceptance criteria and record formal acceptance.

Types of deliverables

TypeExamples
External (to customer)Software release, building, report, trained staff
Internal (for the project)Project plan, design documents, test results
TangibleEquipment, printed materials, a physical space
IntangibleTraining, a new process, improved capability

Deliverables vs milestones vs objectives

TermMeaningExample
DeliverableAn outputApproved website design
MilestoneA significant point in timeDesign approved
ObjectiveThe outcome soughtIncrease online bookings by 25%

Defining deliverables well

  • Describe each deliverable clearly.
  • Set acceptance criteria.
  • Assign an owner.
  • Link it to the WBS.
  • Agree how it will be reviewed and accepted.

Accepting deliverables

Formal acceptance, sometimes called scope validation, confirms the deliverable meets its criteria. Record acceptance in writing to avoid disputes later.

Frequently asked questions

What is a project deliverable?

A unique, verifiable output produced by a project.

What is the difference between a deliverable and an outcome?

A deliverable is an output; an outcome is the change or benefit it enables.

Are documents deliverables?

Yes, if they are required outputs, such as a design specification.

What are acceptance criteria?

Conditions a deliverable must meet to be accepted.

Who accepts deliverables?

The customer, sponsor or designated stakeholders.

What is a key deliverable?

A major output listed in the charter or contract.

How are deliverables tracked?

Through the WBS, schedule and status reports.

Are deliverables the same as milestones?

No. Milestones mark points in time; deliverables are outputs.

What is a deliverables list?

A list of all deliverables with owners, dates and acceptance criteria.

Can deliverables change?

Yes, through change control.
