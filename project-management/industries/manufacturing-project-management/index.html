Manufacturing Project Management: NPI, Phases, Methods and KPIs

Manufacturing project management is the planning and control of projects that create or change how products are made, such as new product introductions (NPI), new production lines, plant expansions, equipment installations and process improvement programmes. It sits between engineering and operations: the project is only finished when the factory can make the product reliably, at the right quality, cost and volume.

Key takeaways

  • Manufacturing project management ends when the factory can make the product reliably at target rate, quality and cost.
  • Stage-gate and APQP structure new product introduction; capital projects add FAT, SAT, commissioning and ramp-up.
  • Lean and Six Sigma shape both the process design and improvement projects.
  • Keep product and production data in PLM, ERP and MES, and coordinate the project in a platform such as Wrike.

What makes manufacturing project management different

  • The factory keeps running. Projects often take place inside a live plant, so downtime, shutdown windows and production schedules constrain the work.
  • Quality systems apply. Standards such as ISO 9001, IATF 16949 in automotive and sector regulations require documented, validated processes.
  • Suppliers and tooling. Moulds, dies, fixtures and machinery can have long lead times and must be qualified before production.
  • Ramp-up matters. Success is measured by how quickly the line reaches its target rate and quality, not by the day it is installed.
  • Cross-functional teams. Design engineering, manufacturing engineering, quality, procurement, logistics, maintenance and production must all sign off.
  • Continuous improvement culture. Many projects are improvement initiatives using Lean manufacturing and Six Sigma.

Phases of a manufacturing project

New product introduction in many companies follows a stage-gate process. In automotive and related supply chains, the AIAG Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) framework is widely used, ending with Production Part Approval Process (PPAP) submission.

APQP phaseWhat happensKey outputs
1. Plan and define the programmeCapture customer requirements, targets and constraintsDesign goals, preliminary bill of materials, process flow, quality targets
2. Product design and developmentDetailed design, design FMEA, prototypes and design verificationDrawings and specifications, DFMEA, prototype build, design verification results
3. Process design and developmentDesign the manufacturing process, layout, tooling and controlsProcess flow chart, process FMEA, control plan, floor layout
4. Product and process validationTrial production runs, measurement system analysis, capability studiesProduction trial results, capability data, PPAP submission
5. Feedback, assessment and corrective actionRun at rate, reduce variation, capture lessonsImprovement actions, customer satisfaction, lessons learned

Capital projects such as installing a new line follow a parallel path: specification, supplier selection, build, factory acceptance test (FAT) at the supplier, installation, site acceptance test (SAT), commissioning and ramp-up.

Common challenges

  • Tooling and equipment delays. Track long-lead items weekly with suppliers and hold FAT at the supplier to catch problems before shipping.
  • Late design changes. Freeze the design before tooling release and assess every change for tooling, validation and cost impact.
  • Limited shutdown windows. Plan installation in detail and rehearse critical tasks before the shutdown.
  • Slow ramp-up. Train operators and maintenance staff before start of production and plan staged rate increases.
  • Handover to production. Agree acceptance criteria with the plant manager at the start, not at the end.

Software for manufacturing project management

Manufacturers use ERP for materials and costs, PLM for product data and engineering changes, MES for shop floor execution and quality management systems for non-conformances. A project management platform coordinates the cross-functional work: gate checklists, schedules, supplier actions, resources and reporting across NPI and capital projects.

Look for Gantt charts with dependencies, stage-gate templates, approval workflows, resource management and portfolio dashboards. Wrike is the strongest all-in-one choice in our tool rankings for this coordination role; keep detailed product and production data in PLM, ERP and MES.

Frequently asked questions

What is manufacturing project management?

Manufacturing project management is planning and controlling projects that create or change how products are made, such as new product introductions, new lines, plant expansions and improvement programmes.

What are the phases of manufacturing project management?

For new products, many firms use stage-gate or APQP phases: plan and define, product design, process design, validation, and feedback and corrective action.

What is APQP in manufacturing project management?

Advanced Product Quality Planning is a structured framework, widely used in automotive supply chains, for planning product and process development so that parts meet customer requirements.

What is NPI in manufacturing project management?

New product introduction: the process of taking a new product from design into reliable, volume production.

How does Lean apply to manufacturing project management?

Lean principles shape process design and improvement projects by removing waste, improving flow and using tools such as value stream mapping and kaizen events.

What KPIs are used in manufacturing project management?

Time to market, OEE, ramp-up time, first pass yield, scrap and rework, capital spend against budget and gate readiness.

What documents are used in manufacturing project management?

A charter, business case, schedule, WBS, risk register, cost estimate, FMEAs, control plans, acceptance test protocols and change requests.

What software is used for manufacturing project management?

ERP, PLM, MES and quality systems for operational data, plus a platform such as Wrike to coordinate schedules, gates and cross-functional actions.

What is the difference between FAT and SAT in manufacturing project management?

A factory acceptance test checks equipment at the supplier before shipping; a site acceptance test checks it after installation in your plant.

Which certifications help in manufacturing project management?

The PMP or PRINCE2 for project control, Lean and Six Sigma belts for improvement work and knowledge of quality standards such as ISO 9001 or IATF 16949.

How do you manage risk in manufacturing project management?

Use FMEAs for product and process risks, a project risk register for schedule and supply risks, and gate reviews to confirm readiness before major commitments.
