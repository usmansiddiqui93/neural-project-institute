Marketing Project Management: Phases, Roles, Tools and Tips

Marketing project management is the planning, coordination and control of marketing work, from single assets to multi-channel campaigns, so that it launches on time, on brand, within budget and against a measurable business goal. It differs from most project work because deadlines are fixed by the market, many people review the same assets, and the work rarely stops: one campaign launches while the next is being briefed.

Key takeaways

  • Marketing project management handles many overlapping campaigns with fixed dates and heavy review cycles.
  • A signed brief, one intake queue and capped review rounds solve most delivery problems.
  • Track delivery KPIs (on-time rate, cycle time) separately from campaign results.
  • Most teams use a hybrid: milestone plans for launches, Kanban or sprints for production.

What makes marketing project management different

A marketing team runs dozens of small, overlapping projects rather than a few large ones. The real constraints are rarely technical; they are attention, approvals and dates that cannot move, such as a product launch, a trade show or a seasonal sale.

  • Fixed launch dates: the date is usually set before the scope is known, so scope and quality are what flex.
  • Many reviewers: brand, legal, product, regional teams and senior leaders all want to approve the same assets, and review rounds are where most schedules slip.
  • Shared specialists: designers, copywriters, video editors and web developers are split across many campaigns at once, so capacity, not budget, is often the binding constraint.
  • Compliance: regulated sectors (financial services, healthcare, alcohol, gambling) need claims checked and approvals recorded before anything goes live.
  • Measurement after launch: a campaign is not finished at launch; it is optimised and reported on, and the results feed the next brief.

Agile ideas help with the volume and change; see Agile marketing for how teams run sprints and backlogs. This guide focuses on how marketing projects are actually planned and delivered.

The marketing campaign life cycle

PhaseWhat happensMain output
1. Request and intakeA stakeholder asks for work; the team checks fit, priority and capacityApproved request
2. BriefObjective, audience, message, channels, budget, mandatory items and success measures are agreedCampaign or creative brief
3. PlanningDeliverables are listed, tasks and owners assigned, review steps and dates set back from the launch dateCampaign plan and schedule
4. Creative developmentConcepts, copy, design, video and landing pages are producedDraft assets
5. Review and approvalRounds of feedback from stakeholders, brand and legal or complianceApproved assets
6. LaunchAssets are trafficked to channels, tracking checked and the campaign goes liveLive campaign
7. Optimise and reportPerformance is monitored, budgets shifted between channels and results reportedResults report and lessons
Tip

Plan backwards from the launch date. Put the final approval at least a few working days before go-live, then set each review round and production step back from there. If the dates do not fit, cut deliverables early rather than squeezing the review time.

Key roles in marketing projects

RoleWhat they do
Marketing project manager or campaign managerOwns the plan, timeline, status and the flow of work between specialists
Requester or business ownerProduct, sales or regional lead who needs the campaign and signs off the brief
Marketing leader (CMO or head of marketing)Sets priorities across campaigns and resolves conflicts over capacity
Content and copyWrites and edits copy, articles, emails and scripts
Design and videoProduces visual and motion assets
Channel specialistsPaid media, email, social, SEO and web teams who launch and optimise
Marketing operationsRuns the martech stack, tracking, data and workflow tooling
Brand, legal and compliance reviewersApprove claims, trademarks, disclaimers and brand use

A RACI matrix is especially useful for approvals: it makes clear who must approve an asset and who only needs to be informed, which cuts review rounds.

Methodologies that fit marketing

ApproachWhen to use it
KanbanOngoing request-driven work such as content production, social and design support; limit work in progress so assets finish
Scrum or Agile sprintsTeams that can plan in two-week cycles and test and learn, such as growth and digital teams; see Agile marketing
WaterfallLarge, fixed-date launches with long lead-time items like print, events or TV
HybridThe most common pattern: a fixed milestone plan for the launch with Kanban or sprints for the creative work inside it

Whatever the method, make work visible on one board or plan, set clear intake rules, and agree the number of review rounds up front.

KPIs for marketing project management

Separate delivery KPIs (how well the team runs projects) from campaign KPIs (how well the campaign performed). Both matter.

KPIWhat it measuresTypical use
On-time launch rateShare of campaigns or assets delivered by the agreed dateHeadline delivery health
Review rounds per assetNumber of feedback cycles before approvalFinding approval bottlenecks
Cycle timeDays from approved brief to live assetSetting realistic timelines and SLAs
ThroughputAssets or campaigns completed per week or monthCapacity planning
Work in progressItems started but not finishedSpotting overloaded teams
Budget varianceActual spend against planMedia and production cost control
Campaign ROI or cost per leadReturn or cost against the campaign goalJudging the campaign itself

Common challenges and how to handle them

ChallengeHow to handle it
Requests arrive from everywhereOne intake form and one queue; triage weekly against agreed priorities
Endless feedback roundsName approvers in the brief, consolidate feedback into one round per stage, cap the rounds
Last-minute changesUse a simple change request for anything that changes scope after the brief is signed
Too much work in progressSet WIP limits and say no or later to new requests when the team is full
Unclear goalsRefuse to start without a signed brief that includes a measurable objective
No time to learnHold a short retrospective after every major campaign and act on one change

Frequently asked questions

What is marketing project management?

Marketing project management is planning and controlling marketing work, from single assets to campaigns, so it launches on time, on budget and against a measurable goal.

What does a marketing project manager do in marketing project management?

They run intake, turn briefs into plans, schedule specialists, chase reviews, track budget and report status until the campaign is live and reported on.

How is marketing project management different from general project management?

It deals with many small overlapping projects, fixed launch dates, heavy review and approval cycles and shared creative capacity.

What are the phases of marketing project management?

Typically intake, brief, planning, creative development, review and approval, launch, then optimisation and reporting.

Is Agile good for marketing project management?

Yes for request-driven and test-and-learn work. Many teams use Kanban or sprints for production and keep a milestone plan for big launches; see Agile marketing.

What templates help with marketing project management?

A creative brief, campaign plan, content calendar, work request form, Gantt chart and budget template cover most needs.

Which KPIs matter in marketing project management?

On-time launch rate, review rounds per asset, cycle time, throughput, budget variance and the campaign's own ROI or cost per lead.

How do you reduce approval delays in marketing project management?

Name approvers in the brief, consolidate feedback, cap review rounds and use a tool with built-in proofing so comments sit on the asset.

What is the best software for marketing project management?

One that combines intake forms, proofing, calendars and workload views; Wrike is a strong all-in-one option, used alongside DAM and marketing automation tools.

How do you prioritise requests in marketing project management?

Use one intake queue and score requests against business goals, deadline and effort, then review priorities weekly with the marketing leader.

Do you need a certification for marketing project management?

Not usually. Practical experience matters most, though a general project management certification or Agile training helps.
