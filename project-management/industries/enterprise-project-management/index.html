Enterprise Project Management (EPM): Phases, Roles and Tools

Enterprise project management (EPM) is the practice of managing all of an organisation's projects as one coordinated system, using shared governance, standard methods, common tools and a single view of the portfolio and its resources. It sits above individual project management: the question is not only 'is this project on track?' but 'are we running the right projects, and do we have the capacity to deliver them?'

Key takeaways

  • Enterprise project management treats all projects as one portfolio with shared governance, methods and tools.
  • Capacity-aware prioritisation is the most valuable EPM decision.
  • Tier governance so small projects are not buried in process.
  • A single platform for intake, resources and reporting makes EPM practical.

What enterprise project management is and what makes it different

In a large organisation, hundreds of projects run at once across departments and countries, competing for the same budget, specialists and management attention. EPM brings them under one framework. It overlaps with project portfolio management and is usually run by an enterprise PMO; see program vs project vs portfolio management for how the levels relate.

  • Scale and complexity. Many projects, programmes and dependencies across business units.
  • Standardisation. Common life cycle, templates, reporting and terminology so projects can be compared.
  • Portfolio decisions. Selecting, prioritising, pausing and stopping projects against strategy and available capacity.
  • Enterprise resource management. Seeing demand and capacity for scarce skills across the whole organisation; see capacity planning.
  • Governance and compliance. Clear approval thresholds, audit trails and controls, often driven by regulation or listed-company requirements.
  • Mixed methods. Agile product teams, Waterfall capital projects and hybrid programmes must all report into one view.

Enterprise project management life cycle

EPM adds a portfolio life cycle around the life cycle of each project:

PhaseWhat happensWho decides
1. Strategy and planningStrategic objectives and investment themes set; annual or quarterly budget envelopes agreedExecutive team
2. Demand intakeIdeas and requests captured through a standard form with rough sizingPMO triage
3. Evaluation and prioritisationBusiness cases scored on value, risk, alignment and resource needPortfolio board or investment committee
4. Approval and resourcingSelected projects funded and staffed; capacity checked against other commitmentsPortfolio board, resource managers
5. Delivery and stage gatesProjects run with their chosen method and report through standard dashboards; gates check continued viabilityProject boards and PMO
6. Portfolio monitoring and rebalancingRegular reviews of health, spend and capacity; projects accelerated, paused or stoppedPortfolio board
7. Closure and benefits trackingProjects closed, benefits measured, lessons fed back into standardsBenefit owners and PMO

Agile organisations run a similar cycle with lean portfolio practices; see agile portfolio management and the Scaled Agile Framework.

Methodologies and frameworks that fit

ApproachBest forNotes
Stage-gate governanceEnterprise-wide control of investmentStandard gates with defined criteria across all projects
PMBOK or PRINCE2 as the base methodConsistent project-level practiceTailor to project size; avoid forcing heavy process on small projects
Portfolio management standardsPortfolio selection and balancingPMI and AXELOS both publish portfolio and programme management standards

Hybrid tieringOrganisations with mixed project typesTier projects by size and risk and set lighter or heavier governance for each tier
Tip

Tier your governance. A two-week internal improvement and a multi-year transformation should not fill in the same twenty-page template.

Essential documents and templates

  • EPM framework or playbook: life cycle, gates, roles, tiers and reporting standards.
  • Intake form: a standard work request template for new demand.
  • Business case template: a consistent structure so investments can be compared.
  • Project charter template: standard authorisation for approved projects.
  • Standard status report: consistent health indicators across the portfolio; see project status report.
  • RAID log template: shared format for risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies, including cross-project dependencies.
  • Project tracker template: a portfolio-level list of projects, owners, stage and health.
  • Skills matrix: supports enterprise resource planning.
  • Lessons learned template: a central repository that informs future projects.

Common challenges and how to handle them

  • Too many projects. Make capacity a hard constraint in prioritisation; starting fewer projects usually finishes more.
  • Bureaucracy. Tier governance and remove steps that do not change decisions.
  • Inconsistent data. Standardise a small set of mandatory fields and automate reporting from the tool rather than collecting spreadsheets.
  • Hidden dependencies. Track cross-project dependencies explicitly at portfolio level.
  • Agile and traditional teams in one portfolio. Report on outcomes and value delivered, and let each team keep its own delivery method.
  • PMO seen as overhead. Show value through faster decisions, better resource visibility and lessons that prevent repeat failures.

Software for enterprise project management

EPM depends on a single source of truth. Spreadsheet-based portfolios rarely scale. Look for:

  • Portfolio and programme hierarchies with roll-up reporting.
  • Enterprise resource management with capacity and demand views.
  • Configurable intake, approval workflows and stage gates.
  • Support for both Agile and Gantt-based project styles.
  • Enterprise security: single sign-on, granular permissions, audit logs, data residency.
  • Integrations with finance, HR, IT service management and development tools.

Wrike is one of the strongest all-in-one options for EPM, combining portfolio views, resource management, request forms, approvals and enterprise security in one platform. Frequently asked questions

What is enterprise project management?

Enterprise project management is managing all of an organisation's projects as one coordinated system with shared governance, standard methods, common tools and a single portfolio view.

What is the difference between enterprise project management and project portfolio management?

Portfolio management focuses on selecting and balancing projects; enterprise project management is broader, also covering standard methods, tools, resources and governance for every project.

What does an enterprise project management office do?

An EPMO sets project standards, runs portfolio governance and reporting, supports resource planning and builds project management capability; see PMO.

What are the benefits of enterprise project management?

Better alignment of projects with strategy, clearer resource visibility, consistent reporting, earlier detection of failing projects and better investment decisions.

What is enterprise project management software?

EPM software provides portfolio views, resource management, intake and approval workflows and enterprise reporting across all projects; Wrike is a strong all-in-one example.

What are the phases of enterprise project management?

Strategy and planning, demand intake, evaluation and prioritisation, approval and resourcing, delivery with stage gates, portfolio monitoring and closure with benefits tracking.

Which methodology is used in enterprise project management?

Usually a stage-gate governance framework with PMBOK or PRINCE2 at project level, plus scaled Agile approaches such as SAFe for product teams.

What KPIs are used in enterprise project management?

Strategic alignment, portfolio health, benefits realised, resource utilisation, schedule and cost performance and time to approve.

How does Agile fit into enterprise project management?

Agile teams keep their delivery methods while the portfolio funds value streams or products and tracks outcomes; see agile portfolio management.

What are common challenges in enterprise project management?

Too many projects, excessive bureaucracy, inconsistent data, hidden dependencies and resistance to central standards.

How do you implement enterprise project management?

Start with a simple portfolio inventory, standard intake and status reporting, a single tool, then add tiered governance and resource management in stages.
