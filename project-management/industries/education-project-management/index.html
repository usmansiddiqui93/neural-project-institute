Education Project Management: Phases, Roles, Tools and Tips

Education project management is the planning and delivery of projects in schools, colleges, universities and training organisations, such as curriculum redesigns, learning platform rollouts, new courses, building works, accreditation reviews and digital learning programmes. Its defining feature is the academic calendar: most changes must land at the start of a term or year, and teaching cannot be disrupted along the way.

  • 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Education project management is planned backwards from term dates and committee cycles.
  • Involving teachers and students in design is the best defence against resistance.
  • Use ADDIE for course design, phased plans for buildings and systems, and Kanban or Agile for ongoing digital work.
  • Keep methods simple because many education projects are led part-time.

What makes education project management different

  • The academic calendar is a hard constraint. A system that misses the start of term may wait a whole year. Holidays are the main window for building work and IT changes.
  • Shared governance. Universities in particular make decisions through committees, senates and boards, which meet on fixed cycles and can add months to approval.
  • Academic autonomy. Teaching staff expect a say in changes that affect their teaching; mandated change without consultation often fails.
  • Many stakeholder groups. Students, parents, teachers, support staff, governors, regulators, inspectors and funders all have interests.
  • Safeguarding and student data. Projects involving children need safeguarding checks, and student records are protected by law (for example FERPA in the US and data protection law in the UK and EU).
  • Accessibility. Learning materials and systems must work for students with disabilities, so accessibility standards such as WCAG should be built into requirements.
  • Limited project capacity. Many schools have no dedicated project managers, so teachers and administrators run projects alongside their day jobs.

Education project life cycle

The life cycle is usually planned backwards from a term start date. For a course or curriculum project, the ADDIE instructional design model (analysis, design, development, implementation, evaluation) is often used inside these phases:

PhaseWhat happensTiming tip
1. Proposal and approvalNeed identified, business case or course proposal approved by the relevant committeeCheck committee dates; they fix the earliest start
2. AnalysisLearner needs, regulatory or accreditation requirements, current stateConsult staff and students before the summer break
3. DesignLearning outcomes, curriculum structure, system or building requirementsAlign with timetabling and enrolment deadlines
4. Development and buildContent creation, platform configuration, procurement, constructionBuilding and infrastructure work in holidays
5. Pilot and trainingPilot with a small group, train staff, prepare guidance for students and parentsTrain staff before they are busy with term start
6. LaunchGo-live at term start with extra supportPlan a support rota for the first weeks of term
7. Evaluation and closureStudent feedback, results, adoption data, lessons learnedEvaluate after a full term or year

Essential documents and templates

  • Project proposal or business case: for committee or governor approval.
  • Project charter: purpose, scope, sponsor and success measures.
  • Project schedule or Gantt chart: mapped to term dates, committee meetings and holidays.
  • RACI matrix: who designs, approves and delivers each part.
  • Training plan: staff training before launch.
  • Communication plan: staff, students, parents and governors.
  • Risk register: including safeguarding, data protection and term-start risks.
  • Budget: aligned with the institution's financial year, which may differ from the academic year.
  • Lessons learned: reviewed before the next intake or rollout.

Common challenges and how to handle them

  • Immovable deadlines. Plan backwards from term start, build in a buffer, and define a minimum viable launch in case something slips.
  • Slow approvals. Put committee dates in the plan and prepare papers well ahead.
  • Staff resistance and workload. Involve teachers in design, release time for project work and avoid launching several changes in the same term.
  • Part-time project managers. Keep the method simple, such as a board and a one-page plan, and protect their project time.
  • Budget cycles. Capital and revenue budgets may be fixed annually; align purchases with funding windows.
  • Multiple initiatives. A small PMO or improvement office helps prioritise; see project management office (PMO).

Software for education project management

Education teams use learning management systems, student information systems and timetabling tools for operations, plus a project tool for planning change. Look for:

  • Simple boards for part-time teams and timelines for term-based plans.
  • Education or nonprofit pricing.
  • Guest access for external partners and contractors.
  • Single sign-on with your institution's identity system.
  • Data protection terms suitable for any student data, though student records should stay in the student system.

For universities and multi-academy trusts running many projects, Wrike is a strong all-in-one option for portfolio dashboards, Gantt plans and request forms. Smaller schools may prefer a simpler board tool; see our tool rankings.

Frequently asked questions

What is education project management?

Education project management is the planning and delivery of projects in schools, colleges and universities, such as new courses, learning platforms and building works, around the academic calendar.

What does a project manager do in education project management?

An education project manager plans and coordinates change projects, manages approvals, budgets and risks, and makes sure staff and students are ready for launch.

Why is the academic calendar important in education project management?

Most changes must go live at the start of a term or year and building work must fit into holidays, so missing a deadline can delay a project by months.

What is ADDIE in education project management?

ADDIE is an instructional design model of analysis, design, development, implementation and evaluation, often used to manage course development projects.

Which methodology is best for education project management?

Phased approaches suit building and system projects, ADDIE suits course design, and Agile or Kanban suit digital content and ongoing improvement work.

What are the phases of education project management?

Proposal and approval, analysis, design, development, pilot and training, launch, and evaluation and closure.

What KPIs are used in education project management?

Readiness for term start, staff training completion, adoption, student satisfaction, attainment or retention changes, budget variance and post-launch support tickets.

What are common challenges in education project management?

Fixed term-start deadlines, slow committee approvals, staff workload and resistance, part-time project leads and annual budget cycles.

Do teachers need education project management skills?

Many teachers lead projects such as curriculum changes and trips, so basic planning, risk and stakeholder skills are valuable; see project management skills.

What software is used for education project management?

Institutions combine their learning and student systems with a project tool; Wrike suits larger portfolios and simple board tools suit small teams.

How do you manage stakeholders in education project management?

Map staff, students, parents, governors and regulators, consult early, and use a communication plan with tailored messages for each group.
