Energy Project Management: Phases, Roles, Tools and Tips

Energy project management is the planning and delivery of projects that produce, transmit or store energy, including wind and solar farms, battery storage, power stations, grid connections, pipelines and oil and gas facilities. These projects are capital-intensive, long-lived and heavily regulated, so the industry relies on formal stage-gate decisions, rigorous safety management and detailed engineering before construction begins.

Key takeaways

  • Energy project management is driven by stage-gate investment decisions and front-end definition.
  • Permits, grid connections and long-lead equipment often decide the schedule.
  • Safety performance is reported first on every energy project.
  • Specialist scheduling, cost and HSE tools sit alongside a work management platform.

What makes energy project management different

  • Large capital and long horizons: decisions made early fix most of the lifetime cost, so front-end planning gets serious investment.
  • Permits and consents: planning permission, environmental assessments, grid connection agreements and licences can take longer than construction.
  • Health, safety and environment (HSE): high-hazard work means permit-to-work systems, safety cases and strict contractor management.
  • Financing: many projects, particularly renewables, are project-financed, so lenders and their technical advisers scrutinise the schedule, contracts and risks.
  • Grid and market dependencies: grid connection dates, power purchase agreements, subsidy auctions and commodity prices shape the business case.
  • Remote and harsh sites: offshore, desert and mountain locations bring weather windows, logistics and specialist vessels or cranes.

Energy project phases

Large oil, gas and power companies commonly use a stage-gate model with names such as Appraise, Select, Define, Execute and Operate. Renewable developers use a development-led sequence. Both are shown below.

Stage-gate (capital projects)Renewables developmentKey decision or output
AppraiseSite origination and feasibilityIs the opportunity worth pursuing?
SelectDevelopment: land, surveys, permits, grid applicationPreferred concept or consented project
Define (FEED)Design, procurement strategy, financingFinal investment decision (FID) or financial close
ExecuteConstruction and installation (often EPC)Mechanical completion
Commission and hand overTesting and energisationCommercial operation date (COD)
OperateOperations and maintenance (O&M)Performance against the business case

Front-end engineering design (FEED) defines the facility well enough to estimate cost accurately and tender the main contracts. Cost estimates typically progress through recognised maturity classes, such as the AACE estimate classes, as definition improves.

Tip

Protect the front end. Rushing FEED or development to hit an investment date usually moves the problems into construction, where changes cost far more.

Essential documents and templates

  • Business case and feasibility: business case template and feasibility study.
  • Project charter or execution plan: project charter template.
  • Scope and WBS: work breakdown structure template.
  • Integrated schedule: Gantt chart template and critical path template; large projects use specialist scheduling tools.
  • Cost estimate and budget: project cost estimate template and budget template.
  • Risk register: risk register template, covering technical, permitting, market and HSE risks.
  • Procurement: RFQ template for equipment and services.
  • Changes: change request template for formal management of change.
  • Permits register, safety case, commissioning plan and handover dossier: industry-specific documents required before operation.

Common challenges and how to handle them

  • Permitting delays: start environmental surveys and consultations early and track consents on the critical path.
  • Grid connection timing: secure connection agreements early and plan around the grid operator's works.
  • Long-lead equipment and supply chain: order transformers, turbines and other long-lead items early and monitor manufacturing progress.
  • Weather windows: schedule offshore and lifting work around seasonal limits with contingency.
  • Interface risk between contractors: define interfaces clearly in contracts and run an interface register.
  • Cost overruns: invest in front-end definition, use probabilistic estimates and apply strict change control.

Software for energy projects

Energy projects run on a stack: enterprise scheduling tools for large critical-path schedules, cost control and earned value systems, document control and engineering data management, and HSE systems for permits and incidents. Skills and certifications

Energy project managers need commercial and contract knowledge, an understanding of engineering, strong HSE awareness and project controls skills. The PMP certification is widely recognised, and many organisations also expect discipline-specific safety training.

Frequently asked questions

What is energy project management?

It is planning and delivering projects that generate, transmit or store energy, from wind and solar farms to power plants, grids and oil and gas facilities.

What are the phases of energy project management?

Commonly Appraise, Select, Define, Execute and Operate, or for renewables: origination, development, financing, construction, commissioning and operation.

What is FEED in energy project management?

Front-end engineering design: detailed early engineering that defines the facility and supports an accurate estimate and the investment decision.

What is EPC in energy project management?

Engineering, procurement and construction: a contract where one contractor designs, buys and builds the facility, often for a fixed price.

How is safety handled in energy project management?

Through a safety management system, permits to work, risk assessments, contractor HSE requirements and close tracking of incident rates.

Which KPIs matter in energy project management?

Safety incident rates, cost performance index, schedule performance, estimate at completion, consent status and commissioning progress.

How does renewable energy project management differ from oil and gas?

Renewables projects are usually smaller and quicker and are often driven by permitting, grid connection and financing; oil and gas projects are larger and more engineering-heavy.

Is Agile used in energy project management?

Mainly for digital, software and smart-grid work. Physical construction uses stage-gates, critical path scheduling and earned value.

What templates are useful in energy project management?

Business case, project charter, WBS, Gantt and critical path, cost estimate, risk register, RFQ and change request templates.

What software is used for energy project management?

Enterprise scheduling, cost control, document control and HSE systems, with a platform such as Wrike for the owner's team and portfolios.

What certifications help in energy project management?

The PMP is widely recognised, alongside industry safety training and technical knowledge of the relevant energy sector.
