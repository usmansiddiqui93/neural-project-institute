Construction Project Management: Phases, Roles, Tools and Tips

Construction project management is the planning, coordination and control of a building or civil works project from the first idea to handover, so that it is delivered safely, to the agreed quality, on time and within budget. It differs from most project work because the output is a one-off physical asset built on a specific site, by a temporary team of separate firms bound together by contracts.

Key takeaways

  • Construction project management runs a one-off physical asset through design, procurement, construction and handover with a temporary team of separate firms.
  • The contract and procurement route decide who carries risk, so the project manager must understand them as well as the programme.
  • Most projects are hybrid: stage-gated overall, Lean planning on site and iterative loops in design.
  • Use specialist field and BIM tools on site and a platform such as Wrike for planning, approvals and reporting across projects.

What makes construction project management different

Every construction project is a prototype: a new site, a new design and usually a new mix of designers, contractors and suppliers. The project manager has to align those firms through contracts rather than line management, while working within constraints that office-based projects rarely face.

  • Safety and law. Construction is a high-hazard industry, so health and safety planning is a legal duty, not an optional extra. In Great Britain, for example, the CDM Regulations 2015 assign specific duties to the client, the principal designer and the principal contractor.
  • Permits and approvals. Planning permission (or zoning approval), building regulations or building code sign-off, and utility connections all sit on the critical path.
  • Contracts drive behaviour. The procurement route and contract form (design-bid-build, design and build, construction management, NEC, JCT, FIDIC, AIA) decide who carries which risk and how change is valued.
  • Physical sequence. Work follows a hard logic: ground before frame, frame before envelope, envelope before fit-out. You cannot finish the roof in sprint one and the foundations in sprint five.
  • Weather, ground and supply chains. Unknown ground conditions, weather and long-lead items such as switchgear, lifts and façade panels are major sources of delay.
  • Many stakeholders. Client, funders, end users, neighbours, local authorities, insurers and utility companies all have a say.

Phases of a construction project

Different countries name the stages differently (the UK uses the RIBA Plan of Work, the US commonly uses AIA design phases), but the flow is broadly the same.

PhaseWhat happensKey outputs
1. Pre-design (inception and feasibility)Client defines the need, budget and site; options and feasibility studies are testedBusiness case, feasibility study, project brief, outline budget
2. DesignConcept, developed and technical design by the architect and engineers; planning or zoning applicationDesign drawings and specifications, planning consent, cost plan updates
3. PreconstructionBuildability reviews, programme development, logistics and site set-up planning, risk and health and safety planningConstruction programme, logistics plan, construction phase plan, risk register
4. ProcurementTendering main contract and trade packages, evaluating bids, placing orders for long-lead itemsTender documents, signed contracts, procurement schedule
5. ConstructionSite works from enabling works and substructure through frame, envelope, services and finishesProgress reports, RFIs, submittals, variations, valuations, inspection records
6. Commissioning and closeoutTesting and commissioning of services, snagging (punch list), handover documents, practical completion, defects periodO&M manuals, as-built drawings, health and safety file, completion certificate, final account
Tip

Agree at the start which stage-naming system everyone will use. A client who says 'Stage 4' and a contractor who says 'construction documents' may be talking about different things.

Client-side project manager (owner's representative)Represents the client, manages the design team and contractor, controls scope, budget and programme
Architect / lead designerLeads the design, coordinates other designers, often administers the contract on smaller jobs

Cost consultant / quantity surveyorPrepares cost plans, manages tendering, values work done and variations, agrees the final account
Main contractorPlans and builds the works, manages subcontractors, runs the site
Contractor's project manager and site managerRun the programme, labour, materials, quality and safety on site
Subcontractors and suppliersDeliver trade packages such as steelwork, M&E, drylining and finishes

Use a RACI matrix to make approvals explicit: who signs off design changes, who can instruct a variation and who accepts a completed package.

Methodologies that fit construction

ApproachWhen it fitsHow it is used
Waterfall / stage-gateAlmost all building projects at the overall levelFixed design stages with client sign-off before moving on; see waterfall project management
Critical path methodProgrammes with hundreds of linked activitiesIdentifies the sequence that sets the completion date; see critical path method
Lean construction and the Last Planner SystemSites with many interdependent tradesTrades plan collaboratively, make weekly work plans and track per cent plan complete (PPC); see lean project management
AgileDesign phase, fit-out packages and digital work around the projectShort design cycles with frequent client reviews; see Agile in construction
Earned value managementLarge or publicly funded projectsMeasures progress and cost against the baseline; see earned value management

Most real projects are hybrid: a stage-gated master programme with Lean planning on site and short iterative loops in design.

KPIWhat it measuresTypical use
Schedule performance index (SPI)Earned value divided by planned valueEarly warning that the programme is slipping
Cost performance index (CPI)Earned value divided by actual costForecasting the final cost; see cost performance index
Change orders as % of contract valueValue of approved variations against the original sumDesign quality and scope control
RFI turnaround timeDays from RFI raised to answerDesign team responsiveness
Per cent plan complete (PPC)Share of committed weekly tasks completedReliability of short-term planning under Last Planner
Accident frequency rateReportable incidents per hours workedSafety performance on site
Snag / punch list closure rateDefects closed against defects raisedReadiness for handover

Report these in a weekly project status report and a monthly client report with an S-curve of planned against actual spend.

Common challenges and how to handle them

  • Design changes after work starts. Freeze design by package, price every change before it is instructed and route it through change control.
  • Long-lead items arriving late. Build a procurement schedule backwards from the date each item is needed on site and place early orders where the contract allows.
  • Unforeseen ground conditions. Commission site investigation early and hold a measured contingency; see contingency plan.
  • Trade clashes on site. Use BIM clash detection in design and weekly coordination meetings on site.
  • Cash flow and disputes. Keep valuation, variation and payment records up to date and agree the final account progressively, not at the end.
  • Poor handover. Plan commissioning, training and O&M documentation from preconstruction rather than leaving them to the final weeks.

Construction project management software

Construction teams usually combine two kinds of software. Specialist construction platforms handle field work: drawings and document control, RFIs and submittals, site diaries, inspections and snagging on tablets, plus BIM model coordination and specialist scheduling tools for large critical path programmes. A general project management platform then sits above them for the client-side portfolio, design management, approvals, reporting and the office work that surrounds every job.

  • Gantt charts with dependencies and baselines.
  • Request and approval workflows for changes and design sign-off.
  • Dashboards that roll up many projects for directors and clients.
  • Access for external partners such as consultants and subcontractors.
  • Integration with document storage and finance systems.

For that coordination layer, Wrike is the strongest all-in-one option in our tool rankings, with Gantt charts, workload views, custom request forms and cross-project dashboards. Pair it with the field and BIM tools your contractors already use rather than trying to make one product do everything.

Frequently asked questions

What is construction project management?

Construction project management is the planning, coordination and control of a building or civil works project from inception to handover, so it is delivered safely, to quality, on time and within budget.

What are the phases of construction project management?

A common breakdown is pre-design, design, preconstruction, procurement, construction, and commissioning and closeout. The UK RIBA Plan of Work and US AIA phases use different names for similar stages.

What does a construction project management plan include?

Scope and objectives, the procurement strategy, the construction programme, cost plan, risk register, health and safety arrangements, quality plan, communication plan and change control procedure.

What is the difference between construction management and construction project management?

Construction management often refers to a specific procurement route where a construction manager coordinates separate trade contracts for the client. Construction project management is the broader discipline of running the whole project.

Is Agile used in construction project management?

Yes, mostly in design, fit-out and planning cycles, often alongside Lean methods such as the Last Planner System. The overall project usually stays stage-gated. See Agile in construction.

What is the Last Planner System in construction project management?

A Lean planning method in which the people who will do the work (the last planners) make reliable weekly commitments, check constraints in advance and measure per cent plan complete.

Which KPIs matter most in construction project management?

Schedule and cost performance (SPI and CPI), change orders as a share of contract value, RFI turnaround, safety rates and snag closure are among the most widely used.

What software is used for construction project management?

Most firms combine specialist construction software for field work, document control and BIM with a general project management platform such as Wrike for portfolio planning, approvals and reporting.

What certifications help in construction project management?

Common choices include the PMP, PRINCE2, membership of the Chartered Institute of Building (CIOB), the CMAA's Certified Construction Manager credential in the US and Lean construction training.

How does risk management work in construction project management?

Risks are identified by phase, given owners and responses, priced where possible and reviewed at regular meetings. Contract forms such as NEC also use early warnings to surface risks quickly.

What is a change order in construction project management?

A change order (or variation) is a formal instruction that changes the scope, cost or time of the contract works. It should be priced and approved before the work is done.

What does closeout involve in construction project management?

Testing and commissioning, snagging, handover of O&M manuals and as-built drawings, practical completion, the defects period and agreement of the final account.
