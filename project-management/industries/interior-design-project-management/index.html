Interior Design Project Management: Phases, FF&E and Tools

Interior design project management is how a designer or studio runs a project from the client brief through concept, design, documentation, procurement and installation to the final reveal. Its defining challenge is managing hundreds of individual items, from furniture and lighting to fabrics and finishes, each with its own approval, price, lead time and delivery, while keeping the client happy and the studio profitable.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 29 August 2026
  • 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Interior design project management is shaped by hundreds of individual products, each with an approval, price and lead time.
  • Work backwards from the install date to set must-order dates for every item.
  • Clear sign-offs and a change log protect the budget and the studio's fee.
  • Use specialist FF&E tools for product data and a platform such as Wrike when you need a multi-project view.

What makes interior design project management different

  • Product-heavy scope. A single project can involve hundreds of furniture, fixtures and equipment (FF&E) items, each needing selection, approval, ordering, tracking and installation.
  • Lead times set the date. Bespoke joinery, upholstery and imported items can take many weeks, so ordering dates drive the programme.
  • Taste is subjective. Approvals depend on client preference, so clear sign-off of samples and boards is essential.
  • Money flows through the studio. Many studios buy on the client's behalf, handle deposits and markups, and carry the risk of mistakes.
  • Coordination with trades. Contractors, joiners, electricians, decorators and installers must work in the right order, often in an occupied home or a live workplace.
  • Regulation. Fire ratings for fabrics and finishes, accessibility and building rules apply, especially in commercial and hospitality projects.

Tip

Give every FF&E item a 'must order by' date calculated from its lead time and the install date. Sort the schedule by that date each week.

Common challenges

  • Slow client decisions. Present a limited number of options and set approval deadlines tied to order dates.
  • Discontinued or delayed items. Hold approved alternatives for critical pieces and track shipments weekly.
  • Budget creep from upgrades. Show the cost of every change before it is approved; see scope creep.
  • Trade sequencing in occupied spaces. Agree working hours, protection and access in advance and share a simple weekly plan.
  • Cash flow. Collect deposits before placing orders and agree payment terms in the contract.

Software for interior design project management

Many studios use specialist interior design software for product libraries, FF&E specifications, client approvals, purchase orders and invoicing, alongside CAD or 3D tools for drawings and visuals. A general project management platform is useful when the studio runs many projects at once, needs resourcing across designers, or works on commercial fit-outs with larger teams.

Look for timeline views, custom fields for item data, approval workflows and client-friendly sharing. Wrike is the strongest all-in-one option in our tool rankings for studios that need that portfolio view; smaller residential studios may find a specialist interior design tool covers most of their needs.

Frequently asked questions

What is interior design project management?

Interior design project management is how a designer or studio runs a project from brief and concept to procurement, installation and handover, while controlling budget, schedule and client approvals.

What are the phases of interior design project management?

Discovery and brief, concept design, design development, documentation, procurement, construction and installation, and handover.

What is FF&E in interior design project management?

FF&E stands for furniture, fixtures and equipment: the movable and installed items specified, ordered and installed by the design team.

How do you manage lead times in interior design project management?

Calculate a must-order-by date for each item from its lead time and the install date, then track orders weekly against those dates.

What documents are used in interior design project management?

A design brief, fee proposal and budget, project schedule, FF&E schedule and tracker, drawings and specifications, change log and close-out pack.

What KPIs matter in interior design project management?

Budget variance, hours against fee, items ordered on time, approval turnaround, delivery issues and snags at handover.

What software is used for interior design project management?

Specialist interior design tools for FF&E and purchasing, CAD and 3D tools, and a project platform such as Wrike for multi-project planning and resourcing.

Who handles interior design project management in a studio?

A project designer or project manager, supported by a procurement coordinator and overseen by the principal designer.

How do you handle client changes in interior design project management?

Record the change, show its cost and time impact, get written approval and update the budget and FF&E schedule.

What is the difference between Cat A and Cat B in interior design project management?

Cat A is a landlord's base fit-out of an office; Cat B is the tenant's fit-out designed around its own needs, often led by the interior designer.

Can Kanban help with interior design project management?

Yes. A Kanban board is an easy way to see every item move from quote to approval, order, delivery and installation.
