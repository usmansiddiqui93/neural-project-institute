Real Estate Project Management: Phases, Roles, KPIs and Tools

Real estate project management is the coordination of a property development or major property improvement from site acquisition and appraisal through planning, financing, construction, leasing or sales, to a stabilised, income-producing or sold asset. Where construction project management focuses on building the asset, real estate project management keeps the investment case at the centre of every decision.

Key takeaways

  • Real estate project management keeps the investment case at the centre of every delivery decision.
  • The life cycle runs from acquisition and appraisal through planning, finance and construction to leasing and exit.
  • Time is money: finance costs and lost income make delays expensive.
  • Use appraisal tools for the numbers and a platform such as Wrike to coordinate advisers and approvals.

What makes real estate project management different

  • The return is the goal. Success is judged on profit, yield and value, not only on time and budget.
  • Finance shapes the timeline. Debt and equity are drawn against milestones, and interest accrues every month the project runs.
  • Market risk. Rents, sale prices, yields and demand can change between purchase and completion.
  • Planning and legal risk. Zoning or planning permission, title, rights of light, easements and leases must all be resolved.
  • Many advisers. Agents, lawyers, valuers, cost consultants, designers and contractors each work on a separate appointment.
  • Revenue workstreams. Leasing or sales marketing runs alongside construction and must be timed to completion.

The same discipline also applies to programmes of work on existing portfolios, such as refurbishments, energy upgrades and tenant fit-outs.

Methodologies and approaches

ApproachUse in real estate
Stage-gate investment approvalsBoard or investment committee approval before each major commitment (purchase, planning submission, contract award)
Waterfall deliveryDesign and construction phases with formal sign-offs
Portfolio managementPrioritising multiple schemes for capital; see program vs project vs portfolio management
KanbanTracking leasing deals, tenant fit-outs and small capex works through stages on a board; see Kanban boards

Tip

Link every scope or programme decision back to the appraisal. A two-month delay can matter more than a small cost saving once finance costs and lost rent are counted.

Common challenges

  • Planning uncertainty. Hold pre-application discussions, build in contingency for conditions and agreements and use conditional purchase contracts where possible.
  • Cost inflation. Fix prices for major packages early and keep a realistic contingency.
  • Market shifts. Run sensitivities and set trigger points to pause, phase or change the scheme.
  • Adviser coordination. Keep a single programme and action log across all appointments.
  • Lender requirements. Agree drawdown and reporting processes before construction starts.

Software for real estate project management

Real estate teams use appraisal and financial modelling tools, construction platforms for site delivery, and property or asset management systems once the building is let. A project management platform brings the workstreams together: acquisition checklists, planning tasks, adviser actions, approvals and investor reporting across a portfolio.

For that cross-scheme coordination, Wrike is the strongest all-in-one option in our tool rankings, thanks to portfolio dashboards, custom fields for scheme data and approval workflows. Keep financial models in your appraisal tool and link them in.

Frequently asked questions

What is real estate project management?

Real estate project management coordinates a property development or improvement from acquisition and appraisal through planning, finance, construction and leasing or sales to a stabilised or sold asset.

What are the phases of real estate project management?

Sourcing and acquisition, feasibility and appraisal, planning, design and procurement, financing, construction, leasing or sales, and hold or exit.

How is real estate project management different from construction project management?

Construction project management focuses on building the asset; real estate project management also manages acquisition, finance, planning, leasing and investment returns.

What does a development manager do in real estate project management?

They run the project for the developer from acquisition to exit, coordinating advisers, approvals, finance and delivery.

What KPIs are used in real estate project management?

Profit on cost, IRR, yield on cost, cost to complete, pre-let or pre-sales levels, lease-up velocity and programme variance.

What documents are needed for real estate project management?

A business case and appraisal, development budget, programme, risk register, due diligence tracker, change log and investor reports.

What software is used for real estate project management?

Appraisal and modelling tools, construction platforms, property management systems and a coordination platform such as Wrike.

What are the biggest risks in real estate project management?

Planning refusal or delay, cost inflation, market changes in rents or values, finance availability and legal or title issues.

Does real estate project management use Agile?

The core delivery is stage-gated, but Kanban boards work well for leasing pipelines, tenant fit-outs and small capital works.

What skills does real estate project management require?

Financial literacy, negotiation, knowledge of planning and property law, adviser coordination and construction awareness.

How does finance affect real estate project management?

Debt and equity are released against milestones and interest accrues over time, so delays directly reduce returns.
