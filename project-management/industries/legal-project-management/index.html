Legal Project Management: Process, Roles, Tools and Tips

Legal project management (LPM) is the application of project management discipline to legal matters, such as litigation, transactions, regulatory work and investigations, so that they are scoped, staffed, budgeted and delivered predictably. Law firms and in-house legal teams use it mainly to give clients cost certainty, support fixed and capped fees, and reduce wasted effort, while lawyers keep full control of legal judgement.

Key takeaways

  • Legal project management brings scope, budgets and planning to legal matters without replacing legal judgement.
  • Define phases and written assumptions at instruction; they underpin every budget conversation.
  • Consistent phase and task coding is what makes future estimates accurate.
  • Report spend against budget often so clients are never surprised.

What makes legal project management different

  • Uncertain path: the other side, the court or a regulator can change the course of a matter at any time.
  • Deadlines set by others: court timetables, limitation periods and completion dates are often fixed externally.
  • Professional duties: confidentiality, privilege, conflicts and regulatory rules sit above any efficiency goal.
  • Billing culture: many matters are still billed by the hour, but clients increasingly ask for budgets, caps and fixed fees.
  • Leverage: work is spread across partners, associates, paralegals and support staff, so delegating each task to the right level drives both cost and quality.

Matter phases

Each matter type has its own recognisable phases. Defining them up front is the foundation of LPM because budgets, staffing and reporting all follow the phases.

Litigation (typical)Transaction (typical)
Case assessment and strategyInstruction, scoping and term sheet
PleadingsDue diligence
Disclosure (discovery)Drafting and negotiation of documents
Witness and expert evidenceRegulatory approvals and conditions
Trial preparationSigning
Trial or hearingCompletion (closing)
Post-trial, appeal or settlementPost-completion tasks

Standard coding schemes such as the UTBMS (Uniform Task-Based Management System) litigation code set let firms record time by phase and task, which makes budgets and comparisons possible.

Tip

Agree scope assumptions with the client at instruction: for example the number of witnesses, the volume of documents for review or the number of drafting rounds. When reality differs, the assumption is your basis for a fair budget conversation.

Methods that fit legal work

  • Phased planning: define phases and tasks, estimate each and track against it; this is LPM's core and works for both litigation and transactions.
  • Scope and assumptions management: written assumptions and change control when the matter moves beyond them.
  • Checklists and process maps: for repeatable work such as due diligence or contract review; see Lean project management for removing waste from routine work.
  • Kanban: in-house teams use boards to manage the flow of contract requests and advice queries.
  • Agile: occasionally used for legal technology and large regulatory remediation programmes.

Common challenges and fixes

  • Lawyer resistance: present LPM as support for the lead lawyer, keep tools light and show clients' demand for cost predictability.
  • Unpredictable matters: budget by phase, re-estimate at each phase boundary and report assumptions alongside numbers.
  • Poor time data: consistent phase and task codes make future estimates far better.
  • Surprise bills: report spend against budget monthly or more often, and raise overruns before they happen.
  • Repeated work: capture precedents and checklists in a knowledge system.

Software for legal project management

Frequently asked questions

What is legal project management?

Legal project management applies project management methods to legal matters so they are scoped, staffed, budgeted and delivered predictably.

Why is legal project management important?

Clients expect cost certainty and alternative fees, and legal project management makes budgets reliable and work more efficient.

What does a legal project manager do in legal project management?

They build matter plans and budgets, track progress and spend, manage scope assumptions and report to the lead lawyer and client.

What are the phases in legal project management for litigation?

Typically case assessment, pleadings, disclosure, evidence, trial preparation, trial and post-trial or settlement.

How does legal project management work for transactions?

Deals are planned through instruction and scoping, due diligence, drafting and negotiation, approvals, signing and completion.

How does legal project management support fixed fees?

By defining scope and assumptions, staffing efficiently, tracking time by phase and flagging when the matter moves beyond the agreed scope.

What KPIs are used in legal project management?

Budget variance by phase, realisation, leverage, deadline compliance, cycle time, matter outcomes and client satisfaction.

What are UTBMS codes in legal project management?

Standard phase and task codes, such as the litigation code set, used to record legal time consistently for budgeting and billing.

Do in-house teams use legal project management?

Yes. In-house and legal operations teams use it to manage request intake, outside counsel budgets and contract turnaround.

What software supports legal project management?

Matter management, document management, e-billing and e-discovery systems, often with a work platform such as Wrike for planning and intake.

Do lawyers need training in legal project management?

Basic LPM training helps lawyers scope, budget and delegate; a general project management certification suits dedicated legal project managers.
