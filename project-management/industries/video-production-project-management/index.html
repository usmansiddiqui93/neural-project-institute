Video Production Project Management: Phases, Roles and Tools

Video production project management is the planning and control of a video from idea to final delivery: shaping the concept, budgeting and scheduling the shoot, running production days, and managing the edit, review and delivery of masters. It is distinctive because most cost is concentrated in a few expensive shoot days that cannot easily be repeated, so almost all the management effort goes into preparation.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team

  • 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Video production project management concentrates on pre-production because shoot days are costly.
  • Gate the project at treatment, script, rough cut and picture lock.
  • Releases, licences and backups are management tasks, not afterthoughts.
  • Cap review rounds per cut to control post-production costs.

What makes video production project management different

  • Fixed, expensive shoot days: crew, kit, talent and locations are booked for specific days; a lost day is a large cost.
  • Physical logistics: locations, permits, travel, catering, insurance and weather all need planning.
  • Rights and releases: talent, location, music and archive footage need signed releases or licences before the video is used.
  • Iterative post-production: edits go through several cuts with feedback at each, and the number of rounds drives cost.
  • Technical delivery: each platform needs its own formats, aspect ratios, captions and loudness levels.

Tip

Agree when picture lock happens and who signs it off. Changes to the edit after picture lock ripple into grading, sound and graphics and are where post budgets overrun.

Key roles

RoleResponsibility
ProducerOwns the project, the budget and the client relationship
Line producer or production managerRuns the budget and logistics for the shoot: crew, kit, locations, schedule
DirectorLeads the creative vision and directs the talent and crew
Production coordinatorIssues call sheets, books travel and keeps paperwork in order
Director of photography (DoP)Responsible for camera and lighting
Editor, colourist, sound mixer, motion designerPost-production specialists
Post-production supervisorPlans and tracks the post schedule and deliverables
Client or commissionerApproves the treatment, script, cuts and final master

On small corporate or social videos one person may cover several of these roles, but the responsibilities still need an owner.

Methods that fit video projects

  • Sequential phases with gates: video is naturally Waterfall at the top level, with approvals at treatment, script, storyboard, rough cut and picture lock.
  • Kanban for content teams: in-house teams producing a steady stream of social and short-form videos often run a Kanban board with columns from idea to published.
  • Iterative post: inside post-production, cuts are reviewed in short cycles, similar to sprints, with a cap on rounds.
  • Critical path for the shoot: location permits, casting and kit bookings form the critical path to the first shoot day.

Essential documents and templates

  • Creative brief and treatment: start with the creative brief template.
  • Budget: split above-the-line costs (creative leads, talent) from below-the-line costs (crew, kit, locations, post); the budget template and project cost estimate template work well.
  • Production schedule and shooting schedule: use the project schedule template or a Gantt chart template for the overall plan.
  • Script, storyboard and shot list: what will be filmed, scene by scene.
  • Call sheet: issued before each shoot day with times, location, contacts, crew list, weather and safety notes.
  • Risk assessment: required for shoot safety; track wider project risks in the risk register template.
  • Releases and licences: talent, location and music paperwork.
  • Deliverables list: every final format and version the client needs.

Common challenges and how to handle them

  • Unclear expectations: get the treatment and script approved before booking crew; change after that costs money.
  • Weather and location problems: build in a contingency day or an indoor cover set and check permit conditions.
  • Too many cooks in review: one consolidated set of notes per cut from one client contact.
  • Missing releases: collect signatures on the day; chasing them later is hard.
  • Data loss: back up rushes in at least two places before cards are reused.
  • Scope growth in post: list the deliverables and versions in the contract and treat extras as change requests.

Frequently asked questions

What is video production project management?

It is planning and controlling a video project from concept through pre-production, the shoot and post-production to final delivery.

What are the stages of video production project management?

Development, pre-production, production (the shoot), post-production, and delivery and distribution.

Who handles video production project management on a shoot?

The producer owns the project; the line producer or production manager runs budgets and logistics, supported by a production coordinator.

What documents are used in video production project management?

Treatment, script, storyboard, shot list, budget, production schedule, call sheets, risk assessments, releases and a deliverables list.

What is a call sheet in video production project management?

A document sent before each shoot day listing call times, location, contacts, crew, schedule, weather and safety information.

How do you budget in video production project management?

Build the budget line by line, usually split into above-the-line and below-the-line costs, and add a contingency.

Why is pre-production so important in video production project management?

Because shoot days are expensive and hard to repeat, so problems must be solved in planning rather than on set.

How do you control revisions in video production project management?

Agree the number of review rounds per cut, consolidate notes from one contact and fix the point of picture lock.

Which KPIs are used in video production project management?

Budget variance, shoot days against plan, shots per day, review rounds per cut, on-time delivery and turnaround time.

What software is used for video production project management?

A PM platform such as Wrike for plans and approvals, plus specialist scheduling, budgeting, editing and video review tools.

Is Agile useful in video production project management?

Partly. The overall project is phased, but in-house content teams use Kanban and post-production benefits from short review cycles.
