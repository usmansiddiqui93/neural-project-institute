Architecture Project Management: Stages, Roles, Fees and Tools

Architecture project management is how an architectural practice plans, resources and controls a design commission, from the client brief through design stages, approvals and construction-phase services, so that the design is delivered to quality and the practice makes a fair return on its fee. It covers two jobs at once: steering the project for the client and managing the practice's own time and money.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team

  • 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Architecture project management serves two goals: delivering the client's design and protecting the practice's fee.
  • Structure the programme and fee around RIBA or AIA stages, with sign-off at each stage.
  • Track hours weekly against stage budgets and treat client changes as potential additional services.
  • Use BIM tools for design and a platform such as Wrike for programmes, resources and approvals.

What makes architecture project management different

  • The fee is the budget. Practices are paid a fixed fee, a percentage of construction cost or hourly rates, and every hour spent beyond the fee comes out of profit.
  • Design is iterative. Clients need to see options and respond, so work loops back before it moves forward.
  • Approvals shape the programme. Planning or zoning, building regulations or code review, heritage or conservation consents and client sign-offs gate each stage.
  • Coordination with consultants. Structural, services and other engineers, cost consultants and specialists must work to the architect's information timetable.
  • Liability. Drawings and specifications carry professional liability, so quality checking and document control are essential.

Design stages

Practices in the UK usually work to the RIBA Plan of Work 2020; US practices often use the AIA phases. The table maps them roughly.

RIBA stageRoughly equivalent AIA phaseMain architect activities
0 Strategic Definition / 1 Preparation and BriefingPre-design / programmingAgree the brief, site appraisal, feasibility, appointment and fee
2 Concept DesignSchematic designOptions, massing, concept proposals, early cost advice
3 Spatial CoordinationDesign developmentCoordinated design with engineers; planning application in the UK
4 Technical DesignConstruction documentsDetailed drawings and specifications for building and tender
5 Manufacturing and ConstructionBidding and construction administrationAnswer queries, review submittals, inspect work, certify where appointed
6 Handover / 7 UseCloseout and post-occupancySnagging, as-built information, post-occupancy evaluation
Tip

Write your fee proposal stage by stage, and close each stage with a client sign-off. This makes it far easier to charge for work repeated because the client changed direction.

Methodologies that fit

ApproachUse in architecture
Stage-gated planFollows the RIBA or AIA stages with sign-off at each, as in waterfall project management
Iterative design sprintsShort cycles of options, review and refinement in concept and developed design; see iterative and incremental development
Kanban for productionA Kanban board to manage drawing production, queries and submittal reviews

Many practices find a hybrid works best: a fixed stage plan for the client and fee, with Agile-style iterations inside each design stage. See Agile for creative teams for ideas that transfer well.

Common challenges

  • Scope creep through 'small' design changes. Log every change, agree whether it is an additional service and record client approval; see scope creep.
  • Fee overruns in early stages. Track hours weekly against stage budgets rather than at invoice time.
  • Waiting for consultant information. Publish an information required schedule with dates and owners.
  • Approval delays. Hold pre-application discussions and build realistic consent periods into the programme.
  • Too many projects per person. Use a resource plan across the practice; see managing multiple projects.

Software for architecture project management

Architects rely on BIM and CAD authoring tools for design, common data environments for sharing models and drawings, and often practice management software for timesheets and invoicing. A project management platform ties the work together: stage programmes, task allocation, resourcing across projects, client approvals and reporting.

Look for timeline views, workload or resource management, time tracking, approval workflows and secure client access. Wrike covers these well and is our top all-in-one pick in our tool rankings; smaller studios may prefer something lighter from the same list.

Frequently asked questions

What is architecture project management?

Architecture project management is how a practice plans, resources and controls a design commission from brief to construction-phase services while protecting its fee.

What are the stages of architecture project management?

In the UK, the RIBA Plan of Work stages 0 to 7; in the US, typically pre-design, schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding and construction administration.

Who handles architecture project management in a practice?

Usually a project architect or dedicated project manager, supported by the director in charge, who owns the client relationship and fee.

How do architects track fees in architecture project management?

By budgeting hours by stage and role, recording time weekly and comparing fee earned with fee spent and hours still needed.

What KPIs are used in architecture project management?

Fee earned versus spent, utilisation, hours to complete, drawing issues on time, additional services recovered and design changes per stage.

Is Agile used in architecture project management?

Yes, in the form of short design iterations and Kanban for drawing production, inside a stage-gated plan that matches the fee and approvals.

What software is used for architecture project management?

BIM and CAD tools for design, a common data environment for information and a project platform such as Wrike for programmes, resources and approvals.

How do you handle client changes in architecture project management?

Log each change, assess its impact on fee and programme, agree whether it is an additional service and get written approval before doing the work.

What is the RIBA Plan of Work in architecture project management?

The RIBA's framework of eight stages (0 to 7) that structures a building project from strategic definition to use.

What documents are needed for architecture project management?

A fee proposal and resource plan, design programme, project brief, responsibility matrix, drawing register, change log and risk register.

What skills does architecture project management require?

Design leadership, fee and resource management, consultant coordination, knowledge of approvals and clear client communication.
