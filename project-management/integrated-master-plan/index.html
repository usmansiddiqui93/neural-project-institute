Integrated Master Plan (IMP) and Integrated Master Schedule (IMS)

An integrated master plan (IMP) is an event-based plan used in large, complex programmes, especially in defence and aerospace. It defines the major events, the accomplishments required for each, and the criteria that show they are complete.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 21 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • An IMP defines events, accomplishments and criteria.
  • The IMS schedules the tasks behind them.
  • Used in large defence and government programmes.

Frequently asked questions

What is an IMP?

An event-based plan defining events, accomplishments and completion criteria.

What is an IMS?

A detailed, time-based schedule of tasks supporting the IMP.

Where are IMPs used?

Mainly in defence, aerospace and large government programmes.

Is an IMP the same as a project plan?

It is a specific form of high-level programme plan focused on events and criteria.

Does an IMP include dates?

Events may have target dates, but detailed timing lives in the IMS.

How does an IMP relate to the WBS?

Accomplishments and tasks map to WBS elements.

Who prepares the IMP?

Usually the contractor, reviewed by the customer.

Is an IMP used with earned value?

Yes, often together with EVM for performance measurement.

What are accomplishment criteria in an IMP?

Objective evidence that an accomplishment is complete.

Is an IMP needed for small projects?

No. It is designed for large, complex programmes.
