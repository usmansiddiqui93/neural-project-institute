Project Management Skills: The Hard and Soft Skills You Need

Project management skills are the technical abilities (such as planning, scheduling, budgeting and risk management) and interpersonal abilities (such as communication, leadership and negotiation) needed to deliver projects successfully.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 3 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Project managers need both technical and interpersonal skills.
  • Build skills deliberately with practice, feedback and structured learning.
  • Prove skills to employers with quantified outcomes and stories.

Hard skills

SkillWhat it involves
Scope definitionWriting clear scope and building a WBS
SchedulingSequencing work, finding the critical path, using Gantt charts
BudgetingCost estimation and cost control
Risk managementIdentifying, assessing and responding to risk
Quality managementDefining acceptance criteria and checking deliverables
ToolsUsing project management software well
MethodsWaterfall, Agile, Scrum, Kanban and hybrids

Soft skills

  • Communication: clear, honest and suited to the audience.
  • Leadership: setting direction and motivating people without formal authority.
  • Negotiation: balancing competing demands on scope, time and resources.
  • Problem solving: breaking messy issues into actions.
  • Decision making: acting on incomplete information and owning the result.
  • Emotional intelligence: reading people and managing your own reactions.
  • Organisation: keeping track of many moving parts.

How to build each skill

  1. Rate yourself 1 to 5 on each skill above.
  2. Pick the two lowest that matter most in your current role.
  3. Find a real opportunity to practise, such as running a planning session or a risk workshop.
  4. Ask for feedback from a colleague or mentor afterwards.
  5. Add structured learning: a course, a book or a certification module.
  6. Re-rate yourself every quarter.

Example self-assessment

SkillScore nowTargetAction
Scheduling24Build the next project schedule with dependencies in a Gantt tool
Negotiation34Lead the next scope trade-off discussion with the sponsor
Budgeting23Shadow the finance partner on monthly cost reviews
Communication44Maintain; ask stakeholders for feedback on reports

Showing skills to employers

  • Describe outcomes, not tasks: "delivered a 6-month CRM rollout two weeks early" beats "responsible for CRM project".
  • Use the STAR format (situation, task, action, result) in interviews.
  • Mention the tools and methods you used.
  • Back claims with a certification where possible.
Tip

Soft skills are hard to prove on paper. Prepare two stories each for conflict, a difficult stakeholder and a project that went wrong.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most important project management skills?

Communication, planning, leadership, risk management and stakeholder management are consistently the most valued.

What are hard project management skills?

Teachable technical skills such as scheduling, budgeting, scope definition, risk analysis and using PM software.

What are soft project management skills?

Interpersonal skills such as communication, leadership, negotiation, conflict resolution and emotional intelligence.

How can I improve my project management skills?

Practise on real projects, ask for feedback, take a structured course and learn from experienced project managers.

Do project management skills include technical skills?

They need enough domain knowledge to understand the work and its risks. Some roles, like technical project manager, need much more.

Which tools should I learn to build project management skills?

A Gantt-capable work management tool such as Wrike, plus whatever your target employers use; see project management tools.

How do I list project management skills on a CV?

Group them into methods, tools and soft skills, and prove each with a quantified result.

Is leadership a project management skill?

Yes. Project managers lead teams that often do not report to them, so influence is essential.

Which project management skills does PMI emphasise?

PMI's Talent Triangle groups skills into Ways of Working, Power Skills and Business Acumen.

Can project management skills be self-taught?

Many can, especially with practice, but a structured course speeds things up and fills gaps.
