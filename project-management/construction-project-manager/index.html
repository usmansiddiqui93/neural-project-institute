Construction Project Manager: Duties, Skills and Career Guide

A construction project manager plans, coordinates and controls a building or infrastructure project from early design through construction to handover. They manage cost, programme, quality, contracts and safety, working with clients, designers, contractors and regulators.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 4 October 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Construction project managers control cost, programme, quality, contracts and safety.
  • Long-lead items, permissions and site risks drive the schedule.
  • Professional body membership and site experience matter as much as general PM certification.

Main responsibilities

  • Developing the project brief, budget and construction programme (schedule).
  • Coordinating architects, engineers, surveyors and contractors.
  • Managing procurement and contracts; see procurement management.
  • Obtaining permits and ensuring compliance with building regulations.
  • Overseeing site health and safety arrangements.
  • Controlling cost, change and quality.
  • Managing handover, snagging and close-out.

Project stages

StageKey activities
Feasibility and briefSite appraisal, outline budget, feasibility study
DesignConcept and detailed design, planning permission, cost plans
ProcurementChoose a contract route, tender, appoint contractors
ConstructionSite works, progress meetings, variations, inspections
Handover and close-outSnagging, commissioning, documentation, defects period

Worked example: a school extension

A council plans a four-classroom extension that must open for the new school year. The project manager works back from the opening date: 12 weeks for fit-out and commissioning, 24 weeks for the main build, 8 weeks for tender and award, and 16 weeks for design and permissions. That leaves almost no float, so they start the planning application early, order long-lead items such as steelwork as soon as the contractor is appointed, and schedule noisy work for school holidays. A contingency of 10% of the build cost is held for unknown ground conditions.

Tip

Track long-lead items on their own list. A late lift, switchgear panel or steel order can delay a whole building.

Skills and qualifications

  • Reading drawings and specifications.
  • Programme planning with Gantt charts and the critical path.
  • Commercial skills: cost plans, valuations and variations.
  • Contract knowledge, such as the forms of contract common in your country.
  • Health and safety leadership.
  • Negotiation and dispute avoidance.

Many construction project managers hold a degree in construction management, engineering, surveying or architecture, and join professional bodies such as the CIOB or RICS in the UK, or CMAA in the US. Safety duties are set by national law, for example the CDM Regulations 2015 in Great Britain.

Frequently asked questions

What does a construction project manager do?

They plan and control building projects, coordinating designers and contractors and managing cost, programme, quality, contracts and safety.

Is a construction project manager the same as a site manager?

No. A site manager runs day-to-day work on site; the project manager oversees the whole project, including design, procurement, budget and client relations.

What qualifications does a construction project manager need?

Commonly a relevant degree or equivalent experience, plus membership of a professional body such as the CIOB, RICS or CMAA.

Is the PMP useful for a construction project manager?

Yes, it is recognised in many markets, though industry-specific credentials and chartered status are often valued more.

How does a construction project manager use a construction programme?

The construction programme is the construction schedule, usually shown as a Gantt chart with dependencies and the critical path. The construction project manager uses it to plan, coordinate and track the work.

What software do construction project managers use?

Scheduling tools, cost and document control systems, and work management platforms such as Wrike or Smartsheet; see project management tools.

How does a construction project manager handle variations?

Variations are changes to the work instructed under the contract, which usually adjust the price and sometimes the time, so they need to be recorded, valued and agreed.

How do I become a construction project manager?

Start as an assistant, site engineer or quantity surveyor, gain site and commercial experience, and work towards professional membership.

How much do construction project managers earn?

Pay depends on country, project size and experience. Check job adverts, professional body surveys and official labour statistics for current figures.

Can a construction project manager use Agile?

Mostly in design and pre-construction, through short planning cycles and visual boards; see Agile in construction.
