Estimate at Completion (EAC): Formulas, Examples and Tips

Estimate at completion (EAC) is the forecast of what a project will cost in total when all the work is done. It combines what has already been spent with an estimate of the cost of the remaining work.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 29 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • EAC forecasts the total cost at completion.
  • Choose the formula that matches the cause of the variance.
  • ETC = EAC - AC; VAC = BAC - EAC; TCPI shows the efficiency needed.
  • Report a range of EACs rather than a single number.

The four EAC formulas

FormulaUse when
EAC = BAC / CPICurrent cost performance is expected to continue (typical variance)
EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)Past variance was a one-off; remaining work will go to plan (atypical variance)
EAC = AC + (BAC - EV) / (CPI x SPI)Both cost and schedule performance will affect remaining work, e.g. a fixed end date
EAC = AC + bottom-up ETCThe original plan is no longer valid and remaining work is re-estimated

Related forecasts

  • Estimate to complete (ETC) = EAC - AC: money still needed.
  • Variance at completion (VAC) = BAC - EAC: expected under- or overrun.
  • TCPI (to BAC) = (BAC - EV) / (BAC - AC): efficiency needed to finish on budget.
  • TCPI (to EAC) = (BAC - EV) / (EAC - AC): efficiency needed to hit the forecast.

Worked example

BAC = $200,000, PV = $80,000, EV = $70,000, AC = $85,000. So CPI = 0.8235 and SPI = 0.875. Work remaining = BAC - EV = $130,000.

FormulaCalculationEACVAC
BAC / CPI200,000 / 0.8235$242,857-$42,857
AC + (BAC - EV)85,000 + 130,000$215,000-$15,000
AC + (BAC - EV) / (CPI x SPI)85,000 + 130,000 / 0.7206$265,408-$65,408
AC + bottom-up ETC85,000 + 140,000$225,000-$25,000

With the typical formula, ETC = 242,857 - 85,000 = $157,857. TCPI to meet BAC = 130,000 / 115,000 = 1.13, while TCPI to meet the EAC of $242,857 = 130,000 / 157,857 = 0.82, which simply matches the current CPI.

Choosing the right EAC

  1. Find out why the variance happened.
  2. If the cause is ongoing (e.g. rates are higher than planned), use BAC / CPI.
  3. If it was a one-off (e.g. a single supplier failure), use AC + (BAC - EV).
  4. If you must hit a fixed date and are behind, consider the CPI x SPI formula.
  5. If assumptions have changed significantly, re-estimate remaining work bottom-up.
  6. Report a range: the formulas together give a useful best-to-worst spread.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the overrun will magically recover (using AC + (BAC - EV) by default).
  • Using a single early CPI reading; wait for a trend.
  • Ignoring committed costs not yet invoiced.
  • Confusing EAC with BAC or with the funding limit.

Frequently asked questions

What is estimate at completion?

The forecast total cost of the project when all work is complete, written EAC.

What is the most common EAC formula?

EAC = BAC / CPI, which assumes current cost efficiency will continue.

What is the difference between EAC and ETC?

EAC is the forecast total cost; ETC is the forecast cost of the remaining work. EAC = AC + ETC.

When should I use EAC = AC + (BAC - EV)?

When the past variance was a one-off and you expect the rest of the work to go to plan.

When should I use the CPI x SPI EAC formula?

When schedule pressure is likely to affect cost, for example when a fixed deadline means extra effort to catch up.

How is EAC used to calculate VAC?

Variance at completion = BAC - EAC. A negative VAC is the forecast overrun.

What does a TCPI above 1 mean when targeting an EAC?

The remaining work must be done more efficiently than planned to meet the target. Values well above 1 are often unrealistic.

How often should EAC be updated?

Every reporting period, alongside the other earned value measures.

Can EAC be lower than BAC?

Yes. If the CPI is above 1, the project is forecast to finish under budget.

Is a bottom-up EAC better than a formula?

Often, when the plan has changed significantly, because it reflects current knowledge. Formulas are quicker and useful as a cross-check.

How is EAC different from a project forecast?

EAC is the cost part of project forecasting, which also covers completion dates and benefits.
