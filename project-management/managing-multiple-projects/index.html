Managing Multiple Projects: A Practical System That Works

Managing multiple projects means running several projects at the same time while keeping priorities, deadlines and resources under control. It depends on a single view of all your work, clear priorities and a steady weekly routine.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 4 October 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Keep all projects in one master view.
  • Agree explicit priorities and let them decide conflicts.
  • Use a weekly routine, batch similar work and delegate routine tasks.

Build one master view

Keep every project, milestone and key task in one place rather than scattered across emails and notebooks. A project management tool with portfolio or dashboard views, such as Wrike, makes this much easier than separate spreadsheets.

  • List every active project with its sponsor, deadline and status.
  • Show the next milestone for each.
  • Show who is working on what to spot overload.

Prioritise explicitly

PriorityCriteriaExample
1Fixed external deadline or high business valueRegulatory reporting change
2Important, flexible timingNew intranet
3Useful, can waitProcess documentation clean-up

Agree priorities with your manager or sponsors. When two projects compete for the same time, the priority list decides, not whoever shouts loudest.

A weekly routine

  1. Monday: review the master view and choose the top three outcomes for the week.
  2. Block focused time in your calendar for each priority project.
  3. Run short, regular check-ins rather than long meetings for every project.
  4. Midweek: update risks and issues across all projects.
  5. Friday: update statuses, send brief reports and plan next week.
Tip

Group similar work together: write all status reports in one session, hold supplier calls back to back. Switching between projects all day wastes time.

Worked example: balancing workload

A project manager runs four projects and has 40 hours a week. Estimates show the projects need 14, 12, 10 and 9 hours, a total of 45. Rather than working late indefinitely, they ask the sponsor of the lowest-priority project to accept a two-week delay to one milestone, delegate meeting notes on two projects to a coordinator, and bring the total back to 39 hours. See resource leveling and capacity planning for the techniques behind this.

Common traps

  • Saying yes to every new project without dropping or delaying anything.
  • Letting the noisiest project take all your attention.
  • Using different tools and formats for each project.
  • Not delegating routine work.
  • Skipping risk reviews on smaller projects until they become urgent.

Frequently asked questions

How many projects is too many when you manage multiple projects?

It depends on project size and complexity. Several small projects may be manageable; one large, complex project can be a full-time job.

What is the best way to manage multiple projects?

Use one master view, agree clear priorities, follow a weekly routine and delegate routine work.

What tool is best for managing multiple projects?

A tool with portfolio dashboards and workload views, such as Wrike; see our project management tools comparison.

How do I prioritise competing work when managing multiple projects?

Agree criteria such as deadlines, value and risk with your manager or sponsors, then rank projects openly.

How do I avoid burnout when managing multiple projects?

Track your capacity, push back with data when overloaded, and protect focused time.

Should I use one plan or separate plans when managing multiple projects?

Separate project plans, but rolled up into one shared view so you can see conflicts.

How do I report efficiently when managing multiple projects?

Use a standard short template and dashboards so reports take minutes, not hours; see project status report.

How does resource management work when managing multiple projects?

Planning people's time across several projects to avoid overload; see resource allocation.

Is managing multiple projects the same as programme management?

No. Programme management coordinates related projects towards shared benefits; managing multiple projects can involve unrelated work.

How do I fit in a new urgent project when I already manage multiple projects?

Assess its priority against current work and agree with stakeholders what will be delayed or dropped to make room.
