The 5 Phases of Project Management (Project Life Cycle)

Every project, whatever its size, moves through a life cycle. The most common model has five phases, each with its own activities, decisions and documents.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Five phases: initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and controlling, closure.
  • Each phase has clear outputs such as the charter, plan and lessons learned.
  • Monitoring and controlling runs throughout the project.

Overview

PhasePurposeKey outputs
1. InitiationDecide whether and why to do the projectBusiness case, project charter, stakeholder list
2. PlanningDefine how the project will be deliveredProject management plan, scope, schedule, budget
3. ExecutionDo the workDeliverables, status updates
4. Monitoring and controllingTrack and correct, throughoutPerformance reports, approved changes
5. ClosureFinish formallyAcceptance, lessons learned, archive

1. Initiation

  • Identify the need or opportunity.
  • Carry out a feasibility study if needed.
  • Write a business case.
  • Create and approve the project charter.
  • Identify key stakeholders.

2. Planning

  • Define scope and create a work breakdown structure.
  • Build the schedule and identify the critical path.
  • Estimate costs and set the budget.
  • Plan risk, quality, communication and procurement.
  • Set the baseline and hold a kickoff.

3. Execution

  • Lead and coordinate the team.
  • Produce deliverables.
  • Manage stakeholder engagement and communication.
  • Procure goods and services.

4. Monitoring and controlling

  • Compare progress with the baseline.
  • Track cost and schedule using earned value.
  • Manage risks and issues; see RAID logs.
  • Process changes through change control.

5. Closure

  • Obtain formal acceptance.
  • Close contracts and accounts.
  • Release the team.
  • Hold a lessons-learned review.
  • Archive documents.

Phases in Agile projects

In Agile projects, planning, execution and review repeat in every iteration rather than happening once. See Agile project management.

Frequently asked questions

Are the phases always sequential?

Initiation and closure happen once, but planning, execution and monitoring overlap and repeat, especially in iterative projects.

Which phase is most important?

Planning sets up success, but monitoring and controlling is what keeps the project on track.
