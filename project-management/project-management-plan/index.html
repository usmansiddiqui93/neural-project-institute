How to Create a Project Management Plan (Step by Step)

The project management plan describes how a project will be executed, monitored, controlled and closed. It integrates the scope, schedule, cost and other plans into one coherent document that guides the team.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 15 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • The project management plan integrates all subsidiary plans and baselines.
  • Build it from the charter: scope, schedule, cost, then the other plans.
  • Scale it to the project and keep it current.

Components

ComponentDescribes
Scope management plan and scope baselineWhat is included and how scope is controlled
Schedule management plan and baselineTimeline and how it is managed
Cost management plan and baselineBudget and how costs are controlled
Quality management planStandards and how quality is assured
Resource management planPeople, equipment and materials
Communications management planWho receives what information, when and how
Risk management planHow risks are identified and managed
Procurement management planHow goods and services are bought
Stakeholder engagement planHow stakeholders are involved
Change management planHow changes are requested and approved

Step by step

  1. Start from the approved project charter.
  2. Define scope and create a work breakdown structure.
  3. Build the schedule and identify the critical path.
  4. Estimate costs and set the budget.
  5. Plan quality, resources, communication, risk and procurement.
  6. Define how changes will be controlled.
  7. Set the baselines for scope, schedule and cost.
  8. Review with stakeholders and obtain approval.
  9. Hold a kickoff meeting.

Tips

  • Scale the plan to the project: a small project may need a few pages.
  • Use visuals such as Gantt charts and RACI matrices.
  • Keep it a living document and update it through change control.
  • Make sure the team has read and understood it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a project management plan?

A document describing how the project will be executed, monitored, controlled and closed.

Is a project plan the same as a schedule?

No. The schedule is one part of the plan.

Who creates the project management plan?

The project manager, with input from the team and stakeholders.

Who approves the project management plan?

The sponsor and key stakeholders.

How long should a project plan be?

As long as needed; scale it to the project's size and risk.

What are subsidiary plans in a project management plan?

Plans for specific areas such as risk, quality and communication that form part of the overall plan.

What are baselines in a project management plan?

Approved versions of scope, schedule and cost used to measure performance; see baseline.

Do Agile projects have a project plan?

Yes, but it is layered and adaptive; see the project plan in Agile.

How often should the project plan be updated?

Whenever approved changes occur and at regular reviews.

What tools help create a project plan?

Project management software with Gantt charts and templates; see project management tools.
