Blocker Clustering: Find the Root Causes of Delays

Every team gets blocked: waiting for approvals, other teams, environments or information. Blocker clustering is a simple technique, popularised in the Kanban community by Klaus Leopold and Troy Magennis, for turning those daily frustrations into data you can act on.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Record every blocker with its reason and duration.
  • Cluster similar blockers and measure their cost in blocked days.
  • Fix the biggest clusters first.

How it works

  1. Mark blockers on cards as soon as an item can't progress, with a short reason and the start date.
  2. Record when the blocker is removed, so you know how long it lasted.
  3. Collect blocker records over a few weeks.
  4. Cluster them in a workshop: group similar blockers into categories, such as waiting for test environments, unclear requirements or external approvals.
  5. Measure each cluster: how many blockers and how many blocked days.
  6. Act on the biggest clusters with targeted improvements.

Example clusters

ClusterCountBlocked daysPossible action
Waiting for test environment1438Automate environment set-up
Unclear requirements921Better refinement and acceptance criteria

Internal vs external blockers

Separate blockers your team can fix from those that need other teams or leaders. Internal clusters can be tackled in the next retrospective; external clusters need data to support a conversation with the other party.

Tips

  • Make marking blockers quick and blame-free.
  • Review clusters monthly.
  • Track whether blocked days fall after each improvement.
  • Combine with a cumulative flow diagram to see the effect on flow.

Frequently asked questions

How long should we collect blocker data?

A few weeks is often enough to see patterns. Continue collecting so you can measure the effect of improvements.

Who should take part in a blocker clustering session?

The team, plus anyone who can help resolve external blockers.
