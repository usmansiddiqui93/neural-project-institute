Cumulative Flow Diagram (CFD): How to Read and Use It

A cumulative flow diagram (CFD) shows how many work items are in each stage of your workflow over time. In one chart, it reveals bottlenecks, growing work in progress and changes in delivery rate.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 13 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A CFD stacks the number of items per stage over time.
  • Widening bands show bottlenecks; a flat Done line shows no delivery.
  • It approximates WIP, cycle time and throughput visually.

How a CFD is built

Each day, the number of items in each column of the board is recorded. The chart stacks these counts as coloured bands over time, usually with Done at the bottom and the earliest stage at the top. Because items are counted cumulatively, the bands never go down for Done.

Reading the bands

What you seeWhat it means
Parallel bands of steady widthStable, healthy flow
A band getting widerWork is piling up in that stage: a bottleneck
A band getting thinnerThat stage is starved of work
Flat top line of DoneNothing is being finished
Steep rise in the top bandLots of new work arriving

Approximations from a CFD

  • Vertical distance between the top of the in-progress bands and the Done line at a point in time ≈ work in progress.
  • Horizontal distance between when items enter progress and when they reach Done ≈ average cycle time.
  • Slope of the Done line ≈ throughput.

Using a CFD in reviews

  • Look at it in service delivery reviews or retrospectives.
  • Find the first band that is widening; that is usually the constraint.
  • Check whether WIP limits are being respected.
  • Compare before and after process changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CFD used for?

To see bottlenecks, WIP trends and delivery rate at a glance.

Can Scrum teams use a CFD?

Yes. A CFD is useful for any team that tracks work through stages.

What is a cumulative flow diagram?

A cumulative flow diagram (CFD) is a stacked area chart showing how many items are in each workflow stage over time. It reveals WIP, bottlenecks and flow at a glance.

How do you read a cumulative flow diagram?

Each coloured band is a stage. The vertical gap between bands shows WIP in that stage, the horizontal distance approximates average cycle time, and the slope of the Done line shows throughput.

What does a widening band mean on a CFD?

Work is entering that stage faster than it leaves, so a queue or bottleneck is forming there.

What does a flat line on a CFD mean?

Nothing is moving through that stage. A flat Done line means nothing has been delivered in that period.

What does a healthy CFD look like?

Bands that rise smoothly and in parallel, with roughly constant thickness, showing stable WIP and steady flow.

How do you create a cumulative flow diagram?

Each day, count the items in each stage plus all items that have passed it, and stack the counts over time. Most digital Kanban tools generate a CFD automatically.

What is the difference between a CFD and a burnup chart?

A burnup shows completed work against total scope. A CFD shows every workflow stage, so it also reveals where work is accumulating.

Can a CFD show cycle time?

It shows approximate average cycle time as the horizontal distance between the line where work starts and the Done line. Use a cycle time scatterplot for item-level detail.

Why does the bottom band of a CFD never go down?

Because it is cumulative: items that reach Done stay counted. Lines only go down if items are removed or moved backwards.
