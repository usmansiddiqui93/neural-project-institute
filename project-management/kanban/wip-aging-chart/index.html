WIP Aging Chart: Spot Stuck Work Before It's Late

Cycle time tells you about items you have already finished. Work item age tells you about items still in progress. A WIP aging chart puts every unfinished item on one chart, so you can act before an item becomes late.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A WIP aging chart plots unfinished items by stage and age.
  • Compare ages with historical cycle time percentiles.
  • Act on old items first in the daily meeting.

What the chart shows

The horizontal axis lists the workflow stages; the vertical axis shows age in days. Each in-progress item is a dot in its current stage, at the height of its age. Background bands show historical cycle time percentiles, for example the 50th and 85th.

How to read it

  • Dots below the 50th percentile: on track.
  • Dots approaching the 85th percentile: at risk; discuss today.
  • Dots above the 85th percentile: already slower than most past items; act now.
  • Old dots in early stages: items that started and stalled.

Using it in the daily meeting

  1. Start with the oldest items, especially those nearing your service level expectation.
  2. Ask what is needed to move each one forward.
  3. Swarm, unblock or split items where needed.
  4. Avoid starting new work while old items are at risk.

Why work item age matters

Age is a leading indicator: it warns you while there is still time to act. Combined with a service level expectation, it turns Kanban from reporting what happened into managing what is happening.

Frequently asked questions

What is work item age?

The time since an unfinished item started, measured from the same start point used for cycle time.

How is a WIP aging chart different from a cycle time scatterplot?

The scatterplot shows finished items; the aging chart shows unfinished ones.
