Kanban Planning: How to Plan and Forecast Without Sprints

Kanban has no sprints, but it is far from unplanned. Kanban teams plan continuously and use their own flow data to make reliable, probabilistic forecasts.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Kanban plans continuously through replenishment.
  • Service level expectations come from cycle time data.
  • Forecast with throughput and probabilities, not single dates.

Continuous planning

  • Backlog: options the team might work on, ordered by value and urgency.
  • Replenishment: at regular meetings, the team and stakeholders choose which options to make ready.
  • Commitment point: when a card crosses it, the team commits to finishing it.
  • Delivery planning: coordinating how finished work reaches customers.

Service level expectations

A service level expectation (SLE) states how long an item is likely to take once started, for example "85% of items finish within 10 days". It is calculated from historical cycle times and gives customers a clear, data-based promise.

Forecasting with throughput

To answer "when will these 40 items be done?", Kanban teams use throughput history:

  • Simple average: if the team finishes about 8 items a week, 40 items take about 5 weeks.
  • Range: using the slowest and fastest recent weeks gives an optimistic and pessimistic date.
  • Monte Carlo simulation: sampling past weekly throughput thousands of times produces a probability for each date, such as "85% likely by 14 June".
Tip

Probabilistic forecasts are more honest than single dates, and they need no estimation meetings: just count finished items.

Planning at scale

When several teams or services interact, Kanban uses operations and strategy reviews to align capacity and priorities. See Kanban cadences.

Frequently asked questions

Do Kanban teams estimate?

Often not in story points. Many Kanban teams split work into similarly sized items and forecast using throughput and cycle time.

What is a commitment point?

The point on the board after which the team commits to completing an item. Before it, items are options that can be dropped.
