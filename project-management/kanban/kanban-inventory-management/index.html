Kanban Inventory Management: The Two-Bin System and More

In inventory management, kanban is a signalling system that triggers replenishment only when stock is used. It keeps inventory low and stock-outs rare, without complex forecasting.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Kanban inventory replenishes only what has been used.
  • The two-bin system is the simplest form.
  • Size kanbans from demand, lead time, safety factor and container size.

The two-bin system

  1. Stock for a part is split into two bins.
  2. Workers use parts from the first bin.
  3. When the first bin is empty, its kanban card is sent to purchasing or the supplying process.
  4. Workers continue using the second bin while the first is refilled.
  5. The refilled bin returns before the second runs out.

Kanban cards

A kanban card shows the part number, description, quantity per container, supplier or source process, and delivery location. Production kanbans authorise making more; withdrawal kanbans authorise moving parts from one area to another. Many companies now use electronic kanban (e-kanban) triggered by scanning.

How many kanbans?

A common formula is:

Number of kanbans = (daily demand × lead time in days × (1 + safety factor)) ÷ container quantity

Tip

Example: demand 200 units a day, replenishment lead time 2 days, safety factor 10%, 50 units per container: (200 × 2 × 1.1) ÷ 50 = 8.8, so use 9 kanbans.

Benefits and limitations

BenefitsLimitations
Lower inventory and storage costsNeeds reasonably stable demand
Simple, visual and self-regulatingRelies on reliable suppliers
Fewer stock-outs and less overstockLess suited to one-off or highly seasonal items

Frequently asked questions

Is kanban inventory the same as Just-in-Time?

Kanban is the signalling method that makes Just-in-Time inventory work.

Can small businesses use kanban inventory?

Yes. A two-bin system is cheap and effective for consumables, spare parts and small retail stock.
