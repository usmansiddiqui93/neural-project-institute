Kanban vs Scrumban: What Is the Difference?

Scrumban is a hybrid of Scrum and Kanban. It keeps some of Scrum's structure, such as regular planning and retrospectives, while adopting Kanban's continuous flow and WIP limits.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrumban blends Scrum's rhythm with Kanban's flow.
  • It suits Scrum teams facing frequent unplanned work.
  • Teams without Scrum can start directly with Kanban.

What Scrumban takes from each

From ScrumFrom Kanban
Regular planning meetingsContinuous pull of work
Retrospectives and reviewsWIP limits per column
Often a team with defined rolesFlow metrics: cycle time, throughput
A regular cadence for reflectionPersistent board with explicit policies

IterationsNoneOptional; often kept for planning rhythm

PlanningReplenishment as neededPlanning on demand or at regular intervals

When to choose Scrumban

  • A Scrum team's sprints are constantly disrupted by urgent work.
  • A team wants to move from Scrum to Kanban gradually.
  • Work mixes planned features with unplanned requests.
  • The team values Scrum's rhythm of reflection but not fixed sprint commitments.

When pure Kanban is better

If you don't use Scrum today, start directly with Kanban. Scrumban is most useful as a bridge for teams already practising Scrum.

Frequently asked questions

Who created Scrumban?

The term was popularised by Corey Ladas in the late 2000s, originally as a way for Scrum teams to transition towards Kanban.

Does Scrumban use sprints?

It can. Some Scrumban teams keep sprint-length planning cycles; others plan whenever the Ready column runs low.
