Personal Kanban: Manage Your Own Work With Two Simple Rules

Personal Kanban applies Kanban to an individual's work. It was popularised by Jim Benson and Tonianne DeMaria Barry, whose 2011 book reduced it to two rules: visualise your work, and limit your work in progress.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Personal Kanban has two rules: visualise work and limit WIP.
  • Start with Ready, Doing and Done.
  • Review weekly and finish before starting.

The two rules

  • Visualise your work: put every task on a board so you can see your real workload.
  • Limit your work in progress: decide how many things you will work on at once, and finish before starting more.

Setting up a personal board

  1. Use a whiteboard, sticky notes on a wall, or a simple app.
  2. Create three columns: Ready, Doing, Done.
  3. Write each task on its own card.
  4. Set a WIP limit on Doing, typically two or three.
  5. Each morning, pull the most important card into Doing.

Useful additions

  • A Backlog column for everything you might do, with Ready holding only this week's priorities.
  • A Waiting column for items blocked on other people.
  • A Today column for daily focus.
  • Colours for work, personal and learning tasks.

Why it works

  • Seeing all your work reduces anxiety and forgotten commitments.
  • Limiting WIP reduces task switching, which costs focus.
  • The Done column shows real progress.
  • Weekly reviews help you notice patterns, such as tasks that keep stalling.

Making it stick

  • Keep the board where you see it every day.
  • Review it weekly and clear the Done column.
  • Say no, or not yet, when Doing is full.
  • Keep tasks small enough to finish in a day or two.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good WIP limit for one person?

Two or three active tasks is common. Start low and adjust after a week or two.

Can families use personal Kanban?

Yes. Many households use a shared board for chores, errands and plans.
