Kanban Meetings and Cadences: Which Ones You Need

Kanban does not prescribe Scrum-style ceremonies, but it does recommend regular feedback loops called cadences. Teams pick the ones they need, at a frequency that suits their work.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Kanban uses cadences: regular feedback loops at different levels.
  • Start with a daily meeting and a replenishment meeting.
  • Walk the board right to left, focusing on finishing.

The seven Kanban cadences

The Kanban Method describes seven cadences, from daily team meetings to quarterly strategy reviews:

CadenceTypical frequencyPurpose
Kanban meeting (daily stand-up)Daily, 15 minutesCoordinate and remove blockers
Replenishment meetingWeekly or as neededSelect new work to commit to
Delivery planning meetingPer deliveryPlan handover to customers
Service delivery reviewEvery two weeksReview service performance against customer expectations
Operations reviewMonthlyReview interactions between services
Risk reviewMonthlyUnderstand and respond to delivery risks
Strategy reviewQuarterlyAlign services with strategy

The daily Kanban meeting

The daily meeting focuses on the work, not the people. Instead of each person giving an update, the team walks the board from right to left, closest to done first, asking what is needed to move each item forward. Blocked and old items get attention first. See WIP aging.

Replenishment

In a replenishment meeting, stakeholders and the team decide which items to pull from the backlog into the Ready column, based on capacity and priority. It replaces sprint planning, and it can be held as often as needed.

Starting small

Most teams begin with just two cadences: a daily Kanban meeting and a weekly replenishment. Add a service delivery review or retrospective once the board is stable, and the higher-level reviews when several teams or services interact.

Frequently asked questions

Does Kanban have retrospectives?

The Kanban Method uses service delivery and operations reviews for improvement, but many teams also hold regular retrospectives.

Is there sprint planning in Kanban?

No. Work is pulled continuously; replenishment meetings decide what to make ready next.
