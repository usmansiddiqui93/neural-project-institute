Cycle Time vs Lead Time: What Is the Difference?

Cycle time and lead time are often confused. Both measure how long work takes, but they start at different points, and they answer different questions.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Lead time runs from request to delivery; cycle time from start to finish.
  • Lead time shows the customer's wait; cycle time shows process speed.
  • Shorten lead time with smaller backlogs; shorten cycle time with lower WIP.

Definitions

Lead timeCycle time
StartsWhen the customer makes the requestWhen the team starts work
EndsWhen the customer receives itWhen the work is finished
Includes backlog waitingYesNo
Question it answersHow long will the customer wait?How fast does our process run?

Exact definitions vary between organisations. What matters is agreeing where each one starts and stops on your board.

An example

A customer requests a report on 1 March. It waits in the backlog until the team starts it on 10 March and finishes on 14 March. The lead time is about 13 days; the cycle time is about 4 days. The customer experiences 13 days, even though the team worked for only 4.

Why both matter

  • Lead time reflects customer experience and the cost of a long backlog.
  • Cycle time reflects how efficient and predictable your process is once work starts.
  • A short cycle time with a long lead time means the backlog is too long or priorities are unclear.

How to improve each

To reduceDo this
Lead timeKeep the backlog short; make clear, quick decisions about what to start; say no or not yet sooner
Cycle timeLower WIP limits, split work, remove waits and blockers

Frequently asked questions

Is lead time always longer than cycle time?

Yes, when lead time includes the time before work starts, because cycle time is part of lead time.

Which should I report to customers?

Lead time, because it reflects their experience. Use cycle time internally to improve your process.
