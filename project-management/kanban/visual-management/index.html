Visual Management: Make Work and Problems Visible

Visual management means arranging information so that anyone can understand the state of work at a glance, without asking or reading reports. It is a cornerstone of Lean and the reason Kanban uses boards.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 18 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Visual management makes work and problems obvious at a glance.
  • Boards, andon and 5S are classic tools.
  • Display only information that leads to action.

Why it works

  • Problems become visible immediately, so they get fixed sooner.
  • Everyone shares the same picture, which reduces status meetings.
  • Standards and expectations are clear.
  • Teams can manage themselves with less supervision.

Common visual management tools

ToolWhat it shows
Kanban boardWork items and their stage
AndonA signal that a problem has occurred and help is needed
5S workplace organisationEverything has a visible place
Performance boardsKey metrics against targets
Shadow boardsWhere each tool belongs; missing tools are obvious
Colour codingStatus, priority or type

5S

  1. Sort: remove what isn't needed.
  2. Set in order: a place for everything.
  3. Shine: clean and inspect.
  4. Standardise: make good practice the norm.
  5. Sustain: keep it up through habits and audits.

In digital work, 5S applies to shared drives, boards and tools as much as physical spaces.

Visual management for project teams

  • A team board with WIP limits and blockers.
  • A milestone timeline on the wall or dashboard.
  • A visible risk list with owners.
  • Simple charts: burnup, cumulative flow.
  • Red, amber and green indicators only where they trigger action.

Frequently asked questions

What is an andon in visual management?

A signal, originally a cord or light on a production line, that any worker can use to flag a problem and call for help.

Does visual management work for remote teams?

Yes, through shared digital boards and dashboards that everyone sees daily.

What is visual management?

Visual management is the practice of making work, standards and problems visible at a glance, so anyone can see the current state and act quickly.

What are examples of visual management?

Kanban boards, andon lights, 5S floor markings, shadow boards for tools, performance charts and colour-coded status indicators.

What are the benefits of visual management?

Faster problem detection, shared understanding, less time spent in status meetings and clearer standards that make deviations obvious.

What is a visual management board?

A board displaying key information for a team or area, such as work status, targets, issues and actions. A Kanban board is one common type.

What is a shadow board in visual management?

A board with the outline of each tool painted or drawn on it, so it is immediately clear where each tool belongs and which are missing.

How is visual management used in project management?

Project teams use Kanban boards, Gantt charts, RAG status indicators, burnup charts and risk heat maps to make progress and problems visible.

What is the difference between visual management and visual controls?

Visual controls are the individual tools, such as signs and markings. Visual management is the wider system of using them to run and improve work.

What makes a good visual management display?

It is simple, current, visible where the work happens, and shows clearly what is normal and what is not, so it prompts action.

How is RAG status used in visual management?

Red, amber, green status is a simple visual signal of whether something is on track, at risk or off track. Define each colour clearly so it is used consistently; see project status reports.
