Lessons Learned in Project Management: Template and Examples

Lessons learned are the knowledge gained during a project about what worked well and what should be done differently. Capturing and sharing them helps the same team, and future projects, repeat successes and avoid repeating mistakes.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 2 October 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Lessons learned capture what worked and what to change.
  • Capture them throughout the project, not only at the end.
  • Record impact, root cause and a specific recommendation with an owner.
  • Lessons only count when they change future practice.

What lessons learned are

A lesson learned is not just an observation such as 'testing was late'. A useful lesson explains what happened, why, what effect it had and what should be done next time. It should be specific enough that someone who was not on the project could act on it.

  • Positive lessons record practices worth repeating.
  • Negative lessons record problems and how to prevent them.
  • Recommendations turn each lesson into a clear action for future work.

In the PMBOK Guide sixth edition, lessons are recorded in a lessons learned register during the project and moved to the organisation's lessons learned repository at closure. PRINCE2 uses a lessons log during the project and a lessons report.

When to capture lessons

WhenWhy
Throughout the projectDetails are fresh and lessons can be applied straight away
At the end of each phase or stageNatural pause to reflect before the next phase starts
After a major issue or successCapture root causes while evidence is available
At project closureReview the whole project and transfer lessons to the organisation
Tip

Agile teams already capture lessons every iteration in the sprint retrospective. Feed the most important ones into a shared register so other teams benefit.

How to run a lessons learned session

  1. Prepare: gather the plan, baselines, status reports, issue log and risk register. Send a short survey in advance.
  2. Invite the right people: core team, key stakeholders, suppliers and, where possible, the sponsor.
  3. Set ground rules: focus on processes, not blame. Use a neutral facilitator if the project was difficult.
  4. Review the timeline: walk through the main events and decisions.
  5. Ask three questions: what went well, what did not go well, and what should we do differently?
  6. Find root causes for the main problems, for example with a fishbone diagram or the 5 whys.
  7. Agree recommendations with an owner for each one.
  8. Record and share the results in the register and repository.

Making lessons stick

The most common failure is that lessons are captured and then never used. Organisations sometimes call these 'lessons documented' rather than learned.

  • Make reviewing past lessons a step in project initiation and planning.
  • Store lessons in a searchable repository with consistent categories.
  • Turn recurring lessons into changes to templates, checklists and methods, often owned by a PMO.
  • Track whether recommendations were actually applied.
  • Share short summaries in community of practice meetings.

Frequently asked questions

What are lessons learned in project management?

Knowledge gained during a project about what worked well and what should be done differently, recorded so it can improve future work.

When should lessons learned be captured?

Throughout the project, at the end of each phase, after significant events and at project closure.

What is a lessons learned register?

A project document that records lessons as they are identified, including impact, root cause, recommendations and owners.

What is the difference between a lessons learned register and repository?

The register belongs to one project. The repository is the organisation's store of lessons from many projects.

Who should attend a lessons learned meeting?

The core team, key stakeholders, suppliers where relevant, and ideally the sponsor, with a neutral facilitator for difficult projects.

What questions should I ask in a lessons learned session?

What went well, what did not go well, why, and what should we do differently next time?

How do you avoid blame in lessons learned?

Set ground rules, focus on processes and decisions rather than individuals, and use a neutral facilitator.

Is a retrospective the same as lessons learned?

They share the same purpose. A retrospective is a regular, short Agile event; a lessons learned review often covers a whole phase or project.

How does the PRINCE2 lessons log record lessons learned?

A PRINCE2 record of lessons gathered during the project, which feeds the lessons report.

Why are lessons learned often ignored?

Because they are captured too late, stored where nobody looks, or not turned into changes to processes and templates.

How do you write a good lesson learned?

Describe what happened, its impact, the root cause and a specific, actionable recommendation with an owner.
