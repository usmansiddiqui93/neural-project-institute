Project Management Office (PMO): Types, Roles and Setup

A project management office (PMO) is a team or function that sets standards for how projects are run and supports, monitors or directly manages them. Its aim is more consistent delivery and better visibility of the organisation's project work.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 4 October 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A PMO standardises and supports project delivery across an organisation.
  • Supportive, controlling and directive PMOs differ in how much control they hold.
  • Start small, deliver visible value and expand services as trust grows.

Types of PMO

TypeLevel of controlWhat it does
SupportiveLowProvides templates, training, advice and lessons learned
ControllingMediumRequires compliance with methods, reviews and reporting
DirectiveHighDirectly manages projects; project managers report into the PMO

PMOs can also exist at different levels: a project or programme office for one large initiative, a departmental PMO, or an enterprise PMO linked to portfolio management. See also the Agile PMO.

Common PMO services

  • Methods, templates and standards, such as a project charter and status report format.
  • Portfolio reporting and dashboards.
  • Project governance, stage gates and assurance reviews.
  • Resource and capacity planning across projects.
  • Training, coaching and a project management community.
  • Tool administration for the organisation's project management software.
  • Capturing and sharing lessons learned.

PMO roles

RoleTypical responsibility
Head of PMOSets direction, reports to executives, owns the PMO's value
PMO managerRuns PMO services day to day
PMO analystCollects data, builds reports and dashboards
PMO coordinator or support officerAdministration, document control and scheduling
Portfolio managerPrioritises and balances the project portfolio

How to set up a PMO

  1. Identify the problems the PMO should solve, such as poor visibility or late projects.
  2. Agree a sponsor and decide the PMO type and scope.
  3. Start with a small set of high-value services, for example a single portfolio report.
  4. Define light standards and templates, and pilot them with friendly projects.
  5. Choose a shared tool so data is collected once; see project management tools.
  6. Measure and publish the PMO's impact.
  7. Expand services as trust grows.
Tip

A PMO that only collects reports is seen as overhead. Make sure project managers get something useful back, such as help with risks or resources.

Example

An organisation runs 25 projects with no shared view. Executives cannot tell which are in trouble. A new supportive PMO introduces a one-page status template and a monthly portfolio dashboard in its first quarter. By the second quarter, two projects with overlapping scope have been merged and three resource conflicts resolved before they caused delays.

Frequently asked questions

What is a PMO?

A project management office: a function that sets project standards and supports, monitors or manages projects across an organisation.

What are the three types of PMO?

Supportive, controlling and directive, ranging from low to high control over projects.

What does a PMO analyst do?

Collects project data, maintains reports and dashboards, and supports governance processes.

What is the difference between a PMO and a project manager?

A project manager leads a single project; a PMO supports and oversees many projects and sets common standards.

What is an enterprise PMO?

A PMO at organisation level that links projects to strategy and portfolio decisions.

Does an Agile organisation need a PMO?

Many keep one, often reshaped as an Agile PMO or value management office focused on flow and outcomes.

Why do PMOs fail?

Common reasons are adding bureaucracy without value, lacking executive support and being unclear about purpose.

How do you measure PMO success?

Through measures such as on-time delivery, forecast accuracy, benefits realised and satisfaction of project managers and executives.

What tools does a PMO use?

Portfolio and work management platforms such as Wrike or Smartsheet, plus reporting and BI tools.

How do I start a career in a PMO?

PMO coordinator and analyst roles are good entry points, especially for people with strong data and organisation skills.
