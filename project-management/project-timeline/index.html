Project Timeline: How to Create One, With Examples

A project timeline is a visual summary of a project's key phases, milestones and deadlines in date order. It shows when the main things will happen, without the detail of every task and dependency, which makes it ideal for sponsors, clients and other stakeholders.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 27 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A project timeline summarises phases and milestones in date order.
  • Derive it from the detailed schedule so dates are realistic.
  • Keep it to one page for sponsors and clients.
  • Show status and update it when the baseline changes.

Timeline vs Gantt chart vs schedule

Project timelineGantt chartProject schedule
DetailPhases and milestonesEvery activity with bars and linksFull data: activities, logic, resources, dates
AudienceExecutives, clients, wider teamProject team and managersProject manager and planners
PurposeCommunicate the big picturePlan and track workCalculate and control dates
Typical sizeOne page or slideMany linesA scheduling file

How to create a project timeline

  1. Start from the scope and main deliverables.
  2. Build or update the detailed schedule first so the dates are realistic rather than guessed.
  3. Pick the phases and milestones that matter to your audience, usually five to fifteen items.
  4. Choose a format: a horizontal line, phase bars, or a simple table.
  5. Mark today's date and show what is complete, on track or at risk.
  6. Add a short key and the date the timeline was last updated.
  7. Update it whenever the schedule baseline changes.

Example: 6-month software rollout

MonthPhaseMilestone
Month 1DiscoveryRequirements signed off (end of month 1)
Months 2-3ConfigurationSystem configured for pilot (week 11)
Month 4PilotPilot complete with 50 users (end of month 4)
Month 5Training and data migrationAll staff trained (week 20)
Month 6Go-live and supportGo-live (week 23); project closed (end of month 6)

Behind this one-page view sits a schedule of perhaps 150 activities. If the pilot slips by two weeks, the timeline shows the knock-on effect on go-live at a glance, which is what the sponsor needs to see.

Tips for a clear timeline

  • Use plain language rather than activity codes.
  • Highlight decisions you need from the audience, not just dates.
  • Show forecast and baseline dates when something has moved.
  • Use colour sparingly and consistently, for example for status.
  • Pair it with a project status report or project roadmap for regular updates.
Tip

Never draw a timeline from wishful dates. Derive it from a schedule with real dependencies, or it will be wrong by the first review.

Tools for timelines

Frequently asked questions

What is a project timeline?

A visual, date-ordered summary of a project's phases, milestones and deadlines.

What is the difference between a project timeline and a Gantt chart?

A timeline shows only key events and phases; a Gantt chart shows every activity with durations and dependencies.

What should a project timeline include?

Phases, key milestones, deadlines, the current date and status, and when it was last updated.

How do you create a project timeline?

Build the detailed schedule, select the key phases and milestones, place them on a dated line or bars, and keep it updated.

How many milestones should a project timeline show?

Usually between five and fifteen, enough to tell the story without clutter.

Is a project timeline the same as a roadmap?

Not quite. A roadmap is more strategic and often less precise about dates; a timeline is tied to the schedule.

Can I make a project timeline in Excel or PowerPoint?

Yes, both work well for one-page timelines, but they do not update automatically when the schedule changes.

How often should a project timeline be updated?

Whenever key dates change, and at least as often as you report status.

What does a project timeline look like in Agile?

Agile teams often use a release timeline or roadmap showing sprints and planned releases; see agile release planning.

Why is a project timeline important?

It gives stakeholders a quick shared view of when things will happen and helps spot slippage early.
