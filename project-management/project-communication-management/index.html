Project Communication Management: Process and Best Practice

Project communication management is the process of making sure the right people receive the right information, in the right format, at the right time. It covers planning what to communicate, sharing it, and checking that it has been understood and acted on.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 2 October 2026
  • 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Communication management covers planning, delivering and monitoring project information.
  • Tailor message, method and frequency to each audience.
  • Channels grow fast: n(n - 1) / 2.
  • Always check that messages are understood and adjust the plan.

Why communication management matters

Many project problems, such as surprised sponsors, duplicated work and disputed scope, come from poor communication rather than poor technical work. Good communication builds trust, speeds up decisions and makes risks and issues visible while they are still easy to fix.

  • Keeps stakeholders informed and engaged.
  • Supports timely decisions by the sponsor and governance boards.
  • Reduces misunderstanding about scope, priorities and responsibilities.
  • Creates a record of decisions and agreements.

The three core processes

The PMBOK Guide sixth edition describes communication management in three processes. They form a simple cycle that repeats throughout the project.

ProcessWhat happensKey output
Plan communications managementAnalyse stakeholder needs and decide who gets what, when and howCommunication plan
Manage communicationsCreate, distribute, store and retrieve project informationReports, presentations, meeting notes
Monitor communicationsCheck that communication is working and adjust the planFeedback, updates to the plan

Communication methods and channels

MethodDescriptionExamplesBest for
InteractiveTwo-way, real-time exchangeMeetings, calls, video, workshopsDecisions, sensitive topics, resolving conflict
PushSent to specific peopleEmail, status reports, newslettersRegular updates people must receive
PullStored for people to access when neededIntranet, shared drive, project tool dashboardsReference material and large audiences

The number of potential communication channels grows quickly with team size. The formula is n(n - 1) / 2, where n is the number of people.

  • 5 people: 5 x 4 / 2 = 10 channels.
  • 10 people: 10 x 9 / 2 = 45 channels.
  • Adding 2 people to a team of 10 gives 12 x 11 / 2 = 66 channels, an increase of 21.
Tip

The channels formula appears often in exam questions. If asked how many channels were added, calculate both totals and subtract.

How to manage project communication step by step

  1. Identify stakeholders and analyse their interest, influence and information needs, for example with a RACI matrix.
  2. Agree objectives for communication: inform, consult, gain approval or build support.
  3. Choose methods and frequency for each audience, matching detail to the reader.
  4. Write the communication plan and agree it with the sponsor.
  5. Deliver consistently, using templates so reports are quick to read.
  6. Gather feedback through check-ins and short surveys, and watch for signs of confusion.
  7. Adjust the plan as stakeholders, phases and risks change.

Worked example

A 9-month ERP rollout has a steering committee, 4 department heads, a 12-person project team, an external supplier and 600 end users. The project manager tailors communication to each group.

AudienceNeedMethodFrequency
Steering committeeProgress, key risks, decisions neededMeeting plus one-page RAG reportMonthly
Department headsImpact on their teams, readiness actionsBriefing callFortnightly
Project teamTasks, blockers, prioritiesStand-up and team boardDaily
SupplierDeliverables, issues, acceptanceJoint progress meetingWeekly
End usersWhat is changing, when, training datesNewsletter and intranet pageMonthly, weekly near go-live

Three months in, feedback shows end users are not reading the newsletter. The project manager adds short team-meeting briefings delivered by department heads, and readiness survey scores improve. This is the monitor step in action.

Common mistakes and tips

  • One message for everyone: executives need decisions and exceptions; teams need detail.
  • Reporting activity, not outcomes: say what has changed and what you need, not just what was done.
  • Hiding bad news: raise problems early, with options.
  • No feedback loop: sending is not the same as communicating. Check understanding.
  • Too many channels: agree where decisions and documents live, for example one project management tool.

Frequently asked questions

What is project communication management?

The planning, delivery and monitoring of project information so that stakeholders receive what they need, when they need it, in a useful format.

What are the processes of communication management?

In the PMBOK Guide sixth edition: plan communications management, manage communications and monitor communications.

How do you calculate communication channels in communication management?

Use n(n - 1) / 2, where n is the number of people. A team of 8 has 28 channels.

What is the difference between push and pull communication in project communication management?

Push communication is sent to specific recipients, such as an email. Pull communication is stored for people to access themselves, such as an intranet page.

What is interactive communication in communication management?

Real-time, two-way exchange such as meetings, calls and workshops. It is best for decisions and complex or sensitive topics.

How much of a project manager's time goes on communication management?

A large share. Most of a project manager's work involves meetings, reporting, negotiating and coordinating with people.

How does a communication plan support communication management?

It is the main output of planning communication management: a document that sets out who needs what information, why, how and how often, and who is responsible for providing it.

How should communication management handle senior stakeholders?

Keep it short, lead with status and decisions needed, focus on exceptions and risks, and follow an agreed reporting rhythm.

What barriers does project communication management need to overcome?

Jargon, information overload, time zones, language differences, unclear ownership and too many tools or channels.

How do you know if project communication management is working?

Ask for feedback, check whether decisions are made on time, and watch for repeated questions, surprises or rework caused by misunderstanding.

What role does the kick-off meeting play in project communication management?

It sets a shared starting point. The kick-off meeting should communicate the project purpose, objectives, scope, key milestones, roles and how the team will communicate, so everyone starts with the same understanding.
