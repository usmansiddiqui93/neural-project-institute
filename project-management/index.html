What is project management?

Project management is the practice of applying knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to deliver a specific result within agreed limits of time, cost, scope and quality. Every project moves through five phases: initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and controlling, and closure. Read the full introduction.

Project management guide: frequently asked questions

What is a project management guide?

A project management guide explains how to plan, run and close projects: the phases, processes, tools and methodologies that help teams deliver results on time and on budget. This free guide from the Neural Project Institute covers all of them, from fundamentals to Agile, Scrum and Kanban.

How do I learn project management from scratch?

Start with the fundamentals (what a project is, the five phases and the main methodologies), then learn planning, scheduling, cost and risk techniques, and practise them on a small real project. Our learning path below lists the guides to read in order.

What are the 5 phases of project management?

Initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and controlling, and closure.

Which project management methodology should I use?

Use a predictive (Waterfall) approach when requirements are stable, an Agile approach such as Scrum or Kanban when they are uncertain, and a hybrid when you need both governance and flexibility.

Do I need a certification to be a project manager?

Not always, but certifications such as PMP, PRINCE2, Scrum certifications or PMAI help you show your knowledge, especially when changing careers.
