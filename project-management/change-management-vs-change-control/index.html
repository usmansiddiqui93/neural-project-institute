Change Management vs Change Control: Key Differences Explained

Change control is the formal process for approving and recording changes to a project's scope, schedule, cost or deliverables. Change management is the wider discipline of helping people and organisations adopt a change, so that the new way of working is actually used and the benefits are realised.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Change control protects baselines; change management protects benefits.
  • Change control decides on changes to scope, time and cost.
  • Change management helps people adopt new ways of working.
  • Check which meaning people intend, especially in IT.

The short answer

Change control asks 'should we change the plan?' Change management asks 'how do we help people change how they work?' One protects the project's baselines; the other protects the project's benefits. Most projects that alter how people work need both.

Example: a new expenses system

  • Change control: midway through the project, finance asks to add mileage claims with map look-ups. The project manager raises a change request, estimates +£9,000 and +3 weeks, and the change control board approves it. The schedule and budget baselines are updated.
  • Change management: 1,200 staff must stop using paper forms. The change team identifies affected groups, briefs line managers first, runs short training sessions, sets up a help channel, and tracks the share of claims submitted through the new system each week. Paper forms are withdrawn once usage passes an agreed level.

Common change management models

ModelCore idea
Kotter's 8 stepsBuild urgency and a guiding coalition, communicate a vision, remove barriers, create short-term wins and anchor the change in culture
ADKAR (Prosci)Individuals move through awareness, desire, knowledge, ability and reinforcement
Lewin's modelUnfreeze current behaviour, make the change, then refreeze the new behaviour

These models describe the people side of change. They sit alongside, not instead of, project controls.

A third meaning: IT change

In IT service management, 'change management' often means controlling changes to live systems: assessing risk, scheduling deployments and approving releases, sometimes through a change advisory board. This is closer to change control than to organisational change management. When people use the term, check which meaning they intend.

How the two work together

  1. Use change management analysis early to understand who is affected and how much.
  2. Feed adoption needs, such as training or phased rollout, into the scope and plan.
  3. Route any resulting scope, cost or schedule changes through change control.
  4. Report adoption measures alongside delivery measures in the project status report.
  5. Carry adoption activities beyond project closure where benefits depend on them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between change management and change control?

Change control manages changes to the project's baselines; change management helps people adopt the change the project delivers.

Is change control part of change management?

They are separate disciplines. Change control is a project control process; change management is about people and organisational adoption. Both are often needed on the same project.

Who is responsible for change management?

The sponsor and business leaders are usually accountable, often supported by a dedicated change manager and the project team.

Who is responsible for change control?

The project manager runs the process; decisions are made by the person or board with the relevant authority.

What is a change control board?

A group that approves or rejects significant changes to a project's baselines.

What is a change advisory board?

In IT service management, a group that advises on and approves changes to live services.

What is ADKAR?

A change management model from Prosci describing five stages of individual change: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability and reinforcement.

Why do projects fail without change management?

The deliverable may be built correctly but not used as intended, so the expected benefits never appear.

Does a small project need change management?

If it changes how people work, yes, though it may be as simple as a briefing, a short guide and a check on usage.

Is change management the same as stakeholder management?

They overlap. Stakeholder management covers everyone with an interest in the project; change management focuses on those whose work or behaviour must change.

What is organisational change management?

The structured approach to moving an organisation and its people from current ways of working to new ones.
