What Is Project Management? Definition, Process and Skills

Project management is the discipline of planning, organising and leading work to deliver a specific result within agreed limits of time, cost, scope and quality. It turns an idea, such as a new product, an office move or a marketing campaign, into a plan people can carry out and leaders can track.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Project management delivers a unique result within limits of time, cost, scope and quality.
  • Projects move through initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and closure.
  • Approaches range from predictive Waterfall to Agile, Scrum and Kanban.

A simple definition

A project is a temporary effort to create a unique result. Project management applies knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to that effort, so it meets its objectives. It covers everything from defining the goal to closing the project and capturing lessons learned.

Why project management matters

  • Clear goals: everyone knows what success looks like.
  • Better decisions: trade-offs between scope, time and cost are made deliberately.
  • Fewer surprises: risks are identified and managed early.
  • Efficient use of people and money.
  • Accountability: roles and responsibilities are clear.
  • Stakeholder confidence: progress is visible and predictable.

The project life cycle

Most projects move through four or five phases:

  1. Initiation: define and authorise the project.
  2. Planning: build the scope, schedule, budget and plans.
  3. Execution: do the work and deliver the outputs.
  4. Monitoring and controlling: track progress and correct course, throughout.
  5. Closure: hand over, release resources and capture lessons.

Key knowledge areas

Traditional project management practice groups the work into areas such as integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communications, risk, procurement and stakeholders. Each has its own techniques, from the work breakdown structure to earned value.

What a project manager does

Project managers plan the work, lead the team, manage stakeholders, control risks and costs, and keep the project aligned with its goals. See project manager roles and responsibilities.

Frequently asked questions

What are the five phases of project management?

Initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and controlling, and closure.

Is project management a good career?

Project management skills are in demand across industries, and certified project managers can work in IT, construction, healthcare, finance and many other fields.

What is the triple constraint?

The link between scope, time and cost: changing one usually affects at least one of the others, with quality at the centre.
