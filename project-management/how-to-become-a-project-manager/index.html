How to Become a Project Manager: Step-by-Step Career Guide

You become a project manager by combining practical experience of coordinating work with a grounding in project management methods, usually supported by a recognised certification. Many people move in from another role rather than starting in project management directly.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 3 October 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most project managers move in from another role or start as coordinators.
  • Keep a detailed log of every project you run.
  • Combine practical evidence with a certification that matches your experience.

Common entry routes

RouteHow it works
Accidental PMYou already coordinate projects in your current job and formalise it
Coordinator firstStart as a project coordinator or assistant project manager and step up
Specialist to PMEngineers, analysts or designers move into leading projects in their field
Graduate schemeSome employers run project management trainee programmes
Career changeTransferable skills from teaching, hospitality, the military or operations

Step-by-step plan

  1. Learn the fundamentals: what project management is, the phases and core documents.
  2. Volunteer to run small projects at work or in the community to build evidence.
  3. Keep a log of projects: goal, size, your role, outcome and lessons.
  4. Pick an entry-level certification such as the CAPM, or the PMAI from the Neural Project Institute.
  5. Learn a mainstream project management tool.
  6. Rewrite your CV around outcomes delivered, using project language.
  7. Apply for coordinator, junior or assistant PM roles, or an internal move.
  8. Once you meet the experience requirement, consider the PMP.

Worked example: building an experience log

Suppose you are an operations analyst. Over 18 months you lead a supplier switch (4 months, 6 people), a new reporting dashboard (3 months, 3 people) and an office move (2 months, 10 people). Logging each with objectives, budget, risks handled and results gives you strong interview stories and a record you can later use for certification applications.

Skills employers look for

  • Planning and scheduling.
  • Clear written and spoken communication.
  • Stakeholder management and negotiation.
  • Risk and issue management.
  • Comfort with budgets and numbers.
  • Familiarity with Agile as well as traditional methods; see Agile vs Waterfall.

See project management skills for detail.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting for a job title before calling yourself experienced: count the projects you have already run.
  • Collecting certificates without practical examples to back them up.
  • Listing duties on your CV instead of results.
  • Ignoring the soft skills that make or break projects.
Tip

To compare pay, look at current job adverts in your area and sector, recent salary surveys from professional bodies such as PMI, and official labour statistics. Figures vary by location and change every year.

Frequently asked questions

Can I become a project manager without a degree?

Yes. Many project managers do not have a degree. Experience and certification matter more to most employers, though the PMP requires more experience if you do not hold a four-year degree.

Can I become a project manager with no experience?

You will usually start in a coordinator or assistant role, or by running small projects in your current job, then step up.

Which certification should I get first to become a project manager?

Beginners often start with the CAPM or an entry-level course; experienced practitioners aim for the PMP. See project management certification.

How long does it take to become a project manager?

It depends on your starting point. People moving from a related role sometimes step up within a year or two; others take longer to build experience.

Is it worth becoming a project manager?

It offers transferable skills used in almost every industry and clear progression to senior, programme and portfolio roles.

How much can you earn once you become a project manager?

Pay varies by country, industry and seniority. Check current job adverts, PMI salary surveys and government labour statistics for up-to-date figures.

What does it mean to become a project manager by accident?

It describes an accidental project manager: someone who ends up running projects as part of another job without formal training.

Do I need to learn Agile to become a project manager?

It helps. Many employers expect familiarity with Agile and Scrum alongside traditional methods; see how to learn Agile.

What entry-level jobs help you become a project manager?

Project coordinator, project administrator, PMO analyst, project support officer and assistant project manager.

Is becoming a project manager a stressful career choice?

It can be, because you are accountable for outcomes you do not fully control. Good planning, early escalation and clear boundaries help.
