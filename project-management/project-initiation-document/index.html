Project Initiation Document (PID): What It Is and What to Include

A project initiation document (PID) brings together the key information needed to start a project on a sound footing. In PRINCE2 it is a core management product created during the Initiating a project process and approved by the project board.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A PID provides a complete baseline for starting a project.
  • It is more detailed than a charter and central to PRINCE2.
  • Tailor its depth to the project's size and risk.

Typical contents

  • Project definition: background, objectives, scope, exclusions, deliverables, constraints and assumptions.
  • Business case.
  • Project approach.
  • Organisation structure and roles.
  • Quality, risk, change and communication management approaches.
  • Project plan with stages, milestones and tolerances.
  • Project controls and reporting arrangements.
  • Tailoring of the method to the project.

PID vs project charter

Project charterPID
PurposeAuthorise the projectProvide a complete baseline for running the project
DetailHigh levelDetailed
Typical usePMBOK-based environmentsPRINCE2 environments
Length2–4 pagesOften longer, depending on size

Tips

  • Tailor the depth to the size and risk of the project.
  • Link to supporting documents rather than repeating them.
  • Make tolerances explicit so management by exception works.
  • Keep it current: it is the baseline for project board decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Who approves the PID?

In PRINCE2, the project board approves it to authorise the project.

When is a PID written?

During initiation, after the project has been approved to start.

Is a PID only for PRINCE2?

The term comes from PRINCE2, but many organisations use PIDs regardless of method.

How long is a PID?

It varies. A small project may need a few pages; a large programme much more.

Does the PID replace the business case?

No. It contains or references the business case.

Can the PID change?

Yes, through change control, typically at stage boundaries.

What is the difference between a PID and a project plan?

The PID includes the project plan, plus the business case, organisation and management approaches.

Is a PID needed for Agile projects?

PRINCE2 Agile still uses a PID, typically lighter and focused on goals, tolerances and ways of working.

Who writes the PID?

The project manager, with input from the team and stakeholders.

What are tolerances in a PID?

Agreed limits for time, cost, scope, quality, risk and benefits within which the project manager can act without escalating.
