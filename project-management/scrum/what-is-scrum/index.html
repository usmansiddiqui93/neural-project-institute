What Is Scrum? The Scrum Framework Explained

Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams and organisations create value through adaptive solutions to complex problems. A small team works in short, fixed-length Sprints, delivering a usable Increment each time and using what it learns to plan the next one.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrum is a lightweight framework for complex work.
  • It has three accountabilities, five events and three artifacts.
  • Work happens in Sprints of one month or less that each deliver a usable Increment.

Scrum in one paragraph

A Product Owner orders the work in a Product Backlog. The Scrum Team turns a selection of that work into an Increment of value during a Sprint of one month or less. The team and its stakeholders inspect the results and adjust for the next Sprint. A Scrum Master helps everyone understand and use Scrum effectively. Repeat.

Where Scrum came from

The term comes from rugby and was used by Hirotaka Takeuchi and Ikujiro Nonaka in their 1986 Harvard Business Review article on product development. Jeff Sutherland and Ken Schwaber developed Scrum for software and presented it in 1995. They maintain the official Scrum Guide, first published in 2010 and last revised in 2020.

How a Sprint works

  1. Sprint Planning: the team sets a Sprint Goal and selects work.
  2. Daily Scrum: a 15-minute daily check on progress.
  3. Development: the team builds a usable Increment.
  4. Sprint Review: stakeholders inspect the result and the backlog is adapted.
  5. Sprint Retrospective: the team improves how it works.

See the Scrum process for more detail.

When to use Scrum

  • Complex work where requirements are likely to change.
  • A dedicated, cross-functional team.
  • A Product Owner who can decide priorities.
  • Work that can be delivered in usable increments.

For continuous, unpredictable work, consider Kanban.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scrum a methodology?

The Scrum Guide calls Scrum a framework rather than a methodology: it is deliberately incomplete, defining only the parts needed to apply empiricism and leaving the rest to the team.

Is Scrum only for software?

No. Scrum was designed for complex product work and is used in marketing, research, education and hardware as well as software.
