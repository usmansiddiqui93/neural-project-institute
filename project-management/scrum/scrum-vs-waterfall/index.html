Scrum vs Waterfall: Key Differences Explained

Scrum and Waterfall represent two different ways of managing work. Waterfall plans everything first and delivers at the end; Scrum delivers in short Sprints and adapts based on feedback.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Waterfall plans up front; Scrum plans every Sprint.
  • Scrum delivers increments; Waterfall delivers at the end.
  • Hybrids are common.

Side by side

WaterfallScrum
PlanningDetailed plan up frontSprint by Sprint, with a Product Goal
DeliveryAt the endA usable Increment every Sprint
RequirementsFixed in a specificationOrdered in a Product Backlog that evolves
RolesProject manager directsProduct Owner, Scrum Master, Developers
ChangeThrough change requestsRe-order the backlog between Sprints
TestingA late phaseWithin every Sprint
ProgressPhase completionWorking Increments

When Waterfall is better

  • Requirements are stable and fully known.
  • The output only has value when complete.
  • Regulation requires sequential, approved phases.

When Scrum is better

  • The solution is uncertain and needs discovery.
  • Users can give regular feedback.
  • Speed to market matters.

Hybrid

Many organisations keep Waterfall-style governance and milestones, with Scrum teams delivering within them. See Agile vs Waterfall.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scrum faster than Waterfall?

Scrum delivers usable results much sooner, though total project duration depends on scope.

Can a Waterfall project switch to Scrum?

Yes, ideally at a phase boundary, by turning remaining requirements into a Product Backlog.
