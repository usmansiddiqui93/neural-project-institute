Scrum Certification: Options, Costs and Training Guide

A Scrum certification is a credential from an organisation such as Scrum.org or Scrum Alliance showing that you understand Scrum, earned by passing an assessment, completing a course or both. The most common starting points are Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I) and Certified ScrumMaster (CSM) for Scrum Masters, and PSPO I or CSPO for Product Owners. This guide compares the main options, their fees and renewal rules (checked on the issuers' websites on 4 November 2026), and shows you how to choose the right certification and training.

Key takeaways

  • Scrum.org certifications (PSM, PSPO, PSD and others) are earned by passing an online assessment; a course is optional and the certification never expires.
  • Scrum Alliance certifications (CSM, CSPO) require a 16-hour course with an approved trainer and renew every two years with Scrum Education Units and a fee.
  • PSM I costs US$200 per attempt: 80 questions, 60 minutes, 85% to pass. CSM: 50 questions, 60 minutes, 74% to pass, with the test included in the course fee.
  • SAFe Scrum Master suits organisations that use SAFe; PMI-ACP suits experienced practitioners who want a broader Agile credential.
  • Compare total cost over three years, not only the first payment: course fees, retakes and renewals change the picture.
  • A certificate gets you interviews; experience on a real Scrum Team gets you the job. Plan both.

What is a Scrum certification?

A Scrum certification is a credential that shows you have learned Scrum to a defined standard, usually based on the 2020 Scrum Guide. Depending on the issuer, you earn it by passing a timed online assessment, by attending a course with an approved trainer, or both. Certifications are role-based: Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developer or leader.

Scrum itself does not require any certificate. The Scrum Guide is free, and anyone can practise the framework. Employers, however, often list a certification in job adverts as a quick signal that a candidate knows the vocabulary and rules, which is why many people take one early in their Scrum career.

It helps to separate two things that are often sold together:

  • Training is the learning experience: a two-day class, a self-paced course, coaching or self-study.
  • Certification is the credential: the assessment or course completion recorded by the issuing body, often with a digital badge you can share.

With Scrum.org you can certify without training; with Scrum Alliance the training is part of the certification. That single difference shapes cost, format and renewal, and it runs through every comparison in this guide.

Scrum.org certifications

Scrum.org was founded by Scrum co-creator Ken Schwaber and Alex Armstrong, and runs the Professional Scrum certifications. All of them are online assessments with a high pass mark, none requires a course, and none expires. Scrum.org's own count, last updated on 1 November 2026, lists 778,506 PSM I holders, 279,802 PSPO I holders and over 1.2 million Professional Scrum certifications in total.

Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I)

PSM I is the most widely held Scrum.org certification and shows a fundamental understanding of Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide.

  • Format: 80 multiple-choice, multiple-answer and true/false questions in 60 minutes.
  • Pass mark: 85%.
  • Fee: US$200 per attempt. Each password is valid for one attempt and does not expire.
  • Languages: English and Japanese, with Simplified Chinese through Scrum.org's China site.
  • Course: not required, but recommended. The official two-day Professional Scrum Master class includes one free attempt, and if you take it within 14 days and score below 85% you get a second attempt free.
  • Renewal: none. The certification is for life and includes a digital badge.

See our guide to the Scrum Master role for how PSM I fits a Scrum Master career.

Scrum Alliance certifications

Scrum Alliance certifications are built around live training. You attend a course taught by an approved trainer, complete any required work or test, and maintain the certification with continuing education.

Certified ScrumMaster (CSM)

  • Course: 16 hours of training, usually spread over two or three days, live online or in person.
  • Test: 50 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes; you need 37 correct (74%).
  • Attempts: the course fee includes two attempts within 90 days of the course; after that, each attempt costs $25.
  • Cost: set by the trainer. Scrum Alliance quotes a range of $250 to $2,495 based on public course prices as of October 2023.
  • Languages: the test is available in 13 languages, including English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Simplified Chinese.

Certified Scrum Product Owner (CSPO)

CSPO requires a 16-hour class, live online or in person. Scrum Alliance currently has no test requirement for CSPO: you earn it by completing the course to your trainer's satisfaction. See our guide to the Product Owner accountability for what the role involves day to day.

SAFe Scrum Master and other options

SAFe Scrum Master

Scaled Agile, Inc. offers the SAFe Scrum Master certification, now taught through its AI-Empowered SAFe Scrum Master course. It covers the Scrum Master's role inside a Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) organisation: working on an Agile Release Train, PI Planning and coordinating with other teams.

  • Exam: 45 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes; pass mark 80%.
  • Routes: attend the course (two exam attempts included if taken within 60 days), or buy a proctored exam without the course for $495 plus tax, which also includes two attempts within 60 days.
  • Renewal: annual, at the SAFe Foundational rate of $195 a year plus tax, with 24 continuing education units over each two-year cycle.

Choose it if you work, or want to work, in a SAFe organisation; for lighter ways to scale Scrum, compare Scrum@Scale. Outside SAFe, PSM I or CSM are more widely recognised as Scrum credentials.

PMI Agile Certified Practitioner (PMI-ACP)

The PMI-ACP covers several Agile approaches, including Scrum and Kanban, and suits practitioners who already have Agile experience. The exam has 120 questions in 180 minutes, and PMI lists the fee as $435 for members and $495 for non-members. Eligibility requires Agile training and Agile project experience; PMI's certification page lists a secondary degree, 21 contact hours of Agile training, 12 months of general project experience in the last five years (a current PMP or PgMP satisfies this) and 8 months of Agile project experience in the last three years (checked 4 November 2026). Renewal is 30 PDUs every three years. Our project management certification guide compares it with the PMP.

ICAgile and PMAI

ICAgile accredits courses run by training providers rather than setting a central exam: you earn its knowledge-based ICAgile Certified Professional (ICP) certifications by completing an accredited class, and its expert level through demonstrated competence. Our own PMAI certification (Project Management Artificial Intelligence) includes Sprintcraft: Agile & Scrum Practitioner, one of six online courses with assessed chapters. It complements, rather than replaces, a Scrum-specific credential.

PSM I vs CSM: which should you choose?

These are the two most common first certifications for Scrum Masters. Both are respected; they differ mainly in how you earn and keep them.

Awarded byScrum.orgScrum Alliance
Course requiredNo (a course is recommended, not required)Yes: 16 hours with an approved trainer
Exam80 questions, 60 minutes, 85% to pass50 questions, 60 minutes, 74% to pass
CostUS$200 per attempt (assessment only)Included in the course fee, which varies by trainer
RenewalLifetime; no renewal feeEvery two years: US$100 and 20 SEUs
Best forSelf-directed learners; people who want the lowest ongoing costPeople who learn best in a live class, or whose employer pays for training

Many people find PSM I harder to pass because of its high pass mark and the lack of a required course, while CSM's value lies in the class itself: two or three days of discussion and exercises with an experienced trainer. If your employer will fund a course, CSM gives you structured training and the credential together. If you are paying yourself, PSM I through self-study is the cheapest recognised route. Check job adverts in your market: in some regions and sectors one is mentioned more often than the other.

What Scrum certification really costs: a worked example

The following example is illustrative. The course fees are assumptions chosen for the calculation; the exam and renewal fees are the issuers' published figures.

Scenario. Sam, a business analyst, wants a Scrum Master certification and is comparing four routes over three years. Sam assumes a public PSM course costs $1,000 and a CSM course $800 in their area.

RouteYear 1Year 2Year 3Three-year total
PSM I by self-study (passes first time)$200 exam$0$0$200
PSM I by self-study (needs a second attempt)$400 (two exams)$0$0$400
PSM class (includes exam attempt)$1,000$0$0$1,000
CSM course (includes two test attempts)$800$0$100 renewal + 20 SEUs$900
SAFe Scrum Master proctored exam, no course$495$195 renewal$195 renewal$885

Three points stand out. First, the cheapest route is self-study for PSM I, but only if you prepare well: each failed attempt adds $200. Second, CSM's renewal is small in money but needs 20 SEUs every two years, which take time. Third, SAFe's annual renewal adds up: over five years it would add $780 on top of the exam. None of this makes one option better; it simply means you should compare the full cost, and ask your employer which costs it will cover.

Tip

Many employers pay for certification as part of a training plan. Ask before you pay for a course yourself, and ask whether they will also fund renewals.

How to choose a Scrum certification step by step

  1. Start with your role. Scrum Master: PSM I or CSM. Product Owner: PSPO I or CSPO. Developer: PSD. Manager or leader: PAL I. If you are unsure of the differences, read our guide to Scrum Team roles first.
  2. Check your context. If your organisation uses SAFe, a SAFe role certification may matter more. If it runs a hybrid of Scrum and Kanban (see Kanban vs Scrum), PSK I is worth a look.
  3. Look at real job adverts. Collect ten adverts for the role you want in your area and note which certifications they name.
  4. Decide how you learn best. Self-directed learners suit Scrum.org assessments; people who value a live class suit Scrum Alliance courses.
  5. Work out the three-year cost, including likely retakes, renewals and the time needed to earn SEUs or CEUs.
  6. Choose a trainer or study plan, checking reviews, the trainer's credentials and that materials follow the 2020 Scrum Guide.
  7. Plan how you will use it. Book a date, then line up practical experience: facilitating a retrospective, supporting backlog refinement or joining a Scrum Team.

How to prepare for the PSM I assessment

PSM I questions test whether you understand the Scrum Guide precisely, including its exact wording on accountabilities, events and commitments. This four-week plan is our suggestion for someone new to Scrum studying about five hours a week.

WeekFocusActivities
1The Scrum GuideRead the Scrum Guide twice. Summarise the three accountabilities, five events and three artifacts with their commitments in your own words.
2Theory and valuesStudy empiricism, the five values and self-management. Take the free Scrum Open practice assessment and note every wrong answer.
3Events and artifacts in depthReview timeboxes, who attends each event and what happens when a Sprint is cancelled. Retake Scrum Open until you score 100% several times in a row.
4Exam readinessDo timed practice at 45 seconds a question. Re-read the guide once more, then book the assessment while it is fresh.
  • Use the Scrum Guide as your primary source; if a study aid disagrees with it, the guide wins.
  • Our guides to Scrum ceremonies, Scrum artifacts and Scrum values cover the most-tested areas, and the Scrum glossary defines the terms.
  • Read each question carefully for words such as "must", "should" and "best".
  • Leave hard questions and come back: 80 questions in 60 minutes leaves little time to linger.

Getting the most from a CSM or CSPO course

Because the course is the certification, preparation pays off in what you learn rather than in a pass mark.

  1. Read the Scrum Guide before day one, so class time goes on discussion rather than definitions.
  2. Bring two or three real problems from your workplace to ask the trainer about.
  3. Take part fully in exercises; they are where most of the learning happens.
  4. Take the CSM test within a few days, while the material is fresh and your two included attempts are still available.
  5. Afterwards, try one new practice with your team within a fortnight, such as a better Sprint Retrospective format.

Is a Scrum certification worth it?

For most people starting out, yes, with limits. A certification is useful when:

  • You are moving into a Scrum Master or Product Owner role and need to get past CV screening.
  • You want a structured way to learn Scrum properly rather than from second-hand descriptions.
  • Your employer funds it, or the role you want names it.

It is less useful on its own. Hiring managers usually ask what you have done with Scrum: how you handled a difficult retrospective, how you helped a team stop carrying work over between Sprints. A certificate cannot answer those questions. See our guide on how to become a Scrum Master to build that experience alongside a certification.

Common mistakes when choosing Scrum training

  • Comparing only the first payment. Renewals, retakes and time spent on SEUs change the total cost.
  • Booking PSM I too early. At an 85% pass mark, a few weak areas cause a fail. Practise until you score well above it.
  • Collecting certificates instead of experience. Two entry-level certificates add less than one year on a real team.
  • Choosing a SAFe credential when you do not work with SAFe, or a pure Scrum credential when your organisation runs SAFe.
  • Using outdated materials. Make sure courses and practice questions reflect the 2020 Scrum Guide, which replaced the term "Development Team" with "Developers".
  • Letting a Scrum Alliance certification lapse because the renewal email went unnoticed. Note the renewal date when you pass.

Frequently asked questions

Which Scrum certification is most recognised?

PSM I from Scrum.org and CSM from Scrum Alliance are the most widely recognised entry-level Scrum Master certifications. Scrum.org's published count shows over 778,000 PSM I holders as of 1 November 2026.

Which is harder, PSM I or CSM?

Most people find PSM I harder: it has an 85% pass mark and no required course. The CSM test has a 74% pass mark and follows a 16-hour course that covers the material.

Can I get Scrum certified without taking a course?

Yes. All Scrum.org certifications, including PSM I and PSPO I, can be earned by passing the online assessment without a course. Scrum Alliance certifications require a course; the SAFe Scrum Master exam can be bought as a proctored exam without the course.

Do Scrum certifications expire?

Scrum.org certifications do not expire and have no renewal fee. Scrum Alliance certifications renew every two years with SEUs and a fee ($100 for CSM or CSPO). SAFe certifications renew annually.

How much does Scrum certification cost?

PSM I and PSPO I cost US$200 per attempt. CSM and CSPO are included in a course fee set by the trainer; Scrum Alliance quotes $250 to $2,495 for CSM based on October 2023 prices. The SAFe Scrum Master proctored exam is $495.

How long does it take to get Scrum certified?

A CSM or CSPO takes the course length, usually two or three days, plus the CSM test. For PSM I, most people new to Scrum need a few weeks of study; experienced practitioners may need only a few days.

What is the best Scrum certification for beginners?

PSM I or CSM for aspiring Scrum Masters, and PSPO I or CSPO for aspiring Product Owners. None requires prior experience. Choose by how you prefer to learn and what employers in your market ask for.

Can I study for a Scrum certification for free?

Yes. The Scrum Guide is free, and Scrum.org offers free open practice assessments. You only pay for the PSM I or PSPO I assessment itself.

Is there a Scrum certification for Developers?

Yes. Scrum.org's Professional Scrum Developer (PSD) assessment covers Scrum and modern development practices, and Scrum Alliance offers Certified Scrum Developer (CSD) through its courses.

Is SAFe Scrum Master a worthwhile certification?

It is useful if you work, or want to work, in an organisation that uses SAFe, because it covers how Scrum Teams operate on an Agile Release Train. Outside SAFe, PSM I or CSM are more widely recognised.

Is PMI-ACP a Scrum certification?

Not exclusively. The PMI-ACP covers several Agile approaches, including Scrum, and requires Agile training and experience. Many people take PSM I or CSM first and the PMI-ACP later.

Can a PSM I holder take Scrum Alliance's advanced certification?

Yes. Scrum Alliance accepts a Scrum.org PSM I or PSM II in place of the CSM as the prerequisite for the Advanced CSM, alongside 12 months of validated Scrum Master experience in the past five years.

What comes after an entry-level Scrum certification?

Real experience first, then an advanced level such as PSM II or A-CSM, or a complementary credential such as PSK I or PSPO I. See our guide on how to become a Scrum Master.
