Servant Leadership in Scrum: What It Means in Practice

The Scrum Guide describes Scrum Masters as true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organisation. Servant leadership means leading by helping others succeed rather than by directing them.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Servant leaders lead by helping others succeed.
  • Remove obstacles, coach, protect focus and grow people.
  • Serving the team sometimes means challenging it.

Origins

Robert K. Greenleaf introduced the idea of servant leadership in a 1970 essay, The Servant as Leader. His test was whether those served grow as persons and become healthier, wiser, freer and more autonomous.

Servant leadership in practice

BehaviourExample
Removing obstaclesGetting a test environment fixed so the team can finish its work
Coaching, not tellingAsking "what options do you see?" instead of giving the answer
Protecting focusRedirecting mid-Sprint requests to the Product Owner
Growing peopleEncouraging team members to facilitate events themselves
ListeningHolding one-to-ones to understand concerns
Building trustAdmitting mistakes openly

Serving is not pleasing

Servant leaders serve the team's long-term effectiveness, not every short-term wish. That sometimes means challenging the team, holding it to the Definition of Done, or telling stakeholders uncomfortable truths. Courage is one of the Scrum values.

Managers as servant leaders

Managers of Scrum Teams also practise servant leadership: setting clear goals, removing organisational barriers, and investing in people's growth, while leaving day-to-day decisions to the team. See how to manage Agile teams.

Frequently asked questions

Is servant leadership weak leadership?

No. It requires strong conviction, courage and the ability to influence without formal authority.

Who practises servant leadership in Scrum?

Above all the Scrum Master, but also Product Owners and managers who support Scrum Teams.
