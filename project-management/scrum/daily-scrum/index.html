Daily Scrum: Purpose, Format, Rules and Examples

The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for the Developers of a Scrum Team to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog, held at the same time and place every working day. Many teams call it the daily stand-up. This guide explains what the Scrum Guide 2020 requires, who attends, how to run a Daily Scrum step by step, which formats work best, and how to fix the most common problems, with a worked example from a real-looking Sprint.

What is the Daily Scrum?

The Daily Scrum is one of the five Scrum events. It is a 15-minute event for the Developers, held at the same time and place every working day, to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary. Its output is an actionable plan for the next day of work.

The Daily Scrum takes place inside the Sprint, between Sprint Planning at the start and the Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective at the end. In a two-week Sprint the team holds it roughly eight or nine times. Each one is a small loop of inspection and adaptation: look at where the team is against the Sprint Goal, then decide what to do next.

It is not a status report to a manager, a problem-solving workshop or a backlog refinement session. Those conversations still matter, but they happen elsewhere.

What the Scrum Guide 2020 says about the Daily Scrum

The 2020 Scrum Guide is still the current official version (checked 25 October 2026). Its Daily Scrum section is short; the table sums up each rule and what it means in practice.

The Scrum Guide saysWhat it means for your team
The purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessaryStart from the Sprint Goal, not from individual activity
It is a 15-minute event for the Developers of the Scrum Team15 minutes at most, whatever the Sprint length; the Developers own it
It is held at the same time and place every working day of the Sprint, to reduce complexityNobody has to ask when or where it is
If the Product Owner or Scrum Master are actively working on Sprint Backlog items, they participate as DevelopersOtherwise they are not required to take part
The Developers can select whatever structure and techniques they wantThe three questions are optional; choose what works
It should focus on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produce an actionable plan for the next dayIf nobody can say what changes today, the event has not done its job
It improves communication, identifies impediments, promotes quick decision-making and removes the need for other meetingsA good Daily Scrum should replace other status meetings, not add to them

What changed from the 2017 Scrum Guide

Topic2017 Scrum Guide2020 Scrum Guide
Who it is forThe Development TeamThe Developers (there is no separate Development Team)
StructureThree example questions: what I did yesterday, what I will do today, any impedimentsNo questions; any structure that focuses on the Sprint Goal
Scrum MasterEnsures the meeting happens, teaches the 15-minute time-box and keeps others from disrupting itNo specific Daily Scrum duties; the general duty to make sure all events take place, are positive and productive, and stay within the time-box

FocusInspect progress toward the Sprint GoalThe same, plus self-management and an actionable plan for the next day
Tip

If your team still uses the three questions, it is not breaking Scrum. They were removed because they became a ritual for many teams, not because they are wrong.

Purpose and benefits of the Daily Scrum

The Daily Scrum exists because plans made at Sprint Planning go out of date quickly. Work turns out bigger or smaller than expected, someone is ill, a test environment fails. A short daily inspection keeps the gap between the plan and reality small, so the team can adapt while there is still time.

  • Inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal. Is the team on course to meet it, and if not, what needs to change?
  • Adapt the Sprint Backlog. Re-order, add or remove tasks, or swap who pairs with whom. The Sprint Goal stays the same; the plan to reach it can change.
  • Surface impediments early, while they are still small and cheap to solve.
  • Make quick decisions without waiting for a weekly meeting.
  • Build self-management. The Developers plan their own day together, rather than being told what to do.
  • Reduce other meetings. If the Daily Scrum works, separate status updates are rarely needed.

Because it relies on an up-to-date Sprint Backlog, the Daily Scrum also supports transparency, one of the three pillars of empiricism in Scrum.

Who attends the Daily Scrum?

The Daily Scrum is for the Developers. Everyone else is optional, and their role depends on whether they are doing Sprint work.

WhoRole at the Daily Scrum
DevelopersRequired. They run the event, inspect progress and agree the plan for the next day.
Scrum MasterTakes part as a Developer only if working on Sprint Backlog items. Otherwise not required, though many attend with a new team to coach and to make sure the event happens and stays within 15 minutes.
Product OwnerTakes part as a Developer only if working on Sprint Backlog items. Often useful nearby to answer questions, but should not turn it into a progress report.
Managers and stakeholdersNot participants. If the team agrees they can observe, they listen and keep questions for afterwards.

Who facilitates? The Developers. The Scrum Master often facilitates for a new team and then steps back, because the aim is for the Developers to manage their own Daily Scrum. Many teams rotate facilitation daily or weekly. The 2017 Scrum Guide said the Development Team was responsible for conducting the Daily Scrum; the 2020 Guide simply makes it the Developers' event.

A useful test is who people look at while they speak. If they look at the Scrum Master or the Product Owner, the event has drifted into reporting. If they look at the board and each other, it is working. For how the Scrum Master can step back well, see our guide to servant leadership in Scrum.

Time-box, time and place

The Daily Scrum is time-boxed to 15 minutes, whether the Sprint is one week or one month. Fifteen minutes is a maximum, not a target: a small team with a clear board may finish in eight.

The Scrum Guide asks for the same time and place every working day, but leaves the choice to the Developers. Things to weigh:

ChoiceWorks well whenWatch out for
Early in the working dayMost people start at similar times; the plan shapes the whole dayLate starters or people with school runs missing it
Late morningHours vary; people have already looked at overnight resultsHalf the day has gone before the plan is adapted
After lunch or end of dayTeams across time zones need an overlapPlans are made for tomorrow; urgent issues wait
Beside a physical boardCo-located teamsRemote colleagues left out in hybrid teams
In a video call with a shared boardRemote or hybrid teamsCameras, audio and screen sharing slowing the start

Start on time, even if someone is missing, and finish on time. Some teams use the "sixteenth minute", a phrase used by Mike Cohn and others: once the Daily Scrum ends, the people needed for a specific problem stay behind to solve it while everyone else gets back to work.

How to run a Daily Scrum, step by step

The Scrum Guide does not prescribe an agenda. The steps below are our recommended starting point; inspect and adapt them at your Retrospective.

Before the Daily Scrum

  1. Update the board. Each Developer moves their items and tasks so the Scrum board shows the real state of work.
  2. Check the Sprint Goal. Make sure it is visible at the top of the board or the start of the call.
  3. Note anything blocked, so it can be raised quickly rather than discovered in the meeting.

A 15-minute agenda

MinutesWhat happensQuestion to answer
0 to 1Restate the Sprint Goal and the day of the SprintWhere are we in the Sprint?
1 to 3Quick confidence check, for example a show of fingers from 1 to 5Do we believe we will meet the Sprint Goal?
3 to 11Walk the board from the column nearest Done back to the startWhat does each item need to move forward today?
11 to 13Impediments and risksWhat is blocking us, and who will act on it?
13 to 15Agree the plan for today and any follow-up conversationsWhat will we do differently from yesterday's plan?

After the Daily Scrum

  1. Hold follow-up conversations with only the people needed: the sixteenth minute.
  2. Update the Sprint Backlog with any re-planning: new tasks, removed tasks or a changed order.
  3. Act on impediments. The Scrum Master helps remove those the Developers cannot remove themselves.
  4. Tell the Product Owner early if the Sprint Goal is at risk, so scope can be negotiated in good time.

Daily Scrum formats compared

Because the Developers choose their own structure, teams use many formats. These are the most common, with our view of when each works best.

FormatHow it worksBest forWatch out for
Three questionsEach person says what they did yesterday, what they will do today and any impedimentsNew teams learning the eventTurns into individual status reports; the Sprint Goal is easily forgotten
Walk the boardGo through items from right to left (closest to Done first), asking what each needs to finishMost teams; focuses on finishing work rather than starting itQuiet members not speaking if nobody owns their item
Sprint Goal firstStart with "Are we on track for the Sprint Goal?" and discuss only what affects itMature teams with a clear goalNeeds a real Sprint Goal, not a list of items
Blockers firstDiscuss blocked and at-risk items before anything elseTeams with many dependenciesCan skip items that are quietly drifting
Flow-basedReview work item age and items over the team's usual cycle time, as in the Kanban Guide for Scrum TeamsTeams using Kanban practices within ScrumNeeds a board that tracks when work started
Traffic lightsEach item is marked green, amber or red, and red items are discussed firstLarger teams or many parallel itemsColours can hide detail; agree what each means

Questions that keep the focus on the Sprint Goal

  • What did we learn yesterday that changes our plan?
  • Which item is closest to Done, and what does it need to finish today?
  • Is anything blocked or waiting? Who can help?
  • Is any item older than we expected? What is holding it up?
  • Are we still confident we will meet the Sprint Goal? If not, what do we change?
  • Who needs to pair or swarm on something today?

Swarming, where several Developers work together on one item, is often the decision that comes out of a good Daily Scrum. For more on item age, see our guide to the WIP aging chart.

Worked example: day 6 of a two-week Sprint

A team of five Developers is building an online returns service for a retailer. The Sprint Goal is: "A customer can request a return online and print a prepaid label." It is day 6 of 10. The board at 9:30 looks like this:

ItemStatusAge (days in progress)Notes
Return request formIn testing4Two minor defects found yesterday
Generate prepaid label (courier API)In progress4Courier test account rejected requests yesterday afternoon
Return confirmation emailIn progress1Template drafted
Returns list for customer service staffTo do0Not needed for the Sprint Goal
Accessibility fixes on return formTo do0Needed to meet the Definition of Done

Walking the board. Starting nearest Done, Priya says the form defects are small and she can fix them by midday. Tom explains that the courier API problem is a credentials issue he cannot solve alone. Sam, who planned to start the customer service list, offers to pair with Tom instead.

Confidence check. Two people show three fingers, three show four: the team is fairly confident, but the label is the risk. The label is essential to the Sprint Goal; the customer service list is not.

Adapted plan for today.

  • Priya fixes the two form defects, then starts the accessibility fixes, which are needed for the Definition of Done.
  • Tom and Sam pair on the courier API. The Scrum Master contacts the courier's support team about the test credentials straight after the Daily Scrum.
  • Ana finishes the confirmation email and reviews Priya's fixes.
  • Leo tests the form again after lunch.
  • The customer service list stays in To do. If the label work is still blocked tomorrow, the Developers will ask the Product Owner whether to drop it from the Sprint.

Follow-up. Tom, Sam and the Scrum Master stay for five minutes to agree what to ask the courier. The whole event took 12 minutes. Nobody gave a list of yesterday's tasks, but everyone left knowing what to do and why.

Handling impediments raised in the Daily Scrum

An impediment is anything that slows the team down or stops progress that the Developers cannot easily resolve themselves. The Scrum Guide makes the Scrum Master accountable for causing the removal of impediments to the Scrum Team's progress, but the Developers solve many themselves.

  1. Raise it briefly. Describe the problem and its effect on the Sprint Goal in one or two sentences.
  2. Decide who acts. A Developer, a pair, the Scrum Master or the Product Owner takes ownership.
  3. Solve it after the event, with only the people needed.
  4. Make it visible. Flag the item on the board, or keep a short impediment list beside it.
  5. Check it the next day. If it is still open, decide whether to escalate or change the plan.
  6. Look for patterns at the Retrospective. Repeated impediments usually point to a process or organisational problem.
Type of impedimentExampleWho usually acts
TechnicalA failing build or test environmentDevelopers, possibly with platform support
KnowledgeNobody understands an old moduleDevelopers pair or ask an expert
RequirementsUnclear acceptance criteriaProduct Owner
External dependencyWaiting for another team or supplierScrum Master and Developers together
OrganisationalDevelopers pulled onto other work mid-SprintScrum Master with management

Daily Scrum vs status meeting vs stand-up

People often use "Daily Scrum" and "daily stand-up" as if they were the same. In practice the stand-up is a general practice used by many Agile and non-Agile teams, while the Daily Scrum is the specific Scrum event with fixed rules.

Daily ScrumDaily stand-up (general)Status meeting
PurposeInspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the planDaily coordination; purpose set by the teamReport progress to a manager
OwnerDevelopersVariesManager or project manager
Time-box15 minutesUsually about 15 minutesOften 30 to 60 minutes
FocusThe Sprint Goal and the next dayToday's workWhat each person has done
StandingNot requiredTraditionally yes, to keep it shortNo
OutputAn actionable plan for the next dayShared awarenessA status update

Standing up is a habit, not a rule: Scrum Alliance notes the stand-up probably came from Extreme Programming, where standing kept meetings short. Sitting is fine if the time-box holds and it is more inclusive for your team.

Remote, hybrid and asynchronous Daily Scrums

Remote and hybrid

  • Share the board on screen and have one person move items as the team talks.
  • Join a minute early to sort out audio and screen sharing, so the 15 minutes is used for the plan.
  • In hybrid teams, have everyone join from their own laptop so remote colleagues are not left listening to a room.
  • Agree on cameras as a team. Cameras help some conversations but are not a Scrum rule, and some people have good reasons to keep them off.
  • Use the chat for links and follow-ups, not for side conversations during the event.

Across time zones

Choose the time with the best overlap and rotate an inconvenient slot if no time suits everyone. If the overlap is very small, some teams hold the Daily Scrum at the start of one location's day and use a written handover for the rest.

Asynchronous check-ins

Some distributed teams replace the live event with written updates in a chat tool. The Scrum Guide describes a 15-minute event, and a thread of updates rarely produces the joint decision-making that adapts the plan. If you use written updates, make them goal-focused ("what changes our plan?") and still meet live on the days when the Sprint Goal is at risk.

Is your Daily Scrum working? A quick health check

Ask the Developers to answer these questions honestly at your next Retrospective. Three or more "no" answers suggest the format needs to change. This checklist is our own suggestion, not part of Scrum.

  • Do we finish within 15 minutes on almost every day?
  • Does everyone know the Sprint Goal without looking it up?
  • Did yesterday's Daily Scrum change what someone did?
  • Are impediments raised early and resolved within a day or two?
  • Do we talk to each other rather than to the Scrum Master or Product Owner?
  • Would the team keep the Daily Scrum if it were optional?

If the answers are mostly "no", try a different format for two Sprints and compare. Changing from three questions to walking the board is often the quickest improvement.

Templates and tools for the Daily Scrum

The most important tool is a board that shows the real state of the Sprint. A physical wall or our free Kanban board template works for a single team. Frequently asked questions

Is the Daily Scrum the same as a stand-up?

Many teams call it a stand-up. The Daily Scrum is Scrum's specific event: 15 minutes, for the Developers, focused on progress toward the Sprint Goal.

How long is the Daily Scrum?

15 minutes at most, regardless of Sprint length. Shorter is fine if the team has agreed its plan for the day.

Who attends the Daily Scrum?

The Developers. The Product Owner and Scrum Master take part as Developers only if they are actively working on Sprint Backlog items.

Does the Scrum Master have to run the Daily Scrum?

No. It is the Developers' event. The Scrum Master makes sure Scrum events take place and stay within their time-box, and often facilitates for new teams before stepping back.

Should the Product Owner attend the Daily Scrum?

Only as a Developer if working on Sprint Backlog items. Many Product Owners stay available afterwards to answer questions without turning the event into a report to them.

Are the three questions still part of the Daily Scrum?

No. The 2020 Scrum Guide removed them. Developers can use any structure that focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produces a plan for the next day.

What time should the Daily Scrum be held?

At the same time and place every working day, chosen by the Developers. Early in the working day is common, but any consistent time works.

Can the Daily Scrum be skipped?

Not in Scrum. If it feels pointless, use the Retrospective to change its format or make it shorter rather than dropping it.

What if a Developer cannot attend the Daily Scrum?

The event goes ahead at the usual time. The absent person can share a short note in advance and catch up with a colleague afterwards.

Can the Daily Scrum be held asynchronously?

Some distributed teams use written updates, but the Scrum Guide describes a 15-minute event. Asynchronous check-ins risk losing the real-time conversation that adapts the plan.

What is walking the board in a Daily Scrum?

A format in which the team reviews items from right to left on the Scrum board, starting with those closest to Done, and asks what each needs to finish.

What is the output of the Daily Scrum?

An actionable plan for the next day of work, reflected in an updated Sprint Backlog, plus any impediments with an owner.

Is the Daily Scrum the only time Developers can adjust the plan?

No. The Scrum Guide notes that Developers often meet throughout the day for more detailed discussions about adapting or re-planning the rest of the Sprint's work.

Do we have to stand up?

No. Standing is a habit that helps keep the meeting short, not a Scrum rule. Sitting or joining remotely is fine if you keep to 15 minutes.
