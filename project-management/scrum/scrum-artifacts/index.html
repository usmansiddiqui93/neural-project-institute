Scrum Artifacts: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog and Increment

Scrum's three artifacts represent work or value. Each is designed to maximise transparency, and each has a commitment that gives focus and a way to measure progress.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrum has three artifacts, each with a commitment.
  • Product Goal, Sprint Goal and Definition of Done give focus.
  • Transparency is the purpose of every artifact.

The three artifacts and their commitments

ArtifactCommitmentOwned byWhat it is
Product BacklogProduct GoalProduct OwnerAn emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product
Sprint BacklogSprint GoalDevelopersThe Sprint Goal, selected items and the plan to deliver them
IncrementDefinition of DoneScrum TeamA concrete, usable stepping stone towards the Product Goal

Product Backlog

  • The single source of work for the Scrum Team.
  • Items at the top are clearer and more detailed than those lower down.
  • Refinement breaks items down and adds detail, description, order and size.
  • The Product Goal describes a future state of the product and is the long-term objective the team plans against.

Keeping artifacts transparent

  • Make all backlogs visible to everyone who needs them.
  • Keep the Product Backlog ordered, not just a pile of ideas.
  • Update the Sprint Backlog daily.
  • Never call work done unless it meets the Definition of Done.

Other useful (non-official) artifacts

Many teams also use burndown and burnup charts, a product roadmap, a release plan and a team working agreement. These are helpful but are not Scrum artifacts. See Scrum metrics.

Frequently asked questions

How many artifacts does Scrum have?

Three: the Product Backlog, the Sprint Backlog and the Increment.

Is the Definition of Done an artifact?

It is the commitment attached to the Increment, not a separate artifact.
