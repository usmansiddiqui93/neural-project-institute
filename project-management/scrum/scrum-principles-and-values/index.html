Scrum Principles and Values: Pillars of Empiricism Explained

Scrum rests on two foundations: empiricism, expressed as three pillars, and five values that guide how people behave. Without them, Scrum's events and artifacts become empty rituals.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrum's pillars are transparency, inspection and adaptation.
  • Its values are commitment, focus, openness, respect and courage.
  • The values make the pillars work in practice.

The three pillars

PillarMeaningIn practice
TransparencyThe process and work are visible to those doing and receiving itVisible backlogs and boards; honest Definition of Done
InspectionArtifacts and progress are inspected frequentlyDaily Scrum, Sprint Review, Retrospective
AdaptationWhen something deviates, adjust as soon as possibleRe-plan the Sprint, re-order the backlog, change practices

See empiricism in Scrum.

The five values

  • Commitment: the team commits to achieving its goals and supporting each other.
  • Focus: on the work of the Sprint and progress towards the goals.
  • Openness: about the work and the challenges.
  • Respect: for each other as capable, independent people.
  • Courage: to do the right thing and tackle tough problems.

Values in everyday behaviour

ValueLooks likeDoesn't look like
OpennessRaising a risk at the Daily ScrumHiding a problem until the Review
CourageTelling stakeholders a feature isn't readyReleasing work that doesn't meet the Definition of Done
FocusFinishing Sprint work before starting new tasksConstant context switching
RespectLetting Developers decide how to do the workAssigning tasks over their heads
CommitmentWorking together towards the Sprint GoalCommitting to unrealistic scope to please others

Other principles often taught with Scrum

Earlier Scrum training materials, and frameworks such as SBOK, list further principles like self-organisation, collaboration, value-based prioritisation, time-boxing and iterative development. These are consistent with the Scrum Guide but not part of its official text.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three pillars of Scrum?

Transparency, inspection and adaptation.

Why does Scrum have values?

Because empiricism only works when people are open, courageous and respectful enough to share honest information and act on it.
