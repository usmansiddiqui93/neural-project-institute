Scrum Principles and Values: Pillars of Empiricism Explained

Scrum rests on two foundations: empiricism, expressed as three pillars, and five values that guide how people behave. Without them, Scrum's events and artifacts become empty rituals.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 5 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrum's pillars are transparency, inspection and adaptation.
  • Its values are commitment, focus, openness, respect and courage.
  • The values make the pillars work in practice.

The three pillars

PillarMeaningIn practice
TransparencyThe process and work are visible to those doing and receiving itVisible backlogs and boards; honest Definition of Done
InspectionArtifacts and progress are inspected frequentlyDaily Scrum, Sprint Review, Retrospective
AdaptationWhen something deviates, adjust as soon as possibleRe-plan the Sprint, re-order the backlog, change practices

See empiricism in Scrum.

The five values

  • Commitment: the team commits to achieving its goals and supporting each other.
  • Focus: on the work of the Sprint and progress towards the goals.
  • Openness: about the work and the challenges.
  • Respect: for each other as capable, independent people.
  • Courage: to do the right thing and tackle tough problems.

Values in everyday behaviour

ValueLooks likeDoesn't look like
OpennessRaising a risk at the Daily ScrumHiding a problem until the Review
CourageTelling stakeholders a feature isn't readyReleasing work that doesn't meet the Definition of Done
FocusFinishing Sprint work before starting new tasksConstant context switching
RespectLetting Developers decide how to do the workAssigning tasks over their heads
CommitmentWorking together towards the Sprint GoalCommitting to unrealistic scope to please others

Other principles often taught with Scrum

Earlier Scrum training materials, and frameworks such as SBOK, list further principles like self-organisation, collaboration, value-based prioritisation, time-boxing and iterative development. These are consistent with the Scrum Guide but not part of its official text.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three core Scrum principles?

Transparency, inspection and adaptation, often called the three pillars of empiricism.

Why do the Scrum values matter?

Because empiricism only works when people are open, courageous and respectful enough to share honest information and act on it.

What are the five Scrum values?

Commitment, focus, openness, respect and courage. The Scrum Team commits to its goals, focuses on the Sprint's work, is open about the work and its challenges, respects each other, and has the courage to do the right thing.

What does transparency mean as a Scrum principle?

Transparency means the process and the work are visible to those performing it and those receiving it. Without it, inspection is based on misleading information and decisions carry more risk.

How does inspection work as a Scrum principle?

Inspection means frequently and diligently examining the Scrum artifacts and progress towards agreed goals to detect problems early. Scrum's events provide the regular points for this.

What is adaptation as a Scrum principle?

Adaptation means adjusting the process or the product as soon as possible when inspection shows something has drifted outside acceptable limits. The Scrum Guide expects adjustments to be made quickly to minimise further deviation.

When were the Scrum values added to the Scrum Guide?

The five values were added to the Scrum Guide in its 2016 update and remain in the 2020 version.

Is self-organisation a Scrum principle?

The 2020 Scrum Guide describes Scrum Teams as self-managing, meaning they decide who does what, when and how. Self-organisation appears in some training frameworks as a principle, but the official text uses the term self-managing.

How can a team live the Scrum values?

Discuss what each value looks like in practice, agree working agreements that reflect them, and revisit them in the Sprint Retrospective. Behaviours such as raising risks early and refusing to cut quality show the values in action.

What happens if a team ignores the Scrum values?

Events and artifacts tend to become rituals, information gets hidden and trust drops, so empiricism stops working. Teams often keep the meetings but lose the benefits.

How do Scrum values relate to the Agile Manifesto?

Both put people, collaboration and responding to change at the centre. The Scrum values describe the behaviours that make Agile principles workable within a Scrum Team. See Agile Manifesto.
