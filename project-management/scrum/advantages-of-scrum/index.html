Advantages of Scrum: Benefits and Limitations

Scrum's popularity rests on practical benefits: faster feedback, clearer priorities and more engaged teams. It also has limits, and knowing them helps you decide when to use it.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 6 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrum delivers value early and adapts to change.
  • It needs an empowered Product Owner and a stable team.
  • It suits complex product work more than routine operations.

Advantages

  • Early, regular value: a usable Increment every Sprint.
  • Fast feedback: stakeholders see real progress at every Sprint Review.
  • Clear priorities: one ordered Product Backlog and one Product Owner.
  • Adaptability: priorities can change between Sprints without heavy change control.
  • Transparency: visible backlogs, boards and goals.
  • Continuous improvement: Retrospectives every Sprint.
  • Team ownership: self-managing teams decide how to do the work.
  • Predictability over time: stable teams develop a reliable pace.

Limitations

  • Needs an engaged Product Owner with authority.
  • Works best with dedicated, stable, cross-functional teams.
  • Can struggle with frequent urgent interruptions; consider Kanban.
  • Scaling across many teams requires extra coordination; see Scrum of Scrums.
  • Easy to adopt the rituals without the values, which produces poor results.

Frequently asked questions

Which advantages of Scrum make teams choose it?

To get usable results sooner, respond to change, and give teams more ownership of their work.

What is the biggest risk to the benefits of Scrum?

Adopting events and roles without the underlying values and empiricism, sometimes called "zombie Scrum".

What are the main benefits of Scrum?

Usable results every Sprint, fast stakeholder feedback, clear priorities through one ordered Product Backlog, and built-in continuous improvement. Teams also gain ownership because they decide how to do the work.

What are the disadvantages of Scrum?

Scrum needs an engaged Product Owner, a stable cross-functional team and regular stakeholder involvement. It can struggle with frequent urgent interruptions and needs extra coordination when many teams work on one product.

Is better quality one of the benefits of Scrum?

It can, because each Increment must meet the Definition of Done and quality must not decrease during a Sprint. The benefit depends on the team keeping a strong Definition of Done rather than cutting corners.

Do small teams get the benefits of Scrum?

Yes. Scrum is designed for small teams, typically ten or fewer people. Very small teams sometimes find the events heavy and shorten them in proportion to Sprint length.

Is lower project risk one of the advantages of Scrum?

Yes. Scrum reduces the risk of building the wrong thing, because stakeholders inspect working results every Sprint. Short Sprints also limit how much time and money is at risk in any one cycle.

Why do some teams never see the benefits of Scrum?

Common causes include a Product Owner without authority, management that undermines self-management, skipped Retrospectives, and Increments that never really meet the Definition of Done. These remove the feedback that makes Scrum work.

Do the advantages of Scrum apply to fixed-price projects?

Only partly. Scrum can be used, but fixed scope, time and cost leave little room for adaptation. Contracts that fix time and budget but allow scope to be re-prioritised fit Scrum better.

When do the disadvantages of Scrum outweigh its benefits?

When the work is routine and predictable, when requests arrive continuously with constantly changing urgency, or when no one can act as an empowered Product Owner. Kanban or a predictive approach may suit better.

Do the advantages of Scrum make it better than Waterfall?

Not in every case. Scrum suits complex work with changing requirements; Waterfall suits stable, well-defined work. See Scrum vs Waterfall.
