Scrum Glossary: Key Scrum Terms Defined

A quick reference to the terms you'll meet when working with Scrum. Official Scrum terms follow the 2020 Scrum Guide; common practices used alongside Scrum are marked as such.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Scrum terms cover accountabilities, events, artifacts and commitments.
  • Some common terms, such as story points and velocity, are practices rather than official Scrum.
  • The Scrum Guide is the authoritative source.

Accountabilities

TermDefinition
Scrum TeamA small, cross-functional, self-managing team of one Product Owner, one Scrum Master and Developers
Product OwnerAccountable for maximising the value of the product; manages the Product Backlog
Scrum MasterAccountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness
DevelopersPeople in the Scrum Team who create any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint
StakeholderAnyone with an interest in the product outside the Scrum Team

Events

TermDefinition
SprintA fixed-length event of one month or less that contains all other events
Sprint PlanningEvent that starts the Sprint by setting the Sprint Goal and plan
Daily Scrum15-minute event for Developers to inspect progress and adapt the plan
Sprint ReviewEvent to inspect the outcome of the Sprint and adapt the Product Backlog
Sprint RetrospectiveEvent to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness
Time-boxThe maximum duration of an event

Artifacts and commitments

TermDefinition
Product BacklogEmergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product
Product GoalThe long-term objective for the Scrum Team; commitment for the Product Backlog
Sprint BacklogSprint Goal, selected Product Backlog items and the plan to deliver them
Sprint GoalThe single objective for the Sprint
IncrementA usable stepping stone towards the Product Goal
Definition of DoneFormal description of the quality the Increment must meet

Common practices (not in the Scrum Guide)

TermDefinition
User storyA short description of a need from the user's point of view
Story pointsRelative units for estimating backlog items
VelocityStory points completed per Sprint
Burndown chartRemaining work plotted against time
Backlog refinementOngoing activity to clarify, split and estimate backlog items
Definition of ReadyOptional checklist for items entering a Sprint
Scrum of ScrumsCoordination among several Scrum Teams
Technical debtFuture cost of shortcuts taken in the product

Principles and values

TermDefinition
EmpiricismMaking decisions based on what is observed
Transparency, inspection, adaptationThe three pillars of empiricism in Scrum
Scrum valuesCommitment, focus, openness, respect and courage
Self-managingThe team decides who does what, when and how

Frequently asked questions

Where can I read the official Scrum definitions?

In the Scrum Guide by Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, available free at scrumguides.org.

Is a Scrum Master a project manager?

No. See how to become a Scrum Master for how the role differs.

What is a Product Backlog item?

A single entry in the Product Backlog, such as a feature, fix or improvement. User stories are one popular way to write them.

What is a time-box in Scrum?

The maximum length of an event. For a one-month Sprint, Sprint Planning has eight hours, the Sprint Review four and the Sprint Retrospective three; the Daily Scrum is always 15 minutes.

What does self-managing mean in Scrum?

The Scrum Team decides internally who does what, when and how. The 2020 Scrum Guide uses self-managing in place of the earlier self-organising.

What is cross-functional in Scrum?

The team has all the skills needed to create value each Sprint, without depending on people outside the team.

What is an impediment in Scrum?

Anything that blocks the team's progress. The Scrum Master helps remove impediments the team cannot resolve itself.

What is a burnup chart?

A chart that shows completed work rising towards a total scope line. Unlike a burndown, it shows scope changes clearly.

What is a spike in Scrum?

A time-boxed piece of research or experimentation to reduce uncertainty before estimating or building an item. It is a common practice, not part of the Scrum Guide.

What is an epic in Scrum?

A large body of work that is split into smaller backlog items. Epics are not defined in the Scrum Guide. See Agile epics.

What is the difference between a Sprint Goal and a Product Goal?

The Sprint Goal is the single objective for one Sprint. The Product Goal is the longer-term objective the team works towards across many Sprints.
