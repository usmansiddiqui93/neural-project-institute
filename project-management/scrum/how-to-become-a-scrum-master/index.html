How to Become a Scrum Master: Steps, Skills, Certification

To become a Scrum Master, learn Scrum from the 2020 Scrum Guide, practise facilitation and coaching on a real team, earn an entry-level certification such as PSM I or CSM if employers in your market ask for one, and collect concrete evidence of improvements you helped a team make. No qualification is legally or formally required. What gets people hired is evidence that they can help a team work better without directing it.

Key takeaways

  • No qualification is required to become a Scrum Master; hiring decisions usually turn on evidence that you have helped a team improve, with a certificate as a screening aid.
  • Employers look for evidence of facilitation, coaching, impediment handling and Scrum knowledge. Plan how you will demonstrate each one, not just learn it.
  • PSM I (Scrum.org): US$200 per attempt, 80 questions in 60 minutes, 85% to pass, no expiry. CSM (Scrum Alliance): 16-hour course, then 50 questions in 60 minutes with 37 correct to pass, renewed every two years. Checked 25 November 2026.
  • The quickest route in is usually internal: take on the accountability for your current team, or for a team in your organisation that lacks a Scrum Master.
  • Use a self-assessment against the core skills to choose what to practise next, then turn each practice into a specific, measurable example for your CV.
  • In the first 90 days in the role, observe before changing anything, and judge yourself by how the team improves, not by how busy you are.

What does it take to become a Scrum Master?

Becoming a Scrum Master means preparing to hold the Scrum Master accountability defined in the 2020 Scrum Guide: establishing Scrum and making the Scrum Team effective by coaching, facilitating and causing impediments to be removed. There is no mandatory qualification; employers usually look for Scrum knowledge, practical facilitation experience and, often, a PSM I or CSM certification.

Three things therefore need building, in roughly this order of importance to most hiring managers:

  • Evidence of practice. Examples of events you facilitated, impediments you helped remove and changes a team made with your help.
  • Accurate Scrum knowledge. The ability to explain why each element of Scrum exists, using the vocabulary of the 2020 Scrum Guide.
  • A recognised credential. Useful for getting past CV screening, but rarely the deciding factor at interview.

This guide is a career route rather than a description of the job. Read what a Scrum Master does for the accountability itself: the three areas of service, a typical day and what the role is not.

What the Scrum Guide expects, and what that means for your preparation

The 2020 Scrum Guide says the Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Guide and for the Scrum Team's effectiveness, and describes Scrum Masters as true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organisation. It lists ways the Scrum Master serves the team, the Product Owner and the organisation, including coaching team members in self-management and cross-functionality, causing the removal of impediments, helping establish empirical product planning and leading the organisation's Scrum adoption.

Each of those duties implies a skill you can practise before you have the job title:

Scrum Guide dutySkill it requiresHow you can practise it now
Coaching team members in self-managementAsking questions instead of giving answersRun one-to-one conversations where you only ask questions for the first ten minutes
Ensuring Scrum events are positive, productive and within the timeboxFacilitation and timekeepingOffer to facilitate a Retrospective or planning session for any team
Causing the removal of impedimentsProblem framing and escalation with evidenceKeep a visible list of blockers on your current work and track how long each takes to resolve
Helping find techniques for Product Backlog managementKnowledge of ordering and splitting techniquesHelp a product owner or manager split one large request into smaller, valuable pieces
Helping establish empirical product planningForecasting from delivery dataTrack how long items actually take on your team and compare it with estimates
Leading, training and coaching the organisation in its Scrum adoptionTeaching and influence without authorityGive a 20-minute explanation of Scrum to colleagues and ask for feedback

Notice what is not in the Guide: assigning tasks, writing status reports, owning the schedule or running the Daily Scrum for the Developers. If your current job is project coordination or management, much of your preparation is unlearning those habits. How to become a Scrum Master step by step

  1. Learn Scrum from the primary source. Read the 2020 Scrum Guide several times until you can explain each accountability, event and artifact and the commitment attached to each artifact. Use our Scrum Guide hub and Scrum glossary to check terms; if a course or blog disagrees with the Guide, the Guide wins.
  2. Assess your starting point. Rate yourself against the core skills in the self-assessment below and pick the two weakest to work on first.
  3. Practise on real work. Facilitate a Retrospective, keep an impediment list, help with backlog refinement or run a small piece of work in Sprints. Aim for repeated practice with the same team, because coaching skill shows up over several Sprints, not in one meeting.
  4. Choose and earn a certification that employers in your market ask for. PSM I and CSM are the usual starting points; see the facts below.
  5. Turn practice into evidence. For each activity, write down the situation, what you did and what changed, with a number where possible.
  6. Find a mentor and a community. An experienced Scrum Master or Agile coach can review your approach to real situations; local meetups and online communities expose you to problems your own team has not had yet.
  7. Apply internally first, then externally. Ask to hold the accountability for your own team, cover for a Scrum Master on leave, or support a team that has none.
  8. Plan your first 90 days before you start, so you observe and build trust before changing anything.
Tip

Steps 3 and 5 are where most candidates fall short. Reading and certification can be finished in weeks; credible examples take months of repeated practice, so start them first.

Self-assessment: how ready are you?

Rate yourself on each skill using the 0 to 4 scale from our skills matrix template: 0 = no experience, 1 = basic awareness, 2 = can do it with support, 3 = can do it independently, 4 = can teach others. Ask a colleague who has seen you work to rate you too, and use the lower score where you disagree.

Worked example: a project coordinator assesses her readiness

The following example is illustrative. Daniela is a project coordinator who has worked alongside a Scrum Team for a year. She rates herself on eight skills.

SkillDaniela's rating (0–4)Evidence behind the rating
Scrum knowledge (2020 Guide)3Has read the Guide several times; scores well in free practice assessments
Facilitation2Has run two Retrospectives with help from the team's Scrum Master
Coaching and questioning1Tends to offer solutions rather than ask questions
Impediment handling3Chases cross-team dependencies and supplier delays as part of her job
Stakeholder collaboration3Organises steering meetings and knows the sponsors well
Conflict handling2Handles disagreements in meetings but avoids raising tension herself
Teaching Scrum to others1Has never explained Scrum to a group
Flow and delivery data2Updates the board but has not used cycle time or throughput

Calculation. Readiness score = sum of ratings ÷ maximum possible score × 100%. The maximum is 8 skills × 4 = 32. Daniela's sum is 3 + 2 + 1 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 17, so her score is 17 ÷ 32 × 100% = 53.1%, an average of 2.1 per skill.

Interpretation. The total matters less than the profile. Daniela's strengths (impediments and stakeholders) come from coordination work and transfer well. Her two ratings below 2, coaching and teaching, are central to the accountability, so they become her first development goals. A useful decision rule: apply for Scrum Master roles once no core skill is below 2 and you have at least one specific example for each skill rated 3 or above.

Plan. Daniela commits about five hours a week for 12 weeks (60 hours): co-facilitating every Retrospective, running a 20-minute Scrum session for the sponsors, and practising question-only coaching conversations with a peer. If she reaches 2 in coaching and teaching and 3 in facilitation, her sum becomes 20 of 32 (62.5%), with no skill below 2. She records the plan in a training plan template, which has columns for skill or goal, training activity, method, dates, cost, owner and status.

Limitations. Self-ratings are subjective, the eight skills carry equal weight although some matter more in a given organisation, and a score is not a hiring standard. Use the result to choose what to practise, not to predict whether you will be hired.

Routes into the role from different backgrounds

Most Scrum Masters move into the role from another job. Each background brings strengths and a typical blind spot.

Current roleStrengths that transferTypical gap to closeGood first move
Project managerStakeholder management, risk awareness, organising workLetting the Developers plan and decide; giving up task assignmentCoach a team through Sprint Planning without proposing the plan yourself; see Agile project manager for hybrid roles
Project coordinatorOrganisation, follow-up, knowledge of stakeholdersCoaching and facilitating groups rather than administeringCo-facilitate Retrospectives and take over the impediment list
Developer or testerUnderstanding of the work, credibility with DevelopersStepping back from solving technical problems personallyHold the accountability for your own team while still contributing as a Developer
Business analystRequirements, backlog items, facilitation of workshopsSeparating the Scrum Master accountability from Product Owner workSupport backlog refinement as a facilitator rather than an author
Team leader or line managerPeople skills, organisational knowledgeLeading without positional authority; the people you coach may report to youPractise with a team you do not manage
Teacher, trainer or HR professionalFacilitation, coaching, explaining ideasUnderstanding how product work flows from idea to releaseLearn the delivery flow of a team in your organisation and shadow its events

Whatever your background, close the gap you have, not a generic one: a former Developer needs coaching practice more than Scrum theory, while a former teacher gains most from shadowing a team's delivery flow for a few Sprints.

Scrum Master certification: which one and when

No certificate is required to hold the Scrum Master accountability, and the Scrum Guide does not mention certification. Employers, however, often list one in job adverts. The two most common entry-level options compare as follows. Figures were checked on the issuers' websites on 25 November 2026.

Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I)Certified ScrumMaster (CSM)
Awarded byScrum.orgScrum Alliance
Course requiredNo; a course is recommended, and Scrum.org's training classes include a free attemptYes: 16 hours of training, usually over two to three days
Assessment80 questions in 60 minutes; 85% to pass (68 correct)50 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes; 37 correct (74%) to pass
CostUS$200 per attempt, plus any taxesIncluded in the course fee set by the trainer; Scrum Alliance quotes US$250 to US$2,495 based on prices as of October 2023
AttemptsEach password is valid for one attempt and does not expireTwo test attempts included, within 90 days of completing the course
RenewalNone: the certification does not expireEvery two years: 20 Scrum Education Units (SEUs) and a US$100 fee

How to choose. If you learn well on your own and want the lowest cost, PSM I by self-study is the cheapest recognised route, but its 85% pass mark punishes gaps in your knowledge. If you value a live class or your employer pays for training, CSM gives you structured learning and the credential together. Check which certification appears more often in adverts for roles you want; preferences differ by country and sector. In an organisation that uses the Scaled Agile Framework, the SAFe Scrum Master certification may matter more.

When to certify. Take the certification once you have read the Guide thoroughly and practised a little, so the course or exam deepens understanding you already have. Certifying before any practice produces candidates who can recite the Guide but struggle to answer scenario questions at interview.

Later levels. After real experience, Scrum.org offers PSM II and PSM III, and Scrum Alliance offers the Advanced CSM (A-CSM) and Certified Scrum Professional – ScrumMaster (CSP-SM). The A-CSM requires a CSM, or a PSM I or PSM II from Scrum.org, plus 12 months of Scrum Master work experience validated within the past five years; the CSP-SM requires an A-CSM and 24 months within the past five years.

Getting Scrum Master experience before you have the title

General ways to gain Agile experience, such as running a personal project in Sprints or volunteering, are covered in our guide to learning Agile and getting certified. The activities below are chosen because each produces evidence of a specific Scrum Master skill.

ActivitySkill it demonstratesEvidence to record
Facilitate the Sprint Retrospective for three or more consecutive SprintsFacilitation; follow-throughFormats used, actions agreed, how many were completed
Run an impediment list for your teamImpediment handling; escalationNumber raised, average days to resolve, one example you escalated
Help prepare a Sprint Review as a working session with stakeholdersStakeholder collaborationWho attended, what feedback changed in the Product Backlog
Coach a new Product Owner on splitting itemsCoaching; backlog techniquesBefore and after size of items; effect on Sprint Planning
Introduce a work-in-progress limit as a team experimentFlow; empiricismItems in progress and cycle time before and after
Explain Scrum to managers or another departmentTeaching; influenceAudience, questions raised, any change that followed

Keep the sprint retrospective template records from sessions you facilitate. Its action items tab has columns for action, owner, due date, success measure and status, which gives you the follow-through evidence interviewers ask about.

Your CV and interview

Writing CV bullets that show the accountability

Describe outcomes the team achieved with your help, not meetings you attended. The examples below are illustrative.

Weak bulletStronger bullet
Ran daily stand-ups and Sprint ceremoniesFacilitated Retrospectives for a six-person team over eight Sprints; the team completed 11 of 14 agreed actions, including a change that removed a two-day wait for test environments
Removed blockers for the teamKept an impediment list for a team of seven; escalated a recurring approval delay to the head of security with data on blocked items, and the approval step was shortened
Experienced in Agile and ScrumPSM I (Scrum.org); co-facilitated Sprint Planning and coached a new Product Owner on splitting items so the team could finish them within a Sprint

Interview scenarios and what they test

Scrum Master interviews usually include scenarios. Interviewers listen for whether you diagnose before acting, respect the accountabilities in the Scrum Guide and involve the team in the solution.

ScenarioWhat a strong answer shows
The Daily Scrum has become a status report to youYou step back, coach the Developers to plan towards the Sprint Goal and do not run the event for them
The Product Owner is rarely availableYou find out why, help the Product Owner protect time and raise the mandate issue with whoever assigned the role
A manager asks you for each Developer's individual velocityYou explain why velocity is a team planning aid, not a performance measure, and offer information that answers the manager's real concern
Stakeholders have stopped attending the Sprint ReviewYou find out what the session gives them now, help the Product Owner redesign it as a working session on the Increment and gather data on attendance before and after
A senior Developer dominates every discussionYou use facilitation techniques that bring in quieter voices and give feedback privately

Prepare one real example for each core skill, using the situation, your action and the result. If an example comes from a personal project or another field, say so; honest small examples are more convincing than inflated ones.

Your first 90 days as a Scrum Master

A new Scrum Master who changes everything in the first week usually loses the team's trust. A staged plan avoids that.

PeriodFocusTypical activitiesSigns it is working
Days 1–30Observe and build relationshipsAttend every event without changing it; meet each Developer, the Product Owner and key stakeholders one to one; read the Definition of Done and the Product BacklogPeople tell you about problems unprompted
Days 31–60Make problems visibleStart an impediment list; share observations neutrally at a Retrospective; agree one or two experiments with the teamThe team chooses and owns its first improvement actions
Days 61–90Work beyond the teamHelp the Product Owner with Product Goal and backlog techniques; take organisational impediments to the people who can decide; review whether early experiments workedRetrospective actions are completed; at least one organisational impediment is resolved

Throughout, measure yourself by the team's progress: Sprint Goals met, actions completed, impediments resolved faster and a team that runs more of its events without you. Our guide to Scrum metrics and KPIs explains which measures help and why velocity should not be used as a target.

Career paths after the Scrum Master role

With experience, Scrum Masters commonly move into Agile coaching across several teams, Release Train Engineer roles in SAFe organisations, delivery management, the Product Owner accountability, or leadership roles in an Agile transformation. Some return to project or programme management with a stronger grasp of adaptive delivery. Advanced certifications such as PSM II, A-CSM or the PMI-ACP make most sense at this stage, when you can relate them to real experience; our project management certification guide compares the wider options.

Common mistakes when moving into the role

  • Collecting certificates instead of examples. A second entry-level certificate adds less than three months of facilitating a real team.
  • Bringing project manager habits unchanged. Assigning tasks or reporting individual progress undermines self-management.
  • Waiting for a vacancy before practising. Facilitation and coaching can be practised in almost any job; candidates who start only after applying have nothing to show at interview.
  • Learning Scrum from secondary sources only. Outdated material still uses terms such as "Development Team", which the 2020 Guide replaced with "Developers".
  • Applying only for external roles. Internal moves are often easier because colleagues have already seen how you work.
  • Overstating experience at interview. Scenario questions quickly reveal it; describe what you actually did.

Frequently asked questions

Can I become a Scrum Master with no experience?

It is possible but difficult, because most employers want examples of facilitation and coaching. Build experience first in your current job, for example by facilitating Retrospectives or keeping an impediment list, and pair it with PSM I or CSM.

Do I need a certification to be a Scrum Master?

No. The Scrum Guide does not require one and no law does. Many employers list PSM I or CSM in job adverts, so a certification helps you get interviews; practical examples usually decide the outcome.

Which is better for a new Scrum Master, PSM I or CSM?

Neither is better in every case. PSM I costs US$200 per attempt, needs no course and never expires, but requires 85% to pass. CSM requires a 16-hour course, includes the test in the course fee and renews every two years with 20 SEUs and US$100 (checked 25 November 2026). See Scrum certification and training.

How long does it take to become a Scrum Master?

An entry-level certification can take a two-day course or a few weeks of self-study. Building enough practical evidence to be credible usually takes several months of repeated practice with a team; the time depends on how much facilitation your current role allows.

Do I need a technical background to become a Scrum Master?

No. You need enough understanding of how your team's work reaches release to ask useful questions, and you can gain it by observing a team. Facilitation and coaching evidence matters more at interview.

Can a project manager become a Scrum Master?

Yes, and many do. Stakeholder and risk skills transfer well. The main adjustment is giving up task assignment and schedule ownership: in Scrum the Developers plan their own work and the Product Owner orders the Product Backlog.

Can a Developer also be the Scrum Master?

Yes. The Scrum Guide does not prevent one person from being a Developer and holding the Scrum Master accountability, and it is common in small teams. Protect time for the Scrum Master work, especially during busy Sprints.

What should I put on my CV as an aspiring Scrum Master?

Specific outcomes a team achieved with your help: events you facilitated, impediments resolved, actions completed and what changed as a result, with numbers where you have them. List your certification, but lead with evidence.

What comes after an entry-level Scrum Master certification?

Real experience first. After about a year as a Scrum Master you can consider PSM II, or the A-CSM, which requires a CSM or a PSM I or II plus 12 months of validated Scrum Master experience within the past five years.
