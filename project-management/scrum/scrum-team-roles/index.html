Scrum Team Roles: Product Owner, Scrum Master and Developers

A Scrum Team is a small, cohesive unit with one goal at a time. Within it, three accountabilities share the work: the Product Owner, the Scrum Master and the Developers. There are no sub-teams or hierarchies.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A Scrum Team has three accountabilities and no sub-teams.
  • Product Owner: value; Scrum Master: effectiveness; Developers: the Increment.
  • Keep teams small, cross-functional and self-managing.

The Scrum Team

  • Typically ten or fewer people, so the team stays nimble.
  • Cross-functional: it has all the skills needed to create value each Sprint.
  • Self-managing: it decides internally who does what, when and how.
  • Focused on one objective at a time: the Product Goal.

The three accountabilities

AccountabilityAccountable forKey activities
Product OwnerMaximising the value of the productProduct Goal; ordering and communicating the Product Backlog
Scrum MasterThe Scrum Team's effectivenessCoaching, removing impediments, facilitating
DevelopersCreating a usable Increment each SprintSprint Backlog, quality, Definition of Done

Why "accountabilities", not roles

The 2020 Scrum Guide speaks of accountabilities rather than roles, to emphasise that these are responsibilities within one team, not job titles or hierarchy levels. "Developers" includes anyone doing the work, such as designers, analysts, testers and writers.

Common mistakes

  • One person acting as both Product Owner and Scrum Master.
  • A Product Owner without authority to make decisions.
  • A Scrum Master acting as a project manager who assigns tasks.
  • Splitting Developers into separate sub-teams such as "dev" and "test".
  • Teams so large that coordination dominates the work.

Frequently asked questions

How many people are in a Scrum Team?

Typically ten or fewer, including the Product Owner and Scrum Master.

Can the Product Owner be a committee?

No. The Product Owner is one person, though they may represent the needs of many stakeholders.

What are the three roles in Scrum?

Product Owner, Scrum Master and Developers. The 2020 Scrum Guide calls them accountabilities rather than roles.

What is the difference between a Scrum Team and a Development Team?

The 2020 Scrum Guide removed the separate Development Team. There is now one Scrum Team, made up of the Product Owner, the Scrum Master and the Developers.

Can one person be Scrum Master and Product Owner?

The Scrum Guide does not explicitly forbid it, but combining the two is widely discouraged because the accountabilities pull in different directions and the checks between them are lost.

Can a Scrum Master also be a Developer?

Yes, the Scrum Guide does not prevent it, though it can be hard to balance both. If they work on Sprint Backlog items, they take part in the Daily Scrum as Developers.

Who manages the Developers in Scrum?

No one inside the Scrum Team manages the Developers' daily work. They are self-managing and decide how to turn Product Backlog items into an Increment.

What does a Developer do in Scrum?

Developers create the plan for the Sprint, instil quality by adhering to the Definition of Done, adapt their plan each day towards the Sprint Goal and hold each other accountable as professionals.

What should a team do if it grows too large?

The Scrum Guide suggests reorganising into multiple cohesive Scrum Teams focused on the same product, sharing the same Product Goal, Product Backlog and Product Owner. See Scrum of Scrums.

Is a stakeholder part of the Scrum Team?

No. Stakeholders, such as customers, users and managers, are outside the Scrum Team but collaborate with it, especially at the Sprint Review.

Are testers part of the Scrum Team?

Yes. In Scrum, anyone doing the work to create the Increment is a Developer, including testers, designers, analysts and writers.
