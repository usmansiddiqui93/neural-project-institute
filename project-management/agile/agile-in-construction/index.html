Is Agile Project Management Applicable to Construction?

Construction seems like the opposite of Agile: you can't release half a building, and changing the foundations late is enormously expensive. Yet many Agile and Lean ideas fit construction well, especially in design, planning and site coordination.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • The overall construction project usually remains predictive.
  • Agile ideas work well in design, look-ahead planning and site coordination.
  • The Last Planner System is the Lean construction equivalent of layered Agile planning.

What doesn't translate directly

  • The final product cannot be delivered in usable increments.
  • Late changes to structure are costly and may be unsafe.
  • Contracts, permits and regulations require detailed upfront plans.
  • Work depends on physical materials, weather and long supply chains.

For these reasons the overall project usually stays predictive, with a baseline schedule and budget. See Agile vs Waterfall.

What works well

  • Design phase: architects and engineers work in iterations with frequent client reviews, using models and visualisations.
  • Short-interval planning: detailed plans are made a few weeks ahead with the people who will do the work.
  • Daily coordination: short site huddles to clear blockers.
  • Visual management: boards showing tasks, constraints and progress.
  • Retrospectives at the end of each phase or major milestone.
  • Continuous improvement of site processes.

Lean construction and the Last Planner System

Lean construction applies Lean principles to building projects. Its best-known tool is the Last Planner System, developed in the 1990s by Glenn Ballard and Greg Howell. It works in layers that mirror Agile planning:

  1. Master schedule: major milestones.
  2. Phase planning: the trades plan each phase together, often by working backwards from milestones.
  3. Look-ahead planning: constraints such as materials, permits and information are identified and removed a few weeks in advance.
  4. Weekly work planning: the "last planners", foremen and crew leads, commit to work they know they can complete.
  5. Learning: the team tracks the percentage of planned work completed and analyses why work was missed.

A practical hybrid

Keep a predictive master plan and contract for governance, and use Agile and Lean practices for design, look-ahead planning and daily site coordination. This combines the certainty owners need with the adaptability crews need.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use Scrum in construction?

Pure Scrum rarely fits the build phase, but its ideas (short planning cycles, daily coordination, reviews and retrospectives) are widely used, especially in design and pre-construction.

What is the Last Planner System?

A Lean construction planning method in which the people closest to the work make weekly commitments, constraints are removed in advance, and completion rates are tracked to improve reliability.
