Is Agile Only for Software? Who Uses Agile Today

Agile was born in software development, but its core idea, delivering value in small steps and learning from feedback, works for almost any kind of knowledge work. Today, Agile is used by teams that never write a line of code.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 24 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Agile's principles apply to any knowledge work facing uncertainty.
  • Marketing, HR, finance, government and construction all use Agile.
  • Adapt the increment and the Definition of Done to your kind of work.

Why Agile travels well

The Agile Manifesto talks about "working software", but the principles behind it are general: focus on the customer, deliver in small increments, collaborate closely, and adjust based on evidence. Any team facing uncertainty and changing priorities can benefit. Replace "working software" with "working results" and most of the Agile Manifesto applies directly.

Who uses Agile

FunctionHow Agile is used
MarketingCampaigns run as short experiments; content planned in sprints; results reviewed weekly. See Agile marketing.
Product and designDiscovery work, prototypes and usability testing in short cycles
HR and people teamsRecruitment pipelines on Kanban boards; new policies piloted with one department first
FinanceRolling forecasts instead of a fixed annual budget; Agile portfolio funding
GovernmentDigital public services built in increments with real users
EducationCourse design in iterations, with student feedback between modules
Research and dataAnalyses shared as interim findings rather than one final report
Hardware and manufacturingPrototypes in short iterations; Lean and Kanban on the shop floor
ConstructionLean construction and short-interval planning. See Agile in construction.

What changes outside software

  • Increments look different. A usable increment might be a published article, a tested campaign, a piloted process or a prototype.
  • Definition of Done is specific to the work. Legal review, brand approval or safety checks replace code review and automated tests. See Definition of Done.
  • Kanban is often the easiest start, because many non-software teams handle a continuous flow of requests.
  • Some work cannot be split and still needs a predictive plan; hybrid approaches are common.

Getting started outside software

  1. Make your current work visible on a simple board.
  2. Limit how many things are in progress at once.
  3. Hold a short weekly planning meeting and a monthly retrospective.
  4. Find the smallest useful thing you can deliver and show it to the customer.
  5. Improve the process a little every cycle.

Frequently asked questions

Is Agile only for software, or can it work for non-technical teams?

Yes. Marketing, HR, finance and many other teams use Agile, usually starting with Kanban boards and short planning cycles.

Which Agile framework is best for non-software teams?

Kanban is often the easiest to start with. Scrum works well when a team does project-like work with a clear product owner.

Is Agile only for software and IT projects?

No. Agile started in software but is used in marketing, HR, finance, government, education, research, manufacturing and construction.

What does an increment look like when using Agile outside software?

A published article, a tested campaign, a piloted process, a prototype or interim research findings, anything usable that generates feedback.

Can HR teams use Agile outside software?

Yes. HR teams often run recruitment pipelines on Kanban boards and pilot new policies with one department before rolling them out.

Can finance teams use Agile outside software?

Yes. Examples include rolling forecasts instead of fixed annual budgets and funding teams through Agile portfolio management.

Is Agile only for software, or is it used in government?

Yes. Many governments build digital public services in increments, testing with real users throughout.

Can Agile outside software work in manufacturing?

Yes, especially for prototyping in short iterations, while Lean and Kanban are long-established on the shop floor.

How do you adapt the Definition of Done for non-software work?

Replace software checks with the quality steps your work needs, such as legal review, brand approval or safety checks. See Definition of Done.

Is Agile only for software, or does business agility go further?

The ability of a whole organisation, not just its delivery teams, to sense change and respond quickly with new products, services or ways of working.

Can Agile outside software work in education?

Yes. Course designers work in iterations and gather student feedback between modules, and some teachers use boards to manage class projects.
