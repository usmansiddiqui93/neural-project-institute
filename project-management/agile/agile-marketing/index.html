Agile Marketing: What It Is and How to Get Started

Agile marketing applies Agile ways of working to marketing: short cycles, small experiments, data-driven decisions and close collaboration. Instead of betting a quarter's budget on one big campaign, Agile marketing teams test, learn and double down on what works.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Agile marketing runs campaigns as small, measurable experiments.
  • Use one backlog, short cycles and regular reviews of results.
  • Scrum suits campaigns; Kanban suits continuous requests.

What makes marketing Agile

  • Short cycles: work is planned in one- or two-week sprints or managed as a continuous flow.
  • Experiments over opinions: ideas are tested with small audiences before full rollout.
  • Customer focus: decisions are guided by customer data and feedback.
  • Cross-functional teams: content, design, analytics and channel specialists work together.
  • Visible priorities: a single backlog of marketing work, ordered by value.
  • Responding to change: plans shift quickly when results, competitors or the market change.

The Agile Marketing Manifesto

In 2012 a group of marketers met in San Francisco and drafted an Agile Marketing Manifesto, adapting the original Agile values. Its themes include validated learning over opinions, customer-focused collaboration over silos, adaptive campaigns over static ones, and many small experiments over a few large bets.

How an Agile marketing team works

  1. Backlog: collect requests, campaign ideas and experiments in one ordered list.
  2. Sprint planning: pick the work for the next one or two weeks, with a clear goal such as "increase trial sign-ups from paid search".
  3. Daily stand-up: 15 minutes to coordinate.
  4. Build and launch: create the smallest version that can be tested.
  5. Measure: check results against the goal.
  6. Review and retrospective: share results with stakeholders and improve the process.

Scrum or Kanban for marketing?

ScrumKanban
Best forCampaigns and launches with clear goalsContinuous requests: content, social, design
CadenceFixed sprintsContinuous flow
PlanningSprint planningPull work when capacity frees up

Many marketing teams combine them, using Scrumban.

Metrics for Agile marketing

  • Outcome metrics: leads, conversions, revenue influenced, retention.
  • Experiment metrics: number of tests run and the share that produce learning.
  • Flow metrics: cycle time from request to launch, and work in progress.
  • Team health and stakeholder satisfaction.

Getting started

  • Put all marketing work on one visible board.
  • Appoint someone to own priorities, like a Product Owner.
  • Agree a simple intake process for requests.
  • Start with two-week sprints or a Kanban board with WIP limits.
  • Review results every sprint and stop what isn't working.

Frequently asked questions

Is Agile marketing just for digital marketing?

It is most common in digital channels, where feedback is fast, but brand, events and content teams use it too.

How long should a marketing sprint be?

One or two weeks is typical; the main thing is to have regular points to review results and re-plan.
