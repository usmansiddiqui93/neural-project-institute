What Is SAFe? The Scaled Agile Framework Explained

The Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) is the most widely adopted framework for applying Agile across large organisations. It provides roles, events and practices to align many Agile teams around shared goals, while keeping the team-level practices of Scrum and Kanban.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 22 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • SAFe aligns many Agile teams through Agile Release Trains and Program Increments.
  • PI Planning and Lean Portfolio Management are its signature practices.
  • It is powerful at scale but can become heavy if adopted mechanically.

Where SAFe comes from

SAFe was created by Dean Leffingwell and first released in 2011. It is maintained by Scaled Agile, Inc., which publishes the framework and runs training and certification. It draws on Agile, Lean, systems thinking and DevOps, and it is updated regularly; check the official site for the current version.

Core values

  • Alignment: everyone understands and works towards the same goals.
  • Transparency: progress, problems and decisions are visible.
  • Respect for people: the people doing the work are trusted and developed.
  • Relentless improvement: the organisation keeps learning and improving.

The four configurations

ConfigurationUse when
Essential SAFeOne or a few Agile Release Trains deliver a product or solution
Large Solution SAFeVery large, complex systems need several trains and suppliers working together
Portfolio SAFeStrategy, funding and governance must be connected to delivery
Full SAFeAll of the above in very large enterprises

Key building blocks

  • Agile teams of about ten or fewer people use Scrum, Kanban or both.
  • Agile Release Train (ART): a long-lived team of Agile teams, typically 50 to 125 people, that plans and delivers together.
  • Program Increment (PI): a planning interval, usually 8 to 12 weeks, often four development iterations followed by an Innovation and Planning iteration.
  • PI Planning: a regular, usually two-day event in which the whole ART plans the next PI together.
  • Lean Portfolio Management: connects strategy and funding with execution.

Key roles

RoleResponsibility
Release Train Engineer (RTE)Servant leader and coach for the ART; facilitates PI Planning
Product ManagementOwns the ART's backlog and vision
System ArchitectGuides technical direction across teams
Business OwnersKey stakeholders accountable for business outcomes
Product Owner and Scrum Master (team coach)Team-level roles, as in Scrum

Benefits and criticisms

Benefits reported by adopters include better alignment between strategy and delivery, more predictable releases, fewer dependency surprises and clearer visibility for leaders.

Criticisms include that SAFe can feel heavy and prescriptive, that it may preserve hierarchy under new names, and that large planning events can become theatre if teams lack real autonomy. Alternatives such as LeSS take a lighter approach.

Tip

Scale only when you need to. If one or two teams can deliver the product, a scaling framework may add more overhead than value.

Frequently asked questions

Is SAFe Agile?

SAFe is built on Agile and Lean principles, and its teams use Scrum and Kanban. Critics argue some implementations become too process-heavy, so how it is adopted matters a lot.

How big must an organisation be for SAFe?

SAFe is designed for programmes of roughly 50 or more people working on one solution. Smaller organisations rarely need it.

What is SAFe in simple terms?

A framework for coordinating many Agile teams that work on the same large product or solution, with shared planning, cadence and governance.

Who created SAFe?

Dean Leffingwell, who first released it in 2011. It is maintained by Scaled Agile, Inc.

What is a Program Increment in SAFe?

A planning interval, usually 8 to 12 weeks, often four development iterations followed by an Innovation and Planning iteration. Recent SAFe versions call it a Planning Interval.

What is an Agile Release Train?

A long-lived team of Agile teams, typically 50 to 125 people, that plans and delivers together. See Agile Release Train.

What are the SAFe configurations?

Essential SAFe, Large Solution SAFe, Portfolio SAFe and Full SAFe, chosen according to size and complexity.

What is a Release Train Engineer?

The servant leader and coach for an Agile Release Train, who facilitates PI Planning and other ART events and helps remove impediments.

What are the criticisms of SAFe?

Critics say it can be prescriptive and process-heavy, and that some implementations rename existing structures without changing how work is done.

What is the difference between SAFe and LeSS?

SAFe adds roles, events and layers for large enterprises; LeSS keeps Scrum's simplicity and scales it with as little extra structure as possible.

What certifications does SAFe offer?

Scaled Agile, Inc. offers role-based certifications such as SAFe Agilist, SAFe Scrum Master and Release Train Engineer. Check its site for current options.
