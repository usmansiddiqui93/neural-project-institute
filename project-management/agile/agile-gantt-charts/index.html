Agile Gantt Charts: When and How to Use Them in Agile Projects

An Agile Gantt chart is a high-level timeline that shows sprints, releases and epics against dates, rather than a detailed schedule of every task. Used this way, a Gantt chart helps Agile teams communicate plans and dependencies to stakeholders without fixing scope in advance.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Gantt charts can work in Agile if they show sprints, releases and epics, not tasks.
  • Use solid bars for the near term and forecast bars further out.
  • Update the chart after each sprint review and treat it as a forecast, not a promise.
  • Pair it with burnup charts and the backlog for a full picture.

Can Gantt charts work in Agile?

A traditional Gantt chart lists every task with fixed dates and dependencies, which suits plans that are expected to stay stable. Agile plans expect change, so a detailed task-level Gantt chart quickly goes out of date. The answer is to change the level of detail: show the time-boxes (sprints and releases) and the larger items (epics and milestones), and let the team manage the detail in the backlog.

  • Useful for: release timelines, cross-team dependencies, fixed external dates, reporting to stakeholders used to Gantt views and hybrid projects.
  • Less useful for: daily task management inside a sprint, where a task board works better.

How to build an Agile Gantt chart

  1. Lay out the time-boxes: add each sprint as a bar of fixed length, for example two weeks.
  2. Add releases and milestones: mark planned release dates and any fixed external dates as milestones.
  3. Place epics, not tasks: show each epic as a bar across the sprints in which it is expected to be worked on, based on velocity.
  4. Show dependencies only between epics or teams: for example, the payments epic needs the provider's API by sprint 4; see task dependencies.
  5. Mark confidence: use solid bars for the next one or two sprints and lighter or dashed bars further out.
  6. Update after each sprint review: move epics as the backlog and forecast change, and record why.

Worked example

A team with a velocity of around 30 points per two-week sprint plans a six-sprint release starting on 6 January.

EpicSize (points)Sprints plannedNotes
Class timetable251Solid; starts immediately
Booking and cancellation402–3Solid
Waiting lists303–4Forecast
Payments454–5Depends on payment provider API by sprint 4
Reminders206Forecast; may move to next release

Total: 160 points across 6 sprints (180 points of capacity), leaving some buffer. The release milestone sits at the end of sprint 6 on 27 March. If the payment API is late, the chart shows at a glance that payments, and possibly reminders, move to the next release.

Gantt chart vs other Agile planning views

ViewShowsBest for
Agile Gantt chartEpics and releases on a timeline with dependenciesStakeholder communication, multi-team coordination
Agile roadmapGoals and themes over time, often without datesStrategy and direction
Release burnup or burndownWork completed versus scope over timeForecasting and tracking progress
Sprint or task boardStatus of each item in the sprintDaily team coordination

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not put sprint-level tasks on the Gantt chart; it creates false precision.
  • Do not treat forecast bars as promises. Explain that scope is the variable, not the date or the quality.
  • Keep one source of truth: link Gantt bars to backlog epics in your tool so they update together. Tools such as Wrike, Smartsheet and monday.com combine timelines with boards; see Gantt chart software.
  • Review the chart at each sprint review, not daily.
  • Use it alongside burnup charts, which show the real rate of progress.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use a Gantt chart in Agile?

Yes, at a high level. Show sprints, releases and epics rather than every task, and update it as plans change.

What is an Agile Gantt chart?

A timeline view of sprints, releases, epics and milestones used to communicate an Agile plan.

Is a Gantt chart Agile or Waterfall?

Gantt charts are most associated with Waterfall planning, but the format itself is neutral. How detailed and fixed the plan is decides whether it fits Agile.

Why do Agile teams avoid Gantt charts?

Detailed task-level Gantt charts imply fixed scope and dates, which conflicts with Agile's expectation of change, and they take effort to keep current.

What do Agile teams use instead of Gantt charts?

Roadmaps, release burnup charts, backlogs and task boards; see Agile tools and techniques.

How do you show sprints on a Gantt chart?

Add each sprint as a fixed-length bar, then place epics across the sprints in which they are planned.

Should a Gantt chart show dependencies in Agile?

Show dependencies between epics, teams and external milestones; avoid task-level dependency chains.

Does Scrum use Gantt charts?

The Scrum Guide does not mention them. Some Scrum teams use a high-level timeline for stakeholder reporting.

How often should an Agile Gantt chart be updated?

After each sprint review, or whenever the forecast changes significantly.

What is the difference between a Gantt chart and a roadmap?

A Gantt chart is date-based and shows scheduled work; a roadmap focuses on goals and direction and may use broad time horizons. See project roadmap.

Which tools offer Gantt charts for Agile teams?

Many tools combine boards and timelines; compare them in our project management tools reviews.
