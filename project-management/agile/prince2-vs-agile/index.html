PRINCE2 vs Agile: Key Differences and How to Combine Them

PRINCE2 vs Agile is less a contest than a comparison of two different jobs: PRINCE2 is a method for governing a project (who decides, when and within what limits), while Agile is a way of delivering the work in short, feedback-driven cycles. Many organisations that use PRINCE2 also run Agile teams, and PRINCE2 Agile is the official guidance for combining them. This guide compares the two side by side, maps roles and documents across, and shows step by step how to run Agile delivery inside PRINCE2 governance, with an illustrative worked example.

Key takeaways

  • PRINCE2 is a project governance method; Agile is a family of values and delivery frameworks such as Scrum and Kanban. They work at different levels, so you can use both.
  • PRINCE2 is not inherently waterfall: its stages are decision points for the project board, and the work inside a stage can be delivered in sprints.
  • PRINCE2 Agile, first published in 2015 and refreshed by PeopleCert in 2025 to align with PRINCE2 7, shows how to combine them.
  • The central idea is fix and flex: fix time and cost, let scope and quality criteria flex within agreed limits, and escalate only when a tolerance will be breached.
  • Map the roles deliberately: the Product Owner usually works for or alongside the senior user, and the Scrum team delivers inside the team manager's work packages.
  • Choose by context: PRINCE2 alone for tightly specified, contract-heavy work; Agile alone for product teams with empowered owners; both when you need accountability and adaptability.

What is the difference between PRINCE2 and Agile?

The main difference between PRINCE2 and Agile is that PRINCE2 governs a project while Agile delivers the work. PRINCE2 defines who authorises what, in management stages, within agreed tolerances. Agile describes how a self-managing team builds and releases value in short iterations, using frequent feedback and a prioritised backlog. Because they operate at different levels, they can be combined.

That is why "PRINCE2 vs Agile" is partly a false choice. PRINCE2 says little about how specialists organise day-to-day work, and Agile frameworks say little about business cases, stage approvals or how a board holds a project to account. PRINCE2 provides the frame; Agile fills it. The real question is how much governance the project needs and how to stop it undermining Agile delivery.

What is PRINCE2?

PRINCE2 (PRojects IN Controlled Environments) is a process-based project management method. It originated with the UK government and is now owned by PeopleCert, which acquired the previous owner, AXELOS, in 2021. The current edition, PRINCE2 7, was published in 2023 and is built from five integrated elements: seven principles, people, seven practices, seven processes and the project context.

How PRINCE2 controls a project

  • Three levels of management. The project board directs, the project manager manages day to day, and team managers deliver the specialist work.
  • Management stages. The project is split into stages. The board approves each stage plan before the stage starts and reviews progress at each stage boundary.
  • Management by exception. The board sets tolerances, and the project manager acts freely within them, escalating only when a tolerance is forecast to be exceeded.
  • Continued business justification. The business case is reviewed throughout. If the project stops being worth doing, it should stop.
  • Focus on products. Outputs are defined up front with product descriptions and quality criteria.

In PRINCE2 7, tolerances can be set against seven performance targets: time, cost, quality, scope, benefits, risk and sustainability. Sustainability was added in the seventh edition; older articles list only six. A business case template is a practical starting point for the document that keeps the whole method honest.

What is Agile?

Agile is an approach to delivering work in small, usable increments, with the people who need the result involved throughout. It traces back to the Agile Manifesto, written by 17 software practitioners in 2001, which sets out four values and twelve principles, such as welcoming changing requirements and delivering working output frequently.

Agile itself is not a method. Teams apply it through frameworks, the most common being Scrum (fixed-length sprints, three accountabilities and five events, as defined in the 2020 Scrum Guide) and Kanban (continuous flow with limits on work in progress). Others include Extreme Programming (XP), DSDM and scaling frameworks such as SAFe.

  • Iterative and incremental. The team delivers something usable every few weeks and learns from it.
  • Empirical. Plans are adjusted based on what actually happened, not what was predicted months earlier.
  • Value-ordered backlog. One person, usually a Product Owner, decides what comes next.
  • Self-managing teams. The people doing the work decide how to do it.

Key differences between PRINCE2 and Agile explained

1. Governance versus delivery

PRINCE2 concentrates on directing and managing: business justification, authorisation, tolerances and reporting. Agile concentrates on delivering: how a team plans, builds, tests, demonstrates and improves. Almost every other difference follows from this.

2. Planning horizon

PRINCE2 asks for a project plan at initiation and a detailed plan for the next stage only, so it is less front-loaded than people assume. Agile also plans at several horizons (roadmap, release, sprint) but expects the backlog to change continuously. A stage plan for an Agile project describes the stage goal, sprints and outcomes, not tasks.

3. Handling change

In PRINCE2, a request for change is an issue. The project manager assesses its impact, and the change authority or board decides, which gives a clear audit trail. In Agile, most changes are simply new or re-ordered backlog items, decided by the Product Owner without ceremony. Combining them means agreeing which changes are routine backlog decisions and which are real issues for change control, for example anything that threatens a Must-have, the budget or the date.

4. Roles and accountability

PRINCE2 makes one person, the executive, accountable for the project, with a hierarchy beneath. Agile spreads decisions: the Product Owner owns value and the team owns how the work is done. They coexist only if the board governs outcomes and tolerances, not tasks.

5. Documentation

PRINCE2 defines management products such as the business case, project initiation documentation, highlight reports and end stage reports. Agile favours working output over comprehensive documentation. PRINCE2 has always expected these products to be tailored, so a highlight report can be a one-page summary of the Sprint Review and the burn-up chart rather than a long narrative.

6. Customer involvement and quality

In PRINCE2 the customer is represented on the board by the senior user, and quality is controlled through product descriptions and quality reviews. In Agile the customer or their proxy sees the product every iteration, and quality is built in through the Definition of Done and acceptance criteria. Agile gives faster feedback; PRINCE2 gives a clearer record of who accepted what.

Is PRINCE2 waterfall?

PRINCE2 is often described as a waterfall method, but that is not accurate. PRINCE2 management stages are decision points for the board, not technical phases such as design, build and test. A stage can contain any mix of activities, including several sprints that each design, build and test a slice of the product.

The confusion arises because many organisations have paired PRINCE2 with sequential delivery. That is a choice about delivery approach, not a requirement of the method. PRINCE2 7 explicitly asks you to tailor the method to the project, including its delivery approach.

Similarities between PRINCE2 and Agile

The two have more in common than the usual comparisons suggest:

  • A focus on value. PRINCE2's continued business justification and Agile's value-ordered backlog both ask whether the work is still worth doing.
  • Learning from experience. PRINCE2 has a principle for it; Agile builds it into every Sprint Retrospective.
  • Clear roles. Both define who is accountable for what, even though the roles differ.
  • Tailoring. Both expect you to adapt them to the context rather than follow them mechanically.

What is PRINCE2 Agile?

PRINCE2 Agile is official guidance on running Agile delivery within PRINCE2 governance. It was first published in 2015. In 2025 PeopleCert released a new version that aligns it with PRINCE2 7 and adds material on performance targets, Agile leadership, team empowerment and mindset, tailoring and scaling. It covers frameworks such as Scrum and Kanban and positions PRINCE2 Agile as compatible with scaling approaches such as SAFe.

PRINCE2 Agile does not replace PRINCE2; it explains how to tailor the same principles, practices and processes for Agile work. Its best-known idea is fix and flex: rather than fixing scope and letting time and cost slip, you fix time and cost and let the amount of scope delivered vary.

AspectTraditional defaultPRINCE2 Agile approachIn practice
TimeOften flexes when scope growsFixedTimeboxed sprints and fixed release dates
CostOften flexesFixedStable team, so cost per sprint is predictable
ScopeFixed in a specificationFlexesLower-priority features can be dropped or deferred
Quality criteriaFixedFlex within limitsNice-to-have criteria can flex; essential quality and the Definition of Done do not
BenefitsFixedFixed or flexAgreed per project with the customer
RiskFixedFixed or flexAgreed per project with the customer

To make scope flex safely, requirements need a clear priority. MoSCoW (Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have this time), which comes from DSDM, is commonly used: Must-haves are the minimum usable product, and tolerances are expressed as how much of the Should and Could work can be deferred.

Tip

Fix and flex only works if the board genuinely accepts that not every requested feature will be delivered. Agree this explicitly at initiation and record it in the project initiation documentation, or the first deferred feature will be treated as a failure.

How PRINCE2 and Agile roles map to each other

PRINCE2 Agile keeps the PRINCE2 roles and places Agile roles inside them. A common mapping looks like this; adjust it to your organisation.

PRINCE2 roleAgile counterpartHow it usually works
ExecutiveSponsor (outside the Scrum Team)Owns the business case and sets tolerances with the board
Senior userProduct Owner or the Product Owner's sponsorRepresents users; the Product Owner often reports to or is appointed by the senior user
Senior supplierDelivery lead or head of engineeringCommits the people and skills the teams need
Project managerNo direct Scrum equivalentManages stages, reporting, risks, dependencies and the board interface; does not assign tasks to the team
Team managerScrum Master, team lead or the team collectivelyAccepts work packages and delivers them as sprints
Project assuranceAgile coach or quality lead (partly)Checks the project is run properly, independently of the project manager
Change authorityProduct Owner for backlog changes within toleranceRoutine scope decisions are delegated; tolerance-breaking changes go to the board

The most important design decision is the Product Owner's authority. If the Product Owner has to refer every priority call to the board, the team will stall. Delegate backlog decisions within scope tolerance to the Product Owner, and keep the Scrum Master focused on the team's effectiveness rather than on PRINCE2 paperwork.

How to combine PRINCE2 and Agile: step by step

Use these steps when a PRINCE2 organisation wants its teams to deliver in an Agile way.

  1. Confirm the project suits an Agile delivery approach. Check that requirements are likely to change, work can be released in usable slices and a customer representative is available every sprint.
  2. Write a business case that tolerates flexible scope. Express benefits in terms of outcomes (for example, applications processed online) rather than a fixed feature list, so that deferring a lower-priority feature does not break the case.
  3. Agree what is fixed and what flexes. With the board, fix time and cost, define the Must-have scope and set tolerances for quality, benefits, risk and sustainability. Record these in the project initiation documentation.
  4. Map roles and delegate authority. Name the Product Owner, confirm what they can decide alone and agree how the project manager, team manager and Scrum Master divide their responsibilities.
  5. Align stages with releases. Make each management stage a whole number of sprints that ends with a usable release. The stage boundary then becomes a review of real output rather than of documents.
  6. Issue work packages as sprint or release goals. The team manager accepts a goal and a set of prioritised items, and the team plans the detail in Sprint Planning.
  7. Report from the team's own data. Base highlight reports on the Sprint Review, burn-up chart and Definition of Done results, not separate status documents.
  8. Escalate by exception. Monitor forecasts against tolerances each sprint. When the forecast shows a Must-have will not fit, or cost or time tolerance will be exceeded, raise an exception report with options for the board.
  9. Close and learn. Confirm benefits measures, hand over the product and feed retrospective themes into the lessons report.

Worked example: Agile delivery inside PRINCE2 governance

The following example is illustrative. A county council needs to replace a paper permit application with an online service. A change in regulation means the service must be live in 30 weeks. The approved budget is £480,000, which funds one Scrum Team of eight people working two-week sprints.

Stages and sprints

StageWeeksSprintsStage goal
1. Initiation1–4NoneBusiness case, Must-have scope, tolerances, team and Product Owner agreed
2. Discovery and prototype5–12Sprints 1–4Tested prototype of the application journey with real applicants
3. Build13–24Sprints 5–10Working service handling the main permit types in a test environment
4. Release and handover25–30Sprints 11–13Live service, staff trained, support handed over

Tolerances agreed by the board

Performance targetFixed or flexTolerance
TimeFixedZero: go-live by week 30
CostFixed±5% (£24,000)
ScopeFlexAll Must-haves delivered; up to 40% of Should and Could items may be deferred
QualityFlex within limitsDefinition of Done, including the council's accessibility standard, is not negotiable
BenefitsFixedOnline share of applications reaches an agreed target within six months of launch
RiskFlexNo high-rated risk without an owner and response
SustainabilityFixedHosted within the council's existing cloud arrangement

What happens mid-project

At the end of Sprint 7, the team's average velocity is 30 points against the 38 assumed in the stage plan. The remaining Must-have backlog is estimated at 210 points, and six sprints remain (Sprints 8 to 13). At current velocity the team can complete about 6 × 30 = 180 points, leaving roughly 30 points of Must-have work undone. That breaches the scope tolerance, so the project manager raises an exception report rather than quietly asking the team to work faster.

OptionEffectWithin tolerance?
A. Add a developer for four sprintsCosts about £36,000 (illustrative)No: exceeds the £24,000 cost tolerance
B. Reclassify card payment as a Should-have and take payments by phone for three monthsRemoves about 35 points of Must-have work; senior user confirms the service is still usableYes, if the board approves the change to Must-have scope
C. Add one sprintDelivers everything two weeks lateNo: time is fixed by regulation

The board chooses option B, approves an exception plan for the rest of the stage and asks the Product Owner to put card payments first after launch. The team never stopped work; the board decided one specific trade-off, which is how management by exception should support Agile delivery.

Tip

Track Must-have scope separately on your burn-up chart. A single line for all scope hides the moment when the minimum usable product is at risk.

Common mistakes when combining PRINCE2 and Agile

  • Fixing scope, time and cost all at once. Something has to give. Decide in advance that it is lower-priority scope.
  • Making stage boundaries document reviews. End each stage with a release or demonstrable increment and review that instead.
  • Asking the team for duplicate reporting. Build highlight reports from the sprint board, burn-up chart and Sprint Review.
  • A Product Owner without authority. If every priority decision goes to the board, the team waits. Delegate scope decisions within tolerance.
  • The project manager acting as task-master. In an Agile team the Developers plan their own work; the project manager manages the project environment.
  • Ignoring the business case after initiation. Continued business justification is a PRINCE2 principle; review it at every stage boundary using real release data.

A risk register template helps you track the delivery risks that most often trigger exceptions, such as dependency delays or an unavailable Product Owner.

Tools for hybrid PRINCE2 and Agile projects

A hybrid project needs two views of the same work: a stage and milestone view for the board and a backlog and board view for the team. Choose a tool that keeps both in one place so reporting comes from live data.

  • Wrike, our top-rated tool, suits hybrid governance well: interactive Gantt charts for stages and milestones, boards for sprint work, request forms and shareable dashboards for highlight reporting.
  • Jira is the natural home for software teams' backlogs, sprints and Agile reports, and is often paired with a portfolio or reporting layer for the board.
  • monday.com is easy to adopt and combines timeline and board views with dashboards, which helps when business teams and delivery teams share one plan.

Whichever tool you use, add a field for Must-have items so scope tolerance shows on a dashboard. An Agile PMO can standardise this across projects.

Frequently asked questions

Is PRINCE2 agile or waterfall?

Neither by definition. PRINCE2 is a governance method whose stages are decision points for the board, not technical phases. It is often paired with sequential delivery, but the work inside each stage can be delivered in sprints.

Can PRINCE2 and Scrum be used together?

Yes. PRINCE2 provides the governance layer (business case, project board, stages and tolerances) and the Scrum Team delivers inside it, with work packages expressed as sprint or release goals.

What is PRINCE2 Agile?

Official guidance from PeopleCert on running Agile delivery within PRINCE2 governance. It was first published in 2015, and a new version aligned with PRINCE2 7 was released in 2025. It covers frameworks such as Scrum and Kanban and how to tailor PRINCE2's principles, practices and processes for Agile work.

What does fix and flex mean in PRINCE2 Agile?

It means fixing time and cost and letting scope and some quality criteria flex within agreed tolerances. Benefits and risk can be fixed or flexed depending on the project. In practice, Must-have scope is protected and lower-priority features are deferred if time runs short.

Does PRINCE2 have a Scrum Master or Product Owner?

Not as named roles. In PRINCE2 Agile, the Product Owner usually works for or alongside the senior user, and the Scrum Master supports a team that delivers work packages under the team manager role.

Who owns PRINCE2?

PRINCE2 originated with the UK government and is now owned by PeopleCert, which acquired AXELOS in 2021. PeopleCert also owns PRINCE2 Agile and runs the certification exams. The current edition of the core method is PRINCE2 7, published in 2023.

How does management by exception work in an Agile project?

The board sets tolerances for time, cost, scope and other targets, and the team works freely within them. The project manager forecasts against those tolerances each sprint, usually from velocity and the remaining Must-have backlog, and escalates only when a tolerance is forecast to be exceeded.

Is PRINCE2 still relevant?

Yes, particularly in the UK, Europe, Australia and the public sector. PeopleCert updated the core method in 2023 and PRINCE2 Agile in 2025.

Which is better for my career, PRINCE2 or an Agile certification?

It depends on your market. PRINCE2 is widely requested for project manager roles in the UK, Europe and the public sector; Scrum and other Agile certifications are common in product and technology roles. Many project managers hold one of each.

Do I need PRINCE2 before taking PRINCE2 Agile?

Not necessarily for the Foundation level, but prerequisites can differ by level and version and PeopleCert sets them. Check PeopleCert's current requirements before you book.

How do PRINCE2 stages relate to sprints?

A management stage usually contains several sprints and ends with a release or demonstrable increment. Making each stage a whole number of sprints means the stage boundary review looks at real output and an updated forecast rather than at documents alone.

What replaces a highlight report in an Agile team?

Nothing needs replacing; it changes form. A short report built from the Sprint Review outcome, the burn-up chart, forecasts against tolerances and key risks gives the board what it needs without extra paperwork.
