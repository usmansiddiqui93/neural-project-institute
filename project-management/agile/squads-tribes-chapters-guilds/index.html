Squads, Tribes, Chapters and Guilds: The Spotify Model Explained

Squads, tribes, chapters and guilds are terms from the so-called Spotify model, described in a 2012 paper by Henrik Kniberg and Anders Ivarsson. It shows one way to keep many Agile teams autonomous while still aligned.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Squads deliver, tribes coordinate, chapters develop skills, guilds share knowledge.
  • The model balances team autonomy with organisational alignment.
  • Copy the principles, not the labels.

The building blocks

UnitWhat it isPurpose
SquadA small, cross-functional, self-organising team with a long-term missionDeliver a part of the product end to end
TribeA group of squads working in a related area, kept small enough for people to know each other (the paper suggests under about 100)Coordinate related squads
ChapterPeople with the same skill within a tribe, such as testers or designers, led by a chapter lead who is also their line managerMaintain skills and standards
GuildA voluntary community of interest across the whole organisationShare knowledge across tribes

How it balances autonomy and alignment

Squads decide how to build their part of the product; leaders provide a clear mission and context. Chapters give people a professional home and consistent practices across squads. Guilds spread knowledge informally. The result is a matrix in which delivery is organised by squad and tribe, and people development by chapter.

Important caveat

The paper described how Spotify worked at one point in time. Spotify has said its organisation kept evolving and that the model was never meant to be copied as a framework. Organisations that adopt the labels without the underlying culture of trust, autonomy and continuous improvement often struggle.

Tip

Borrow the ideas, not the names. The lasting lessons are: small autonomous teams, clear missions, communities of practice and leaders who provide context rather than control.

When to consider it

  • Several product teams need autonomy but must share technology and standards.
  • You want people to grow their craft while working in cross-functional teams.
  • Leadership is ready to trust teams and decentralise decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Spotify model a framework?

No. It was a description of one company's way of working at one time, not a prescriptive framework.

What is the difference between a chapter and a guild?

A chapter is a formal group of people with the same skill within one tribe, with a chapter lead. A guild is an informal, voluntary community across the whole organisation.
