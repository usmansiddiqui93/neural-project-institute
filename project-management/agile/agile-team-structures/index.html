Agile Team Structures: Types, Roles and How to Choose One

An Agile team structure is the way people, skills and responsibilities are organised so that a small, cross-functional team can deliver value end to end. Common structures include feature teams, component teams, generalist and specialist mixes, and platform teams, often grouped into larger units as organisations scale.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 7 September 2026
  • 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Agile teams are small, cross-functional, self-managing and long-lived.
  • Feature teams reduce hand-offs; component and platform teams provide depth and shared services.
  • Design structures around the flow of value and minimise dependencies.
  • Review structures periodically, but keep teams stable.

What makes a team Agile

  • Cross-functional: the team has all the skills needed to turn a backlog item into a finished result.
  • Small and stable: typically around ten people or fewer; the 2020 Scrum Guide says a Scrum Team is typically 10 or fewer.
  • Self-managing: the team decides how to do its work.
  • Long-lived: teams stay together across many projects so that they learn and improve.
  • Focused on one goal or product at a time.

StructureHow it worksStrengthsWatch out for
Feature teamOwns complete customer features across all layersFast end-to-end delivery, fewer hand-offsNeeds broad skills; shared code needs good practices
Component teamOwns one part of the system, such as payments or searchDeep expertise, consistent architectureFeatures need several teams; more dependencies
Generalist teamMembers can work across many types of taskFlexible, avoids bottlenecksMay lack deep expertise
Specialist teamMembers each hold a distinct skillHigh quality in each disciplineBottlenecks when one skill is overloaded
Hybrid (T-shaped) teamMembers have one deep skill and broader secondary skillsBalances depth and flexibilityTakes time and support to build

Tip

Most organisations use a mix: mainly feature teams, supported by a small number of platform or component teams for shared services.

How to design your team structure

  1. Start from the value stream: map how work flows from request to customer; see value stream mapping.
  2. Choose the team type: prefer feature teams aligned to customer journeys or products, and add component or platform teams only where expertise or scale demands it.
  3. Size the teams: aim for around five to nine members, and split larger groups.
  4. Fill skill gaps: list the skills needed to finish work without hand-offs, then hire, train or pair to cover gaps.
  5. Minimise dependencies: if a team regularly waits on another, adjust boundaries.
  6. Define how teams coordinate: use practices such as a Scrum of Scrums or shared planning.
  7. Review regularly: check flow, dependencies and morale every few months and adapt.

Example: from component to feature teams

An online retailer has four component teams (web front end, mobile, checkout services and database) of six people each. A typical feature needs all four, so items wait in queues and a simple change takes six weeks. The company re-forms into three feature teams aligned to customer journeys (browse, buy, account), each with front-end, mobile, back-end and testing skills, plus one small platform team of six for shared infrastructure. With fewer hand-offs, most features are now completed by a single team within one or two sprints.

Structures at scale

  • Team of teams: several teams working on one product share a backlog and coordinate; see Large-Scale Scrum (LeSS).
  • Agile Release Train: in SAFe, typically 5–12 teams plan and deliver together; see Agile Release Train.
  • Squads, tribes, chapters and guilds: a model popularised by Spotify; see squads, tribes, chapters and guilds.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Agile team structure?

There is no single best structure. Feature teams suit most product work, with platform or component teams where specialist depth or shared services are needed.

How big should an Agile team structure be?

Small, typically around ten people or fewer. Larger groups should usually split into several teams.

What is a cross-functional Agile team structure?

A team that has all the skills needed to deliver a finished result without relying on other teams.

Which Agile team structure is better: feature teams or component teams?

A feature team delivers complete customer features across the system; a component team owns one part of the system.

What is a T-shaped team member in an Agile team structure?

Someone with deep expertise in one area and working knowledge of several others, which helps the team share work.

Does an Agile team structure need a project manager?

Not within Scrum, which has no project manager role. Some organisations keep project managers for cross-team coordination; see Agile project manager.

Who leads in an Agile team structure?

Agile teams are self-managing. The product owner sets priorities, the Scrum Master supports effectiveness, and the team decides how to do the work.

What is a platform team in an Agile team structure?

A team that builds and runs shared tools or services so that other teams can deliver faster.

Should an Agile team structure be co-located?

Co-location helps communication, but many teams work well remotely or in hybrid setups with good tools and agreed working hours.

How often should an Agile team structure change?

Teams benefit from stability, so avoid frequent reshuffles. Review structures every few months and change them when dependencies or goals shift.

How does an Agile team structure coordinate multiple teams?

Through shared backlogs, joint planning, regular cross-team meetings and scaling frameworks; see scaled Agile framework.
