What Is Agile Swarming? How Teams Finish Work Faster

Swarming is when several team members work together on the same backlog item until it is done, instead of each person working on a separate item. It is one of the simplest ways to finish work faster and reduce the amount of half-done work.

Why swarming works

When everyone works on separate items, many items are in progress at once and none are finished until late in the sprint. Swarming reduces work in progress (WIP), which shortens cycle time, exposes blockers sooner and spreads knowledge across the team.

When to swarm

  • A high-priority item is at risk of not finishing in the sprint.
  • An item is blocked and needs several skills to unblock it.
  • The board shows too many items in progress.
  • A new team member needs to learn part of the product.
  • A production incident needs urgent attention.

How to start swarming

  1. At the Daily Scrum, ask "What can we finish today?" rather than "What will you start?"
  2. Set WIP limits on your board.
  3. Break the item into tasks that can be done in parallel.
  4. Pair or mob on the hardest parts.
  5. Celebrate finished items, not busy people.

Swarming vs mobbing vs pairing

PracticeWhoHow
PairingTwo peopleShare one task at one screen
SwarmingSeveral peopleSplit one item into parallel tasks
MobbingThe whole teamEveryone on one task at one screen

Pitfalls

  • Too many people on a tiny item, so they get in each other's way.
  • Swarming only in emergencies, so it never becomes a habit.
  • No shared definition of when the item is done; agree it upfront using the Definition of Done.

Key takeaways

  • Swarming means several people finish one item together.
  • It reduces work in progress and speeds up delivery.
  • Ask "what can we finish?" rather than "what can we start?"

Frequently asked questions

Does swarming reduce productivity?

Individual utilisation may drop, but the team finishes more items, sooner, with fewer handoffs and less rework.

Is swarming only for software teams?

No. Any team can swarm, for example a marketing team finishing a campaign launch together.
