Agile Glossary: 80+ Agile Terms Defined in Plain English

This Agile glossary defines the terms you will meet in Agile, Scrum, Kanban and scaled Agile, in plain English. Where a term has its own guide, follow the link for more detail. Official Scrum terms follow the 2020 Scrum Guide.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 9 September 2026
  • 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Agile vocabulary spans values, frameworks, roles, events, artifacts and metrics.
  • Many common terms are practices, not official Scrum terms.
  • Agree shared definitions within your team to avoid confusion.
  • Follow the links to in-depth guides for any term you need to apply.

A to C

TermDefinition
Acceptance criteriaConditions a backlog item must meet to be accepted; see user stories
Acceptance test-driven development (ATDD)Writing acceptance tests with the customer before building a feature
AgileAn approach to work based on short cycles, feedback and adaptation; see what is Agile
Agile coachSomeone who helps teams and leaders adopt Agile ways of working
Agile ManifestoThe 2001 statement of four values and twelve principles; see Agile Manifesto
Agile mindsetAttitudes that favour learning, collaboration and adaptability; see Agile mindset
Agile Release Train (ART)In SAFe, a long-lived team of Agile teams; see Agile Release Train
BacklogAn ordered list of work; see product backlog
Backlog refinementOngoing work to clarify, split and estimate backlog items; see backlog refinement
BlockerAnything stopping work from progressing; see blocker clustering
Burndown chartA chart of work remaining over time
Burnup chartA chart of work completed and total scope over time
CadenceA regular rhythm of events, such as two-week sprints
CapacityHow much work a team can take on in a period; see Agile capacity planning
CeremonyInformal name for a recurring Agile meeting; see Agile meetings
Continuous deliveryKeeping software always releasable; see continuous delivery
Continuous integration (CI)Merging and testing code changes frequently, often several times a day
Cross-functional teamA team with all the skills needed to deliver finished work
Cumulative flow diagramA chart showing items in each workflow stage over time; see CFD
Cycle timeTime from starting work on an item to finishing it; see cycle time

How to use this glossary

  • Agree shared definitions within your team, especially for terms like "done" and "ready".
  • Remember that many terms (story points, velocity, epics) are common practices, not part of the Scrum Guide; see the Scrum glossary for official Scrum terms.
  • Use the linked guides to go deeper on any term.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most important Agile terms to know?

Start with sprint, backlog, user story, Definition of Done, velocity, retrospective, Kanban board and WIP limit.

What is the difference between Agile and Scrum?

Agile is a broad approach based on values and principles; Scrum is a specific framework that applies them. See Agile vs Scrum.

What is a sprint in Agile?

A fixed time-box, usually one to four weeks, in which a team delivers a usable increment.

What does WIP mean in Agile?

Work in progress: items that have been started but not finished.

What is velocity in Agile?

The amount of work, often in story points, a team completes per sprint, used for forecasting.

What is an epic in Agile?

A large body of work that is broken down into smaller user stories; see Agile epics.

What is a spike in Agile?

A short, time-boxed piece of research or experimentation to answer a question before committing to build something.

What does MVP mean in Agile?

Minimum viable product: the smallest version of a product that lets a team learn from real users.

What is the difference between a burndown and a burnup chart in Agile?

A burndown shows work remaining; a burnup shows work completed and total scope, so scope changes are visible.

Are Agile story points part of Scrum?

No. Story points are a common estimation practice, but the Scrum Guide does not prescribe them.

What is a time-box in Agile?

A fixed maximum duration for an event or activity, such as a 15-minute Daily Scrum.
