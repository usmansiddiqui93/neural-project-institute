What Is a Sprint in Agile? Length, Goals and Events

A sprint is a short, fixed period, one month or less, in which an Agile team turns a set of backlog items into a usable result. Sprints give Agile teams their heartbeat: a regular rhythm of planning, building, reviewing and improving.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 13 September 2026
  • 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A sprint is a fixed time-box of one month or less with one Sprint Goal.
  • Two weeks is the most common length.
  • Each sprint includes planning, daily coordination, a review and a retrospective.

Sprint basics

  • Fixed length. Usually one to four weeks; two weeks is most common. The length stays the same from sprint to sprint so the team can learn its pace.
  • One goal. Each sprint has a Sprint Goal, a single objective that gives the work focus.
  • A usable result. By the end, the team produces an Increment that meets the Definition of Done.
  • Back to back. A new sprint starts immediately after the previous one ends.

Choosing a sprint length

LengthGood forWatch out for
1 weekFast-moving products, quick feedbackPlanning overhead feels high
2 weeksMost teams; balances feedback and overhead—
3–4 weeksComplex work that needs longer to show resultsSlower feedback; risk of mini-waterfalls inside the sprint

Pick the shortest length in which the team can deliver something meaningful, and keep it stable for at least several sprints before changing it.

What happens during a sprint

  1. Sprint Planning. The team agrees why the sprint matters (the Sprint Goal), what it can deliver, and how it will do the work.
  2. Daily Scrum. A 15-minute daily check on progress towards the Sprint Goal.
  3. Development work. The team builds, tests and integrates backlog items, and refines upcoming ones.
  4. Sprint Review. The team shows the result to stakeholders and adjusts the backlog using their feedback.
  5. Sprint Retrospective. The team inspects how it worked and picks improvements for the next sprint.

See Agile meetings and ceremonies for how to run each one well.

Can the sprint change?

Scope can be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as the team learns more, but changes that would endanger the Sprint Goal are avoided. If the Sprint Goal itself becomes obsolete, for example because the market shifts, only the Product Owner can cancel the sprint. This is rare.

Tips for successful sprints

  • Commit to less than you think you can do, especially in early sprints.
  • Write a Sprint Goal that describes an outcome, not a list of tasks.
  • Keep items small enough to finish within a few days.
  • Finish work before starting new work; see swarming.
  • Track progress daily with a burndown chart or board.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a sprint and an iteration?

A sprint is Scrum's name for an iteration. Other Agile methods use "iteration" for the same idea.

What happens to unfinished work at the end of a sprint?

It returns to the Product Backlog, where the Product Owner re-orders it. It does not count as done, and it is not automatically carried into the next sprint.

How long is a sprint?

One month or less in Scrum. Most teams use one to four weeks, and two weeks is the most common choice.

What is a Sprint Goal?

A single objective for the sprint that gives the team focus and flexibility. The team commits to the goal rather than to every individual item.

Can a sprint be cancelled?

Yes. In Scrum, only the Product Owner can cancel a sprint, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. Cancellation is rare.

Can you extend a sprint?

No. The sprint is time-boxed. Unfinished work returns to the Product Backlog and the next sprint starts on schedule.

Is there a break between sprints?

No. In Scrum, a new sprint starts immediately after the previous one ends.

What is a sprint zero?

An informal term for a preparation period before the first real sprint, used to set up tools and an initial backlog. The Scrum Guide does not define it, and many coaches recommend keeping it short or skipping it.

What is a sprint backlog?

The Sprint Goal, the Product Backlog items selected for the sprint and the team's plan for delivering them. The Developers own it and update it throughout the sprint.

How many user stories should be in a sprint?

There is no fixed number. Many teams find that several small stories, each finishable in a few days, work better than a few large ones. Use recent velocity as a guide.

Who decides what goes into a sprint?

The Product Owner orders the backlog and proposes the goal; the Developers decide how much they can take on. See Agile capacity planning.
