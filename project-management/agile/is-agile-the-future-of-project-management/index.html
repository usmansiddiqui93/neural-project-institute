Is Agile the Future of Project Management?

Agile has moved from a niche software practice to the mainstream. But the future of project management is unlikely to be "Agile only". It is more likely to be adaptive: choosing the right approach for each piece of work, and increasingly supported by AI.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 26 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Agile is mainstream, but hybrid approaches are the norm.
  • Organisations are moving towards products and business agility.
  • AI will automate routine PM tasks and raise the value of judgement and leadership.

Agile is now mainstream

Most organisations use Agile practices somewhere, especially in technology and product teams, and many project managers now work in Agile or hybrid environments. Agile ideas such as short feedback loops, visible work and empowered teams have become standard good practice.

Hybrid is the norm

Most real projects combine predictive and Agile elements: predictive governance, budgets and milestones, with Agile delivery inside them. Professional standards have moved in the same direction, describing a range of approaches rather than a single "correct" method. See Agile vs Waterfall.

From projects to products and business agility

  • More organisations fund long-lived product teams instead of one-off projects.
  • Portfolio decisions are reviewed quarterly rather than annually; see Agile portfolio management.
  • Business agility, the ability of the whole organisation to adapt, is replacing team-level Agile as the goal.

The role of AI

AI is already changing project work: drafting plans and reports, summarising meetings, analysing risks and forecasting delivery from historical data. This makes adaptive planning cheaper and faster, and shifts the project manager's value further towards judgement, stakeholder relationships and leadership.

Skills project managers will need

  • Fluency in both predictive and Agile approaches, and the judgement to choose between them.
  • Facilitation, coaching and stakeholder management.
  • Product thinking and a focus on outcomes.
  • Data literacy and the ability to work effectively with AI tools.
  • Change leadership.

Frequently asked questions

Will Agile replace Waterfall as the future of project management?

Unlikely. Predictive approaches remain the best fit for stable, regulated and physical projects. The trend is towards choosing and combining approaches.

Will AI replace project managers in the future of project management?

AI will automate many administrative tasks, but the human work of leading people, managing stakeholders and making judgement calls remains central.

Is Agile still relevant to the future of project management in 2026?

Yes. Agile practices are mainstream in technology and product teams, and most organisations now combine them with predictive elements in hybrid approaches.

Is hybrid project management the future of project management?

Combining predictive governance, budgets and milestones with Agile delivery inside them. It is the norm on many real projects.

Is Agile declining, or is it the future of project management?

Some organisations have scaled back heavy, framework-driven transformations, but the core Agile ideas of short feedback loops and empowered teams remain standard good practice.

What role does business agility play in the future of project management?

The ability of a whole organisation to adapt quickly, which many see as the next goal beyond team-level Agile.

How is AI changing the future of Agile?

AI drafts plans and reports, summarises meetings, analyses risks and forecasts delivery from historical data, making adaptive planning faster and cheaper.

What skills will the future of project management require?

Fluency in predictive and Agile approaches, facilitation, stakeholder management, product thinking, data literacy, AI skills and change leadership.

Is the future of project management moving from projects to products?

Many are, by funding long-lived product teams instead of one-off projects and reviewing portfolio decisions quarterly.

Given the future of project management, should I learn Agile or traditional methods?

Both. Most roles now expect you to choose and combine approaches to fit the project. See how to learn Agile and get certified.

Does PMI see Agile as part of the future of project management?

Yes. PMI offers the PMI-ACP certification, and its PMP exam covers predictive, Agile and hybrid approaches.
