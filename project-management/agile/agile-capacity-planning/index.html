Agile Capacity Planning: How Much Can Your Team Take On?

Capacity planning answers a simple question: how much work can this team realistically take on in the coming sprint or quarter? Getting it right prevents overcommitment, burnout and missed goals.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Capacity is available time; velocity is historical output.
  • Adjust average velocity by changes in availability.
  • Reserve capacity for unplanned work.

Capacity vs velocity

Capacity is the time the team has available. Velocity is how much work it has historically completed in that time. Good planning uses both: velocity as the baseline, adjusted for changes in capacity.

Calculating sprint capacity

  1. List each team member and their available working days in the sprint, after holidays, training and other commitments.
  2. Multiply by the hours they can focus on sprint work each day; few people have more than five or six.
  3. Subtract time reserved for meetings, support and unplanned work.
  4. Compare total available time with normal capacity, and scale your average velocity accordingly.
Tip

Example: average velocity is 30 points. Two of five team members are on leave for half the sprint, so capacity is about 80% of normal. Plan for roughly 24 points.

Planning for interruptions

  • Track how much unplanned work arrives each sprint.
  • Reserve that share of capacity, or rotate one person to handle interruptions.
  • If unplanned work is large and unpredictable, consider Kanban instead of fixed sprints.

Capacity planning across teams

At the portfolio level, capacity is usually expressed as the number of teams or sprints available per quarter. Leaders match demand from the roadmap against this capacity and decide what not to do. In SAFe, this happens during PI Planning.

Common mistakes

  • Planning at 100% of available hours.
  • Ignoring holidays and part-time availability.
  • Assuming new team members are fully productive from day one.
  • Adding people late to speed up a late project, which usually slows it down at first.

Frequently asked questions

What is a focus factor?

The share of available time a team actually spends on planned sprint work. Many teams find it is between 60% and 80%.

Should we plan in hours or story points?

Use story points and velocity for what to commit to, and hours or days only to check availability.
