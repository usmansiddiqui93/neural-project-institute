What Is a Project? Definition, Characteristics and Examples

A project is a temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service or result. That definition, used across project management standards, has two key words: temporary and unique.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A project is temporary and creates something unique.
  • Operations are ongoing and repetitive.
  • Related projects form programmes; strategic groups form portfolios.

Characteristics of a project

  • Temporary: it has a defined start and end.
  • Unique: the output is new in some way.
  • Specific objective: there is a clear result to achieve.
  • Constraints: limits on time, budget, resources and quality.
  • Progressive elaboration: details become clearer as the project proceeds.
  • A dedicated team, often assembled for the purpose.

Projects, programmes and portfolios

Related projects can be managed together as a programme to achieve benefits none could deliver alone. A portfolio groups projects and programmes to meet strategic goals. See project, programme and portfolio management.

Frequently asked questions

Is a recurring task a project?

No. Recurring work is operations. A project ends once its objective is achieved.

How small can a project be?

Any temporary effort with a unique result can be managed as a project, though small ones need only light planning.

What is a project in project management?

A temporary effort with a defined start and end, undertaken to create a unique product, service or result.

What are the characteristics of a project?

It is temporary, produces something unique, has a specific objective, works within constraints, is progressively elaborated and usually has a dedicated team.

What is the difference between a project and operations?

A project is temporary and creates something unique; operations are ongoing and repetitive. Implementing a payroll system is a project; running payroll every month is operations.

What is progressive elaboration?

Adding detail to a project's plans as more information becomes available, rather than trying to know everything at the start.

What are examples of projects?

Migrating email to a new platform, building a school, launching a marketing campaign, opening a clinic, introducing a new HR process or organising a conference.

What is the difference between a project and a programme?

A programme is a group of related projects managed together to achieve benefits none could deliver alone. See project vs programme vs portfolio.

What are project constraints?

Limits on time, budget, resources, scope and quality. Changing one usually affects the others.

Who starts a project?

Usually a sponsor who identifies a need or opportunity and authorises the project, often through a project charter.

What is a project deliverable?

Any unique, verifiable product, result or capability that a project must produce to complete a phase or the project.
