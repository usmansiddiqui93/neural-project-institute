Iterative and Incremental Development Explained

Iterative and incremental development are two related ideas behind most modern delivery approaches. Iterative means improving through repeated cycles; incremental means delivering in usable pieces.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Iterative means improving through cycles; incremental means delivering in pieces.
  • Agile combines both.
  • Use them when requirements are uncertain.
Table of contents
  1. Key takeaways
  2. Definitions
  3. Using both together
  4. History
  5. When to use it

Definitions

IterativeIncremental
IdeaRefine the solution through repeated cyclesBuild the solution piece by piece
ExampleA rough design improved over several reviewsLogin first, then search, then payments
BenefitLearning and quality improve each cycleUsers get value sooner

Using both together

Agile frameworks such as Scrum combine both: each Sprint delivers a usable increment, and each cycle improves earlier work based on feedback. The result is early value plus continuous learning.

History

Iterative and incremental development has been used since at least the 1950s and 1960s on large engineering programmes, long before the word Agile was coined. See the history of Agile.

When to use it

  • Requirements are uncertain or likely to change.
  • Early feedback from users is valuable.
  • The solution can be delivered and used in parts.
  • Risk needs to be reduced step by step.

Frequently asked questions

Is iterative the same as Agile?

Agile uses iterative and incremental development, but adds values such as customer collaboration and self-organising teams.

Can Waterfall be iterative?

Some predictive projects add iterations within phases, especially for design and prototyping.
