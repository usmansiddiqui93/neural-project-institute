Project Management Certification: Which One to Choose

A project management certification is a credential that shows you have met a defined standard of project management knowledge and, for some credentials, experience. In short: the PMP suits experienced project managers, the CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation or APM PFQ suit beginners, and Scrum or PMI-ACP credentials suit Agile roles. This guide compares the main options, their requirements and renewal rules, and shows you how to choose.

Key takeaways

  • Choose by experience, country and way of working, not by fame alone.
  • The PMP is the best-known credential for experienced project managers; PMI launched a new PMP exam in July 2026.
  • Beginners can start with the CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, APM PFQ, CompTIA Project+ or a structured course such as the PMAI.
  • Agile roles usually start with a Scrum certification; the PMI-ACP suits practitioners with Agile experience.
  • Many credentials must be renewed, for example 60 PDUs every three years for the PMP.
  • Always check current fees and eligibility on the awarding body's official website before you book.

What is a project management certification?

A project management certification is a credential awarded by a professional body after you pass an exam or assessment against a published standard. Some, such as the PMP, also require verified project experience and ongoing learning to stay valid. Employers use certifications as evidence that you understand a shared vocabulary and recognised methods.

Certification vs certificate

The words are often used interchangeably, but they are not the same thing. A professional certification usually involves eligibility checks, a proctored exam and renewal rules. A certificate of completion shows you finished and passed a course or programme. Both can be valuable: a course certificate is often the quickest way to build knowledge, while a professional certification carries more weight in job adverts that name a specific credential.

Who awards project management certifications?

  • PMI (Project Management Institute): PMP, CAPM, PMI-ACP, PgMP, PfMP and specialist credentials. See what is PMI.
  • PeopleCert (owner of AXELOS): PRINCE2 Foundation and Practitioner, PRINCE2 Agile.
  • APM (Association for Project Management, UK): Project Fundamentals Qualification (PFQ) and Project Management Qualification (PMQ).
  • IPMA (International Project Management Association): a four-level certification scheme delivered through national associations.
  • Scrum.org and Scrum Alliance: Professional Scrum Master (PSM) and Certified ScrumMaster (CSM), among others.
  • CompTIA: Project+, popular in IT.
  • Course providers and universities: for example the Google Project Management Certificate on Coursera, and our own PMAI certification.

Certifications for experienced project managers

PMP (Project Management Professional)

The PMP is PMI's flagship credential and the one most often named in project manager job adverts worldwide. According to PMI's certification page, you need 35 hours of project management education (or an active CAPM) and, depending on your education, between three and five years of experience leading projects within the last ten years: five years with a secondary diploma, four with an associate's degree or vocational diploma, three with a bachelor's degree, and two if your degree is from a PMI-accredited programme.

The exam has 180 questions in 240 minutes. PMI launched a new version of the PMP exam in July 2026. PMI states that the domain weightings changed from People 42%, Process 50% and Business Environment 8% to People 33%, Process 41% and Business Environment 26%, and that scenarios now include AI, sustainability and stakeholder engagement, with a stronger focus on outcomes and value. Our PMP certification guide covers the application and study plan, and 35 contact hours explains the education requirement.

PRINCE2 Practitioner

PRINCE2 Practitioner tests whether you can apply and tailor the PRINCE2 method to a real project scenario. It is widely requested in the UK, parts of Europe, Australia and the public sector. You need PRINCE2 Foundation or another qualifying credential accepted by PeopleCert first.

APM Project Management Qualification (PMQ)

The PMQ from the UK's Association for Project Management covers the breadth of project management knowledge and suits people with some project experience. It is a common step for UK professionals working towards APM's higher qualifications and Chartered Project Professional status.

PgMP and PfMP

PMI's Program Management Professional (PgMP) and Portfolio Management Professional (PfMP) are for senior practitioners who manage groups of related projects or whole portfolios. Both require extensive experience. If you are unsure which level you work at, read program vs project vs portfolio management.

Entry-level certifications for beginners

If you have little formal project experience, choose a credential with no experience requirement, then aim for an experienced-level certification later.

CAPM (Certified Associate in Project Management)

PMI's entry-level credential. PMI lists a secondary education, 23 hours of project management education and a 150-question, 180-minute exam. It is a natural stepping stone to the PMP, and an active CAPM also satisfies the PMP's education requirement.

PRINCE2 Foundation

Tests your understanding of PRINCE2's principles, practices and processes. There are no prerequisites, and it is often the first credential for project support roles in the UK and Europe.

APM Project Fundamentals Qualification (PFQ)

A UK entry-level qualification covering the fundamentals of project management. It is a good fit for team members and new coordinators who want a recognised starting point.

CompTIA Project+

A vendor-neutral credential that covers the project life cycle, roles, tools and basic Agile concepts. It is popular with IT professionals who manage smaller projects alongside technical work.

Google Project Management Certificate

A self-paced online programme delivered through Coursera. It is a structured introduction for career changers and covers traditional and Agile project management. It is a course certificate rather than a professional certification with an eligibility check, so many learners use it as preparation for the CAPM or a first coordinator role.

PMAI certification

The PMAI (Project Management Artificial Intelligence) certification from the Neural Project Institute is our own programme of six assessed online courses: project management foundations, scope, schedule and cost control, Agile and Scrum, risk and quality, leadership and stakeholder communication, and AI for project managers. Each course gives a verifiable certificate, and the PMAI certificate is awarded when you pass every chapter of all six. It is a certificate of completion, not an accreditation by PMI, PeopleCert or any other organisation, so it works best as an affordable, modern foundation alongside or before a professional certification.

Agile and Scrum certifications

If you work in software, product or any team that runs sprints, an Agile credential may be more useful than a traditional one.

  • Certified ScrumMaster (CSM) from Scrum Alliance: requires a live course with an approved trainer, followed by an online test. Renewal is every two years.
  • Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I) from Scrum.org: an online assessment with no mandatory course, and the credential does not expire.
  • PMI-ACP from PMI: for practitioners with Agile experience. PMI currently lists 21 hours of Agile training plus one of several experience routes, for example two years of Agile experience in the past five years, or one year with a relevant degree or another Agile certification; an active PMP also qualifies. Renewal is 30 PDUs every three years.
  • PRINCE2 Agile: combines PRINCE2 governance with Agile delivery; useful in organisations that already use PRINCE2.
  • SAFe certifications: for organisations scaling Agile across many teams; see Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe).

Our Scrum certification and training guide compares CSM and PSM in detail, and how to learn Agile and get certified suggests a learning path.

AI project management certifications

AI is now part of mainstream project management. PMI's July 2026 PMP exam includes AI in its scenarios, and PMI also offers a dedicated credential, the PMI Certified Professional in Managing AI (PMI-CPMAI), based on the CPMAI methodology for running AI and data projects.

There are two different needs here. If you manage projects that build AI systems, a specialist credential such as PMI-CPMAI focuses on data, model development and deployment phases. If you want to use AI tools to plan, report and communicate better as a project manager, a broader course is usually more practical. The Foresight course in the PMAI certification covers that second need. For the tools themselves, see our guide to AI project management tools.

How to choose the right certification

  1. Check job adverts in your target market. Search for the roles you want in your country and sector, and note which credentials appear most often.
  2. Match the credential to your experience. If you cannot yet meet the PMP's experience rules, start with an entry-level option.
  3. Match it to how your organisation works. Predictive, Agile or hybrid? See project management methodologies if you are unsure.
  4. Consider cost, study time and renewal. Include training, exam, membership and ongoing learning in your budget.
  5. Check the official rules. Eligibility, exam formats and fees change; confirm them with the awarding body before you pay.

Example decisions

  • A career changer with no project experience in the US takes a structured introductory course, then the CAPM, and applies for project coordinator roles. Our project coordinator guide describes the role.
  • A UK local government analyst who supports projects chooses PRINCE2 Foundation because job adverts in that sector name it, then plans for Practitioner.
  • A software team lead with three years of Scrum experience takes the PSM I, then the PMI-ACP to show broader Agile knowledge.
  • An operations manager with a bachelor's degree and four years of leading projects goes straight for the PMP.

Costs, study time and renewal

Costs change often and vary by country, so we do not publish fee figures that may go out of date. Plan for these components:

Study time depends on the credential and your background. Entry-level exams such as PRINCE2 Foundation or PSM I can be prepared for in days or a few weeks; the PMP usually takes a few months of part-time study.

PMI credentials are maintained with professional development units: 60 PDUs every three years for the PMP, 30 for the PMI-ACP and 15 for the CAPM. Our PDU guide explains how to earn them.

Tip

Ask your employer whether they fund certification. Many training budgets cover exam fees and preparation courses, especially when the credential is linked to your development plan.

Are project management certifications worth it?

For most people, yes, if the credential matches the jobs you want. The benefits are practical:

  • More interviews. Many job adverts list a specific certification, and some recruiters filter on it.
  • A shared language. You learn the terms and frameworks used in standards such as the PMBOK Guide and PRINCE2.
  • Structured knowledge. Studying fills gaps you may not notice on the job, such as risk, procurement or earned value.
  • Credibility with clients and sponsors, particularly in consulting, government and regulated sectors.

A certification does not replace experience. Employers still want evidence that you have planned work, managed stakeholders and delivered results. The strongest applications combine a relevant credential with concrete examples.

How to prepare and pass

  1. Download the official exam outline or syllabus and use it as your checklist.
  2. Complete any required training with an approved provider.
  3. Build a study plan with fixed weekly hours and a booked exam date.
  4. Use the official guide or standard as your main reference and add one good prep book.
  5. Take timed practice exams and review every wrong answer.
  6. Relate topics to your own projects; scenario questions reward practical understanding.
  7. Rest the day before the exam and check the technical requirements if you test online.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a certification you are not yet eligible for.
  • Picking the most famous credential when your market prefers another.
  • Relying on outdated exam information; the PMP changed in July 2026.
  • Buying unofficial training that does not count towards eligibility requirements.
  • Forgetting renewal requirements and letting a credential lapse.
  • Relying on certification alone without practical experience.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best project management certification?

For experienced practitioners, the PMP is the most widely recognised globally. For beginners, the CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation or APM PFQ are better starting points. The best choice is the one employers in your market ask for and that matches your experience.

Which project management certification is best for beginners?

The CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, APM PFQ, CompTIA Project+ and Scrum certifications have no experience requirement. Structured courses such as the Google Project Management Certificate or the PMAI are also good starting points.

Can I get a project management certification without experience?

Yes. The CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, APM PFQ, CSM and PSM I do not require project experience. The PMP, PMI-ACP, PgMP and PfMP do.

Is PMP or PRINCE2 the better certification?

Neither is better overall. The PMP is popular globally and especially in North America; PRINCE2 is common in the UK, Europe and the public sector. Many experienced PMs eventually hold both. See PMBOK vs PRINCE2.

What changed in the PMP exam in 2026?

PMI launched a new PMP exam in July 2026. Domain weights moved to People 33%, Process 41% and Business Environment 26%, and scenarios now include AI, sustainability and stakeholder engagement. PMI also lists eligibility based on experience within the last ten years.

How much does a project management certification cost?

It varies by credential, country and whether you are a member of the awarding body. Budget for the exam fee, any required training, study materials and renewal, and check the official website for current fees.

How long does it take to get a project management certification?

Entry-level credentials can take a few days to a few weeks of study. The PMP usually takes a few months of part-time preparation, plus the time to complete the application.

Do project management certifications expire?

Many do. PMI credentials require PDUs every three years (60 for the PMP, 30 for the PMI-ACP, 15 for the CAPM), PRINCE2 certifications must be renewed after three years, and the CSM renews every two years. PSM I does not expire.

Which project management certification is best for Agile?

CSM or PSM I for Scrum team roles, PMI-ACP for experienced Agile practitioners, and SAFe credentials for organisations scaling Agile.

Is there a project management certification for AI?

Yes. PMI offers the PMI-CPMAI for managing AI projects, and the new PMP exam includes AI in its scenarios. Our PMAI certification includes a course on using AI as a project manager.

Is the Google Project Management Certificate worth it?

It is a well-structured introduction for career changers. It is a course certificate rather than a professional certification, so many learners follow it with the CAPM or another recognised credential.

Can I get a project manager job with only a certification?

A certification helps you get interviews, but employers also want evidence that you have coordinated real work. Volunteer projects, coordinator roles and project tasks in your current job all count.

Are online project management certifications respected?

It depends on the awarding body, not the delivery format. PMI, PeopleCert and Scrum.org exams can all be taken online with remote proctoring. Recognised bodies and clear standards matter most.
