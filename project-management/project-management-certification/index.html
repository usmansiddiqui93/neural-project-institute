Project Management Certifications: Which One Should You Choose?

A project management certification is a credential that shows you have met a defined standard of knowledge and, for some, experience. The right one depends on your experience, your industry, your country and whether you work in traditional, Agile or hybrid environments.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Pick a certification that matches your experience, sector and country.
  • PMP suits experienced PMs; CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, Scrum or PMAI suit beginners.
  • Always check current fees and eligibility on the official website.

How to choose

  1. Check what employers in your target sector and country ask for in job adverts.
  2. Match the certification to your experience: entry-level if you are new, PMP if you qualify.
  3. Decide whether you work mainly in traditional, Agile or hybrid settings.
  4. Consider cost, study time and renewal requirements.
  5. Check the official body's website for current eligibility rules and fees.

Example decision

A coordinator with one year of experience, working in a US software company, would usually start with the CAPM or a Scrum certification and aim for the PMP once they have the required experience. A UK public-sector analyst would often choose PRINCE2 Foundation first.

Costs and renewal

Exam fees, membership discounts and training prices change regularly, so always check the awarding body's official website before budgeting. Many certifications require ongoing learning to stay current: PMI credentials use professional development units, for example 60 PDUs every three years for the PMP.

Tip

Ask your employer whether they fund certification. Many training budgets cover exam fees and preparation courses.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a certification you are not yet eligible for.
  • Picking the most famous credential when your market prefers another.
  • Forgetting renewal requirements and letting a credential lapse.
  • Relying on certification alone without practical experience.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best project management certification?

For experienced practitioners the PMP is the most widely recognised globally; for beginners the CAPM or an entry-level course is more suitable.

Which certification is best for beginners?

The CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, a Scrum certification or the PMAI are all options that do not require prior experience.

Is PMP or PRINCE2 better?

The PMP is popular globally and in North America; PRINCE2 is common in the UK, Europe and public sector. See PMBOK vs PRINCE2.

How much do project management certifications cost?

Fees vary and change. Check the official body's website for current exam fees and member discounts, and add the cost of training.

Do certifications expire?

Many do. PMI certifications require PDUs to renew every three years; check each body's renewal rules.

Can I get a PM job with only a certification?

It helps you get interviews, but employers also want evidence that you have coordinated real work.

Do I need a certification to be a project manager?

No, but it is often requested in job adverts and can strengthen your application.

Which certification is best for Agile?

Scrum certifications for team roles, and PMI-ACP for experienced Agile practitioners.

How long does it take to get certified?

Entry-level credentials can take a few weeks of study; the PMP usually takes a few months of preparation.

Are online certifications respected?

It depends on the awarding body. Recognised bodies and clearly defined standards matter more than delivery format.
