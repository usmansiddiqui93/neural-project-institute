35 Contact Hours for PMP: What Counts and the 2026 Rules

The 35 contact hours for the PMP are the 35 hours of formal project management training you must complete before you apply for the exam, unless you hold an active CAPM, which waives the requirement. From 1 December 2026, live instructor-led training only counts if it comes from a PMI Authorized Training Partner or an eligible accredited academic programme, while self-paced courses can still come from any provider. This guide explains what counts, how to count and record your hours, and how the new rule affects training you have already done.

Key takeaways

  • You need at least 35 hours of project management training aligned to the PMP Examination Content Outline, completed before you submit your application. An active CAPM waives the requirement.
  • From 1 December 2026, live instructor-led training only counts if delivered by a PMI Authorized Training Partner (ATP), a China Registered Education Provider or an eligible accredited higher-education programme, including GAC-accredited programmes.
  • Courses completed before 1 December 2026 remain eligible under the old rules, and self-paced courses from any organisation still count if they meet PMI's quality checks.
  • Training hours do not expire and can be combined from several courses. Count only time spent receiving project management instruction.
  • You do not upload certificates when you apply, but PMI asks for proof if you are audited, so keep a record of every course.
  • The training is separate from experience: you also need 3, 4 or 5 years of project leadership within the last 10 years depending on your education, or 2 years with a GAC-accredited degree.

What are the 35 contact hours for the PMP?

The 35 contact hours are the formal project management education PMI requires before you can apply for the Project Management Professional (PMP) exam. One contact hour is one hour of instruction in project management topics aligned to the PMP Examination Content Outline. The hours must be completed before you submit your application, and an active CAPM certification can be used instead.

PMI now usually calls them training hours or the 35-hour training requirement, but candidates, course providers and older PMI handbooks still say "contact hours". They mean the same thing.

The training requirement sits alongside two other parts of PMP eligibility: your education (the highest qualification you hold) and your professional project experience. PMI is clear that training and experience do different jobs. Training shows you have studied project management; experience shows you have actually led and directed project work. One cannot replace the other.

RuleWhat PMI says (checked 25 October 2026)
AmountAt least 35 hours of qualifying project management training
ContentMust align with the current PMP Examination Content Outline (People, Process, Business Environment)
TimingCompleted before you submit your application
Live training from 1 December 2026Only from a PMI Authorized Training Partner, a China Registered Education Provider or an eligible accredited higher-education programme, including GAC programmes
Self-paced trainingCan come from any organisation if it aligns with the current outline
ExpiryTraining hours do not expire
Combining coursesAllowed; hours do not have to come from one course or provider
WaiverAn active CAPM waives the training requirement

PMP eligibility in 2026: where the 35 hours fit

Before you spend money on training, check that you meet the experience requirement, because that is usually what decides when you can apply. PMI's PMP page sets out four routes, all of which need the 35 hours (or an active CAPM).

Highest educationProject experience neededTraining
Secondary diploma (high school, GED or equivalent)5 years (60 months)35 hours or active CAPM
Associate degree or vocational/trade school diploma4 years (48 months)35 hours or active CAPM
Bachelor's degree or higher3 years (36 months)35 hours or active CAPM
Bachelor's or postgraduate degree from a PMI GAC-accredited programme2 years (24 months)35 hours or active CAPM

Three details matter when you add up experience:

  • The 10-year window. All the experience you claim must fall within the last 10 years.
  • Leadership, not just participation. PMI describes qualifying experience as taking responsibility for planning, decisions, delivery and outcomes in a professional setting. Your job title does not have to be "project manager", and predictive, Agile and hybrid projects all count.
  • No double counting. Overlapping project time is counted once, and each project is listed separately on the application.

Worked example: checking experience with overlapping projects

Daniel has a vocational diploma, so he needs 48 months. He plans to apply in January 2027, which means only experience from January 2017 onwards counts.

ProjectDatesMonths in the 10-year windowOverlapMonths counted
A: Warehouse relocationMar 2015 to Feb 20172 (Jan to Feb 2017)None2
B: ERP rolloutJan 2019 to Jun 202018None18
C: New distribution centreApr 2020 to Mar 2022243 months with project B (Apr to Jun 2020)21
D: Fleet tracking systemSep 2023 to Aug 202412None12
Total5653 months

Daniel has 53 unique months against 48 needed, so he meets the experience rule. Project A barely contributes because most of it falls outside the window. His remaining task is the 35 hours, which is what the rest of this guide covers.

Tip

Draft your project descriptions while you plan your training. A one-paragraph summary per project, using terms such as scope, risk and stakeholders, makes the application faster and is easier to support if you are audited.

The 1 December 2026 rule for live training

In September 2026 PMI confirmed a change to which live courses can be used for the 35 hours. In PMI's words: "Beginning 1 December 2026, live training will qualify only when delivered by a PMI Authorized Training Partner or an eligible accredited academic program, including GAC-accredited programs." PMI's PMP page adds that a separate China Registered Education Provider (REP) pathway applies in China.

From that date, live hours count only if your provider is one of these:

  • A PMI Authorized Training Partner (ATP). PMI describes ATPs as organisations it has vetted and approved to deliver official instructor-led PMP training using PMI-developed course content, taught by PMP-certified instructors who have completed a PMI trainer course. PMI publishes a searchable ATP directory on pmi.org.
  • A China Registered Education Provider, for candidates in China.
  • An eligible accredited higher-education programme, including programmes accredited by PMI's Global Accreditation Center (GAC).

Several things have not changed:

  • The requirement is still 35 hours (or an active CAPM).
  • Self-paced, on-demand courses can still come from any organisation, provided they align with the current content outline.
  • Training hours still do not expire, and you can still combine courses.
  • Courses completed before 1 December 2026 remain eligible under the rules in place at the time, even if you apply after the change.

What counts as live and what counts as self-paced

The rule turns on the format of the course, so it helps to be precise. PMI's application asks you to say, for each course, whether it was live instructor-led or self-paced/on-demand.

FormatTypical examplesProvider rule from 1 December 2026
Live instructor-ledClassroom courses, scheduled virtual classes with a trainer, bootcamps, in-house workshops led by a trainerATP, China REP or eligible accredited academic programme only
Self-paced, on-demandRecorded video courses you complete in your own time with an end-of-course assessment, including PMI's own PMP Exam Prep CourseAny organisation, if the course meets PMI's checks

A virtual class with a live trainer at a fixed time is live training, even though you attend online. "Online" does not mean self-paced.

Tip

What matters is the date you finish the course, not the date you book or pay. If you are part-way through a live non-ATP course, finish it before 1 December 2026 and keep the completion certificate, or plan to make up the hours with an ATP or self-paced course.

What counts towards the 35 hours

PMI's guidance is that qualifying training is formal project management instruction aligned to the current PMP Examination Content Outline. In practice, these sources usually qualify:

  • PMI Authorized Training Partner courses, live or self-paced.
  • PMI's own PMP Exam Prep Course, which is self-paced. PMI says the training details can be applied to your application automatically once you finish.
  • Self-paced courses from other organisations that state their hours, include an end-of-course assessment and issue a completion record.
  • College or university courses on project management. If project management was only part of a broader course, count only the hours spent on those topics. From 1 December 2026, live university teaching needs to be part of an eligible accredited programme.
  • Employer-sponsored project management training, if it was completed before 1 December 2026 or, after that date, if it is self-paced or delivered by an approved provider.
  • Older PMP prep courses. PMI says training taken for an earlier version of the exam can still count, but compare it with the current outline so you know what to revise.

The content matters as much as the source. The current outline groups the exam into three domains, so qualifying training typically covers topics such as:

Exam domain (weight)Example topics a qualifying course coversRelated guides
People (33%)Leading teams, conflict, stakeholder engagement, servant leadership, building shared understandingStakeholder management, project manager responsibilities
Process (41%)Scope, schedule, cost, risk, quality, procurement, choosing predictive, Agile or hybrid approachesWork breakdown structure, critical path method, earned value
Business Environment (26%)Governance, benefits and value, compliance, organisational change, project context such as AI and sustainabilityProject charter, Agile vs Waterfall

Agile and hybrid training counts as long as it is project management instruction aligned to the outline. PMI's outline treats Agile and predictive approaches as equally valid, so a good course covers both.

What does not count

These are the most common reasons hours are rejected or have to be removed during an audit:

  • Books, practice exams and other self-study on their own. PMI says these "do not count by themselves". Reading the PMBOK Guide is useful revision, but it is not training.
  • Marketing labels. PMI warns that a course advertised as "35 hours" or "35 PDUs" does not automatically qualify. The course itself has to meet the requirements.
  • Time that is not instruction. Lunch, breaks, registration, networking and travel do not count. Only time spent receiving project management instruction does.
  • Non-project-management content. In a general business, leadership or technical course, only the project management portion counts.
  • Live training from a non-approved provider completed on or after 1 December 2026.
  • Project experience. Leading projects counts towards the experience requirement, not the training hours.
  • Training you plan to do. Hours must be complete before you submit the application.

Be cautious, too, with any provider that guarantees you will pass. PMI specifically advises care with such claims.

How to count contact hours correctly

PMI's rule is simple: one hour of project management instruction equals one training hour. For example, PMI says a college course that meets for three hours a week for 15 weeks gives 45 hours. The worked examples below show how this applies to less tidy situations.

Worked example 1: a one-day classroom course

A course runs from 09:00 to 17:00. It includes a 60-minute lunch and two 15-minute breaks.

ItemTime
Scheduled day8 hours
Less lunch−1 hour
Less two breaks−0.5 hours
Instruction time to claim6.5 hours

A three-day course with the same timetable gives 19.5 hours, not 24. Good providers state the instruction hours on the certificate; if yours shows only dates, work out the figure from the timetable and keep the timetable with your records.

Worked example 2: a university module with partial project management content

A module called Operations and Project Management meets for three hours a week for 12 weeks (36 hours). The syllabus shows that five of the 12 weeks covered project planning, scheduling, risk and project governance; the rest covered supply chain and operations.

CalculationResult
Total module time3 hours × 12 weeks = 36 hours
Weeks on project management5
Hours to claim3 hours × 5 weeks = 15 hours

Keep the syllabus, because it is the document that shows which weeks were project management. A transcript alone usually shows only the module title.

Worked example 3: combining three sources to reach 35

Priya wants to apply in February 2027. She lists the training she already has, then fills the gap.

SourceFormatCompletedCounts?Hours
Employer's two-day project management course (non-ATP)LiveSeptember 2026Yes, completed before 1 December 202613
University module (project management weeks only)Live, accredited university2021Yes, training does not expire15
Self-paced online PMP course with end-of-course assessmentSelf-pacedJanuary 2027Yes, any provider for self-paced10
Total38 hours

Priya now has 38 hours, comfortably above 35. Had she filled the gap with a live weekend workshop from a non-ATP provider in January 2027, those hours would not count. Building in a small margin above 35 protects you if an auditor decides part of a course was not project management.

Tip

Use a simple training plan or project tracker to log each course, its format, completion date, hours and where you saved the evidence.

How to get your 35 contact hours: step by step

If you are starting from scratch or topping up existing training, follow this sequence.

  1. Confirm you meet the experience rule first. Count your unique months of project leadership within the last 10 years against the 36, 48, 60 or 24 months your education requires. There is no point finishing training a year before you are eligible to apply, although the hours will not expire.
  2. Check whether you hold an active CAPM. If you do, you do not need the 35 hours. If it has lapsed into its suspension period, renew it before you apply.
  3. Audit the training you already have. List every project management course, university module and employer programme you have completed, with the hours of instruction and any evidence. Remember that live courses completed before 1 December 2026 keep their eligibility.
  4. Work out the gap. Subtract your qualifying hours from 35, then add a small margin.
  5. Choose a format for the remaining hours. For live training after 1 December 2026, choose a PMI Authorized Training Partner (use PMI's directory to check) or an eligible accredited programme. For self-paced training, check the course against PMI's criteria in the next section.
  6. Complete the course, including the final assessment, and download the certificate. Save the course outline or syllabus with it.
  7. Record each course on your application. Enter the provider, course title, hours and format, then submit once the total reaches 35. Keep all evidence until well after you pass, in case of audit.

Choosing a course format

Each route has different strengths. The right choice depends on how you learn, your budget, your deadline and whether your employer is paying. The table below is our summary; PMI's rules are as stated earlier.

FormatCounts after 1 December 2026?StrengthsWatch out for
ATP live course (classroom or virtual)YesPMI-developed content, PMP-certified instructors, structure and accountability, chance to ask questionsFixed dates; usually the most expensive option
PMI PMP Exam Prep Course (self-paced)YesBuilt by PMI around the outline; PMI says completion details can be applied to your application automaticallyNeeds self-discipline
Other self-paced online courseYes, if it meets PMI's checksFlexible and often low cost; wide choiceQuality varies; check the hours, assessment and certificate
Accredited university or GAC programmeYes, for eligible programmesDeep learning; a GAC degree also reduces the experience requirement to two yearsLong and costly if taken only for the hours

Questions to ask a provider before you pay

PMI's guidance on self-paced courses translates into a short checklist. A reputable provider can answer every question in writing.

  1. Does the course give formal project management instruction, not just practice questions?
  2. Is it aligned to the current PMP Examination Content Outline (the 2026 version, with People, Process and Business Environment domains)?
  3. Does it state the number of training hours clearly?
  4. Does it include an end-of-course assessment?
  5. Will I receive a certificate or completion record showing my name, the provider, the course title, the hours and the completion date?
  6. For live courses after 1 December 2026: are you a PMI Authorized Training Partner? Check the answer in PMI's ATP directory rather than relying on a logo.

Can you get the 35 hours free?

Sometimes. The most common no-cost routes are training you have already completed (old university modules or employer courses), employer-funded courses, and free self-paced courses that meet every point on the checklist above. Free webinars and chapter talks are useful for learning and for PDUs once certified, but they are rarely formal, assessed courses, so do not rely on them for the 35 hours. If budget is tight, compare the cost of a self-paced course with the value of having the hours properly documented.

Using the CAPM instead of 35 hours

PMI states that an active Certified Associate in Project Management (CAPM) certification waives the PMP training requirement. This is one reason many people take the CAPM early in their career and the PMP later.

  • Active CAPM: waives the 35 hours.
  • Expired CAPM: does not waive them.
  • CAPM in its suspension period: renew it before you apply if you want to use the waiver.
  • You still need the experience. The CAPM replaces the training only, not the 36 to 60 months of project leadership.

The CAPM itself needs 23 hours of project management education and no project experience. Our CAPM certification guide covers the exam and study plan.

Worked example: a CAPM holder moving to the PMP

Sam passed the CAPM in March 2024, holds a bachelor's degree and has led projects since January 2023. By March 2026 he has 38 months of qualifying experience, above the 36 he needs. His CAPM is active until March 2027, so he applies in late 2026, selects the CAPM option for training and needs no further course. Had he waited until his CAPM lapsed, he would have had to renew it or complete 35 hours first.

Tip

Even with the waiver, consider a PMP prep course. The CAPM covers fundamentals; the PMP tests judgement in scenario questions, so most candidates still benefit from structured preparation.

Recording training on your application and passing an audit

PMI's online application asks you to list each course with its provider, title and number of hours, and to say whether it was live instructor-led or self-paced. You do not upload certificates or transcripts when you submit. PMI selects some applications for audit, and all training is subject to audit, so keep evidence for every course you list.

Evidence to keepBest forWhat it should show
Completion certificateCourses from training providers and ATPsYour name, provider, course title, hours, completion date
Transcript plus syllabusUniversity and college coursesThe module, dates and which topics were project management
Employer training recordIn-house programmesCourse name, dates, hours and the trainer or provider
Course description or timetableAny course where the certificate lacks hoursInstruction time, so you can justify the hours claimed

If you are audited, the process described on PMI's site and in our PMI guide works like this:

  1. You are told your application has been selected before you pay the exam fee.
  2. You have 90 days to send copies of your education certificate, signed confirmation of your project experience and your training certificates.
  3. PMI reviews the documents, which it says takes about five to seven business days once everything is received.
  4. If the audit is approved, you pay the exam fee and schedule your exam.

Audits are far easier when your records match what you entered. Store everything in one folder, named by course, and keep it until your certification is well established.

Contact hours vs PDUs

Contact hours and PDUs are both measured in hours of learning, which is why they are often confused. They serve different purposes at different stages.

Contact (training) hoursPDUs
PurposeQualify you to apply for the examMaintain your certification after you pass
When earnedBefore you submit your applicationDuring each three-year certification cycle
How many35 for the PMP; 23 for the CAPM60 every three years for the PMP; 15 for the CAPM
What countsFormal training aligned to the exam content outlineEducation plus giving back to the profession, split across the Talent Triangle
ExpiryDo not expireCount only towards the cycle in which they are earned (with limited carry-over)

A course marketed as "35 PDUs" may or may not meet the training requirement; judge it against PMI's criteria.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Booking a live course from a non-approved provider that finishes after 1 December 2026. Check ATP status in PMI's directory before you pay.
  • Claiming scheduled time instead of instruction time. Remove breaks, lunch and admin from your count.
  • Claiming a whole module when only part was project management. Keep the syllabus and claim only the relevant hours.
  • Relying on an expired CAPM. Only an active CAPM waives the requirement.
  • Applying before the training is finished. All 35 hours must be complete when you submit.
  • Assuming a "35 hours" label means the course qualifies. PMI says it does not automatically.
  • Keeping no evidence. You do not upload certificates, but you will need them if audited.
  • Treating the 35 hours as exam preparation. Completing the hours does not mean you are ready to pass. Plan practice questions and revision on top.
Tip

Collect experience evidence at the same time. A project charter, risk register or lessons learned log you produced helps you describe your projects accurately and supports an audit.

After the 35 hours: next steps

Once your training and experience are in place, the rest of the process moves quickly:

  • Submit your application. Complete the education, experience and training sections and submit.
  • Pay and schedule. After approval, pay the exam fee. PMI says you then have one year from approval to take the exam, with up to three attempts in that year.
  • Know the exam. The current exam has 180 questions in 240 minutes, with two 10-minute breaks, weighted People 33%, Process 41% and Business Environment 26%. You can sit it at a Pearson VUE test centre or online.
  • Plan for renewal. After you pass, earn 60 PDUs every three years to keep the PMP active.

Frequently asked questions

What are the 35 contact hours for the PMP?

They are the 35 hours of formal project management training PMI requires before you can apply for the PMP exam. The training must align with the current PMP Examination Content Outline and be completed before you submit your application. An active CAPM waives the requirement.

Do the 35 contact hours have to come from a PMI Authorized Training Partner?

Only for live training completed from 1 December 2026, which must come from an ATP, a China Registered Education Provider or an eligible accredited higher-education programme, including GAC programmes. Self-paced courses can come from any organisation if they align with the current outline.

Does training I completed before 1 December 2026 still count?

Yes. PMI says courses completed before 1 December 2026 remain eligible under the rules in place at the time, even if you apply after the change. Keep the completion certificate as evidence.

What if my live course finishes after 1 December 2026?

PMI's protection applies to courses completed before 1 December 2026, so a live course from a non-approved provider that finishes on or after that date is unlikely to count. Finish before the date, choose an ATP, or make up the hours with a qualifying self-paced course.

Do PMP contact hours expire?

No. PMI says qualifying project management training does not expire, so a course from several years ago can still count. Check it against the current outline so you know which topics to revise.

Can I get the 35 contact hours online?

Yes. Self-paced online courses from any provider count if they give formal instruction aligned to the outline, state their hours, include an end-of-course assessment and issue a completion record. Live virtual classes count too, but from 1 December 2026 only from an approved provider.

Can I get the 35 contact hours for free?

Sometimes, through training you have already completed, employer-funded courses or free self-paced courses that meet PMI's criteria. Webinars and chapter talks rarely qualify because they are not formal, assessed courses.

Does my degree count towards the 35 hours?

Project management courses within a degree can count, but only the hours spent on project management topics. A degree does not count as 35 hours automatically. A degree from a GAC-accredited programme also reduces the experience requirement to two years.

Does the CAPM replace the 35 contact hours?

An active CAPM waives the PMP training requirement. An expired CAPM does not, and a CAPM in its suspension period must be renewed first. You still need the project experience.

Can I combine several courses to reach 35 hours?

Yes. PMI says training does not have to come from one course or provider. List each course separately on the application and keep evidence for each.

Do I need to upload certificates with my application?

No. You list each course's provider, title, hours and format, but you only send certificates or transcripts if your application is selected for audit.

Can project experience replace the 35 hours?

No. Training and experience are separate requirements. Only an active CAPM can replace the training hours.

Are contact hours the same as PDUs?

No. Contact hours qualify you to apply for the exam; PDUs maintain your certification afterwards. The PMP needs 60 PDUs every three years.

Does Agile training count towards the 35 hours?

Yes, if it is formal project management instruction aligned to the current PMP Examination Content Outline, which covers predictive, Agile and hybrid approaches.
