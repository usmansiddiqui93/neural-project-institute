Critical Success Factors in Project Management

Critical success factors (CSFs) are the few conditions that must go right for a project to succeed. Identifying them early focuses attention on what matters most.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 19 September 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • CSFs are the few conditions that must go right.
  • Link each CSF to a KPI.
  • Keep the list short and review it regularly.

Common critical success factors

  • Clear, agreed objectives.
  • Active, committed sponsorship.
  • Engaged stakeholders and users.
  • Realistic plans and estimates.
  • Competent, stable team.
  • Effective communication.
  • Proactive risk management.
  • Adequate funding and resources.

CSFs vs KPIs

CSFKPI
What it isA condition for successA measure of performance
ExampleExecutive sponsorshipSponsor attends 90% of steering meetings

How to identify CSFs

  1. Review objectives and stakeholder expectations.
  2. Ask: what must go right for us to succeed?
  3. Learn from similar past projects.
  4. Limit the list to three to five.
  5. Link each CSF to a measurable indicator.
  6. Review them regularly.

Frequently asked questions

What are critical success factors?

The few conditions that must be met for a project to succeed.

How many CSFs should a project have?

Three to five is typical.

Who identifies CSFs?

The project manager with the sponsor and key stakeholders.

Are CSFs the same as objectives?

No. Objectives are results; CSFs are conditions that enable them.

How are CSFs measured?

Through KPIs linked to each factor.

Do missing critical success factors explain why projects fail?

Often, yes. Projects commonly fail because a critical success factor, such as sponsorship or clear objectives, is missing.

Is sponsorship a CSF?

It is one of the most commonly cited CSFs.

When should CSFs be defined?

During initiation and planning.

Can CSFs change?

Yes, if the project's context changes significantly.

Where are CSFs documented?

In the charter or project management plan.
