Project Coordinator: Role, Duties, Skills and Career Path

A project coordinator keeps a project organised by handling its scheduling, tracking, documentation and communication, so the project manager and team can focus on delivery. It is one of the most common ways into a project management career. This guide covers what coordinators do in each phase, the skills employers look for, worked examples of a coordinator's work, a sample job description, how to get your first role and how to step up to project manager.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 4 October 2026
  • Last updated 4 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Project coordinators run the administration and communication that keep projects moving: schedules, trackers, logs, meetings, reports and follow-ups.
  • The project manager is accountable for the outcome; the coordinator supports delivery and makes few decisions beyond administrative ones.
  • Organisation, clear writing, persistence with follow-ups and confidence with spreadsheets and work management tools matter more to employers than a specific degree.
  • You can get a coordinator job without direct project experience by showing transferable work, such as organising events, rotas, moves or system roll-outs.
  • The CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation and PMAI are good first certifications; the PMP comes later, once you have led projects.
  • Coordinators who learn why each process exists, and ask to own small pieces of work, move fastest into assistant PM and project manager roles.

What is a project coordinator?

A project coordinator is a member of the project team who organises the day-to-day administration of a project: maintaining the schedule and trackers, arranging meetings and recording actions, keeping documents and logs up to date, preparing reports and acting as a first point of contact. They work under the project manager, who remains accountable for the project's results.

The title varies by organisation and country. Similar roles are called project administrator, project support officer, project assistant or PMO coordinator. Some coordinators support one large project; others support several smaller ones or sit in a project management office serving many project managers. Judge a job by its duties rather than its title.

To see the role in context, compare it with the full set of project manager roles and responsibilities.

What does a project coordinator do?

Job descriptions differ by sector, but most coordinator work falls into six groups.

Scheduling and planning support

  • Entering tasks, dates and owners into the project schedule and keeping it current.
  • Flagging tasks that are late or at risk to the project manager.
  • Booking resources, rooms, equipment and supplier visits.

Tracking and reporting

  • Maintaining the action tracker and chasing owners before deadlines.
  • Collecting updates from workstreams and drafting the project status report.
  • Keeping dashboards in the project tool accurate.

Documentation and logs

  • Managing document storage, version control and naming conventions.
  • Updating the RAID log of risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies, and the change log.
  • Preparing templates and packs for governance meetings.

Communication and meetings

  • Scheduling meetings, sending agendas, taking notes and circulating actions.
  • Sending updates in line with the communication plan.
  • Answering routine questions from the team and stakeholders.

Finance and procurement administration

  • Raising purchase orders and tracking invoices against the budget.
  • Collecting timesheets and expense claims.
  • Keeping supplier contact lists and contract dates.

Onboarding and logistics

  • Arranging access, equipment and inductions for new team members.
  • Organising workshops, site visits, training sessions and travel.

Project coordinator responsibilities by phase

The balance of work shifts as the project moves through its life cycle. This table shows typical coordinator tasks in each phase.

PhaseTypical coordinator tasks
InitiationSet up the project folder and tool workspace; arrange the kick-off; draft the stakeholder contact list
PlanningEnter tasks into the schedule; book planning workshops; format the project plan and logs; collect estimates from team leads
ExecutionRun the action tracker; arrange meetings; raise purchase orders; onboard new team members; chase deliverables
Monitoring and controllingCollect progress updates; draft status reports; update the RAID and change logs; prepare governance packs
ClosureCollect sign-offs; gather handover documents; organise the lessons learned session; archive records

Essential project coordinator skills

Employers hire coordinators mainly for reliability. The skills below are the ones that show up most in job adverts and interviews, with practical ways to build each. For the wider skill set, see project management skills.

SkillWhat it looks like in the roleHow to build it
OrganisationNothing slips through the gaps; files and trackers are easy to findRun your own work from a single task list for a month
Attention to detailDates, names and figures in reports are right first timeProofread every report against its source before sending
Written communicationShort, clear updates and minutes with actions, owners and datesRewrite a long email as five bullet points
Follow-upPolite but persistent chasing before deadlines, not afterSet reminders two days before each action is due
SpreadsheetsFilters, sorting, simple formulas and conditional formattingBuild a budget tracker that totals spend by category
Work management toolsComfortable setting up tasks, views and dashboardsUse the free plan of a mainstream tool for a personal project
Basic PM knowledgeUnderstands schedules, risks, budgets and change controlStudy for an entry-level certification
Problem spottingNotices early warning signs and raises themIn each meeting, write down one thing that could go wrong
Tip

Learn the reason behind each process, not only how to fill in the form. A coordinator who knows why the risk log exists asks better questions, and that is what gets noticed.

Worked examples of a coordinator's work

Duty lists say what coordinators do; these examples show what good work looks like. All three are illustrative and come from the same office relocation project.

Example 1: an action tracker

ActionOwnerDueStatus
Send revised floor plan to facilitiesJ. Patel12 MarchDone
Confirm furniture delivery dateSupplier14 MarchChased twice
Book IT for desk set-upCoordinator15 MarchOpen
Agree parking allocationHR lead18 MarchOpen

A simple tracker like this, reviewed at every meeting, stops actions being forgotten and gives the project manager early warning. The project tracker template gives you a ready-made version with priorities and progress.

Example 2: a weekly status update

Overall status: Amber. The move date of 4 April is still achievable, but furniture delivery is at risk.

  • Done this week: floor plan approved; IT cabling survey complete; staff briefing sent.
  • Next week: confirm furniture delivery; book removal crew; start desk allocation.
  • Risk: furniture supplier has not confirmed 28 March delivery. If it slips beyond 1 April, the move date is at risk. Action: project manager to escalate to the supplier's account manager by 15 March.
  • Decision needed: approve $2,400 for weekend IT installation (within contingency).

Notice the structure: status first, then progress, then risks with an action and owner, then any decision needed. Project managers value a coordinator who can draft this without being asked.

Example 3: a meeting follow-up

Within an hour of a meeting, send a short note with three headings: decisions made, actions (each with an owner and date) and items carried forward. Keep it under 150 words. A meeting notes template makes this quick and consistent.

Example 4: handling a slipping task, step by step

On 13 March the furniture supplier still had not confirmed delivery. A good coordinator handles this as a short, repeatable process rather than a string of reminders:

  1. Check the facts. Confirm the due date, the agreed delivery date in the order and who last spoke to the supplier.
  2. Assess the impact. Using the schedule, work out what depends on the task: here, IT installation and the move date of 4 April.
  3. Chase with a specific request. Ask the supplier for a confirmed date by a set time, for example by noon on 14 March.
  4. Record it. Update the tracker status to Chased and add the risk to the RAID log with its impact.
  5. Escalate with options. If there is no answer by the deadline, brief the project manager with the facts, the impact and two or three options, such as a temporary furniture hire or moving the IT work first.
  6. Close the loop. Once a date is confirmed, update the schedule, tell the teams affected and close or downgrade the risk.

The project manager makes the decision; the coordinator makes sure it is made early, with the right information. Our guide to project scheduling explains how dependencies carry delays through a plan.

Project coordinator job description (sample)

Hiring managers can adapt this outline, and candidates can use it to check how their experience matches a typical advert.

Role summary. We are looking for a project coordinator to support our project managers in delivering projects on time and within budget. You will maintain schedules and trackers, coordinate meetings and communications, keep project documents in order and prepare progress reports.

Key responsibilities

  • Maintain project schedules, action trackers and RAID logs in our work management tool.
  • Organise meetings, prepare agendas, take notes and follow up actions.
  • Collect progress updates and draft weekly status reports.
  • Raise purchase orders, track invoices and update the budget tracker.
  • Manage project documentation, version control and archiving.
  • Act as the first point of contact for team and stakeholder queries.

Skills and experience

  • Strong organisation and attention to detail.
  • Clear written and spoken communication.
  • Confidence with spreadsheets and project management software.
  • Some experience of coordinating work, events or administration (project experience desirable).
  • An entry-level project management certification such as the CAPM (desirable).

How to become a project coordinator

Most coordinators come from administration, customer service, operations, events, teaching or graduate roles. These steps work whether you are starting out or changing careers.

  1. Learn the fundamentals. Understand the project phases, schedules, risks, budgets and status reporting.
  2. List your transferable experience. Anything you organised with a goal, a deadline and other people counts.
  3. Get practical with a tool and templates. Set up a small project in a free plan of a work management tool, with a schedule, tracker and status report.
  4. Take an entry-level certification. The CAPM or PMAI shows commitment and gives you the vocabulary.
  5. Rewrite your CV around outcomes, using project language: scope, deadline, stakeholders, budget, result.
  6. Look inside your organisation first. Internal moves are often easier than external applications because people already know your reliability.
  7. Apply widely, including roles titled project administrator, project support officer and PMO assistant.

Qualifications and certifications

Many coordinator roles do not require a degree; organisation, communication and tool skills often matter more. Some employers, especially in engineering, construction, public sector and healthcare, do ask for a degree or sector knowledge.

A certification is not mandatory, but it helps you pass CV screening when you have little direct experience.

CertificationWho it suitsKey facts
CAPM (PMI)New coordinators who want a widely recognised credentialNeeds a secondary diploma and 23 hours of project management education; no experience required
PRINCE2 FoundationCoordinators in the UK, Europe, Australia and organisations that use PRINCE2Tests knowledge of the PRINCE2 method; no prerequisites

Project coordinator interview questions

Interviewers want evidence that you are organised, reliable and calm when things change. Prepare a short example for each of these.

QuestionWhat a good answer shows
Tell me about a time you kept several tasks on track at once.How you prioritise and what system you use
How do you chase someone who keeps missing deadlines?Persistence with tact, escalation when needed
Describe a mistake you made in a report or schedule.Honesty, how you fixed it and what you changed
How would you set up a tracker for a new project?Practical tool knowledge: owners, dates, status, filters
What would you do if two managers gave you conflicting priorities?Clarifying with the project manager instead of guessing
What does a RAID log contain?Basic project management knowledge
How do you keep stakeholders informed?Regular, concise updates matched to the audience

Use the situation, task, action and result structure, and finish each answer with a measurable result where you can.

Project coordinator salary

Pay varies widely, so we do not quote a single figure. The main factors are:

  • Country and city: large cities usually pay more, with higher living costs.
  • Sector: construction, engineering, IT, pharmaceuticals and finance often pay more than non-profit or education.
  • Scope: coordinating several projects, a large budget or external clients usually raises pay.
  • Experience and certifications: a CAPM or PRINCE2 credential can help at the margin; proven experience matters more.
  • Contract type: contract and temporary roles often pay a higher day rate without employment benefits.

For reliable figures, compare several current job adverts for the same title and location, national salary statistics and PMI's project management salary survey. Treat single-source online averages with caution because their samples and definitions differ.

From project coordinator to project manager

Many project managers started as coordinators. The move usually takes a deliberate plan rather than waiting to be noticed.

  1. Months 1–3: master your current duties so they run smoothly, and learn why each process exists.
  2. Months 3–6: offer to draft the status report and the risk review; shadow the project manager in steering meetings.
  3. Months 6–9: ask to own a small workstream or project end to end, with your own plan, budget and stakeholders.
  4. Months 9–12: take an entry-level certification if you have not already, and keep a log of every project you lead.
  5. Then: apply for assistant or junior project manager roles, internally first.
StageTypical roleWhat changes
1Project administrator or coordinatorSupporting projects; learning the processes
2Assistant or junior project managerOwning workstreams and small projects
3Project managerAccountable for whole projects
4Senior project managerLarger, riskier projects and mentoring others
5Programme manager, portfolio manager or head of PMOSeveral projects and organisational strategy

Is project coordinator a good career?

For many people it is a strong start. You see how projects work from the inside, meet people across the organisation and build a record that leads to more senior roles. The trade-offs are real too: much of the work is routine, you have responsibility without much authority, and in some organisations the role is treated as purely administrative. If you want to progress, choose employers that give coordinators ownership of small pieces of work and that have a visible path to project manager.

Your coordinator years can help with a future PMP application, which requires experience leading projects within the last 10 years (three to five years depending on education) plus 35 contact hours or an active CAPM. Time spent purely on administration may not count, so record the project work you led or directed, such as owning a workstream.

Tip

Offer to draft the status report. Writing it forces you to understand the whole project, and project managers are usually glad of the help.

Frequently asked questions

What does a project coordinator do?

A project coordinator handles a project's scheduling, tracking, documentation and communication: maintaining trackers and logs, arranging meetings and following up actions, drafting reports and acting as a first point of contact, so the project manager and team can focus on delivery.

Is project coordinator an entry-level job?

Often, yes. It is one of the most common entry-level roles in project management, although some employers ask for a year or two of administrative or coordination experience.

Can I become a project coordinator with no experience?

Yes, if you can show transferable experience such as organising events, managing rotas, supporting an office move or coordinating volunteers. Describe it in project terms and consider an entry-level certification.

Do I need a degree to be a project coordinator?

Not always. Many employers value organisation, communication and tool skills more than a degree, though some sectors ask for one.

What certification is good for a project coordinator?

The CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation and PMAI are all good starting points. The CAPM needs 23 hours of project management education and no experience.

What is the difference between a project coordinator and a project manager?

The project manager is accountable for the project's outcome and makes scope, schedule, cost and risk decisions. The coordinator supports delivery through administration, tracking and communication.

What is the difference between a project coordinator and a project administrator?

The titles overlap. Administrators usually focus on documents, bookings and finance admin; coordinators also track progress, chase actions and communicate with the team.

Is project coordinator a stressful job?

It can be busy, especially near deadlines, because coordinators juggle many small tasks and people. Good systems, early reminders and clear priorities from the project manager make it much more manageable.

How much does a project coordinator earn?

It depends on country, city, sector, scope and experience. Compare several current job adverts for your location, national salary statistics and PMI's salary survey rather than relying on a single online average.

What software do project coordinators use?

Work management tools such as Wrike, monday.com, Asana or Smartsheet, plus spreadsheets, email, chat and video calls.

How long do people stay project coordinators?

It varies. Many move to assistant or junior project manager roles once they can show they have led small projects or workstreams.

Does project coordinator experience count towards the PMP?

It can, but only the time you spent leading and directing project work, such as owning a workstream. Purely administrative time may not count, so keep a detailed log.

What makes a great project coordinator?

Reliability, attention to detail, persistent but polite follow-up, clear writing and the habit of spotting problems early and raising them.

Can a project coordinator become a project manager?

Yes. It is one of the most common routes. Ask to own small projects, learn the reasons behind each process and build an experience log.
