Project Coordinator: Role, Responsibilities and Career Path

A project coordinator handles the administration and communication that keep a project running: scheduling meetings, tracking tasks, maintaining documents and keeping people informed. It is one of the most common entry points into a project management career.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 4 October 2026
  • 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Project coordinators keep projects organised through scheduling, tracking and communication.
  • It is a common and effective entry route into project management.
  • Learning why processes exist helps coordinators step up to PM roles.

Core responsibilities

  • Scheduling meetings, taking notes and following up actions.
  • Tracking tasks and deadlines in the project tool.
  • Maintaining project documents, logs and file storage.
  • Preparing reports and dashboards for the project manager.
  • Processing purchase orders, timesheets and invoices.
  • Acting as a first point of contact for team questions.

Coordinator vs project manager

Project coordinatorProject manager
AccountabilitySupports deliveryAccountable for outcomes
PlanningMaintains the planCreates and owns the plan
DecisionsFew, mostly administrativeScope, schedule, cost and risk decisions
StakeholdersDay-to-day communicationNegotiation and escalation

Essential skills

  • Organisation and attention to detail.
  • Clear written communication.
  • Confidence with spreadsheets and project management software.
  • Basic understanding of schedules, risks and budgets.
  • Following up politely but persistently.

Example: a coordinator's action tracker

ActionOwnerDueStatus
Send revised floor plan to facilitiesJ. Patel12 MarchDone
Confirm furniture delivery dateSupplier14 MarchChased twice
Book IT for desk setupCoordinator15 MarchOpen

A simple, current tracker like this, reviewed in each meeting, prevents actions being forgotten and gives the PM early warning of delays.

Growing from coordinator to project manager

  1. Learn why each document and process exists, not just how to fill it in.
  2. Ask to run small projects or workstreams.
  3. Take an entry-level certification such as the CAPM or PMAI.
  4. Step up to assistant project manager or junior PM.
  5. Track your experience for a future PMP.
Tip

Offer to draft the status report. Writing it forces you to understand the whole project, and PMs are usually glad of the help.

Frequently asked questions

What does a project coordinator do?

Handles scheduling, documentation, tracking and communication so the project manager and team can focus on delivery.

Is project coordinator an entry-level job?

Yes, it is one of the most common entry-level roles in project management.

Do I need a degree to be a project coordinator?

Not always. Organisation, communication and tool skills often matter more, though some employers ask for a degree.

What certification is good for a project coordinator?

The CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation or an entry-level course such as the PMAI.

What is the difference between a project coordinator and a project administrator?

Titles overlap. Administrators usually focus purely on admin; coordinators also track progress and communicate with the team.

How long do people stay project coordinators?

It varies; many move to assistant or junior PM roles once they can show they have led small projects.

What software do project coordinators use?

Work management tools such as Wrike, Asana or Smartsheet, plus spreadsheets, email and video calls.

How much does a project coordinator earn?

It depends on country, sector and employer. Check current job adverts and salary surveys for reliable figures.

What makes a great project coordinator?

Reliability, attention to detail, persistence with follow-ups and the ability to spot problems early.

Can a project coordinator become a project manager?

Yes. It is one of the most common routes; see how to become a project manager.
