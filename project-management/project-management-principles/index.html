Principles of Project Management: 12 Principles Explained

Methods and tools change, but good project management rests on a stable set of principles. Recent professional standards, including the seventh edition of PMI's PMBOK Guide, express these as twelve principles that apply to every approach, from Waterfall to Agile.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Principles guide decisions where processes don't.
  • Twelve widely used principles cover stewardship, value, tailoring, risk and change.
  • They apply across predictive, Agile and hybrid approaches.

The twelve principles

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Be a diligent, respectful and caring stewardAct with integrity and care for people, resources and the environment
Create a collaborative team environmentClear roles, shared agreements and mutual support
Effectively engage with stakeholdersInvolve stakeholders early and often
Focus on valueJudge success by the value delivered, not just outputs
Recognise and respond to system interactionsSee the project as part of a wider system
Demonstrate leadership behavioursMotivate, influence and support others
Tailor based on contextAdapt the approach to the project, team and organisation
Build quality into processes and deliverablesPrevent defects rather than inspect them out
Navigate complexityUse iteration and feedback when outcomes are uncertain
Optimise risk responsesAddress threats and opportunities proportionately
Embrace adaptability and resiliencyPlan for change and recover from setbacks
Enable change to achieve the envisioned future stateHelp people adopt new ways of working

The list follows the principles in the PMBOK Guide, seventh edition (2021), in our own words. Check PMI for the current edition.

Why principles matter

Principles guide decisions when no process covers the situation. They also make it easier to work across methods: a Scrum team and a PRINCE2 project board can share the same principles even if their practices differ.

Applying them

  • Use them as a checklist when tailoring your approach.
  • Review them in lessons-learned sessions.
  • Discuss them with stakeholders to set shared expectations.

Frequently asked questions

How many principles of project management are there?

The PMBOK Guide, seventh edition, lists twelve. Other frameworks, such as PRINCE2, have their own seven principles.

Do Agile and traditional projects share the same principles?

Largely yes. Value, collaboration, adaptability and quality apply to every approach.
