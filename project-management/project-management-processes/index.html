Project Management Processes: Process Groups and Knowledge Areas

Traditional project management describes the work as processes organised in two ways: by when they happen (process groups) and by what they cover (knowledge areas). Understanding both helps you see what a complete project plan needs.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Five process groups describe when work happens.
  • Ten knowledge areas describe what must be managed.
  • Tailor processes to the project's size and risk.

The five process groups

Process groupPurpose
InitiatingDefine a new project or phase and obtain authorisation
PlanningEstablish scope, objectives and the course of action
ExecutingComplete the work defined in the plan
Monitoring and controllingTrack, review and regulate progress and performance
ClosingFormally complete the project or phase

Process groups are not phases: they repeat within each phase and overlap. See project management phases.

The ten knowledge areas

Knowledge areaCovers
IntegrationCoordinating all parts of the project; see integration management
ScopeWhat is and isn't included; see scope
ScheduleTiming and sequencing; see scheduling
CostEstimates, budgets and control; see cost management
QualityMeeting requirements; see quality management
ResourcesPeople, equipment and materials
CommunicationsInformation flow; see communication management
RiskUncertainty; see risk management
ProcurementBuying from suppliers; see procurement management
StakeholdersEngaging people affected; see stakeholder management

This structure was the backbone of the PMBOK Guide up to its sixth edition. The seventh edition moved to principles and performance domains, but the knowledge areas remain a practical checklist.

Tailoring processes

Not every project needs every process at the same depth. A two-week internal project may need a one-page plan; a multi-year infrastructure programme needs formal processes for each area. Tailor based on size, risk, complexity and regulation.

Frequently asked questions

How many project management processes are there?

The PMBOK Guide, sixth edition, defined 49 processes across five process groups and ten knowledge areas.

Are process groups the same as phases?

No. Phases divide the project over time; process groups recur within each phase.
