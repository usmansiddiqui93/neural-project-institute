Project Governance: Framework, Roles and Structure Explained

Project governance is the framework of roles, responsibilities, decision rights and processes that directs and oversees a project. It sets who decides what, how decisions are made, and how the project is held accountable to the organisation that funds it.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Governance defines who decides what, and how the project is held to account.
  • Typical structures include a sponsor, board, PM, PMO and assurance.
  • Tolerances and escalation routes make management by exception work.
  • Good governance is proportionate: enough control without slowing everything down.

Governance vs project management

Project governanceProject management
PurposeDirect and overseePlan and deliver
Key questionAre we doing the right project, the right way, and is it still worth it?How do we deliver the agreed outcomes?
WhoSponsor, steering committee or project board, PMOProject manager and team
Typical decisionsApprove funding, accept major risks, approve stage progression, close or stop the projectAssign work, manage day-to-day issues, make changes within tolerance

Typical governance structure

  • Sponsor: owns the business case and is ultimately accountable for the project's success.
  • Steering committee or project board: senior decision-making group. In PRINCE2 the project board has three roles: executive, senior user and senior supplier.
  • Project manager: runs the project day to day within delegated authority; see project manager roles.
  • Project management office (PMO): sets standards, supports reporting and may provide assurance.
  • Assurance: independent checks on whether the project is being run properly, sometimes by internal audit or a review team.
  • Change control board: decides on significant changes; see change control.

Governance framework template

ElementExample content
Decision rightsSponsor approves stage progression; PM approves changes under £5,000
TolerancesTime ±2 weeks per stage; cost ±5%; scope changes need board approval
MeetingsBoard monthly and at stage gates; team weekly
ReportingWeekly status to sponsor; monthly highlight to board; exception report within 2 days of forecast breach
EscalationIssue owner → PM → sponsor → board
AssurancePMO health check at the end of each stage
DocumentsCharter, business case, plan, RAID log, decision log

Worked example

A £2 million office relocation sets a cost tolerance of ±5% (£100,000). In month four the project manager forecasts an overrun of £140,000 because of extra electrical work. As this is outside tolerance, she raises an exception report to the board with three options: absorb the cost from contingency, reduce the scope of meeting-room technology, or delay the move by six weeks. The board approves using £90,000 of contingency and trimming £50,000 of scope, records the decision in the decision log and resets the tolerance for the remaining stages.

Signs of weak governance

  • Nobody can say who has authority to make a particular decision.
  • The sponsor is absent or delegates everything.
  • Projects continue after the business case has clearly failed.
  • Reports are optimistic until a sudden crisis.
  • Governance is so heavy that small decisions take weeks.

Frequently asked questions

What is project governance?

The framework of roles, decision rights, processes and controls that directs and oversees a project.

What is the difference between governance and management?

Governance sets direction, makes key decisions and holds the project to account; management plans and delivers the work.

Who is responsible for project governance?

The sponsor and steering committee or project board, supported by the PMO and assurance functions.

What is a steering committee?

A group of senior stakeholders that provides direction, makes major decisions and resolves escalated issues.

What is a stage gate?

A formal decision point at the end of a phase where the project is reviewed before approval to continue.

What are project tolerances?

Permitted deviations from plan for time, cost, scope and other measures, within which the project manager can act without escalating.

What is management by exception?

A principle, central to PRINCE2, where senior managers intervene only when forecasts exceed agreed tolerances.

What is the role of the PMO in governance?

A PMO typically sets standards, consolidates reporting, supports decision-making and may carry out assurance.

How does governance work in Agile projects?

Agile governance tends to rely on frequent reviews of working product, clear product ownership, funding by value stream or product, and lighter but more frequent decision points. See agile PMO.

What is a decision log?

A record of significant decisions, including who made them, when, why and what options were considered.

What happens if a project has no governance?

Decisions stall or are made by the wrong people, risks go unescalated and projects can continue long after they stop being worthwhile.
