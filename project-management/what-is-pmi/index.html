What Is PMI? Project Management Institute Explained

PMI, the Project Management Institute, is a global not-for-profit professional association that sets standards for project management, awards certifications such as the PMP and CAPM, and runs a worldwide network of members and local chapters. Founded in the United States in 1969, it publishes the PMBOK Guide, now in its eighth edition. This guide explains what PMI does, every current certification and how to earn one, what membership includes, and how to decide whether joining is worth it for you.

Key takeaways

  • PMI is a not-for-profit professional body, not a government regulator: its certifications are voluntary, but the PMP is one of the most widely requested project management credentials in job adverts.
  • PMI currently lists 13 certifications, from the entry-level CAPM (no experience needed) to the PMP, PgMP and PfMP, plus specialist credentials in Agile, risk, scheduling, business analysis, PMOs, construction, sustainability and AI.
  • The PMBOK Guide 8th edition (November 2025) is built on six principles, seven performance domains and five focus areas with 40 processes.
  • The PMP exam changed in July 2026, and from 1 December 2026 live instructor-led training only counts towards the 35 hours if it comes from a PMI Authorized Training Partner or an eligible accredited programme.
  • Membership is optional. It usually pays for itself if you are about to sit a PMI exam, because members get lower exam fees and a free digital PMBOK Guide.
  • Most PMI certifications last three years and are renewed by earning PDUs: 60 for the PMP, 15 for the CAPM.

What is PMI?

PMI (the Project Management Institute) is a global, not-for-profit professional association for project, programme and portfolio management. It develops recognised standards such as the PMBOK Guide, sets and examines certifications including the PMP and CAPM, and supports practitioners through membership, local chapters, research, events and learning. PMI is headquartered in Newtown Square, Pennsylvania, USA.

PMI describes its wider community as more than 800,000 project professionals worldwide, organised in part through over 300 local chapters. It has no legal power to license project managers: nobody needs PMI's permission to manage a project. Its influence comes from the fact that employers, clients and governments in many countries recognise its standards and credentials.

Three terms are often confused, so it helps to separate them early.

TermWhat it isExample
PMIThe organisationThe body that writes the standard and runs the exam
PMBOK GuidePMI's flagship standard and guide to good practiceThe book you study from; see our PMBOK Guide explainer
PMPA certification awarded by PMIThe credential you add after your name; see PMP certification

A short history of PMI

PMI's own history page records that it began with a meeting of five founders at the Georgia Institute of Technology in Atlanta in 1969. The milestones below show how a small US association grew into a global standards and certification body.

YearMilestone
1969Founded by James Snyder, Eric Jenett, Gordon Davis, Ned Engman and Susan Gallagher; incorporated in Pennsylvania
1970sFirst chapter formed in Houston and the first Seminars and Symposium held in Atlanta
1984First PMP exam held; by the end of 1989 PMI had issued 264 PMP certifications
1996First edition of A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)
Late 2000s to early 2010sCertification range expands, including the PgMP for programme managers and the PMI-ACP for Agile practitioners
2021PMBOK Guide 7th edition moves from processes to 12 principles and 8 performance domains
2024PMI launches its PMI:Next strategy and the PMIxAI initiative
November 2025PMBOK Guide 8th edition published
July 2026Updated PMP exam launched, with a much larger Business Environment domain

The direction of travel is clear: PMI has moved from a single, process-heavy standard towards a broader view of delivering value with predictive, Agile and hybrid approaches, and more recently towards AI and sustainability.

PMI standards and the PMBOK Guide

The PMBOK Guide is PMI's best-known publication and the main reference behind its exams. It is a standard and guide to good practice, not a methodology: it describes what good project management involves, and leaves you to tailor how you do it. Methods such as PRINCE2 or Scrum are more prescriptive.

What is in the PMBOK Guide 8th edition?

According to PMI, the eighth edition was published in November 2025 and runs to 408 pages, including The Standard for Project Management. It keeps the principle-based approach of the seventh edition but brings back process guidance in a non-prescriptive way.

  • Six principles: adopt a holistic view, focus on value, embed quality, lead accountably, integrate sustainability, and build empowered teams.
  • Seven performance domains: governance, scope, schedule, finance, stakeholders, resources and risk.
  • Five focus areas: initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing, which echo the process groups of older editions.
  • 40 processes organised within those focus areas, presented as guidance rather than mandatory steps.

7th edition vs 8th edition

FeaturePMBOK Guide 7th edition (2021)PMBOK Guide 8th edition (2025)
Principles12 principles6 consolidated principles
Performance domains8 (stakeholders, team, development approach and life cycle, planning, project work, delivery, measurement, uncertainty)7 (governance, scope, schedule, finance, stakeholders, resources, risk)
ProcessesRemoved from the guideReintroduced: 40 processes in 5 focus areas
EmphasisValue delivery and tailoringValue, governance, sustainability and practical how-to guidance

If you studied an older edition, the core ideas you learned, such as scope, scheduling and risk management, are all still there. The project management principles and processes guides explain them in plain English.

Other PMI standards and practice guides

  • The Standard for Program Management and The Standard for Portfolio Management.
  • Standards for risk management and earned value management.
  • Practice standards for scheduling, estimating and work breakdown structures.
  • Practice guides including the Agile Practice Guide, benefits realisation, requirements management and business analysis.
  • A standard and practice guide for sustainability in project management (GPM P5).

Members can read PMI's standards digitally as part of membership, and PMIstandards+ offers searchable online access.

How to get a PMI certification step by step

The process is similar for every PMI exam-based credential. These steps follow the PMI Certification Handbook (revised June 2026).

  1. Check eligibility. Read the certification's exam content outline and handbook on pmi.org. For the PMP, PMI asks for project leadership experience within the last 10 years (five years with secondary education, four with an associate or vocational qualification, three with a bachelor's degree, two with a degree from a PMI GAC-accredited programme) plus 35 hours of project management training or an active CAPM.
  2. Complete the required education. The PMP needs 35 hours; the CAPM needs 23 hours. Keep certificates, because you will need them if you are audited. See 35 contact hours for the PMP.
  3. Decide whether to join PMI first. Member exam prices only apply if you are a member before you pay, so join before step 5 if you plan to.
  4. Submit the online application. Describe each project: your role, the dates, and what you did across the project life cycle. Write in your own words and be specific.
  5. Respond to an audit if selected. All applications can be audited and a percentage are chosen at random. You are told before payment, and you then have 90 days to send copies of your diploma, signed experience confirmations and course certificates. PMI says the review then takes about five to seven business days.
  6. Pay the exam fee. PMI emails your eligibility ID once payment is made.
  7. Schedule the exam. Book with Pearson VUE at a test centre or online. You have one year from approval (or from passing the audit) to take the exam, and up to three attempts in that year.
  8. Pass and maintain. Your certification cycle starts on the day you pass. Earn and report PDUs over the next three years and pay the renewal fee to keep it active.
Tip

If you plan to meet the PMP's 35 hours through live, instructor-led training, check the date. From 1 December 2026, PMI only accepts live training from a PMI Authorized Training Partner, a China Registered Education Provider or an eligible accredited academic programme. Training completed before that date still counts under the old rules, and self-paced courses can still come from any provider.

To make the rules concrete, here is a fictional candidate working through them.

Amira has a bachelor's degree and has worked as a project manager at an engineering firm since March 2022. By November 2026 she has about four years and eight months of project leadership experience, all within the last 10 years. She wants to sit the PMP in early 2027.

Her experience descriptions might read: "Led a 12-person team to deliver a substation upgrade; developed the work breakdown structure and schedule, managed a risk register, chaired weekly stakeholder reviews and closed the project with a lessons-learned report." Short, specific statements like this, using PMI's vocabulary, are easier to verify in an audit than vague job descriptions.

Tip

Collect evidence while you work. A signed project charter, a risk register and a WBS you produced make it easy to describe your experience accurately.

Keeping your certification: PDUs and renewal

Most PMI certifications are valid for three years. To renew, you earn Professional Development Units (PDUs), where one PDU equals one hour of learning or giving back to the profession, and then pay a renewal fee, which PMI says is separate from any membership fee.

PMI groups learning under its Talent Triangle: Ways of Working (technical project skills), Power Skills (leadership and communication) and Business Acumen (strategy and the wider organisation). Each certification has a minimum in each area.

PMI membership: types, benefits and costs

Membership is optional, and you can hold any PMI certification without it. PMI's membership page (checked 25 October 2026) lists these options.

OptionWhat it isNotes
Free PMI.org accountRegistration onlyFree eLearning, articles and job board browsing
30-day trial membershipShort trial of member benefitsPMI says no credit card is needed and it does not renew automatically
Professional membershipFull individual membership12 months, then billed annually
Student membershipDiscounted membershipFor full-time college or university students
Gift membershipMembership bought for someone elseUseful for managers and mentors
Chapter membershipMembership of a local chapterBeing bundled with PMI membership in more countries under PMI's new membership model

What members get

  • Member pricing of up to 30% off certifications, learning and events.
  • Free digital editions of the PMBOK Guide and other PMI standards.
  • PMI Infinity, PMI's AI-powered coach for members.
  • Free eLearning courses, which can also earn PDUs.
  • The Project Management Journal and a business reading centre.
  • Early access to job board postings and PMI's salary survey tool.
  • Project templates and premium webinars.

How much does PMI membership cost?

PMI does not show prices on its public membership page; they appear at checkout and can vary by country. Independent guides updated in 2026 quote individual membership at roughly US$129 to US$139 a year plus a one-off US$10 application fee, with lower student rates. Chapter dues are extra in some places and vary by chapter. Treat these figures as indicative and confirm the price in your PMI account before you pay.

Tip

Look out for PMI's new membership model. PMI is bundling chapter membership with global membership country by country; the third phase, covering countries including the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Australia, Singapore, Mexico and Nigeria, went live on 1 February 2026.

Is PMI membership worth it? A worked example

The honest answer depends on what you will use in the next 12 months. The quickest test is a break-even calculation:

Membership pays for itself if: (non-member exam fee minus member exam fee) + other savings you will actually use ≥ membership dues + application fee.

Here is an illustration using round, hypothetical numbers. Replace them with the prices shown in your own PMI checkout, as fees vary by region and change over time.

ItemNon-memberMember
Membership dues and application fee0140
PMP exam fee (hypothetical)650430

In this example the member saves 140 in the year they sit the exam, before counting free eLearning, PDUs and events. The saving grows if you also retake an exam, since re-examination fees are also discounted for members.

When membership is usually worth it

  • You will sit a PMI exam in the next year.
  • You need PDUs and will use the free courses and chapter events to earn them.
  • You want the PMBOK Guide and other standards for reference at work.
  • You are job hunting and will use the job board, salary data and networking.

When you can probably skip it

  • You have just renewed your certification and will not use member resources until the next cycle.
  • Your employer already provides PMIstandards+ or training that covers your PDUs.
  • You work mainly with PRINCE2, APM or Scrum and only need occasional PMI content.

Many people join the month before booking an exam and decide later whether to renew. That is a perfectly reasonable approach.

How to join PMI step by step

  1. Create a free account at pmi.org with the name that appears on your ID, because it will be used for certification records.
  2. Optionally start the 30-day trial to look at the resources before paying.
  3. Choose Professional, Student or Gift membership and complete checkout.
  4. Add a local chapter if one is not already bundled in your country.
  5. Download the PMBOK Guide and any other standards you need from your member account.
  6. Set up PMI Infinity and enrol in free eLearning courses that match your goals.
  7. If you are about to apply for an exam, check that your membership shows as active before you pay the exam fee.

Membership runs for 12 months and renews annually. If it lapses, you keep any certifications you hold, as long as you keep meeting their renewal requirements.

PMI chapters and volunteering

A PMI chapter is a local, volunteer-run branch of PMI for a city, region or country. PMI's history notes that the first chapter formed in Houston in the 1970s; today PMI refers to more than 300 chapters worldwide.

Chapters typically offer:

  • Evening talks and workshops, many of which earn PDUs.
  • Mentoring schemes and study groups for the PMP and CAPM.
  • Networking with local employers and recruiters.
  • Volunteering roles, from event organisation to board positions, which count as giving-back PDUs and build leadership experience.

The value varies with how active your local chapter is and whether you can attend. Look at its events calendar for the last six months before deciding how much time to invest. Volunteering on a chapter board is also a good way to practise project management skills such as stakeholder engagement and running a budget.

Common mistakes when dealing with PMI

  • Joining after paying the exam fee. Member pricing only applies if your membership is active before you pay.
  • Vague experience descriptions. Copying a job description makes an audit harder. Describe what you did on each project in PMI's terms.
  • Assuming any course counts. From 1 December 2026, live training for the PMP's 35 hours must come from an approved provider. Check before you book.
  • Studying from an old edition only. The PMP exam now reflects the 2026 exam content outline. Use current materials alongside the PMBOK Guide.
  • Treating the PMBOK Guide as a methodology. It is a standard; you still need to tailor an approach, whether waterfall, Agile or hybrid.
  • Leaving PDUs until the last month. Spread them across the three years and report them as you go.
  • Overlooking cheaper routes. Student membership and the CAPM are inexpensive ways in if you are early in your career.

Frequently asked questions

What does PMI stand for?

PMI stands for the Project Management Institute, a global not-for-profit professional association for project management founded in the United States in 1969.

Is PMI a government body?

No. PMI is an independent not-for-profit association. Its certifications are voluntary, but many employers and public bodies recognise them.

What is the difference between PMI and PMP?

PMI is the organisation; the PMP (Project Management Professional) is one of the certifications it awards. You get PMP certified by PMI.

Do I need to be a PMI member to take the PMP?

No. Membership is optional. Members pay a lower exam fee and get a free digital PMBOK Guide, which is why many candidates join before applying.

How much does PMI membership cost?

PMI shows prices at checkout and they vary by country. Independent guides updated in 2026 quote about US$129 to US$139 a year plus a one-off US$10 application fee, with lower student rates. Check your PMI account for the current price.

What is the latest edition of the PMBOK Guide?

The eighth edition, published in November 2025. It has six principles, seven performance domains and five focus areas containing 40 processes. See the PMBOK Guide.

Is the PMBOK Guide free for PMI members?

Yes. PMI lists free digital editions of the PMBOK Guide and its other standards among member benefits. Non-members can buy it from PMI's store.

Which PMI certification should I get first?

With little or no experience, start with the CAPM. With three or more years leading projects and a degree, go straight for the PMP. Agile practitioners often choose the PMI-ACP.

How many PDUs do I need to renew?

60 PDUs every three years for the PMP, PgMP, PfMP and PMI-PBA; 30 for most specialist certifications; and 15 for the CAPM.

What happens if my PMI application is audited?

You are told before you pay and have 90 days to send copies of your education certificates, signed confirmation of your experience and your training certificates. PMI says the review takes about five to seven business days once everything is received.

How long is a PMI exam eligibility period?

One year from application approval, or from passing the audit. You can attempt the exam up to three times in that year.

Is PMI recognised in the UK and Europe?

Yes. The PMP is widely recognised internationally, although in the UK many roles also ask for PRINCE2 or APM qualifications. See PMBOK vs PRINCE2.

Should I choose PMI or APM?

If you work mainly in the UK public or private sector, APM qualifications are well known. If you work internationally or for a multinational, the PMP usually travels further. Many people hold both.

What is a PMI chapter and should I join one?

A chapter is a local, volunteer-run branch of PMI that runs events, mentoring and volunteering. Join if you can attend events regularly; they are a low-cost way to earn PDUs and build a network.
