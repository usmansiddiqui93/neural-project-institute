Task Dependencies: Types, Examples and How to Manage Them

A task dependency is a logical relationship between two activities that controls when one can start or finish relative to the other. Dependencies turn a list of activities into a schedule that can calculate dates and a critical path.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 23 September 2026
  • 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Dependencies define the logical order of activities.
  • The four types are FS, SS, FF and SF; FS is the most common.
  • Classify them as mandatory or discretionary, and internal or external.
  • Discretionary links are the main lever for fast tracking.

The four dependency types

TypeRuleExample
Finish-to-start (FS)B cannot start until A finishesWrite the report, then review it
Start-to-start (SS)B cannot start until A startsStart proofreading once writing has started
Finish-to-finish (FF)B cannot finish until A finishesTesting cannot finish until development finishes
Start-to-finish (SF)B cannot finish until A startsThe old system cannot be switched off until the new one has started

Finish-to-start is by far the most common. Start-to-finish is rare and often a sign the logic could be written more simply.

Categories of dependency

CategoryMeaningExample
Mandatory (hard logic)Required by the nature of the work or a contractFoundations before walls
Discretionary (soft logic)A preferred order based on best practiceFinish design before starting build
ExternalDepends on something outside the projectPlanning permission from the council
InternalWithin the project team's controlDeveloper finishes code before tester starts

Discretionary dependencies are the first place to look when you need to fast track a schedule, because they can be changed if you accept extra risk.

How to identify and record dependencies

  1. List all activities from the activity list.
  2. For each activity ask: what must happen before this can start, and what is waiting for it to finish?
  3. Record the predecessor and successor and the type (FS, SS, FF or SF).
  4. Add any lead or lag, such as a 3-day wait for concrete to cure.
  5. Mark external dependencies clearly and assign someone to monitor them.
  6. Draw or generate a network diagram and check that every activity connects to the start and finish.

Worked example

A brochure project has four activities: A Write copy (5 days), B Design layout (4 days), C Proofread (2 days), D Print (3 days).

LinkTypeEffect
A to BSS + 2 daysDesign starts 2 days after writing starts
A to CFSProofreading starts when writing finishes (day 5)
B to CFFProofreading cannot finish before layout finishes
C to DFSPrinting starts after proofreading

Using day 0 as the start: A runs 0 to 5, B runs 2 to 6, C runs 5 to 7 (its finish of 7 is after B's finish of 6, so the FF link is satisfied), and D runs 7 to 10. The brochure is ready after 10 days instead of 14 if all links had been finish-to-start.

Managing dependencies

  • Review external dependencies in every status meeting and log them in a RAID log.
  • Avoid hard-coded dates; let dependencies drive the schedule.
  • Watch for activities with many predecessors, which are common points of delay.
  • On Agile teams, make cross-team dependencies visible on a board or during PI planning.
Tip

Missing dependencies are a frequent reason a schedule looks too optimistic. Ask each owner to confirm their inputs, not just their own duration.

Frequently asked questions

What is a task dependency?

A logical link between two tasks that determines when one can start or finish relative to the other.

What are the four types of task dependencies?

Finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish and start-to-finish.

Which type of task dependency is most common?

Finish-to-start: the successor cannot start until the predecessor finishes.

What is a mandatory task dependency?

A dependency required by physical, legal or contractual reasons, also called hard logic.

What is a discretionary task dependency?

A preferred sequence chosen by the team based on best practice, also called soft logic or preferential logic.

What is an external task dependency?

A dependency on something outside the project's control, such as a supplier delivery or a regulator's approval.

What is an example of a start-to-finish task dependency?

A new security guard shift must start before the previous shift can finish. It is the least common type.

How do task dependencies affect the critical path?

They determine which activities form the longest path. Changing or removing a dependency can change the critical path.

What is a task dependency in Agile?

Usually a piece of work one team needs from another team or system before it can complete a story.

How do I show task dependencies on a Gantt chart?

Most tools draw arrows between bars when you set predecessors; see Gantt chart.
