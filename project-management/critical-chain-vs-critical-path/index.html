Critical Chain vs Critical Path: Key Differences Explained

The critical path is the longest chain of dependent activities, calculated assuming resources are unlimited. The critical chain is the longest chain once both dependencies and resource limits are taken into account, with safety time pulled out of individual tasks and pooled into buffers that protect the finish date.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Critical path considers dependencies; critical chain adds resource limits.
  • Critical chain moves safety out of tasks into project and feeding buffers.
  • Track buffer consumption against work completed.
  • Choose critical chain where shared resources and multitasking cause delays.

TrackingDates and floatBuffer consumption vs chain completed
MultitaskingNot addressed directlyDiscouraged; one task at a time
OriginLate 1950sEliyahu Goldratt, 1997, based on the theory of constraints

How critical chain works

  1. Build the network and level resources so no one is double-booked; the longest resource-levelled path is the critical chain.
  2. Cut each task estimate to a focused, aggressive estimate, often around half of a padded estimate.
  3. Add a project buffer at the end of the critical chain, sized from the safety removed.
  4. Add feeding buffers where non-critical chains join the critical chain.
  5. Start tasks as soon as inputs are ready and finish them without multitasking.
  6. Track buffer consumption against chain progress, often on a fever chart with green, amber and red zones.

Worked example

A critical path has three tasks with padded estimates of 10, 8 and 6 days.

ApproachTask durationsBufferPlanned finish
Critical path10 + 8 + 6 = 24 daysNone shownDay 24
Critical chain5 + 4 + 3 = 12 daysProject buffer: 50% of 12 days removed = 6 daysDay 18

If the critical chain is 40% complete and 2 of the 6 buffer days (33%) have been used, the project is in good shape because buffer is being used more slowly than work is completed. If 4 buffer days (67%) were already gone at 40% complete, the project manager would plan recovery action.

Pros and cons

Critical pathCritical chain
StrengthsWidely understood and supported by tools; clear datesAccounts for resource conflicts; reduces hidden padding; simple buffer signals
WeaknessesIgnores resource limits; padding is consumed by Parkinson's law and student syndromeNeeds culture change and trust; fewer tools support it; individual due dates are de-emphasised

When to use each

  • Use the critical path for most projects, especially with contractual dates per activity.
  • Consider critical chain when shared resources are the main constraint, multitasking is rife, and estimates are routinely padded.
  • Some organisations combine them: CPM for logic and dates, plus a visible project buffer and resource levelling.
Tip

Critical chain only works if people are not penalised for missing aggressive task estimates. Measure the buffer, not individuals.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between critical chain and critical path?

The critical path considers only dependencies; the critical chain considers dependencies and resource limits, and uses buffers instead of task-level padding.

Who created critical chain project management?

Eliyahu Goldratt, who introduced it in his 1997 book Critical Chain.

What is a project buffer?

Time added at the end of the critical chain to protect the finish date from variation in task durations.

What is a feeding buffer?

A buffer placed where a non-critical chain joins the critical chain, so delays on the feeding chain do not hit the critical chain.

How big should the project buffer be?

A common rule of thumb is half the safety removed from tasks on the chain, though other sizing methods exist.

What is a fever chart?

A chart that plots buffer consumed against chain completed, with zones showing when to watch or act.

What is student syndrome?

Starting work at the last possible moment, which wastes any safety built into an estimate.

What is Parkinson's law?

The idea that work expands to fill the time available, so early finishes rarely get passed on.

Is critical chain better than critical path?

It can be in resource-constrained, multi-project environments, but it needs cultural change. CPM remains the more common approach.

Can I use critical chain in normal scheduling tools?

Some tools support it directly; in others you can add buffers as activities manually. Compare options on our tools page.
