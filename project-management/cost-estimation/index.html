Cost Estimation in Project Management: Methods and Examples

Cost estimation is the process of predicting how much money a project's work will need. A good estimate states not just a number but its range of accuracy, the assumptions behind it and how it was produced.

Written byNeural Project InstituteProject management editorial team
  • Published 27 September 2026
  • 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Choose the estimating method to match how much you know about the work.
  • Always quote a range and document the basis of estimate.
  • Three-point and PERT estimates make uncertainty visible.
  • Re-estimate as the project progresses and knowledge improves.

Main estimating methods

MethodHow it worksBest used when
Analogous (top-down)Uses the actual cost of a similar past project, adjusted for differencesEarly on, with little detail
ParametricMultiplies a unit rate by a quantity, such as cost per square metreWork is repeatable and good historical rates exist
Bottom-upEstimates each work package and adds them upThe WBS is well defined
Three-pointUses optimistic, most likely and pessimistic valuesUncertainty is significant
Vendor bid analysisCompares supplier quotesWork will be bought in
Expert judgmentDraws on people who have done similar workAlongside any other method

Estimate accuracy ranges

Estimates become more accurate as the project is better understood. Commonly quoted ranges are:

Estimate classTypical rangeWhen
Rough order of magnitude (ROM)-25% to +75%Initiation, ideas and feasibility
Budget estimateAround -10% to +25%Planning, once scope is outlined
Definitive estimateAround -5% to +10%Detailed planning, once the WBS is complete

Always quote the range, not just a single figure, so decision makers understand the uncertainty.

How to estimate project costs

  1. Confirm the scope and break it down into work packages.
  2. Identify the resources each package needs: people, equipment, materials, services.
  3. Choose a method for each package and apply current rates.
  4. Add the cost of risk responses and a contingency reserve based on the risk register.
  5. Include indirect costs, licences, travel, training and inflation for long projects.
  6. Document the basis of estimate: assumptions, sources, exclusions and the accuracy range.
  7. Review with the people who will do the work and with finance.

Worked examples

Parametric: an office fit-out costs about $1,200 per square metre. For 250 square metres: 1,200 x 250 = $300,000.

Analogous: a previous 40-screen app cost $80,000, or $2,000 per screen. A new 50-screen app of similar complexity: 50 x 2,000 = $100,000, before adjusting for differences.

Three-point (PERT): optimistic $8,000, most likely $12,000, pessimistic $22,000. PERT estimate = (8,000 + 4 x 12,000 + 22,000) / 6 = 78,000 / 6 = $13,000. The triangular average would be (8,000 + 12,000 + 22,000) / 3 = $14,000.

Bottom-up: design $18,000 + build $54,000 + testing $16,000 + training $7,000 = $95,000, plus a contingency reserve of $9,500 (10%) = $104,500.

Tips for better estimates

  • Estimate effort first, then convert to cost; see effort estimation.
  • Have the people who will do the work estimate it.
  • Keep a library of actual costs from past projects.
  • Separate contingency from the base estimate so it can be managed openly.
  • Re-estimate at each phase as knowledge improves (progressive elaboration).
  • Watch for optimism bias and pressure to hit a target number.

Frequently asked questions

What is cost estimation in project management?

It is predicting the money needed to complete the project's work, stated with a range of accuracy and the assumptions behind it.

What are the four main cost estimating techniques?

Analogous, parametric, bottom-up and three-point estimating are the most widely used.

What is the most accurate cost estimating method?

Bottom-up estimating is usually the most accurate because it is based on detailed work packages, but it takes the most time.

What is a rough order of magnitude cost estimate?

A very early estimate, commonly quoted as -25% to +75% accurate, used to decide whether an idea is worth pursuing.

What is the PERT formula for cost estimating?

PERT estimate = (optimistic + 4 x most likely + pessimistic) / 6. It weights the most likely value most heavily.

What is the difference between analogous and parametric cost estimating?

Analogous estimating scales the total from a similar past project. Parametric estimating multiplies a unit rate by a quantity, such as cost per metre.

What is a basis of estimate in cost estimation?

Supporting documentation explaining how an estimate was produced: assumptions, sources, rates, exclusions, risks and the accuracy range.

Should contingency be included in the cost estimate?

Contingency for identified risks is added to the estimate to form the cost baseline, but it should be shown separately rather than hidden in task estimates.

Who should prepare cost estimates?

The people doing the work, supported by the project manager, cost specialists and finance, ideally reviewed against historical data.

How does cost estimation work for Agile projects?

Agile teams often estimate cost per sprint (team cost x number of sprints) and combine it with relative estimates of scope; see story points.

Why is cost estimation on projects often wrong?

Common reasons are unclear scope, optimism, missing items such as indirect costs, unmanaged risks and pressure to quote a low number.
