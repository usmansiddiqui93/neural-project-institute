Assistant Project Manager: Duties, Skills and Career Path

An assistant project manager supports a project manager in planning and delivering a project and takes ownership of parts of it, such as the schedule, risk and issue logs, supplier follow-up, reporting or a whole workstream. The role sits between project coordinator and project manager and is one of the most common routes into project management. This guide covers the duties, skills, qualifications, pay, a sample job description, a worked example and a step-by-step plan to become a project manager.

Key takeaways

  • An assistant project manager owns parts of a project's delivery under a project manager's direction, which goes further than the administrative focus of a coordinator.
  • Core duties are schedule upkeep, progress tracking, risk and issue logs, reporting, supplier and team follow-up, budget support and, increasingly, a workstream of your own.
  • The title is especially common in construction, engineering and large IT programmes, and duties vary by sector.
  • Employers look for organisation, communication, scheduling and budgeting basics and comfort with project software. An entry-level certification such as the CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation or PMAI helps.
  • To step up, own a workstream end to end, keep an evidence log of what you led and practise the decisions a project manager makes.
  • Pay depends on country, sector and project size; compare current job adverts and official salary data rather than relying on one average.

What is an assistant project manager?

An assistant project manager (APM) is a project professional who helps a project manager plan, track and deliver a project, and who is responsible for defined parts of it, such as the schedule, risk log, procurement follow-up or a workstream. They make decisions within agreed limits and escalate anything that affects scope, cost, time or quality beyond those limits.

Organisations usually add an assistant project manager when a project is too large or busy for one person to manage alone: several workstreams, many suppliers, a busy site or a demanding reporting cycle. It is also a deliberate development role, giving someone a structured way to learn from an experienced project manager before running projects themselves.

Assistant project manager vs project manager assistant

The two titles sound alike but usually mean different jobs. A project manager assistant (or project administrator) provides administrative support: diaries, minutes, document control and travel. An assistant project manager does part of the management work: tracking progress, managing risks and issues, coordinating suppliers and sometimes leading a workstream. Always read the duties in the job advert rather than relying on the title.

Assistant, junior and associate project managers

Junior project manager and associate project manager are often used for a similar level. The usual difference is that a junior or associate PM runs small projects of their own with light oversight, while an assistant PM works inside a larger project under one project manager. In practice the titles overlap heavily, and pay bands are often the same, so judge a role by its duties and decision rights.

Decision rights: what an assistant PM decides, recommends or escalates

The clearest difference between the roles is decision rights. Agree them with your project manager in the first week; the examples below are illustrative.

Decide aloneRecommend to the project managerEscalate immediately
Updating task dates within the workstream's floatMoving a milestoneAny slip to a key milestone or contractual date
Chasing suppliers and booking meetingsApproving a change requestSafety, legal or data protection concerns
Logging and assigning low-impact issuesSpending contingencyForecast overspend beyond tolerance
Formatting and issuing the status reportChanging a red/amber/green ratingStakeholder complaints at senior level

Assistant project manager duties and responsibilities

Duties vary by employer, but most fall into seven areas.

Planning and scheduling

  • Maintain the project schedule, update progress and flag slipped tasks.
  • Help build work breakdowns and estimates with the team.
  • Track milestones and dependencies, and check the impact of late tasks.

Tracking and reporting

  • Collect progress updates and draft the weekly status report.
  • Prepare packs for progress meetings and steering groups.
  • Keep dashboards and trackers current.

Risks, issues and changes

  • Maintain the RAID log or risk register: record items, chase owners and update ratings.
  • Log change requests and assess their impact for the project manager; see change control.

Budget and procurement

  • Raise purchase orders, track invoices and reconcile actual costs against the budget.
  • Follow up supplier deliveries and support tendering; see project procurement management.

Stakeholders and meetings

  • Run routine progress meetings for a workstream, issue agendas and record actions.
  • Act as day-to-day contact for suppliers and team members.

Owning a workstream

As you gain experience, the project manager will hand you a workstream or small sub-project to run end to end, such as fit-out of one floor, a data migration or a supplier contract. This is the duty that does most to prepare you for promotion.

Documentation and closure

  • Keep project records and document control in order.
  • Help with handover, snagging or acceptance and project closure, including lessons learned.

Assistant project managers in construction

On construction projects the assistant project manager usually works for the contractor or the client's project manager, focusing on the commercial and coordination side: procurement packages, subcontractor programmes, information requests, cost reports and handover. That makes it different from a site manager, who runs day-to-day work and safety on site, and from a project engineer, a title used by many contractors for a technical coordination role at a similar level. Titles vary by country and company, so compare the duties.

Construction is where the title is most common. If that is your route, read our guide to the construction project manager role. For technology projects, see IT project manager.

A typical week

This illustrative week is for an assistant project manager on a medium-sized office refurbishment with five suppliers.

DayMain activities
MondayUpdate the schedule from Friday's progress; flag two slipped tasks to the PM; issue this week's look-ahead to suppliers
TuesdayChase the flooring supplier's delivery date; update the procurement tracker; check three invoices against purchase orders
WednesdayRun the weekly progress meeting for the mechanical and electrical workstream; record actions and update the RAID log
ThursdayDraft the status report; review risk ratings with owners; prepare a change request impact note for the PM
FridayWalk the site or review the latest outputs; agree next week's priorities with the PM; note lessons and update your evidence log

A meeting agenda template and a project tracker template cover most of the paperwork in a week like this.

Tip

Build a 20-minute daily routine: check overnight emails for anything that changes a date, update the tracker, then decide the three follow-ups that matter most today. Small, regular updates beat a Friday afternoon scramble.

Qualifications and certifications

There is no single required qualification. Many employers ask for a degree, often in a related field such as construction management, engineering, business or IT, but experience as a coordinator, in a trade or in operations is often accepted instead. An entry-level certification shows you understand the fundamentals and are serious about the career.

Assistant project manager salary

Pay varies widely, so treat any single average with care. The main factors are:

  • Country and region. Large cities usually pay more, with higher living costs.
  • Sector. Construction, engineering, energy and technology often pay more than non-profit or public-sector roles.
  • Project size and complexity. Larger budgets and more suppliers usually mean higher pay.
  • Experience and credentials. Moving from coordinator-level duties to owning workstreams makes the biggest difference.

For context in the US, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median annual wage of $102,320 for project management specialists in May 2025, with employment projected to grow 7% from 2025 to 2035. That category includes experienced project managers, so it is not a figure for assistant roles, which sit lower on the ladder. PMI's Earning Power salary survey (14th edition, November 2025, 21 countries) found that PMP holders reported median salaries 17% higher than those without it, which is relevant once you step up.

For a realistic figure, compare 15–20 current job adverts in your area and sector, and check national salary surveys from professional bodies and official statistics.

Sample assistant project manager job description

Use this illustrative job description as a starting point if you are hiring, or as a checklist of what to evidence if you are applying.

Role summary

The assistant project manager supports the project manager in delivering [project type] on time, within budget and to the required quality. You will maintain the schedule and project controls, coordinate suppliers and team members, and lead a workstream under the project manager's direction.

Responsibilities

  • Maintain the project schedule and report progress against milestones.
  • Maintain the RAID log, chase owners and escalate issues in line with agreed tolerances.
  • Prepare weekly status reports and meeting packs.
  • Coordinate suppliers and subcontractors, track deliveries and support procurement.
  • Track costs, purchase orders and invoices against the budget.
  • Lead a defined workstream, including its meetings, actions and reporting.
  • Support handover, closure and lessons learned.

Requirements

  • Experience as a project coordinator or in a related project, technical or operational role.
  • Working knowledge of scheduling, budgeting and risk management.
  • Strong written and verbal communication; confident with suppliers and stakeholders.
  • Proficiency with project management software and spreadsheets.
  • A degree or relevant experience; CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation or similar is an advantage.

Worked example: handling a supplier delay

This illustrative example shows the judgement that separates an assistant project manager from a coordinator. You are the APM on an office refurbishment. On Tuesday the flooring supplier tells you its delivery will be eight working days late.

  1. Confirm the facts. Get the new date in writing and ask what is causing the delay and how confident the supplier is.
  2. Check the schedule impact. Flooring precedes furniture installation, which has two days of float. An eight-day delay therefore pushes the furniture milestone by six days and the client move-in date is at risk.
  3. Look for options within your authority. Ask whether part of the order can be delivered on time for the first two floors, and whether the furniture supplier can start on those floors first.
  4. Escalate with a recommendation. Because a key milestone is affected, you escalate the same day. Your note gives the facts, the impact (six days on the furniture milestone, move-in at risk) and two options: phased delivery and floor-by-floor installation at no extra cost, or an alternative supplier at an estimated extra £4,000 with a four-day saving.
  5. Record it. Log the issue in the RAID log, raise a change request if the PM chooses the second option and update the schedule once a decision is made.
  6. Close the loop. Tell the affected suppliers and the team, confirm the new plan in the next status report and note the lesson: confirm delivery dates in writing two weeks before need.

The PM approves the phased option. Move-in is protected, and you have evidence for your log: "identified a supplier delay, assessed schedule impact and proposed a phased delivery that protected the move-in date at no extra cost".

How to become an assistant project manager

Most people reach the role from one of four starting points.

Starting pointTypical route
Project coordinator or administratorTake on more ownership (a workstream, the RAID log, supplier management), then apply internally or externally
GraduateGraduate scheme or junior role in construction, engineering or IT, often with a related degree
Trade or technical specialistMove from site, engineering or technical work into project support, using your technical knowledge as an advantage
Career changerUse transferable experience from operations, events, marketing or administration, plus an entry-level certification
  1. Learn the fundamentals: project phases, scheduling, budgeting, risk and stakeholder management.
  2. Take an entry-level certification such as the CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, APM PFQ or PMAI.
  3. Get hands-on with a work management tool and spreadsheets.
  4. Build evidence: volunteer to run small projects at work or in the community and record what you delivered.
  5. Rewrite your CV around outcomes, using project language.
  6. Apply for assistant, junior or associate project manager roles, and ask about internal moves.

How to step up from assistant project manager to project manager

Promotion usually comes from showing you can already do the job. This illustrative 12-month plan works in most organisations.

MonthsFocusEvidence to collect
1–3Agree decision rights; master the schedule, RAID log and reportingStatus reports issued; issues caught early
4–6Own a workstream end to end, including its meetings and budgetWorkstream delivered on time; costs reconciled
7–9Lead a supplier or stakeholder relationship; present at a steering meetingDecisions you shaped; feedback from stakeholders
10–12Run a small project or cover for the PM during leaveA complete project, from plan to closure

Keep an evidence log throughout: project, your role, what you led, the outcome and the lesson. It feeds your promotion case and, later, a PMP application. PMP eligibility requires 3, 4 or 5 years of project experience within the last 10 years depending on your education (2 years with a GAC-accredited degree), plus 35 contact hours or the CAPM. Experience counts when you led and directed project work, so describe what you led, not what you assisted with. From 1 December 2026, live PMP training must come from a PMI Authorized Training Partner.

Tip

Shadow your project manager in steering meetings. Seeing how decisions are framed and escalated is one of the fastest ways to learn the judgement the next role needs.

Your first 30 days as an assistant project manager

The first month sets how much responsibility you are given. Use it to understand the project, agree your role and become reliable.

  1. Week 1: Learn the project. Read the business case or charter, schedule, budget, RAID log and the last three status reports. List what you do not understand and ask.
  2. Week 1: Agree your role. Sit down with your project manager and agree your duties, decision rights, reporting routine and how they want to be updated.
  3. Week 2: Meet the people. Introduce yourself to suppliers, team leads and key stakeholders. Ask each what they need from the project team and what usually goes wrong.
  4. Week 2: Take over the routines. Start running the schedule update, RAID log and status report draft, with the PM reviewing your work.
  5. Week 3: Find one improvement. Fix something small but visible, such as an out-of-date tracker, a confusing report or missing action owners.
  6. Week 4: Review. Ask your PM what is going well and what to change, and agree what you will own next, ideally a workstream.

Common mistakes new assistant project managers make

  • Being a messenger, not a manager. Passing on problems without impact or options. Always bring a recommendation.
  • Escalating too late. Hoping a slip will recover. Raise it as soon as a key date is at risk.
  • Escalating everything. Agree decision rights so you can act within them.
  • Updating documents without reading them. The RAID log is a management tool; ask what each item means for the project.
  • Staying in the background. Ask to run meetings and present updates.
  • Not recording achievements. Without an evidence log, promotion cases and PMP applications are much harder.
  • Copying last week's status report. If the report never changes, people stop reading it. Lead with what changed, what is at risk and what decision is needed.
  • Owning tasks instead of outcomes. Closing actions is good; making sure they achieved their purpose is better. Check that a chased delivery actually arrived and was right.
  • Neglecting relationships with suppliers and site or delivery teams. Persistence works best when people trust you. Be clear, polite and consistent, and thank people when they deliver.

Tools assistant project managers use

You will spend much of your day in project software, so fluency is a real advantage.

  • Wrike, our top-rated work management platform, is a strong all-round choice for schedules, Gantt charts, dashboards, request forms and cross-team reporting.
  • Smartsheet suits organisations that like spreadsheet-style planning and reporting.
  • Microsoft Planner works well in Microsoft 365 organisations.
  • Asana and monday.com are popular for cross-team task tracking and visual workflows.
  • Spreadsheets remain essential for cost tracking, procurement logs and quick analysis.

If your employer lets you choose, start with ready-made structures such as a RAID log template and a project schedule template, then move them into your team's tool.

Frequently asked questions

What does an assistant project manager do?

Supports the project manager by maintaining the schedule, RAID log and reports, coordinating suppliers and team members, tracking costs and, increasingly, leading a workstream within agreed limits.

Is assistant project manager an entry-level job?

Often it is a step above entry level, after a coordinator, administrator or technical role, but some employers hire graduates directly, especially in construction and engineering.

Is an assistant project manager higher than a project coordinator?

Usually, yes. Coordinators focus on administration and communication; assistant project managers own parts of delivery and make more decisions. Titles vary, so compare the duties.

What is the difference between an assistant project manager and a project manager assistant?

A project manager assistant provides administrative support. An assistant project manager does part of the management work, such as tracking, risk management and leading a workstream.

What qualifications does an assistant project manager need?

Employers often ask for a degree or relevant experience. An entry-level certification such as the CAPM, PRINCE2 Foundation, APM PFQ or PMAI strengthens an application.

Do you need a degree to be an assistant project manager?

Not always. Many employers accept coordinator, trade or operational experience instead, although some sectors, such as engineering, prefer a related degree.

How much does an assistant project manager earn?

It depends on country, sector, project size and experience. Compare current job adverts and salary surveys for your market; broad official figures for project management include experienced project managers.

How long does it take to go from assistant project manager to project manager?

It varies by employer, often one to three years. Owning a workstream, running a small project and keeping an evidence log speed it up.

Does assistant project manager experience count towards the PMP?

Experience counts when you led and directed project work, such as running a workstream. Describe what you led, and check PMI's current rules before applying.

What skills does an assistant project manager need?

Organisation, written communication, scheduling and budgeting basics, risk awareness, stakeholder handling, tool fluency and good judgement about what to escalate.

What does an assistant project manager do in construction?

Supports the project manager with procurement packages, subcontractor coordination, programme updates, information requests, site meetings, cost reports and snagging.

What certification is best for an assistant project manager?

The CAPM is a widely recognised starting point; PRINCE2 Foundation or APM PFQ suit UK and public-sector employers; Scrum certifications suit Agile teams. The PMP comes later.

What tools should an assistant project manager learn?

A work management platform such as Wrike, Smartsheet, Asana or monday.com, plus spreadsheets and the reporting tools your employer uses.

Is assistant project manager a good job?

For people who want to manage projects, it is one of the best learning roles: you see the whole project, build evidence and learn from an experienced PM. The trade-off is that you have responsibility without full authority, so agree your decision rights early.

What comes after assistant project manager?

Usually project manager, then senior project manager or programme manager. Some move into PMO, technical project manager or product roles.
