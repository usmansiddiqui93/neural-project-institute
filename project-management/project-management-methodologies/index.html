Project Management Methodologies, Frameworks and Approaches

The words methodology, framework and approach are often used interchangeably, but they mean slightly different things. Knowing the main options, and how they differ, helps you choose the right way to run each project.

By Neural Project InstitutePublished 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Approach, framework, methodology and standard mean different things.
  • Choose based on requirement stability, deliverability in pieces and governance.
  • Hybrid approaches are common.

Approach, framework, methodology

TermMeaningExample
ApproachThe overall philosophy for delivering workPredictive, adaptive (Agile), hybrid
FrameworkA structure with some rules, leaving room to adaptScrum, SAFe
MethodologyA defined system of practices, processes and rulesPRINCE2, Waterfall
StandardA description of good practicePMBOK Guide, ISO 21502

Main methodologies compared

MethodologyCore ideaBest for
WaterfallSequential phases, detailed up-front planStable, well-defined projects
AgileShort iterations, continuous feedbackUncertain, evolving requirements
ScrumSprints, roles, events and artifactsProduct development teams
KanbanVisual flow and WIP limitsContinuous work
PRINCE2Stages, roles and management by exceptionGovernance-heavy projects
LeanMaximise value, eliminate wasteProcess improvement
Six SigmaReduce variation with data (DMAIC)Quality improvement
Critical ChainSchedule around resources with buffersResource-constrained portfolios
Extreme Project ManagementHighly flexible, for fast-changing projectsInnovation projects

How to choose

  1. How stable are the requirements? Stable favours predictive; uncertain favours Agile.
  2. Can the work be delivered in usable pieces?
  3. What governance and regulation apply?
  4. How available are customers for feedback?
  5. What does your organisation already use?
Tip

Many organisations use a hybrid: predictive governance and milestones, with Agile delivery by teams.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most popular project management methodology?

Agile approaches, especially Scrum, are the most widely used in technology; Waterfall and hybrid approaches remain common in construction, engineering and government.

Can you combine methodologies?

Yes. Hybrid approaches are common and often the most practical.
