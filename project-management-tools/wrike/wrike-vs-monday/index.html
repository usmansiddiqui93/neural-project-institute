Wrike vs monday.com: Which Work Management Tool Wins in 2026?

Wrike vs monday.com is a choice between depth and ease of adoption. Wrike is the better fit for organisations that need detailed scheduling, resource and portfolio management, approvals and reporting in one platform; monday.com is the better fit for teams that want colourful, flexible boards that non-technical colleagues pick up in minutes. This comparison covers ease of use, planning, resources, automation, AI, pricing at real team sizes, user ratings and how to decide.

Key takeaways

  • Wrike scores 9.4/10 (our #1) and monday.com 9.1/10 (#2); Wrike wins four of seven categories, monday.com wins ease of use, and two are tied.
  • Wrike goes deeper on Gantt scheduling (critical path and baselines), resource and capacity planning, proofing and approvals, and portfolio-level reporting.
  • monday.com is easier to adopt, more visual, and rated higher by users on G2 (4.7 vs 4.2) and Capterra (4.6 vs 4.4).
  • Paid plans start at $10 per user/month for Wrike Team and $9 per seat/month for monday.com Basic (annual billing, checked 7 October 2026), but monday.com sells seats in buckets with a 3-seat minimum.
  • monday.com caps automation and integration actions by plan (250 a month each on Standard); serious automation starts on Pro.
  • Both offer a 14-day free trial without a credit card, so test one real workflow in each before deciding.

Quick verdict

Choose Wrike if you run many projects at once and need interactive Gantt charts with critical path and baselines, workload and resource planning, request intake with approvals, and reporting across a whole portfolio. Choose monday.com if fast adoption matters most and you want visual boards that every department can shape into its own workflow with little help.

In our scoring, Wrike (9.4/10, ranked #1 in our top 10 project management tools) wins on feature depth, planning and resource management, Agile and software teams, and enterprise readiness. monday.com (9.1/10, #2) wins clearly on ease of use, and the two tie on collaboration and value for money. The gap is small: both are excellent platforms, and the right one depends on how complex your work is.

Put simply, monday.com is the easier platform to roll out, and Wrike is the more complete platform once work gets complex.

The core difference: depth vs ease of adoption

Both tools manage tasks, projects, requests and processes, and both offer list, board, table, calendar and Gantt views, dashboards, forms, automation and AI. The difference is in design philosophy.

Wrike is built around a structured hierarchy of spaces, folders, projects and tasks, with custom item types, fields and workflows. It is designed to be the single system of record for complex work, so it puts scheduling, resource management, proofing and reporting in the same platform. That depth takes longer to set up and learn.

monday.com is built around boards: flexible, spreadsheet-like tables where each row is a piece of work and each column holds information about it. Anyone can create a board and add columns in seconds, which is why non-technical teams adopt it quickly. Advanced capabilities, such as time tracking, workload, portfolio and resource management, arrive on the Pro and Enterprise plans.

What are Wrike and monday.com?

Wrike

Wrike was founded in 2006 by Andrew Filev and has been owned by private equity firm Symphony Technology Group since 2023. It says it is used by more than 20,000 organisations. It covers tasks and projects, request forms, custom workflows, interactive Gantt charts, dashboards, workload and resource planning, proofing and approvals, AI agents and, on top tiers, advanced reporting and BI.

monday.com

monday.com Ltd. was founded in Tel Aviv in 2012 and has been listed on Nasdaq since 2021. It sells several products on one platform, including work management, CRM, service and monday dev for software teams. This comparison focuses on monday work management, which covers boards and views, dashboards, WorkForms, automations, docs, AI and, on Enterprise, portfolio and resource management.

Ease of use and onboarding

Ease of adoption is monday.com's best-known advantage, and it earns its 9/10 in our scoring. A new user understands a board in minutes, colour-coded statuses make progress obvious, and more than 200 templates give a working start. Small teams can run real work on day one. The learning curve rises for connected boards, cross-board dashboards, complex automations and permissions, and G2 reviewers mention it for advanced setups.

Wrike scores 7/10. Creating, assigning and updating tasks is straightforward, but designing spaces, workflows, custom fields, request forms, dashboards and permissions for a whole department takes planning. Capterra reviewers consistently praise Wrike's flexibility and visibility while mentioning setup complexity, onboarding effort and a dense interface. Wrike provides training through Wrike Discover, templates and professional services for larger rollouts.

Tip

Whichever tool you choose, appoint one administrator to own standards: statuses, fields, templates and naming. In monday.com this prevents board sprawl; in Wrike it keeps the configuration manageable as more teams join.

For complex, date-driven work such as launches, events or construction-style schedules, Wrike's support for lead and lag, float and the critical path helps you see what a delay will affect before it happens. Resource, workload and portfolio management

This is where Wrike pulls ahead, and it drives most of its lead in our planning and resource score (10/10 vs 8/10).

Wrike shows team workload and allocated effort, accounts for working hours, non-working days and holidays, and supports time tracking with approved timesheets. Pinnacle adds advanced resource and capacity planning with job roles, budgeting and financial tracking, and advanced reporting and BI. Because projects share the same structure and fields, PMOs can roll many projects into portfolio views and dashboards.

monday.com adds a workload view and time tracking on Pro, which is often enough for basic capacity planning in small and mid-sized teams. Its pricing page lists portfolio management and resource management as Enterprise features, along with multi-level permissions.

For a PMO or professional services firm that needs formal resource allocation across many projects, both answers involve a quote-based plan (Wrike Pinnacle or monday.com Enterprise). Ask each vendor for a demo of exactly the resource and portfolio views you need.

Request intake, approvals and proofing

Both tools turn form submissions into work. Wrike's request forms map answers to task fields, use conditional logic and can be shared externally, and rules route each request to the right person. monday.com's WorkForms support conditional questions, branding and an AI form builder, and each submission creates an item on a board. Our work request template lists the fields worth collecting in either tool.

The difference is what happens next. Wrike has built-in proofing and approvals: reviewers mark up images, videos and documents and approve with named approvers, which is a long-standing strength for creative and marketing teams. monday.com handles approvals through statuses and automations, which works for simple sign-offs; that is why our feature table marks its proofing as Partial.

Automation and workflows

Both tools use "when this happens, do that" rules: when a status changes, assign someone; when a form is submitted, create a task; when a date approaches, send a reminder. monday.com's automation recipes are among the easiest to set up in the category. Wrike's rules work with its custom statuses and request forms to route work through multi-step processes, and AI Essentials (from Team) can create forms and automation rules from plain-language descriptions.

The practical difference is metering. monday.com counts automation actions and integration actions each month: 250 of each on Standard, 25,000 of each on Pro, and up to 250,000 combined on Enterprise (checked 7 October 2026). A busy team can use 250 actions quickly, so serious automation on monday.com starts on Pro. In Wrike, the more advanced automation, AI agents and cross-tool automation through Wrike Integrate sit on higher tiers or add-ons, so check which plan your workflows need.

AI features

Both vendors have made AI central to their platforms, with usage-based allowances (checked 7 October 2026).

  • Wrike splits AI into two tiers. AI Essentials, from Team, covers content creation and editing, comment summaries, natural-language creation of forms and rules, AI highlights and mobile inbox prioritisation. AI Elite, with a pooled monthly allowance of actions from Business, adds Wrike Copilot (questions about your work data), AI dashboard widgets and configurable AI agents for request validation, triage, risk checks, auto-assignment and portfolio reporting. Allowances grow on Pinnacle and Apex.
  • monday.com uses a shared monthly pool of AI credits: at least 1,000 on Basic, 2,000 on Standard and 3,000 on Pro, with custom amounts on Enterprise. Credits power monday sidekick (an assistant that summarises, drafts and updates work), prebuilt and custom AI agents for intake, planning, status reporting and risk analysis, AI blocks on boards, and the Notetaker (from Standard).

Both treat agents as configurable automation with permissions, logs and admin controls, not as autonomous staff. Wrike's agents are tightly connected to its request and portfolio workflows; monday.com's sidekick is the more accessible everyday assistant. Heavy AI users should model costs on either platform.

Reporting and dashboards

Wrike's dashboards, shareable from Team, pull tasks, statuses, workload and metrics from across projects onto one screen. Pinnacle and Apex add advanced reporting and BI for portfolio, financial and resource analysis. Capterra reviewers rate Wrike's project visibility very highly, though some mention a learning curve in configuring reports.

monday.com's dashboards are visually excellent, with charts, battery widgets, Gantt and workload widgets, scheduled reports and AI summaries. The key limit is how many boards one dashboard can combine: 1 on Free and Basic, 5 on Standard, 20 on Pro and 50 on Enterprise. A team running a dozen projects on separate boards will need Pro for one cross-project dashboard, and some Capterra reviewers mention workarounds for reporting across many boards.

Collaboration and guest access

Both tools score 9/10 for collaboration in our review. Each task or item has its own conversation with comments, @mentions and files, so decisions stay with the work. Wrike adds formal approvals and shared dashboards; monday.com adds collaborative docs and unlimited free read-only viewers on paid plans, which is useful for executives and clients who only need to look. monday.com guests (external editors) start on Standard.

Users of both mention notification overload in busy accounts. Agree notification settings and @mention habits early, and use automations to notify the right person rather than everyone.

Integrations

monday.com advertises more than 850 integrations and apps, counting its marketplace, including Slack, Microsoft Teams, Gmail, Outlook, Zoom, Jira, GitHub, Salesforce, HubSpot, Zapier and Make, plus an API and an MCP server for AI assistants. Remember that integration actions count against the monthly limit on your plan, and some marketplace apps carry their own cost.

Wrike's directory lists around 60 integrations, including Microsoft Teams, Slack, Salesforce, Google Sheets, Power BI, Tableau, Miro, ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot, plus an open API. Two add-ons go further: Wrike Integrate automates workflows across cloud and on-premises systems, and Wrike Sync keeps work aligned through two-way sync with 22 systems, including Jira, GitHub, Salesforce and HubSpot. Both are included in Apex and sold as add-ons on other plans.

If you need a long list of ready-made connectors, monday.com has the edge. If you need deep two-way sync between business teams and engineering tools, Wrike Sync is a strong option.

Security and administration

Both are mature enterprise platforms. monday.com's trust centre lists SOC 1, SOC 2 and SOC 3 reports, ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018, 27032 and 27701, CSA STAR and TX-RAMP, with a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement on Enterprise. SAML single sign-on, SCIM, audit logs and IP restrictions are Enterprise features.

Wrike offers granular permissions, customisable user types, locked spaces and single sign-on on higher tiers, and its website displays AICPA, BSI and CSA STAR compliance badges. Wrike Lock, an add-on, lets organisations control the encryption keys protecting their data, which is valued in regulated industries. On either platform, confirm current certifications and their scope in the vendor's trust documentation during procurement.

Free plans and trials

Wrike's Free plan includes board and table views and basic task management; monday.com's Free plan covers up to 2 seats, 3 boards and 3 docs. Neither free plan includes Gantt charts or automation. Wrike offers a 14-day free trial and monday.com a 14-day trial of its Pro plan; both say no credit card is required. How to switch between Wrike and monday.com

Switching platforms is mostly a design and adoption exercise, not a data one.

  1. Inventory active work. List live projects, recurring processes, forms and dashboards. Archive finished work in the old tool or export it.
  2. Map structures. monday.com boards, groups and items roughly map to Wrike projects or folders, sections and tasks. Decide how columns become Wrike custom fields (or the reverse) before importing.
  3. Import a sample. Both tools import spreadsheets, so export a board or project to Excel or CSV and test field mapping for owners, dates and statuses on one project.
  4. Rebuild forms and automations. These do not transfer. Recreate the essential ones first, and check monday.com's monthly action limits or Wrike's plan features for what you need.
  5. Pilot with one team for two to four weeks, using the trial, then adjust the structure.
  6. Set a cut-over date and keep the old tool read-only for reference for a few months.
Tip

If you are moving to Wrike from monday.com, use the change as a chance to standardise: agree one set of statuses and custom fields per type of work, rather than recreating every board's columns one for one.

Frequently asked questions

Is Wrike better than monday.com?

Wrike is better for complex, multi-project work that needs detailed scheduling, resource planning, approvals and portfolio reporting; monday.com is better for fast adoption and visual, flexible workflows. In our review Wrike scores 9.4/10 and monday.com 9.1/10, while monday.com has higher user ratings on G2 and Capterra.

Which is cheaper, Wrike or monday.com?

It depends on team size. Wrike Team costs $10 per user/month for 2 to 15 users, which is often cheaper for small teams. monday.com Standard ($12) and Pro ($19) per seat are usually cheaper than Wrike Business ($25) for larger teams, but seats are sold in buckets with a 3-seat minimum (annual prices, checked 7 October 2026).

Why does monday.com charge for seats I do not use?

monday.com sells paid plans in seat buckets that start at 3 seats and then rise in multiples of 5, so a team of 6 pays for 10 seats. Free viewers do not use a seat, but everyone who edits does. Wrike charges per user, with a 2 to 15 user range on Team and 5 to 200 on Business.

Which is easier to use, Wrike or monday.com?

monday.com. Its visual boards and templates let non-technical teams start in minutes, and we score it 9/10 for ease of use against Wrike's 7/10. Wrike is straightforward for basic tasks but takes more setup and training for custom workflows, dashboards and reporting.

Which has better Gantt charts, Wrike or monday.com?

Wrike. Its interactive Gantt charts, from the Team plan, support all four dependency types with lead and lag, automatic rescheduling, critical path, float and baselines. monday.com's timeline and Gantt views start on Standard, with milestones and critical path on Pro according to its plan documentation.

Which is better for resource management?

Wrike. It includes workload views and time tracking with timesheets, and Pinnacle adds advanced resource and capacity planning, budgeting and BI. monday.com offers a workload view and time tracking on Pro, with full resource and portfolio management on Enterprise.

Which is better for marketing and creative teams?

Both are strong. Wrike suits teams that need request forms, built-in proofing and named approvers on creative assets. monday.com suits teams that want colourful campaign boards and content calendars that everyone adopts quickly.

Which is better for agencies?

Wrike suits agencies that need resource visibility across clients, time tracking and approvals in one platform. monday.com suits agencies that want simple client boards with free viewers. Time tracking needs Pro on monday.com, and resource management across clients needs Enterprise.

Which is better for large enterprises?

Both serve large enterprises. Wrike scores higher for scalability in our review, thanks to portfolio and resource management, advanced reporting and BI, Wrike Integrate and Sync, and Wrike Lock. monday.com Enterprise offers portfolio and resource management, strong security certifications and a 99.9% uptime SLA.

Which has better AI, Wrike or monday.com?

Both are capable. Wrike includes AI Essentials from Team and adds Copilot and configurable AI agents through AI Elite from Business. monday.com includes sidekick, AI agents and AI blocks on paid plans, powered by a monthly pool of AI credits. Choose based on the workflows you want AI to support and model usage costs.

Do Wrike and monday.com have free plans and trials?

Yes. Wrike's Free plan includes board and table views and basic task management; monday.com's Free plan covers up to 2 seats, 3 boards and 3 docs. Wrike offers a 14-day free trial and monday.com a 14-day Pro trial, and both say no credit card is required.

Which has more integrations?

monday.com advertises more than 850 integrations and apps, including its marketplace. Wrike lists around 60 integrations, plus Wrike Integrate for cross-system automation and Wrike Sync for two-way sync with 22 systems such as Jira, GitHub and Salesforce.

Can I switch from monday.com to Wrike?

Yes. Export boards to Excel or CSV, map columns to Wrike custom fields, import a sample project to check owners, dates and statuses, then rebuild forms and automations and pilot with one team during Wrike's 14-day trial.

What are the best alternatives to Wrike and monday.com?

Asana for cross-team task management linked to goals, ClickUp for many features at lower prices, and Smartsheet for spreadsheet-style enterprise planning. See our Wrike alternatives and monday.com alternatives.
