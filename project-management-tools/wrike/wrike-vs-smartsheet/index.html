Wrike vs Smartsheet: Which Is Better in 2026?

Wrike vs Smartsheet comes down to how your team likes to work. Wrike is a broad, all-in-one work management platform with request intake, proofing, resource planning and portfolio reporting, and it is our #1 tool overall (9.4/10). Smartsheet turns a familiar spreadsheet grid into connected project plans with strong Gantt charts and enterprise portfolio controls (8.6/10). Below we compare them on features, pricing, AI, ease of use and user ratings so you can pick the right one.

Key takeaways

  • Overall: Wrike scores 9.4/10 and Smartsheet 8.6/10 in our review; Wrike wins four of seven categories and the other three are ties.
  • Choose Wrike for cross-team workflows, structured request intake, proofing and approvals, and resource and portfolio planning in one platform.
  • Choose Smartsheet if your organisation thinks in spreadsheets and wants grid-based plans, roll-up reports and standardised portfolios through Control Center.
  • Price: Wrike has a free plan and paid plans from $10 per user/month; Smartsheet has no free plan and starts at $9 per member/month (both billed annually).
  • Ease of use is a tie (7/10 each): Smartsheet feels instantly familiar to Excel users, while Wrike's breadth takes longer to configure but pays off for multi-team work.

Wrike vs Smartsheet: Quick Verdict

Wrike is the better choice for most growing teams and enterprises that want projects, requests, approvals, resources and reporting in one place. It wins on feature depth, planning and resource management, collaboration and Agile support in our scoring, and it is the more natural fit for marketing, creative, professional services and cross-functional teams.

Smartsheet is the better choice for spreadsheet-minded organisations, such as operations, finance, construction and many PMOs, that want to turn scattered Excel trackers into governed, connected plans. Its grid, Gantt view and cross-sheet reports are excellent, and Control Center is one of the strongest tools on the market for provisioning standardised projects at scale.

Both are mature, enterprise-ready platforms, and both tie on ease of use, value for money and scalability in our review. The deciding question is usually the working style you want: a configurable work platform with many views (Wrike) or a powerful spreadsheet-style planner (Smartsheet).

What Is Wrike?

Wrike is a cloud-based work and project management platform founded in 2006 and owned by Symphony Technology Group since 2023. It organises work into spaces, folders, projects and tasks, and lets each team see the same work as a list, board, table, Gantt chart, calendar or dashboard.

Its strength is breadth: interactive Gantt charts with the critical path and baselines, request forms with conditional logic, custom workflows and item types, proofing and approvals, workload and resource planning, cross-project dashboards and, on higher tiers, budgeting, advanced reporting and BI. Wrike AI adds summaries, writing help, Copilot and configurable AI agents.

Wrike suits teams that want one system of record for all their work, rather than a task tool, a proofing tool and a resource tool stitched together.

What Is Smartsheet?

Smartsheet is a cloud-based work management platform launched in 2006 and taken private by Blackstone and Vista Equity Partners in January 2025. Work lives in sheets: grids of rows and columns where each row can be a task with an owner, dates, dependencies, a status and a discussion thread.

On top of sheets, Smartsheet adds Gantt, board, calendar, timeline and table views, forms, no-code automation, cross-sheet reports and dashboards. Enterprise customers get Portfolios and Scenario Planning, and Advanced Work Management adds Control Center, Dynamic View and Data Shuttle for running standardised portfolios.

Smartsheet suits organisations that already run projects and processes in spreadsheets and want them shared, automated and governed.

Ease of Use and Learning Curve

Both tools score 7/10 for ease of use in our review, but for different reasons.

Smartsheet is quick to start for anyone who knows Excel. A sheet looks like a spreadsheet, you can import an existing file, and basic editing needs almost no training. The learning curve arrives later: cross-sheet formulas, report logic, automation and permission design all take time, and the grid is less visual than board-first tools, which can slow adoption among non-spreadsheet users.

Wrike is straightforward for creating, assigning and updating tasks, but its breadth means more decisions up front: spaces, workflows, custom fields, request forms, dashboards and permissions. Reviewers consistently praise its flexibility and visibility while mentioning onboarding effort and a dense interface. Once configured, though, most users only see the views and fields that matter to them.

Tip

In a trial, rebuild one real process in each tool with the people who will use it. Spreadsheet-heavy teams usually feel at home in Smartsheet within a day; cross-functional teams usually get more from Wrike once workflows and request forms are set up.

Views, Planning and Gantt Charts

Both tools are strong for date-driven work, which is one reason PMOs shortlist them together.

Wrike

Wrike's interactive Gantt charts are included from the Team plan. They support all four dependency types with lead and lag, automatic rescheduling, milestones, the critical path, float and baselines for planned-versus-actual tracking. Board and table views are available on every plan, including Free, and dashboards pull work together across projects.

Smartsheet

Smartsheet's Gantt view is available on all plans and sits on the same rows as the grid, so editing a date in either place updates both. It supports dependencies, milestones and critical path highlighting, and row hierarchy turns a flat list into phases and subtasks. Board and calendar views are on every plan, and timeline view starts on Business.

Verdict: a close call. Smartsheet's grid-plus-Gantt is excellent for people who plan in rows; Wrike adds baselines and more ways to view the same work, which suits mixed teams. Our free Gantt chart template is a useful way to sketch a plan before you choose.

Resource and Portfolio Management

This is the biggest functional difference between the two, and Wrike scores 10/10 against Smartsheet's 9/10 for planning and resource management in our review.

Resource and capacity planning

Wrike shows workload and capacity across the team, accounts for working hours, non-working days and holidays, and supports time tracking with approved timesheets. Advanced resource and capacity planning, role-based planning and budgeting are part of the Pinnacle plan, so organisations that need them get them inside the same platform.

Smartsheet offers workload tracking from the Business plan and Scenario Planning on Enterprise. Fuller resource management (capacity planning, time tracking, demand management, skill-based assignments and project budgets) is a separate Resource Management add-on with its own set-up.

Portfolio management

Smartsheet is very strong here. Portfolios on Enterprise give a standard view of related projects, and Control Center (in Advanced Work Management or as an add-on) provisions new projects from blueprints, pushes changes across existing projects and rolls up portfolio reporting.

Wrike handles portfolios through shared spaces, custom item types and fields, cross-project dashboards and, on Pinnacle and Apex, advanced reporting and BI. Its advantage is that portfolio data, resource data and the day-to-day work all live in the same system.

For background on the techniques, see capacity planning, resource allocation and program vs project vs portfolio management.

Collaboration, Requests and Approvals

Wrike scores 9/10 and Smartsheet 8/10 for collaboration in our review.

Wrike keeps conversations on the work itself: comments, @mentions, attachments, activity streams and formal approvals with named approvers. Request forms with conditional logic create and route tasks automatically, and external forms let clients submit work without a Wrike account. Built-in proofing lets reviewers mark up images, videos and documents, a long-standing strength for creative and marketing teams.

Smartsheet handles collaboration at row and sheet level: conversations, @mentions, attachments and notifications. Forms with conditional logic feed rows straight into sheets, and proofing is available on Business and above. Its user model is cost-friendly for stakeholders: Contributors are free and can view and comment, and Guests are free on Business and above.

If your work starts as a request from another team or a client, Wrike's intake-to-approval flow is the more complete experience. Our free work request template shows the information a good intake form should capture.

Automation and AI

Automation

Both tools offer no-code, trigger-and-action automation. Wrike automates assignment, status changes, notifications and request routing, with more advanced automation, AI agents and cross-tool automation through Wrike Integrate on higher tiers or as add-ons. Smartsheet automates reminders, approval requests, row updates and moves, and document generation; Pro is limited to 250 automations per month, while Business and above are unlimited.

AI

Wrike AI comes in two tiers. AI Essentials (content creation and editing, comment summaries, natural-language form and rule creation, AI highlights) is included from Team. AI Elite (Wrike Copilot, AI dashboard widgets, configurable AI agents and the Whiteboard assistant) starts with an allowance on Business that grows on Pinnacle and Apex.

Smartsheet AI includes Smart Assist (summaries, project health checks and actions), Smart Columns that apply AI to every row, an AI dashboard builder, formula generation and data analysis on all plans, plus an MCP server that connects sheets to external assistants from the Business plan.

Smartsheet's AI is especially handy for querying and summarising structured sheet data; Wrike's agents go further in automating workflow steps such as validating requests and routing work.

Reporting and Dashboards

Smartsheet reporting is built on roll-ups: reports pull matching rows from many sheets, dashboards present them to stakeholders, and Portfolios and Control Center add portfolio-level reporting. Pro dashboards are limited to 10 widgets. Data can also be pushed to Power BI or Tableau.

Wrike includes shareable dashboards from the Team plan, with widgets for status, overdue work, workload and progress across projects, and advanced reporting and BI on Pinnacle and Apex. In Capterra's topic summaries, around 96% of Wrike reviews that mention project visibility and dashboards are positive.

Both can produce a reliable weekly project status report without manual copying, as long as projects use consistent fields.

Integrations

Smartsheet says it has over 175 integrations, including Microsoft Teams, Outlook, Slack, Google Workspace, Salesforce, Jira, DocuSign, Power BI and Tableau. Bridge offers low-code cross-system workflows, the API is available on Business and above, and Data Shuttle automates imports and exports.

Wrike lists around 60 integrations, including Microsoft Teams, Slack, Salesforce, Google Sheets, Power BI, Tableau and Miro, plus an open API. Wrike Integrate automates workflows across other systems, and Wrike Sync offers two-way sync with 22 tools such as Jira, GitHub, HubSpot and Excel; both are included in Apex and available as add-ons on other plans.

If you depend on a long list of enterprise connectors, Smartsheet's catalogue is larger. If you need engineering and business teams to share live work across tools, Wrike Sync is a strong option.

Security and Administration

Both platforms are built for enterprise governance and score 10/10 for scalability in our review.

  • Wrike: granular permissions, customisable user types (full, collaborator and read-only), locked spaces, admin controls and SSO on higher tiers. Wrike Lock (add-on) lets customers control the encryption keys that protect their data. Wrike's site displays AICPA, BSI and CSA STAR compliance badges.
  • Smartsheet: its Trust Center lists SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, FedRAMP and HIPAA (with a BAA), with AES-256 encryption at rest, SAML SSO on Enterprise, SCIM and admin-enforced MFA. Enterprise customers can add customer-managed keys, data regions and Safeguard policies.

Smartsheet's FedRAMP listing is particularly relevant for US public-sector buyers. For any regulated use, confirm current certifications and plan availability in each vendor's trust documentation, and set up sound governance whichever tool you choose.

How to Switch Between Wrike and Smartsheet

  1. Pick one pilot project that represents your typical work, rather than migrating everything at once.
  2. Map the structure. Smartsheet sheets, rows and columns map roughly to Wrike projects, tasks and custom fields; parent rows become tasks with subtasks.
  3. Export and clean the data. Export the sheet to a spreadsheet file, remove formulas and keep one row per task with owner, dates and status.
  4. Import. Wrike can import Excel files on Team plans and above (up to 5,000 tasks per import) and map names in the "Assigned to" column to users. Smartsheet can create new sheets from imported Excel files.
  5. Rebuild what does not migrate: dependencies, automations, request forms, reports and dashboards usually need to be set up again.
  6. Run both tools in parallel for one reporting cycle, then switch the team over and archive the old workspace.

Plan the move like any small project, with an owner, a timeline and a short training session.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Wrike or Smartsheet?

In our review, Wrike scores 9.4/10 and Smartsheet 8.6/10. Wrike wins on feature depth, planning and resource management, collaboration and Agile support, and the two tie on ease of use, value and scalability. Wrike is better for cross-functional work in one platform; Smartsheet is better for spreadsheet-minded teams and standardised portfolios.

Is Wrike similar to Smartsheet?

They overlap a lot: both offer Gantt charts, boards, forms, automation, dashboards, AI and enterprise security. The difference is the model. Smartsheet is built around spreadsheet-style sheets, while Wrike is built around tasks, projects and custom workflows that you can view in many ways.

Is Wrike cheaper than Smartsheet?

Smartsheet is slightly cheaper per seat on list price: Pro is $9 and Business $19 per member/month, against Wrike's Team at $10 and Business at $25 per user/month (billed annually, checked 7 October 2026). Wrike has a free plan, Smartsheet's Contributors are free, and add-ons can change the total for either tool.

Does Smartsheet have a free plan like Wrike?

No. Smartsheet does not list a free plan; it offers a 30-day free trial of the Business plan. Wrike has a Free plan with basic task management and board and table views, plus a 14-day trial of paid features.

Which is easier to learn, Wrike or Smartsheet?

Both score 7/10 for ease of use in our review. Smartsheet is quicker to start for Excel users because the grid is familiar. Wrike needs more set-up because of its many options, but once workflows and views are configured, everyday use is straightforward.

Which is better for resource management?

Wrike. It includes workload and capacity views on paid plans and advanced resource planning and budgeting on Pinnacle. Smartsheet offers workload tracking on Business and Scenario Planning on Enterprise, but full resource management is a separate add-on.

Which is better for portfolio management and PMOs?

Both are strong. Smartsheet's Control Center is excellent for provisioning standardised projects from blueprints. Wrike combines portfolio dashboards, custom item types, resource planning and BI on its top tiers in the same system as the day-to-day work.

Which has better Gantt charts?

Both have capable Gantt charts with dependencies and the critical path. Smartsheet includes Gantt on every plan, tied to its grid. Wrike includes it from the Team plan and adds baselines for planned-versus-actual tracking.

Which has better AI features?

They focus on different things. Wrike's AI Elite tier adds Copilot and configurable AI agents that automate workflow steps, from the Business plan. Smartsheet includes Smart Assist, Smart Columns, formula generation and an AI dashboard builder on all plans, which is especially useful for structured sheet data.

Is Wrike or Smartsheet better for marketing teams?

Wrike is usually the better fit for marketing and creative teams, thanks to request forms, custom workflows, proofing and approvals. Smartsheet works well for marketing calendars and plans, especially with Brandfolder for digital assets.

What are the limitations of Smartsheet compared with Wrike?

Smartsheet has no free plan, is less visual than board-first tools, charges extra for full resource management and some portfolio apps, and reviewers report slower performance on very large sheets. In return it offers a familiar grid and excellent roll-up reporting.

Can I move from Smartsheet to Wrike?

Yes. Export your sheets to Excel, clean the data and import it into Wrike, which accepts Excel files on Team plans and above. Dependencies, automations, forms and dashboards usually need rebuilding, so pilot one project first.

Which is closer to Excel, Wrike or Smartsheet?

Smartsheet. Its sheets look and behave much like spreadsheets, with formulas and cell links. Wrike's table view also feels familiar to spreadsheet users, but Wrike is organised around tasks and workflows rather than cells.
