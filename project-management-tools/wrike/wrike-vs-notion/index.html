Wrike vs Notion: Which Is Better for Project Management?

Wrike vs Notion compares a dedicated work management platform with a flexible, docs-first workspace. Notion is excellent for wikis, specs and lightweight project tracking that you design yourself; Wrike gives you ready-made project management with Gantt scheduling, workload views, request intake, approvals and cross-project reporting. In our review Wrike scores 9.4/10 and Notion 8.0/10: choose Notion when knowledge and flexibility matter most, and Wrike when structured delivery across many projects does.

Quick verdict

Notion is the better choice when your work is mostly written: product specs, meeting notes, wikis, editorial calendars and light project boards that connect to them. It is flexible, pleasant to use and strong value, and it suits small and medium teams that are happy to design their own system. Its limits show when projects multiply: there is no native time tracking or resource management, its timeline has no critical path or baselines, and reporting has to be built by hand.

Wrike is the better choice when delivery itself is the challenge: many projects at once, several departments or clients, incoming requests, approvals, and managers who need to see workload and status without asking. It takes longer to set up and learn, and it is not a wiki, but it provides the project controls that Notion leaves you to build. That depth is why Wrike ranks first in our top 10 project management tools.

Tip

If your team spends more time writing about the work than scheduling it, start with Notion. If people keep asking who is overloaded, which projects are late and where a request came from, Wrike is the better fit.

What is Wrike?

Wrike is a cloud-based work and project management platform for teams from a handful of people to large enterprises. Work is organised into spaces, folders, projects and tasks with custom statuses and fields, and the same items can be viewed as a list, board, table, Gantt chart, calendar or dashboard.

Its strength is the operational side of project delivery: request forms that create and route work, custom workflows for each team, proofing and approvals, workload and capacity planning, portfolio views and reporting, with AI and automation on top.

What is Notion?

Notion is a connected workspace built from pages and databases. Every page is made of blocks, and any page can hold a database whose items have properties such as status, owner and dates. Project management in Notion is built on those databases, shown as tables, boards, timelines, calendars or charts, and linked to each other with relations and rollups.

Teams use Notion for wikis, specs, meeting notes, roadmaps, sprint boards and request forms, with Notion AI, Notion Agent and Custom Agents adding AI assistance. It is neither a to-do app nor a classic project suite: it is a set of building blocks that can become a project system. Our Notion review covers it in depth.

Project planning and scheduling

Gantt charts and timelines

Wrike's interactive Gantt charts start on the Team plan. They support all four dependency types with lead and lag, automatic rescheduling, milestones, the critical path, float and baselines for comparing plan with actual. This is what date-driven work such as launches, events and client programmes needs.

Notion's timeline view plots database items with date ranges on a Gantt-style chart. You can drag items to change dates, and Notion highlights dependencies so teams can spot blocked work. It works well for roadmaps and high-level plans, but Notion's documentation does not describe critical path, baselines or lead and lag, so complex schedules are harder to manage.

Sprints and Kanban

Both tools offer Kanban-style boards on every plan, including Free. Notion can add sprints to a task database, with a sprint board, sprint planning and backlog views, sprints of one to eight weeks and automatic rollover of unfinished work. Wrike supports Agile boards, sprints and custom workflows, and its AI agents can handle routine sprint operations. Neither replaces a specialist engineering tracker, but both cover the basics of sprint planning; Wrike scores higher in our Agile category (8/10 vs 6/10).

Docs, wikis and knowledge management

This is Notion's home ground. Pages are fast to write, easy to nest and can embed databases, files, designs and code; wikis gather them into a searchable knowledge base, and Business adds verified pages. Because each database item is itself a page, a task can contain its full brief, decisions and discussion, so the reasoning behind the work stays next to the work.

Wrike stores briefs in task and project descriptions, attached files and comments, and offers Wrike Whiteboard as an add-on at $15 per user/month. That is enough for project context, but Wrike is not designed as a company wiki, which is why our feature table marks its docs and wiki support as partial. If you use Wrike, keep a simple project brief template for each project and link longer documentation from the project.

Resource management, time tracking and portfolios

This is the clearest difference between the two tools. Wrike shows workload and capacity across projects: who is assigned to what, how much effort is allocated and who is overloaded, with working hours and holidays considered, plus time tracking with approved timesheets. Pinnacle adds advanced resource and capacity planning, budgeting and financial tracking, and portfolio views let a PMO see status and risk across every project.

Notion has no native time tracking, workload view or resource scheduling. Teams approximate them with number properties for estimates, rollups and third-party time-tracking integrations, which works for small teams but becomes fragile at scale.

We score Wrike 10/10 and Notion 5/10 for planning and resource management. For agencies billing hours, professional services firms and anyone doing capacity planning or resource allocation, this usually decides the comparison.

Collaboration, request intake and approvals

We score both tools 9/10 for collaboration, but they collaborate differently. Notion is built for real-time co-editing: several people can work on a page at once, comment on any block, @mention colleagues and pages, and invite guests to specific pages (10 on Free, unlimited from Plus). On Capterra, its collaborative workspace is one of the most positively reviewed themes.

Wrike focuses collaboration on the work item: comments, @mentions, files, activity streams, approvals with named approvers and proofing of images, videos and documents. Request forms with conditional logic create complete, routed tasks, and external forms let clients submit work through a link. Notion forms also send responses straight into a database, but conditional logic needs Business, and there is no built-in proofing.

For marketing, creative and agency teams that live on briefs, reviews and sign-offs, Wrike's approvals and intake are a practical advantage. Our free work request template shows what a good intake form should collect, whichever tool you choose.

Automation and AI

Automation

Wrike's rules engine reacts to status changes, form submissions and dates by assigning people, changing statuses, creating subtasks or notifying teams; Wrike Integrate (an add-on, included in Apex) extends automation across other systems. Notion's database automations run when a page is added, a property changes or a schedule fires, and can edit pages, send notifications, post to Slack, send Gmail emails and call webhooks. They need a paid plan and are simpler than Wrike's for multi-step logic.

AI

Wrike includes AI Essentials from the Team plan: content creation and editing, comment summaries, natural-language creation of forms and rules, AI highlights and mobile inbox prioritisation. From Business, a pooled monthly allowance of AI Elite actions unlocks Wrike Copilot, AI dashboard widgets, AI agents and the Whiteboard assistant, with larger allowances on Pinnacle and Apex.

Notion AI, including Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes and Enterprise Search, is included on Business and Enterprise; Free and Plus get a limited trial. Since 4 May 2026, Custom Agents run on Notion credits, a paid add-on for Business and Enterprise. Notion's AI is particularly good at writing, meeting notes and searching across connected apps; Wrike's is more focused on project operations such as triaging requests, checking risks and reporting on portfolios.

Whichever you choose, AI agents in both tools consume usage allowances on every run, so budget for them before rolling them out widely. Our guide to AI project management tools compares how vendors package AI.

Reporting and dashboards

Wrike includes shareable dashboards from the Team plan, with widgets for tasks by status, overdue work, workload and project progress, and reports across projects. Pinnacle and Apex add advanced reporting and BI across portfolios, resources and financials.

Notion builds reporting from database views, formulas, rollups and charts (one chart on Free, unlimited from Plus), and Business adds page analytics. A well-built dashboard page can show projects by status and overdue tasks by owner, but there are no packaged portfolio, workload or burndown reports, so someone has to build and maintain them. If leadership needs consistent reporting across many projects, see our guide to managing multiple projects for what that reporting should show.

Ease of use and setup

We score both tools 7/10 for ease of use, for opposite reasons. Notion is easy to start: writing pages and ticking off tasks takes minutes. The learning curve arrives when you build a project system with relations, rollups, formulas and automations, and G2 users list a steep learning curve for advanced setups among its main drawbacks. A blank workspace is liberating for some teams and paralysing for others, and workspaces can sprawl without clear owners. Performance is the most consistent Capterra complaint, especially with very large databases.

Wrike gives you project management features ready to use, so you configure rather than build. Basic task management is quick to learn, but designing spaces, workflows, custom fields, request forms and dashboards takes time, and the interface is denser. Its learning curve is the most common complaint in Wrike reviews, so plan for an administrator and some onboarding.

Integrations

Wrike's directory lists around 60 integrations, including Microsoft Teams, Slack, Salesforce, Google Sheets, Power BI, Tableau and Miro, with connectors for ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot and an open API. Wrike Sync (powered by Unito) provides two-way sync with 22 systems, including Jira, GitHub, HubSpot and Airtable, and Wrike Integrate automates workflows across cloud and on-premises systems; both are add-ons outside Apex.

Notion's integrations are strong at bringing context into pages: Slack and Google Drive connections from Plus; premium connections such as GitHub and Asana on Business; and Jira issues, Figma designs and GitHub pull requests embedded in pages. A public API supports custom integrations. Deep two-way project syncing is more limited than in Wrike.

Security and administration

Notion's security page states that it holds a SOC 2 Type 2 attestation and ISO 27001, 27017, 27018 and 27701 certifications, and offers a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement. SAML SSO, private teamspaces and granular database permissions start on Business; SCIM, the audit log, advanced security controls and zero data retention with AI providers are Enterprise features. Notion states that it does not train AI models on customer content.

Wrike offers granular permissions, customisable user types (full, collaborator and read-only), locked spaces, admin controls and single sign-on on higher tiers. Its website displays AICPA, BSI and CSA STAR compliance badges, and Wrike Lock, an add-on, lets you control the encryption keys that protect your data. For both vendors, confirm current certifications and their scope during procurement.

Which should you choose?

Choose Wrike if…

  • PMOs and professional services firms managing many projects and resources
  • Marketing and creative teams that need proofing and request intake
  • Organisations replacing several point tools with one platform
  • One of the most complete feature sets in the category matters to you

Choose Notion if…

  • Start-ups and small teams wanting docs and tasks together
  • Product teams managing specs and roadmaps
  • Excellent docs and knowledge base matters to you
Wrike reviewNotion review

Some common situations:

  • A start-up that needs a wiki, roadmap and task board in one place: Notion, on Plus or Business.
  • A product or engineering team writing specs and running light sprints: Notion for planning and documentation; see software project management.
  • A marketing or creative team handling many requests, reviews and sign-offs: Wrike, for request forms, proofing and approvals; see creative project management.
  • An agency or professional services firm tracking time and capacity across clients: Wrike.
  • A PMO standardising delivery across many projects: Wrike, for portfolio dashboards, baselines and resource planning.
  • A small team on a tight budget with simple projects: Notion, or Wrike's Free plan if you expect to grow into structured project management.

Still undecided? Our guide on how to choose project management software sets out the questions to ask.

Has your team outgrown Notion?

Many teams start in Notion and stay happily. These signs suggest your project work needs a dedicated platform such as Wrike:

  • Managers cannot see who is overloaded without asking people individually.
  • You need time tracking for billing or for estimating future work.
  • Delays on one task need to show their knock-on effect on the end date.
  • Requests arrive in chat and email and nobody owns triage.
  • Status reporting across projects takes hours of manual work each week.
  • Several half-maintained project databases exist and nobody trusts them.
  • Clients or other departments need a controlled way to review and approve work.

If two or three of these apply, run a pilot of Wrike on one real project while keeping Notion for documentation.

Using Wrike and Notion together

Wrike and Notion are often complementary rather than direct substitutes. A practical split is to keep Notion as the knowledge base, for handbooks, specs, meeting notes and decision logs, and run project delivery in Wrike, for tasks, schedules, requests, approvals, workload and reporting. Link each Wrike project to its Notion spec, and link from the spec back to the Wrike project.

Agree a simple rule so nobody has to check two places for the same thing, for example "if it has an owner and a due date, it lives in Wrike". You will pay for two tools, so check that each one has a clear job.

How to switch from Notion to Wrike

  1. Write down what Notion is not doing for you, such as workload, time tracking, intake or reporting. These become your success criteria.
  2. Export your project databases from Notion as CSV. Keep the documentation in Notion or export pages separately if you are moving everything.
  3. Design your Wrike structure first: spaces, a project template, statuses, and only the custom fields you will really use. Our project tracker template is a useful checklist.
  4. Import or rebuild one live project and run it during Wrike's 14-day free trial with the people who will use it every day.
  5. Set up request forms and dashboards so requesters and managers see the benefit straight away.
  6. Move remaining projects in waves, link each one to its Notion documentation, and archive the old Notion databases so there is one source of truth for delivery.

Moving from Wrike to Notion works the same way in reverse: export Wrike data to a spreadsheet, design your Notion databases and relations, then import and test with one project.

Alternatives to consider

If neither tool feels right, ClickUp combines docs with richer built-in project management, Asana offers dedicated task, goal and portfolio management with less setup, and monday.com is visual and highly customisable.

Frequently asked questions

Is Wrike better than Notion?

For structured project management, yes: Wrike scores 9.4/10 in our review against Notion's 8.0/10 and wins on feature depth, planning and resource management, Agile and scalability. Notion wins on value and is the better choice for docs, wikis and lightweight project tracking.

Can Notion replace Wrike?

For small teams with simple projects, often yes. For teams that need time tracking, workload and capacity planning, critical path scheduling, request intake with approvals or packaged cross-project reporting, Notion usually cannot replace Wrike without significant custom building and third-party tools.

Is Notion good for project management?

Yes, for lightweight to moderately complex projects. Notion offers task databases, boards, timelines, sprints, forms and automations connected to your docs, but it lacks native time tracking, resource management, baselines and packaged portfolio reporting.

Which is cheaper, Wrike or Notion?

Notion is usually cheaper. Both start at $10 per user/month billed annually (Wrike Team, Notion Plus), but Notion Business is $20 against Wrike Business at $25. Notion AI needs Business, while Wrike includes AI Essentials from Team (checked 7 October 2026).

Do Wrike and Notion have free plans?

Yes. Wrike's Free plan includes task management with board and table views. Notion's Free plan is aimed at individuals and limits blocks once a workspace has two or more members. Wrike also offers a 14-day free trial of paid plans.

Does Notion have Gantt charts like Wrike?

Notion has a timeline view that works like a simple Gantt chart, with date ranges and dependencies. It does not offer critical path or baselines, which Wrike's interactive Gantt charts include.

Does Notion have time tracking or resource management?

No. Notion has no native time tracking, workload view or resource scheduling; teams use number properties, rollups or third-party integrations instead. Wrike includes workload views and time tracking, with advanced capacity planning on Pinnacle.

Which is easier to learn, Wrike or Notion?

We score both 7/10. Notion is easier to start with but harder when you build complex databases; Wrike gives you ready-made project features but takes longer to configure and has a denser interface.

Which has better AI, Wrike or Notion?

They focus on different things. Notion AI is strong for writing, meeting notes and searching across connected apps, and is included from Business. Wrike AI focuses on project operations, such as summaries, request triage, risk checks and portfolio reporting, with AI Essentials from Team and Copilot and agents from Business.

Which is better for start-ups?

Notion is usually the better first tool for start-ups that want a wiki, roadmap and task board in one place. Wrike's Free or Team plan suits start-ups that already run many client or cross-team projects.

Which is better for agencies and marketing teams?

Wrike, in most cases. Its request forms, proofing and approvals, time tracking and workload views fit agency and marketing workflows. Notion works well for editorial calendars and briefs but has basic approvals and no time tracking.

Can I use Wrike and Notion together?

Yes. A common pattern is Notion for documentation and knowledge, and Wrike for project delivery, with each project linked to its spec. Agree which work lives where so nothing is tracked twice.

How do I move from Notion to Wrike?

Export your Notion project databases as CSV, design your Wrike spaces, statuses and fields, then import or rebuild one live project and pilot it during Wrike's 14-day free trial. Move remaining projects in waves and keep documentation linked.
