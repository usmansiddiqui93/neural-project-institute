Wrike Review 2026: Is It Worth It?

Wrike is a work and project management platform that brings tasks, projects, request intake, workflows, collaboration, reporting and resource planning into one place, for teams ranging from a handful of people to large enterprises. Its biggest strength is the depth of its workflow customisation and cross-project visibility; that same depth is why it takes longer to set up and learn than simpler tools. This review covers pricing, project management, collaboration, automation, AI, integrations, ease of use, reporting and overall value.

What Is Wrike?

Wrike is cloud-based project and work management software. It combines classic project management (plans, dependencies, Gantt charts, milestones) with broader work management: handling incoming requests, running repeatable workflows, tracking resources and reporting across a whole portfolio of work.

Organisations use it to manage:

  • Tasks and projects, from one-off jobs to multi-month programmes
  • Requests, collected through structured forms instead of email and chat
  • Campaigns and creative workflows, including reviews and approvals
  • Resource planning, showing who is working on what and who has capacity
  • Reporting and portfolio management, through dashboards and cross-project views
  • Workflow automation, using rules and AI agents to move work along

Instead of forcing one project style, Wrike lets teams look at the same work through different views, such as a list, a board, a table, a Gantt chart or a dashboard. It runs in the browser and as desktop and mobile apps, and it works for both traditional, plan-driven project management and more flexible, continuous operational work. It is not simply a to-do list app: its value comes from structure, shared data and visibility across teams.

Who Owns Wrike?

Wrike was founded in 2006 by Andrew Filev. It was acquired by Citrix in 2021, separated from Citrix in 2022 after Citrix was taken private, and has been owned by private equity firm Symphony Technology Group (STG) since 2023. Wrike says it is used by more than 20,000 organisations worldwide.

For buyers, the practical point is that Wrike is a mature, independently operated product with a long track record rather than an early-stage startup.

How Does Wrike Work?

Wrike organises work in a simple hierarchy that scales up as your needs grow:

  • Spaces are the top-level areas, usually one per team, department or major client.
  • Folders group related work inside a space, such as "Q4 campaigns" or "Client A".
  • Projects are folders with a start date, end date, owner and status.
  • Tasks and subtasks are the individual pieces of work, each with assignees, due dates and a status.
  • Custom fields add the data your team needs, such as budget, channel, priority or client.
  • Views show the same items as a list, board, table, Gantt chart or calendar, and dashboards summarise work across projects.

A typical workflow looks like this:

Request comes in → task or project created → work assigned → status tracked → team collaborates → progress reported → project completed

For example, a sales team submits a request form for a new product brochure. Wrike creates a task in the marketing space, assigns it to a designer, and moves it through statuses such as In design, In review and Approved. Comments and files stay on the task, and the marketing manager sees progress on a dashboard without chasing anyone for an update.

Wrike Key Features

Task Management

Tasks are the core building block. Each task can have subtasks, one or more assignees, start and due dates, dependencies, priorities, attachments, comments and a custom status. Recurring tasks handle routine work such as monthly reports, and every task has a clear owner, which makes accountability visible.

This matters most when many people or departments touch the same work. In a small team, a shared list is often enough. Once marketing, design, legal and sales all contribute to a launch, knowing exactly who owns each step, and what it is waiting on, is what keeps the work moving.

Project Management

Wrike supports full project planning: milestones, dependencies, timelines, project status and project-level custom fields such as budget, client or risk level. Because projects share the same structure and fields, managers can track many projects at once and roll them up into portfolio views and dashboards.

This is where Wrike separates itself from lighter tools. Teams juggling dozens of simultaneous projects can see deadlines, status and workload across all of them, rather than opening each project individually.

Gantt Charts

Wrike's interactive Gantt charts are included from the Team plan upwards. They support all four dependency types with lead and lag time, automatic rescheduling when a predecessor moves, milestones, the critical path, float, baselines for planned-versus-actual tracking, and drag-and-drop rescheduling.

For complex, date-driven projects such as product launches, events or construction-style schedules, this is genuinely useful: you can see what a delay will knock on to before it happens. For simple task tracking, the Gantt chart is more than most teams need, and a board or table will usually be quicker.

Kanban Boards

The board view shows tasks as cards in columns based on their status, and you drag a card to move it along. Columns reflect your custom statuses, so a creative team's board might run Requested, In design, In review, Approved. This gives a fast visual picture of where work is piling up.

Board view is available even on Wrike's Free plan, which makes it a reasonable way to try the product.

Table View

Table view presents work like a spreadsheet, with sorting, filtering, custom-field columns and bulk editing. It is the quickest way to manage hundreds of tasks or requests at once, and it is often what convinces spreadsheet-heavy teams to move into Wrike: the layout feels familiar, but the data is live and connected to the rest of the work.

Dashboards

Dashboards pull tasks, statuses, workload and metrics from across projects onto one screen. Typical widgets show tasks by status, overdue work, team workload and project progress, and each person can build views that suit them. Shareable dashboards are included from the Team plan.

Dashboards are most valuable for managers and stakeholders who need the big picture without opening every project. A department head can see which projects are late and who is overloaded in a few seconds.

Custom Workflows

Wrike lets each team define its own statuses and workflow stages, so marketing, IT and client services can each work their own way inside one platform. A few examples:

  • Marketing: Brief → Planning → In production → Review → Scheduled → Live.
  • Creative requests: Requested → Accepted → In design → Client review → Approved.
  • IT: New → Triage → In progress → Testing → Resolved.
  • Operations: Submitted → Approved → Scheduled → Done.
  • Client projects: Scoping → Delivery → Client sign-off → Invoiced.

Combined with automation, tasks can move through these stages and notify the right people automatically. Customisation is one of Wrike's major strengths, but it also drives the learning curve: every extra status, field and rule is something an administrator has to design and every user has to understand.

Request Forms

Request forms give stakeholders a structured way to ask for work. Each answer maps to a field on the task or project it creates, conditional logic shows follow-up questions, and rules can assign the work, set its status and file it in the right place. External forms let clients or non-Wrike users submit requests through a link.

For example, a marketing team can use one form to collect design requests, including the deadline, channel, campaign, brief and attachments. Instead of requests arriving by email or chat with half the information missing, every request lands in Wrike complete and already routed. If you are not ready for software, our free work request template follows the same idea.

Wrike Automation

Wrike's automation engine uses rule-based actions: when something happens (a status changes, a form is submitted, a date passes), Wrike does something (assigns a person, changes a status, sends a notification, creates a subtask). Automated assignment and request routing remove much of the repetitive coordination work.

For teams handling large volumes of requests, such as creative services, marketing operations or IT, this is practical and saves real time. Not every capability is on every plan, though: the more advanced automation, AI agents, and cross-tool automation through Wrike Integrate sit on higher tiers or add-ons.

Wrike AI Features

AI is now a substantial part of Wrike, not a minor add-on. Wrike splits it into two tiers. According to its help centre, AI Essentials covers content creation and editing, comment summaries, natural-language creation of forms and automation rules, AI highlights, Board AI and mobile inbox prioritisation. AI Elite covers Wrike Copilot, AI-generated dashboard widgets, AI agents and the Whiteboard assistant.

Wrike's pricing page includes AI Essentials from the Team plan, and Team stays on Essentials only. From the Business plan, accounts also get a pooled monthly allowance of AI Elite "actions", which grows on Pinnacle and Apex, and extra action packs can be bought. Agents consume actions for each step they run, so heavy automation needs planning.

AI Writing and Editing

Wrike AI can draft and rewrite text inside tasks: turning rough notes into a project brief, tightening a status update, or producing a first draft of documentation. It is most useful for the routine writing that surrounds project work, not as a replacement for a dedicated writing tool.

AI Summaries

Long comment threads can be summarised in one click, which helps managers and new team members catch up on a task without reading dozens of messages. On busy projects, this saves real time.

Wrike Copilot

Copilot is an AI assistant that answers questions about your work data in plain language, such as "What's the status of our Q3 launch?" or "What changed this week?". Wrike says it can also surface risks and dependencies, show who is overloaded or has capacity, and generate charts and action items. It is part of AI Elite, so it is available from the Business plan's allowance upwards.

Wrike AI Agents

AI agents are configurable automations that can take actions inside Wrike. Wrike describes agents for request validation (sending back incomplete requests), task triage and routing, compliance and risk checks, sprint and project operations (rolling over work, updating statuses, writing summaries), auto-assignment at scale and portfolio reporting. Agents are set up through a conversational builder, with testing before deployment, admin controls and an audit trail of what they did.

These are best understood as smart workflow automation, not autonomous employees. They follow the instructions and permissions you give them, they use up your AI action allowance on every run, and someone still needs to own the process they support. Availability and volume depend on your plan.

Wrike Collaboration Features

Collaboration happens on the work itself: comments and @mentions on tasks, file attachments, shared tasks, activity streams, notifications and formal approvals with named approvers. Keeping conversations attached to the task means decisions are recorded where the work lives, rather than scattered across email and chat.

Collaboration is strongest when Wrike becomes the single source of truth, so different departments see the same status and history. The downside is noise: on Capterra, notifications are one of the more frequent complaints, with reviewers describing them as excessive and hard to tune. Agree notification settings and @mention habits early, or people will start ignoring them.

Wrike Reporting and Analytics

Wrike's reporting works at two levels. Operational dashboards and reports, available on paid plans, show project status, workload, overdue tasks and trends, and are what most teams use day to day. Advanced reporting and BI, on Pinnacle and Apex, adds deeper analysis across portfolios, financials and resources for organisations that need executive and PMO-level insight.

Users on Capterra generally rate Wrike's visibility highly: in the topic summaries Capterra publishes, around 96% of reviews mentioning project visibility, dashboards and progress tracking are positive. Some reviewers mention a learning curve in configuring reports, so budget time for an administrator to set up the first set of dashboards properly.

Resource and Capacity Management

Wrike shows team workload and capacity: who is assigned to what, how much effort is allocated, and who is overloaded. Managers can rebalance work, account for working hours, non-working days and holidays, and use time tracking with approved timesheets.

Advanced resource and capacity planning, including role-based planning and budgeting, is listed among Wrike's Pinnacle capabilities. It is aimed at mature organisations such as PMOs and professional services firms rather than small task-management teams.

Wrike Integrations

Wrike's integration directory lists around 60 integrations covering CRM, messaging, BI, file storage, Microsoft, Google and software development tools. Examples include Microsoft Teams and Slack, Salesforce, Google Sheets, Power BI, Tableau, Miro, and connections for ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot. HubSpot is supported through Wrike Sync, and developers can use Wrike's open API.

Wrike Integrate

Wrike Integrate automates workflows across third-party software, including cloud and on-premises systems, using ready-made "recipes" and no-code builders. It is an additional capability: it is included in the Apex plan and sold as an add-on for other plans, so do not assume it comes with every subscription.

Wrike Sync

Wrike Sync, powered by Unito, keeps work aligned through two-way synchronisation between Wrike and other tools. Wrike says it supports 22 systems, including Jira, GitHub, Salesforce, HubSpot, Airtable, Excel and Zendesk. A common use is connecting engineering and business teams: developers stay in Jira or GitHub while marketing and product track the same items in Wrike. Like Integrate, Sync is included in Apex and otherwise an add-on.

Wrike Ease of Use

Initial Setup

Setting Wrike up well means designing spaces, project structures, workflows, custom fields, dashboards, request forms and permissions. Wrike provides templates, but because almost everything is configurable, setup takes longer than with simpler tools. A small team can be running in a day; a department-wide rollout with custom workflows and reporting is a project in itself.

Learning Curve

Basic task management is relatively straightforward, and most people can create, assign and update tasks within minutes. Advanced workflows, automations and reporting take more training, and complex organisations benefit from formal onboarding. New team members can initially find the number of options overwhelming. This is one of the most consistent themes in current Capterra feedback: reviewers value Wrike's flexibility and capabilities but repeatedly mention setup complexity and the effort of onboarding.

Interface

The interface is detailed and flexible, offers many views, and puts a lot of information within reach. The same density cuts the other way: it can feel crowded, deeply nested project structures can become hard to navigate, and the amount of information on screen can make finding things less intuitive. Wrike is easy to use for simple things and demanding for complex ones, so it is not fair to call it "easy to use" without that qualification.

Wrike Mobile App

Wrike's iOS and Android apps let you check and update tasks, respond to comments and @mentions, receive notifications, see progress and handle quick updates while away from your desk. AI Essentials includes mobile inbox prioritisation to surface what needs attention first.

Some recent Capterra reviewers describe the mobile experience as less capable or less polished than desktop. That is a recurring concern rather than a verdict that the app is poor; treat mobile as a companion for keeping up to date, and do planning, configuration and reporting on desktop.

Wrike Free Plan

The Free plan costs $0 and includes web, desktop and mobile apps, basic project and task management, and board and table views. It is realistic for individuals, freelancers or a very small team trying Wrike out or managing simple work. It is not equivalent to the paid platform: Gantt charts, shareable dashboards and most of the workflow, reporting and resource features that make Wrike worth choosing start on paid plans.

Wrike Team Plan

At $10 per user/month billed annually, Team is for 2 to 15 users and adds AI Essentials, shareable dashboards and interactive Gantt charts. It suits small teams that need real project planning and visibility, but its 15-user cap means growing teams will soon need Business.

Wrike Business Plan

At $25 per user/month billed annually, Business supports 5 to 200 users and adds templatised workspaces, standard integrations, more customisable workflows and an AI Elite starter allowance (which unlocks Copilot and agents). This is the plan most departments and multi-team organisations should evaluate first.

Wrike Pinnacle

Pinnacle is quote-based and adds advanced resource and capacity planning, budgeting and financial tracking, advanced reporting and BI, and three times the AI Elite actions of Business. It is aimed at mature project-management organisations, such as PMOs, agencies and professional services firms, that need resource and financial control across many projects.

Wrike Apex

Apex is also quote-based and is Wrike's enterprise plan for human- and AI-led workflows at scale. It adds ten times the AI Elite actions of Business, unlimited whiteboards, Wrike Integrate and Wrike Sync. It is not a small-business plan: it is designed for large organisations automating work across many teams and systems.

Wrike Free Trial

Wrike offers a 14-day free trial, and its pricing page says no credit card is required. Use the trial to rebuild one real workflow, such as your actual request process or a live project, with the people who will use it. Clicking around the interface will not tell you whether Wrike fits; running real work through it will.

What Do Wrike Customers Say?

On Capterra, Wrike is rated 4.4/5 from 3,041 reviews, with sub-scores of 4.3 for features, 4.2 for value for money and 4.3 for customer service (checked 7 October 2026). On G2, it is rated 4.2/5 from 4,537 reviews (checked 5 October 2026). The themes below summarise these third-party reviews in our own words, not Wrike's testimonials.

Positive themes. Reviewers most often praise centralising projects in one place, visibility across departments, flexible custom workflows, clear accountability, dashboards and reporting, the ability to manage large volumes of work, and cross-functional collaboration. In Capterra's topic summaries, collaboration (about 93% positive), project visibility (about 96% positive) and workflow automation (about 95% positive) stand out.

Negative themes. The most common complaints are the learning curve and complicated onboarding, interface density, pricing for small teams (about half of reviews discussing price are negative), minor bugs, excessive notifications, mobile limitations, and the time it takes to configure advanced features. Some long-time users also mention frustration when features change or are retired.

When reading reviews yourself, check whether a review was invited or incentivised by the vendor. Both Capterra and G2 label these, and filtering for non-incentivised reviews gives a more cautious picture. Read the critical reviews from companies similar in size to yours, not just the five-star ones.

Wrike Customer Support

Wrike provides a Help Center with feature guides, interactive training through Wrike Discover, webinars, and a user community. Higher plans and enterprise agreements add more hands-on onboarding and support options, and professional services are available for complex rollouts.

Support experience is mixed in third-party reviews: Capterra's customer service sub-score is a solid 4.3/5, but individual experiences vary. For large rollouts, ask during the sales process exactly which onboarding and support are included in your plan.

Wrike Security

Wrike offers granular access permissions, customisable user types (full, collaborator and read-only), account administration controls, locked spaces, and enterprise identity features such as single sign-on on higher tiers. Its website displays AICPA, BSI and CSA STAR compliance badges; confirm current certifications and their scope in Wrike's trust documentation as part of procurement.

Wrike Lock, an add-on, lets organisations control the encryption keys that protect their data in Wrike, so access can be revoked by the customer. It is a strong control for regulated industries, but it is one layer of security rather than a guarantee on its own: permissions, user management and good governance still matter.

Is Wrike Worth It?

Wrike is likely worth it if you:

  • Manage many projects at the same time.
  • Need clear ownership and status visibility across the organisation.
  • Have several departments collaborating on the same work.
  • Need custom workflows and structured request intake.
  • Rely on dashboards and reporting for decisions.
  • Need more advanced resource and capacity planning.
  • Are willing to invest time in setup and training.

It may not be worth it if:

  • Your team only needs a shared to-do list.
  • You want almost zero setup.
  • You manage only a handful of basic projects.
  • Budget is the overriding priority.
  • Your users are unlikely to adopt a complex platform.

Our verdict: Wrike's depth is both its biggest advantage and its biggest drawback. For organisations that will use its workflows, visibility, reporting and resource management, it is one of the most complete platforms available, which is why it ranks first in our project management software review. For small teams with simple needs, the same depth adds cost and complexity they do not need. Try it on a real workflow during the free trial before committing.

Frequently asked questions

Is Wrike good project management software?

Yes, for the right team. Wrike offers deep project and work management, strong customisation, cross-project visibility and solid reporting, and it rates 4.4/5 on Capterra. The trade-off is a steeper learning curve than simpler tools, so it suits teams with complex, multi-project work more than those needing a basic task list.

Is Wrike free?

Yes. Wrike has a Free plan at $0 per user/month with web, desktop and mobile apps, basic task and project management, and board and table views. Features such as Gantt charts, shareable dashboards and advanced workflows require a paid plan.

How much does Wrike cost?

Wrike costs $0 on the Free plan, $10 per user/month on Team and $25 per user/month on Business, with Pinnacle and Apex priced on request. Paid prices are monthly equivalents billed annually per user, and Business and higher plans are annual only (checked 7 October 2026).

Does Wrike have a free trial?

Yes. Wrike offers a 14-day free trial, and its pricing page states that no credit card is required. Use it to test a real workflow with your team.

Is Wrike easy to use?

Partly. Basic task and project management is accessible and quick to learn, but advanced configuration, such as custom workflows, automation and reporting, creates a steeper learning curve than simpler project management tools. Most organisations benefit from some onboarding.

What is Wrike best used for?

Wrike is best for project management across many projects, cross-functional work, marketing and creative workflows, PMO operations, resource planning, structured request management and reporting.

Is Wrike better than Asana?

Neither is universally better. Wrike suits teams that need deeper workflow customisation, reporting and resource management; Asana suits teams that prioritise simpler, faster adoption. See our Wrike vs Asana comparison.

Is Wrike better than monday.com?

It depends on the use case. Wrike offers more project management depth and resource control for complex, multi-project work; monday.com is usually easier to adopt and more visual. See our Wrike vs monday.com comparison.

Does Wrike have Gantt charts?

Yes. Interactive Gantt charts with dependencies, critical path and baselines are included from the Team plan.

Does Wrike have Kanban boards?

Yes. Wrike's board view works as a Kanban board with custom statuses, and it is available on every plan, including Free.

Does Wrike have AI?

Yes. AI Essentials (content creation and editing, comment summaries, natural-language form and rule creation, AI highlights, mobile inbox prioritisation) is included from the Team plan. AI Elite (Copilot, AI dashboard widgets, customisable AI agents and the Whiteboard assistant) starts on Business, with usage allowances that grow on Pinnacle and Apex.

What companies is Wrike best for?

Wrike is particularly well suited to mid-sized and enterprise organisations, agencies, marketing and creative teams, professional services firms and PMOs that manage complex workflows across many projects.

What are the main disadvantages of Wrike?

The main disadvantages are the learning curve, the effort needed to set it up well, a dense interface, the cost of advanced features on higher tiers or add-ons, and more functionality than very small teams need.

Does Wrike integrate with Slack and Microsoft Teams?

Yes. Wrike integrates with both Slack and Microsoft Teams, alongside around 60 other integrations including Salesforce, Google Sheets, Power BI, Tableau and Miro.

Is Wrike suitable for small businesses?

It can be, especially for small businesses that need structured workflows or client request intake. But small teams that only need basic task management may find simpler, cheaper tools a better fit.
