What is Wrike?

Wrike was founded in 2006 and is used by marketing, creative, professional services, PMO and operations teams worldwide. Its strength is breadth: tasks and projects, interactive Gantt charts, dashboards, workload and resource planning, cross-project portfolio views, request forms, automation, proofing and approvals, and advanced reporting all live in one platform.

That breadth makes Wrike our top pick for organisations that want a single system of record for all their work, rather than stitching together a task tool, a resource tool, a proofing tool and a reporting tool.

Key features

Planning and scheduling

Interactive Gantt charts with dependencies, critical path and baselines on paid plans, plus board, table and calendar views.

Resource and workload management

Workload charts show who is over- or under-allocated; higher tiers add effort allocation, job roles and capacity planning.

Portfolio and cross-project visibility

Spaces, folders and custom item types let PMOs track many projects with consistent fields, dashboards and reports.

Proofing and approvals

Built-in markup and approval workflows for images, videos and documents, a long-standing strength for creative and marketing teams.

Automation and integrations

No-code automations, request forms with conditional logic, and integrations with common tools; Wrike Integrate and Wrike Sync on top tiers.

AI

Wrike AI helps summarise work, draft content, and flag risks; AI allowances grow with plan level.

Reporting and BI

Dashboards on all paid plans; advanced reporting and BI on Pinnacle and Apex.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • One of the most complete feature sets in the category
  • Strong resource, workload and portfolio management
  • Built-in proofing and approvals for creative work
  • Highly configurable custom item types, fields and workflows
  • Scales well from teams to large enterprises with strong governance

Cons

  • Steeper learning curve than simpler tools
  • Interface can feel dense for new users
  • Advanced resource, budgeting and BI features sit on higher, quote-based tiers
  • Team plan is capped at 15 users

Who should use Wrike?

Great for

  • PMOs and professional services firms managing many projects and resources
  • Marketing and creative teams that need proofing and request intake
  • Organisations replacing several point tools with one platform

Look elsewhere if

  • Very small teams that only need a simple to-do board
  • Teams unwilling to invest time in setup and training

Frequently asked questions

What is Wrike?

Wrike is a project and work management tool from Wrike, Inc., launched in 2006. Wrike is a full-featured work management platform that brings project planning, resource management, portfolio views, creative proofing and business intelligence into one place. It takes longer to learn than lighter tools, but few competitors match its depth once a team scales.

How much does Wrike cost?

Free; paid from $10/user/month. Plans: Free: $0; Team: $10/user/month (annual); Business: $25/user/month (annual); Pinnacle: Contact sales; Apex: Contact sales. Prices checked 5 October 2026.

Yes. It suits small teams willing to invest some setup time; simpler options include Trello and Basecamp.

Is Wrike good for large enterprises?
