Wrike vs ClickUp: Which Is Better in 2026?

Wrike vs ClickUp is a choice between two feature-rich, all-in-one platforms with different priorities. Wrike is a structured work management platform with strong resource planning, request intake, proofing and portfolio reporting, and it is our #1 tool overall (9.4/10). ClickUp packs tasks, Docs, Chat, sprints and dashboards into very low-priced plans (8.9/10). Below we compare them on features, pricing, AI, ease of use and user ratings so you can choose with confidence.

Key takeaways

  • Overall: Wrike scores 9.4/10 and ClickUp 8.9/10 in our review. Wrike wins planning and resource management, collaboration and scalability; ClickUp wins Agile support and value for money.
  • Choose Wrike for structured cross-team workflows, request intake and approvals, resource and capacity planning, and enterprise-scale portfolios.
  • Choose ClickUp for the most features per dollar, built-in Docs and Chat, and strong sprint tools for product and software teams.
  • Price: ClickUp starts at $7 and Wrike at $10 per user/month (billed annually), but ClickUp's full AI costs $9 to $28 per user/month extra, while Wrike includes AI Essentials from its Team plan.
  • Ease of use is a tie (7/10 each): both take deliberate set-up; Wrike's challenge is depth, ClickUp's is breadth, and ClickUp reviewers report more lag on large Workspaces.

Wrike vs ClickUp: Quick Verdict

Wrike is the better choice for growing teams and enterprises that need structured workflows across departments, dependable resource and capacity planning, request intake and approvals, and portfolio reporting at scale. It leads in planning and resource management (10 vs 8), collaboration (9 vs 8) and scalability (10 vs 8) in our scoring.

ClickUp is the better choice for budget-conscious teams that want to replace several apps with one: tasks, Docs, Chat, Whiteboards, Goals, time tracking and sprints, all at low per-user prices. It leads on value for money (9 vs 7) and Agile and software teams (9 vs 8).

Both tools tie on ease of use and feature depth. If your organisation is scaling and needs governance, Wrike is the safer long-term platform; if you are a start-up or small team that values breadth and price, ClickUp is hard to beat.

What Is Wrike?

Wrike is a cloud-based work and project management platform founded in 2006 and owned by Symphony Technology Group since 2023. Work is organised into spaces, folders, projects and tasks, with custom item types, fields and statuses, and the same work can be viewed as a list, board, table, Gantt chart, calendar or dashboard.

Wrike's strength is structured breadth: interactive Gantt charts with the critical path and baselines, request forms with conditional logic, custom workflows, proofing and approvals, workload and resource planning, cross-project dashboards and, on higher tiers, budgeting, advanced reporting and BI. Wrike AI adds summaries, writing help, Copilot and configurable AI agents.

What Is ClickUp?

ClickUp is an all-in-one work platform founded in 2017 and headquartered in San Diego. Its hierarchy runs from Workspace to Spaces, Folders, Lists and tasks, and ClickUp says it offers more than 15 views, from List and Board to Gantt, Workload and Mind Map.

Alongside project management, ClickUp includes Docs and wikis, Whiteboards, ClickUp Chat, Forms, Goals, native time tracking and sprint tools. Its AI layer, ClickUp Brain, is sold mainly as paid add-ons. The result is one of the broadest feature sets available at a low price, with the trade-off that a good set-up takes deliberate design.

Ease of Use, Setup and Performance

Both tools score 7/10 for ease of use in our review, and both reward teams that plan their set-up before inviting everyone.

Wrike is quick for everyday tasks, and its learning curve comes from depth: spaces, workflows, custom item types, request forms, dashboards and permissions. Reviewers praise its visibility and flexibility while mentioning onboarding effort, a dense interface and notification volume. Once an administrator has configured workflows, most people work in a small number of familiar views.

ClickUp's learning curve comes from breadth. New users meet many menus, views and ClickApps at once, and Capterra reviewers rate its ease of use 4.3/5, the lowest of its four sub-scores. Performance is the most persistent criticism: in Capterra's topic summaries, about 78% of the 290 reviews mentioning lag and slowdowns are negative, mostly in large boards and busy Workspaces. Frequent product updates also move familiar controls.

Tip

Test both tools with a realistic volume of tasks, not an empty demo workspace. That shows you ClickUp's performance and Wrike's configuration effort before you commit.

Views, Planning and Gantt Charts

Both tools offer interactive Gantt charts with all four dependency types, automatic rescheduling, milestones, the critical path and baselines.

  • Wrike: Gantt charts are included from the Team plan, with lead and lag, float and drag-and-drop rescheduling. Board and table views are available on every plan, including Free, and dashboards span projects.
  • ClickUp: Gantt is a trial on Free Forever and unlimited from Unlimited, with slack time and baselines on paid plans. Timeline, Activity and Mind Map views are fully unlocked on Business. Board view includes WIP limits per column, which is unusual at this price.

Verdict: a draw for scheduling. ClickUp offers more view types; Wrike's Gantt is part of a more structured planning and reporting model that suits multi-project, date-driven work.

Resource Management and Portfolios

This is Wrike's clearest advantage, and it scores 10/10 against ClickUp's 8/10 for planning and resource management in our review.

Wrike shows workload and capacity across the team, accounting for working hours, non-working days and holidays, with time tracking and approved timesheets. Pinnacle adds advanced resource and capacity planning, role-based planning, budgeting and financial tracking, and advanced reporting and BI. That makes Wrike a natural fit for PMOs, agencies and professional services firms that plan people across many projects.

ClickUp includes native time tracking, Goals and Portfolios from the Unlimited plan, a Workload view showing each person's assigned effort, and portfolio-level workload management on Business. These are practical for small and mid-sized teams, but less mature than Wrike's resource and financial planning.

Agile and Sprint Support

ClickUp scores 9/10 and Wrike 8/10 for Agile and software teams in our review.

ClickUp treats sprints as dated Lists, rolls unfinished work into the next sprint automatically, supports sprint points on Business, and reports burndown, burnup, velocity and cumulative flow. GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket integrations link code to tasks, and Docs hold specs next to the backlog.

Wrike supports Agile teams through boards, custom workflows and templates, and its AI agents can handle sprint operations such as rolling over work, updating statuses and writing summaries. Wrike Sync keeps items aligned with Jira or GitHub, so developers can stay in their tools while business teams track the same work in Wrike.

Docs, Chat and Collaboration

Wrike scores 9/10 and ClickUp 8/10 for collaboration in our review, but they collaborate in different ways.

ClickUp brings documents and conversation into the tool. Docs support nested pages, real-time co-editing and comments that become tasks, and any Doc can be a wiki. ClickUp Chat adds channels, direct messages, threads and calls, and assigned comments turn feedback into action items. This is a real consolidation opportunity if your team is willing to move conversations out of Slack or Teams.

Wrike keeps collaboration on the work itself: comments, @mentions, attachments, activity streams and formal approvals with named approvers. It integrates with Slack and Microsoft Teams rather than replacing them, and Wrike Whiteboard is available as an add-on. The result is a cleaner record of decisions on each task, which matters when many departments touch the same work.

Requests, Proofing and Approvals

Both tools turn requests into tasks through forms with conditional logic, and both let people outside the tool submit requests.

Wrike goes further on structured intake and review. Each form answer maps to a field, rules assign and route the work, and built-in proofing lets reviewers mark up images, videos and documents before formal approval. For marketing, creative and agency teams with many stakeholders, this is one of Wrike's strongest areas.

ClickUp Forms are unlimited from the Unlimited plan (one on Free Forever) and include proofing on files, with unlimited proofing on Business. They cover most request workflows well at a lower price.

Automation and AI

Automation

Wrike automates assignment, status changes, notifications and request routing with rule-based triggers and actions, with advanced automation, AI agents and cross-tool automation through Wrike Integrate on higher tiers or add-ons. ClickUp offers more than 100 automation templates and an AI builder, but executions are metered: 100 on Free Forever, 1,000 on Unlimited, 5,000 per month on Business and 250,000 per month on Enterprise.

AI

Wrike AI is included in the plans. AI Essentials (writing and editing, comment summaries, natural-language form and rule creation, AI highlights) comes with Team, and AI Elite (Copilot, AI dashboard widgets, configurable AI agents and the Whiteboard assistant) starts with a pooled allowance on Business that grows on Pinnacle and Apex.

ClickUp Brain is mostly an add-on. Free Forever has limited trial access; Brain AI ($9 per user/month) adds the assistant, AI writing, enterprise search and AI chat with a choice of models; Everything AI ($28 per user/month) adds the AI Notetaker, AI Fields, AI automations and more Super Agent usage. ClickUp's Super Agents can create tasks, draft documents and run multi-step workflows.

Both vendors' AI is capable; the main difference for buyers is cost.

Reporting and Dashboards

Wrike includes shareable dashboards from the Team plan, with widgets for status, overdue work, workload and progress across projects, and advanced reporting and BI on Pinnacle and Apex. In Capterra's topic summaries, around 96% of Wrike reviews that mention project visibility and dashboards are positive.

ClickUp dashboards are actionable (you can change statuses or reassign tasks from them) and can be shared with clients, but they are a trial on Free Forever and Unlimited; unlimited dashboards with advanced cards start on Business.

For a weekly project status report, both work well. For executive and PMO-level reporting across a large portfolio, Wrike's top tiers go further.

Integrations

ClickUp claims more than 1,000 integrations, including those through Zapier and Make. Native options include Slack, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, GitHub, GitLab, Google Drive, HubSpot, Figma and Tableau, plus a public API, webhooks and an MCP server. It also has importers for Wrike, Asana, Trello, Jira and others.

Wrike lists around 60 integrations, including Microsoft Teams, Slack, Salesforce, Google Sheets, Power BI, Tableau and Miro, plus an open API. Wrike Integrate automates workflows across other systems, and Wrike Sync offers two-way sync with 22 tools such as Jira, GitHub and HubSpot; both are included in Apex and available as add-ons on other plans.

Security and Administration

Wrike scores 10/10 and ClickUp 8/10 for scalability and enterprise readiness in our review.

  • Wrike: granular permissions, customisable user types (full, collaborator and read-only), locked spaces, admin controls and SSO on higher tiers. Wrike Lock (add-on) lets customers control the encryption keys that protect their data. Wrike's site displays AICPA, BSI and CSA STAR compliance badges.
  • ClickUp: its security page lists SOC 1, SOC 2 and SOC 3 reports and several ISO certifications, including ISO 27001 and ISO 42001, with AES-256 encryption at rest. Two-factor authentication is on every plan, Google SSO on Business, and SAML SSO, SCIM, audit logs and data residency on Enterprise.

Both are credible for business use. Wrike's governance features and customer-managed encryption make it the stronger choice for large, regulated organisations; confirm current certifications in each vendor's trust documentation during procurement.

How to Switch Between Wrike and ClickUp

  1. Choose a pilot project that reflects your normal work, and agree who owns the migration.
  2. Design the target structure first. Wrike spaces, folders and projects map roughly to ClickUp Spaces, Folders and Lists; agree statuses and custom fields before importing.
  3. Import the data. ClickUp has a built-in importer for Wrike. To move into Wrike, export your data to a spreadsheet and use Wrike's Excel import (Team plans and above, up to 5,000 tasks per import), mapping the "Assigned to" column to users.
  4. Rebuild automations, forms and dashboards, which rarely transfer cleanly between tools.
  5. Train the team on the new structure and notification settings, then run both tools in parallel for one reporting cycle.
  6. Switch over and archive the old workspace once the pilot project is running smoothly.

Plan the move with a clear owner and timeline, and check our Kanban board template if you want a simple holding board during the transition.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Wrike or ClickUp?

In our review, Wrike scores 9.4/10 and ClickUp 8.9/10. Wrike wins on planning and resource management, collaboration and scalability; ClickUp wins on value for money and Agile support. Wrike is better for structured, multi-team work at scale; ClickUp is better for budget-conscious teams that want many tools in one.

Is ClickUp cheaper than Wrike?

Yes, on base price. ClickUp Unlimited is $7 and Business $12 per user/month billed annually, against Wrike's Team at $10 and Business at $25 (checked 7 October 2026). ClickUp's full AI costs $9 to $28 per user/month extra, while Wrike includes AI Essentials from Team, so compare totals with AI included.

Do Wrike and ClickUp have free plans?

Yes. Wrike's Free plan includes basic task management with board and table views. ClickUp's Free Forever plan includes unlimited members and tasks, Docs, boards and sprints, but only 60MB of storage and trial access to Gantt charts, dashboards and time tracking.

Which is easier to use, Wrike or ClickUp?

Both score 7/10 for ease of use in our review. Wrike's learning curve comes from its depth of configuration; ClickUp's comes from the number of features and views. ClickUp reviewers also report more lag in large Workspaces, so test both with realistic data.

Is Wrike or ClickUp better for small teams?

ClickUp usually suits small teams on a tight budget, thanks to its free plan and low prices. Wrike's Team plan (2 to 15 users) is a strong option for small teams that need Gantt charts, dashboards and structured workflows and expect to grow.

Which is better for agencies?

Wrike is stronger for agencies that need client request intake, proofing and approvals, and resource and budget planning across clients. ClickUp is a good low-cost option for agencies with simpler delivery processes and many staff.

Which is better for resource management?

Wrike. It offers workload and capacity views on paid plans and advanced resource planning, budgeting and financial tracking on Pinnacle. ClickUp has a Workload view and portfolio workload on Business, which suit smaller teams.

Which is better for Agile and Scrum teams?

ClickUp, in our scoring (9/10 vs 8/10). It has native sprints, sprint points, burndown, burnup and velocity reporting, and Git integrations. Wrike supports Agile teams through boards, workflows, AI agents and Wrike Sync with Jira and GitHub.

How do Wrike AI and ClickUp Brain compare?

Both offer AI writing, summaries, assistants and agents. The main difference is packaging: Wrike includes AI Essentials from Team and an AI Elite allowance (Copilot and agents) from Business, while ClickUp's full AI requires the Brain AI ($9) or Everything AI ($28) add-on per user per month.

Is ClickUp slow compared with Wrike?

Lag is the most common complaint in ClickUp reviews, especially in large boards and busy Workspaces, while Wrike's most common complaints are its learning curve and dense interface. Performance depends on your data, so test both with a realistic volume of tasks.

Does Wrike have docs and chat like ClickUp?

Not to the same extent. ClickUp has built-in Docs, wikis and ClickUp Chat. Wrike keeps collaboration in task comments, @mentions and approvals, integrates with Slack and Microsoft Teams, and offers Wrike Whiteboard as an add-on.

Can I migrate from Wrike to ClickUp, or the reverse?

Yes. ClickUp has a built-in Wrike importer. To move into Wrike, export your ClickUp data to a spreadsheet and use Wrike's Excel import, available on Team plans and above. Automations, forms and dashboards usually need rebuilding.

Which is better for large enterprises?

Wrike, in our scoring (10/10 vs 8/10 for scalability). It offers advanced resource planning, BI, Wrike Integrate and Sync on its top tiers, granular governance and customer-managed encryption through Wrike Lock. ClickUp Enterprise adds SAML SSO, SCIM, audit logs and data residency.
