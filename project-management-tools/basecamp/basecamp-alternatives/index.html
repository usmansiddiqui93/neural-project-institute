Best Basecamp Alternatives in 2026

The best Basecamp alternatives are Wrike for teams that need Gantt charts, workload planning and reporting, monday.com and Asana for easier structured project management, and Trello or ClickUp for tight budgets. Basecamp itself is excellent for calm, communication-centred projects with clients, so the right replacement depends on what you are missing. Below we compare nine alternatives on features, pricing, free plans and user ratings, show how flat and per-user pricing compare at different team sizes, and explain how to move your projects across.

Key takeaways

  • Wrike (9.4/10) is the strongest all-round alternative for teams that have outgrown Basecamp and need Gantt dependencies, workload views, proofing and dashboards.
  • monday.com and Asana add structure, timelines and automation while staying easy to learn, which matters if clients and non-technical staff use the tool.
  • Trello (from $5/user/month) and ClickUp (from $7/user/month) are the cheapest per-user options, but Basecamp's flat price can still be cheaper for larger teams.
  • Count both people and active projects before you switch: Basecamp charges per account, most alternatives charge per user.
  • Jira suits software teams running sprints, Microsoft Planner suits Microsoft 365 organisations, and Notion suits teams that want docs and wikis alongside projects.

Why teams look for Basecamp alternatives

Basecamp, made by 37signals, does a few things very well. Each project gets one page with a message board, to-do lists, a Kanban-style Card Table, a schedule, group chat (Campfire), automatic check-ins, and docs and files. Clients can be invited with controlled visibility, and paid plans charge a flat monthly price rather than per user. It is one of the easiest project tools to learn, and it rates 4.4/5 on Capterra.

That simplicity is deliberate, and it is also why teams eventually look elsewhere. The most common reasons are:

  • No Gantt dependencies. Basecamp's schedules and Lineup show dates, but you cannot link tasks so that one slipping moves the next. Projects with task dependencies and fixed deadlines need a tool that models them.
  • No resource or workload planning. There is no view of who is over- or under-loaded across projects, which makes capacity planning a spreadsheet exercise.
  • Limited reporting. Basecamp offers activity views and Hill Charts, but no custom dashboards, budget tracking or portfolio roll-ups.
  • Little custom structure. There are no custom fields or priorities, and only one level of subtasks, so standardising processes across many projects is harder.
  • Active-project caps. Every plan except Unlimited limits the number of active projects, so a small team running many projects can end up on the $299/month plan.
  • No built-in AI (as of October 2026). Basecamp has opened its API and command-line interface to AI agents you run yourself, but it has no built-in AI summaries or writing help.
  • No native Slack or Microsoft Teams integration. 37signals positions Campfire and pings as the alternative; third-party connectors exist.
  • Not designed for Agile. There are no backlogs, sprints or story points.

When staying with Basecamp makes sense

If your main problem is scattered email and chat, most of your projects are client-facing, and you rarely need dependencies or formal reporting, Basecamp may still be the best fit. Its flat pricing also becomes more attractive as headcount grows. In that case, consider adding a lightweight Gantt or time-tracking integration rather than switching.

Basecamp alternatives reviewed

Here is how each alternative compares with Basecamp specifically: what it adds, what you give up and who should consider it.

1. Wrike: best all-round Basecamp alternative

Wrike is our highest-rated tool (9.4/10) and the natural next step for teams that have outgrown Basecamp's simplicity. It adds interactive Gantt charts with dependencies on paid plans, workload views, dashboards, request forms with conditional logic, automation, and built-in proofing and approvals for creative work. Guest access lets you bring clients into projects, which matters if client collaboration is why you chose Basecamp in the first place.

The trade-off is that Wrike takes longer to set up and learn, and it charges per user: a free plan, then Team at $10/user/month for 2 to 15 users and Business at $25/user/month (billed annually). It suits agencies, marketing teams and PMOs that now need scheduling, resources and reporting.

2. monday.com: best for easy, visual workflows

monday.com keeps much of Basecamp's approachability but adds far more structure. Boards can be switched between table, Kanban, timeline, Gantt and calendar views, and dashboards combine data from several boards. Recipe-style automations handle routine updates. It is one of the easiest tools for non-technical teams to adopt.

Plans are sold in seat bundles with a three-seat minimum: Basic is $9, Standard (with timeline and Gantt) $12 and Pro $19 per seat/month, billed annually, and the free plan covers two seats. Automation and integration actions are capped by plan. See our monday.com review and monday.com vs Basecamp.

3. Asana: best for structured task management

Asana gives you what Basecamp's to-do lists lack: custom fields, multiple views (list, board, calendar, timeline and Gantt), dependencies, forms, rules and dashboards from the Starter plan, with portfolios, goals, workload and approvals on Advanced. Paid plans include unlimited free guests, which helps agencies working with clients.

Starter costs $10.99/user/month and Advanced $24.99/user/month (billed annually), and the free Personal plan is for individuals and very small teams. Asana is more task-centred than Basecamp, so long-form discussion and announcements need a little more thought.

4. Notion: best for docs, wikis and projects together

If Basecamp's Docs and Files and message boards are what your team values most, Notion goes much further on documentation. Pages, wikis and databases live in one workspace, and project trackers can be built from databases with board, timeline and calendar views. Notion AI adds writing and summarising help.

Project management in Notion requires design effort, and it has no native time tracking or resource management. Plus starts at $10/member/month and Business at $20 (yearly rate), with a free plan.

5. Trello: best for simple Kanban at a low price

Trello is the closest match to Basecamp's Card Table, with intuitive boards, lists and cards, Butler automation and a large library of Power-Ups. It is very easy to learn and the free plan supports up to 10 collaborators per workspace. Premium adds calendar, timeline, table and dashboard views.

Trello lacks Basecamp's message boards, check-ins and docs, so many teams pair it with a chat tool. Standard is $5 and Premium $10 per user/month (billed annually).

6. ClickUp: best value for feature-rich teams

ClickUp packs tasks, docs, whiteboards, chat, goals, time tracking, Gantt charts and sprints into one low-priced workspace. For a team leaving Basecamp because it needs more features without a big price rise, it is hard to beat on value: Unlimited is $7/user/month and Business $12/user/month (billed annually), with a Free Forever plan.

The trade-off is complexity: ClickUp is the opposite of Basecamp's calm, focused design, and its most capable AI features are paid add-ons.

7. Smartsheet: best for spreadsheet-style planning

Smartsheet suits operations, finance and PMO teams that think in rows and columns. Its sheets support dependencies, Gantt charts with critical path, forms, automation, cross-sheet reports and dashboards, with strong enterprise governance on higher plans.

There is no free plan (Pro is $9/member/month billed yearly, with a 30-day trial), and its grid-first interface is less inviting for clients than Basecamp. Free Contributors can view and comment on shared work.

8. Jira: best for software and Agile teams

If you are a software team using Basecamp for development work, Jira is the specialist tool. It provides backlogs, sprints, story points, velocity and burndown reports, configurable workflows and deep developer integrations. The free plan covers up to 10 users.

Jira is complex to administer and not designed for clients or non-technical teams, so agencies often keep a separate tool for client communication.

Basecamp is the only tool in our top 10 with flat, per-account pricing. Its plans differ mainly by the number of active projects and storage (checked on Basecamp's pricing page on 7 October 2026):

Two lessons follow. First, small teams can often switch to a per-user tool for the same or less money, especially if they need features Basecamp lacks. Second, larger teams pay much more per month after switching, so the move needs to be justified by the time saved on scheduling, reporting and resource planning. Remember to price guest or client access too: Asana includes unlimited free guests on paid plans, Smartsheet's Contributors are free, and monday.com includes free viewers from Basic, but rules differ by tool.

Tip

Count your active projects as well as your people. A three-person team with 30 active projects needs Basecamp Unlimited at $299 a month, which is more than most per-user tools would cost for three people.

How to choose the right Basecamp alternative

A short, structured evaluation avoids swapping one compromise for another:

  1. List what you use in Basecamp today. Note which tools on the project page your team relies on: message boards, to-dos, Card Tables, schedules, check-ins, Campfire, docs and client access.
  2. Name the gap. Write down the specific problems that triggered the search, such as dependencies, workload, reporting or AI. If the only issue is cost, compare plans before switching tools.
  3. Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves. Our guide to project management software features explains what each feature does and when you need it.
  4. Check the client and guest model. If clients work in your projects, confirm how each tool licenses and restricts external users.
  5. Work out the 12-month cost using your expected headcount and the plan you will actually need, including add-ons such as AI.
  6. Trial two tools on a real project, ideally one with a client, for two weeks. Judge notifications, permissions and reporting with real data.

How to migrate from Basecamp

Basecamp organises everything around the project page, so most of the work in a migration is deciding where each kind of information will live in the new tool:

In BasecampTypical equivalent in other tools
ProjectProject, folder, board or space
To-do lists and to-dosTask lists or sections with tasks and subtasks
Card TableKanban or board view
Schedule and LineupCalendar, timeline or Gantt view
Message BoardProject updates, comments or a docs page
Campfire chat and pingsBuilt-in chat, or Slack or Microsoft Teams
Automatic Check-insStatus updates, recurring forms or stand-up integrations
Hill ChartsProgress fields, status reports or dashboards
Docs and FilesDocs, wiki pages or connected cloud storage
Client accessGuest, client or viewer permissions
  1. Take an inventory. List active projects, owners, clients with access and any integrations. Archive projects that are finished; they do not need to move.
  2. Decide what to migrate. Move open to-dos, upcoming dates and current documents. Keep historical discussions in Basecamp's archive or export them for reference rather than recreating them.
  3. Build the structure first. Set up project templates, statuses, custom fields and views in the new tool before importing anything. Most tools import tasks from CSV or spreadsheet files; check each vendor's help centre for current importers.
  4. Set permissions and guest access for clients and contractors, and test what an external user can see.
  5. Pilot with one or two live projects for a few weeks, then migrate the rest in waves.
  6. Agree new working norms: where announcements go, how status is reported and which notifications people should switch on. Our guide to managing multiple projects helps when you start running a portfolio in one place.
  7. Close Basecamp deliberately. Keep it read-only for a short overlap period, export anything you must retain, then cancel. Basecamp has no contract, but you cannot downgrade a paid account to the Free plan.
Tip

Do not try to copy Basecamp exactly. The point of switching is to use what the new tool does better, such as dependencies, workload views or dashboards, so redesign your process around those.

Our verdict on Basecamp alternatives

Basecamp remains a good tool for calm, communication-centred projects with clients, and its flat pricing is unusual value for larger teams. When projects need dependencies, workload planning, structured fields or reporting, it is time to look elsewhere. For most teams in that position, Wrike is our first recommendation because it covers scheduling, resources, approvals and reporting in one platform while still supporting client collaboration. If ease of adoption matters most, shortlist monday.com and Asana; if budget matters most, ClickUp and Trello.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Basecamp?

For most teams that have outgrown Basecamp, Wrike is the best alternative because it adds Gantt charts with dependencies, workload views, proofing, dashboards and guest access in one platform. monday.com and Asana are easier to learn, and ClickUp and Trello are the cheapest options.

Is there a free alternative to Basecamp?

Yes. Trello (up to 10 collaborators per workspace), ClickUp (Free Forever), Wrike, Notion and Jira (up to 10 users) all have free plans that support more than Basecamp's one-project Free plan. Asana's Personal plan and monday.com's two-seat free plan are better for trials than for teams.

What is cheaper than Basecamp?

For small teams, Trello Standard ($5/user/month) and ClickUp Unlimited ($7/user/month), both billed annually, are usually cheaper than Basecamp Studio at $59/month. For teams of more than about 10 people, Basecamp's flat pricing is often cheaper than any per-user tool.

Which Basecamp alternative is best for agencies?

Wrike is the strongest choice for agencies that need approvals, proofing, resource planning and client reporting. Asana and monday.com are easier to roll out and handle client collaboration well. Check how each tool licenses guest users before you switch.

Which Basecamp alternative has Gantt charts?

Wrike, Smartsheet, monday.com (Standard and above), Asana (Starter and above), ClickUp (Unlimited and above) and Microsoft Planner's paid plans all offer Gantt or timeline views with task dependencies. Basecamp has schedules but no dependencies.

What is the easiest alternative to Basecamp?

Trello and monday.com are the easiest tools to learn in our review, followed by Asana. All three are suitable for clients and non-technical staff.

Is Asana better than Basecamp?

Asana is better for structured task management, timelines, goals and portfolios; Basecamp is better for simple, communication-centred projects and flat pricing. See our Asana vs Basecamp comparison.

Is Trello better than Basecamp?

Trello is better if you mainly need simple, low-cost Kanban boards. Basecamp is better if you want message boards, check-ins, docs and client access in one place with flat pricing. See Trello vs Basecamp.

Which Basecamp alternative is best for enterprises?

Wrike and Smartsheet score highest for scalability and enterprise readiness in our review, with portfolio management, governance and advanced security on their higher plans.

Does Basecamp have AI, and which alternatives do?

As of October 2026, Basecamp has no built-in AI features, but its API, CLI and connectors let AI agents work in Basecamp. Wrike, monday.com, Asana, Smartsheet, Notion, Trello and Jira include AI features on some plans; ClickUp and Microsoft Planner sell AI as add-ons.

Can I move my Basecamp projects to another tool?

Yes. Most tools import tasks from CSV or spreadsheet files, and some offer direct importers. Migrate active work first, archive finished projects, set up structure and permissions before importing, and pilot with one or two projects.

Why do people leave Basecamp?

The most common reasons are the lack of Gantt dependencies and resource management, limited reporting, little custom structure, active-project caps on most plans and the absence of built-in AI. Many teams stay because of its simplicity and flat pricing.

Is Basecamp still worth using in 2026?

Yes, for agencies, small businesses and remote teams whose main need is organised communication with clients and colleagues. Teams that need structured planning and reporting will be better served by an alternative. Read our Basecamp review.
