How to compare project management tools yourself

A comparison narrows the field; a short, structured evaluation makes the decision. This is the process we recommend:

  1. Write down the work the tool must support. List your project types, team size, methods (Scrum, Kanban, Waterfall or hybrid) and who outside the team needs access.
  2. Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves. For example: Gantt charts with dependencies, resource planning, sprints, proofing, client guest access or Microsoft 365 integration. Our guide to project management software features explains what each one does.
  3. Shortlist two or three tools using the tables above, then read their head-to-head comparisons.
  4. Trial them on a real project, not a demo board. Ask the people who will use it every day to do their normal work in it for a week or two.
  5. Check integrations, security and administration with IT: single sign-on, permissions, data residency and the apps you already use.
  6. Work out the total cost for your team size over a year, including the plan you will need in 12 months and any paid add-ons such as AI.
  7. Plan adoption: templates, training and a clear owner matter more to success than the last few features.

Free plans and trials

Most tools in our review offer a free plan or free trial, but the limits differ a great deal. At our last check, Jira's free plan covered up to 10 users, Trello's free plan suited small teams, and monday.com's free plan was limited to two seats. Smartsheet discontinued its free plan in 2025 and now offers a 30-day free trial instead. Basic Microsoft Planner is included with many Microsoft 365 business subscriptions.

Free plans are best for testing a tool or running small projects. Features such as Gantt charts, automation, resource management and admin controls usually sit on paid plans, so check the pricing table in each comparison against your must-have list.

Frequently asked questions

How many project management tool comparisons are there?

There are 45: every possible pair among the 10 tools in our review (Wrike, monday.com, Asana, ClickUp, Smartsheet, Jira, Microsoft Planner, Notion, Trello and Basecamp).

Which project management tool is best overall?

Wrike has the highest overall score in our review (9.4), thanks to its depth in planning, resource management and scalability. monday.com (9.1) and Asana (9.0) follow closely. The best tool for you depends on your team size, methods and budget.

How are winners decided in the comparisons?

We score each tool from 1 to 10 on seven categories and compare them category by category, alongside pricing, features and published G2 and Capterra ratings. Each page ends with a verdict on which type of team each tool suits.

Which project management tool is easiest to use?

Trello scores highest for ease of use in our review, followed by monday.com, Asana and Basecamp. Simple tools trade some depth for speed of learning.

Which tool is best for Agile and software teams?

Jira leads our Agile category, with ClickUp second. Both support backlogs and sprints; Jira has deeper developer tooling, while ClickUp adds docs, goals and dashboards for wider teams.

Which project management tool is best value?

ClickUp, Notion and Trello score highest for value for money in our review. ClickUp offers the most features for the price; Trello and Notion are inexpensive and have useful free plans.

Which project management tools have a free plan?

Wrike, monday.com, Asana, ClickUp, Jira, Notion, Trello and Basecamp offer free plans with different limits. Smartsheet offers a free trial instead, and basic Microsoft Planner is included with many Microsoft 365 subscriptions.

Which tool is best for small teams?

Small teams often start with Trello, Basecamp, Notion or the free plans of ClickUp and Asana. Growing teams that expect to need Gantt charts and resource planning should also look at Wrike's Team plan.

Which tool is best for enterprises?

Wrike and Smartsheet score highest for scalability and enterprise readiness, with monday.com and Asana close behind. Jira is a common enterprise choice for software organisations.
