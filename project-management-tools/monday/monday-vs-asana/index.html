monday.com vs Asana (2026): Which Is Better for Your Team?

monday.com vs Asana is one of the closest contests in work management: monday.com scores 9.1/10 in our review and Asana 9.0/10. Choose monday.com if you want colourful, flexible boards that any team can shape into its own workflow; choose Asana if you want structured cross-team task management with clear ownership, linked to goals and portfolios. Below we compare current pricing (including monday.com's seat buckets), automation limits, AI, reporting, resource management and user ratings so you can decide for your own team.

  • Overall: monday.com 9.1/10 vs Asana 9.0/10. monday.com wins on feature depth; the other six categories are tied in our scoring.

  • Automation: Asana includes unlimited rules on every paid plan; monday.com caps actions at 250 a month on Standard and 25,000 on Pro.

  • If resource, portfolio and approval depth decides it, also look at Wrike, our #1-ranked tool.

Quick verdict

Both tools are excellent, easy-to-adopt work management platforms, and most teams would be well served by either. The difference is in their philosophy. monday.com gives you a flexible board, a large library of column types and colourful dashboards, and lets each team build the workflow it wants. Asana gives you a clear structure of teams, projects and tasks with exactly one owner per task, and connects that work to portfolios and company goals.

  • Pick monday.com if adoption across non-technical teams, visual dashboards and custom workflows (requests, campaigns, operations) matter most.
  • Pick Asana if you coordinate work across many teams, manage by goals or OKRs, or want unlimited automation rules without counting actions.
  • Consider Wrike if you need deeper resource planning, portfolio control and creative approvals in one platform; it is our highest-rated tool overall.

What is monday.com?

monday.com is a board-based work management platform launched in 2012 and listed on Nasdaq since 2021. Every piece of work is an item on a board, and columns hold its owner, status, dates, budget or any other data you need. The same board can be shown as a table, Kanban board, calendar, timeline or Gantt chart, and dashboards pull numbers from several boards into one view. monday.com also sells separate CRM, service and software development (monday dev) products on the same platform.

Its biggest strength is how quickly people pick it up. Colour-coded status columns make a board readable at a glance, more than 200 templates give a working start, and WorkForms and automations turn requests into assigned, tracked work. The trade-offs are a seat-bucket pricing model, metered automation actions and AI credits, and several project management features (time tracking, workload, portfolio and resource management) reserved for higher plans.

What is Asana?

Asana is a work management platform founded in 2008 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange since 2020. Work is organised into teams, projects (split into sections) and tasks, and each task has exactly one assignee. Above projects sit portfolios, which group related projects for status reporting, and goals, which show what the work is for. A completed task can update the progress of a portfolio or goal, which Asana calls its Work Graph.

Asana's strengths are a clean interface, dependable cross-team coordination and the way goals and portfolios connect daily tasks to strategy. The trade-offs are that those distinctive features start on the Advanced plan, AI use is metered, and its resource and financial controls are lighter than those of PMO-focused platforms.

On paper, monday.com looks cheaper: $9, $12 and $19 per seat/month billed annually against Asana's $10.99 (Starter) and $24.99 (Advanced) per user/month billed annually, or $13.49 and $30.49 billed monthly. In practice, the comparison depends on which plan you need and how many people you have.

Plan for plan: which tiers match?

The plans do not line up neatly. Asana Starter is roughly equivalent to monday.com Standard: both add timeline and Gantt views, dashboards, forms, guests and automation. Asana Advanced sits somewhere between monday.com Pro and Enterprise: it adds time tracking and workload (Pro on monday.com) but also portfolios, goals, approvals and proofing, which monday.com either reserves for Enterprise or handles through boards and statuses. monday.com Basic ($9) has no Asana equivalent; it removes Free plan limits but has no timeline, Gantt, guests or automations, so most project teams skip it.

Seat minimums and buckets

monday.com's support documentation says paid plans start at 3 seats and then rise in groups of 5 (5, 10, 15 and so on). A team of 6 therefore pays for 10 seats. Free viewers do not use a seat, but everyone who edits does. monday.com asks organisations with more than 40 users to request a quote. Asana's pricing page lists no seat minimum on paid plans and says you can add unlimited members.

Free plans and trials

Both free plans are now aimed at individuals and pairs. monday.com Free allows up to 2 seats, 3 boards and 3 docs, with no timeline, Gantt or automations. Asana Personal allows up to 2 seats per project and team with unlimited tasks and projects, and list, board and calendar views, but no timelines, dashboards, forms, rules or AI. For trials, monday.com offers 14 days of Pro with no credit card, and Asana offers a self-serve Advanced trial with no credit card; Asana's pricing page does not state the length.

Ease of use

Both tools score 9/10 for ease of use in our review, and both are among the easiest capable platforms to roll out. They are easy in different ways.

monday.com is easy to start. A new user understands a colourful board within minutes, and adding a column or status takes seconds. The learning curve arrives later, when you connect boards, design dashboards across many boards, set permissions and build automations within the action limits. Without agreed standards, large accounts can end up with many slightly different boards for the same process.

Asana is easy to stay organised in. Its structure (team, project, section, task, one owner) is opinionated, which means less design work up front and more consistency across teams. My Tasks and Inbox give each person a clear daily view. The harder parts are portfolios, goals, workload and AI Studio, which depend on teams keeping projects up to date.

Tip

Whichever you choose, appoint one owner for templates, fields and naming conventions before you invite the wider organisation. It is the single biggest factor in whether reporting works six months later.

Views, planning and Gantt charts

Both tools offer list or table, Kanban board, calendar, timeline and Gantt views. Asana includes list, board and calendar views on its free plan; on monday.com, the timeline, Gantt and calendar views start on Standard.

For scheduling, monday.com's Standard plan adds timeline and Gantt views with a dependency column and drag-to-reschedule. According to monday.com's plan documentation, Pro adds milestones and the critical path, and its Gantt feature page also lists baselines. Asana's timeline and Gantt views start on Starter, show dependency chains, and let you set a target schedule and compare progress against it. Asana's product pages do not describe critical path analysis, so if your projects are tightly sequenced, test both on a real plan.

For most marketing, operations and client projects, either tool is enough. For large, date-driven programmes with many dependencies, look at a scheduling-focused tool such as Wrike or read our guides to Gantt charts and the critical path method. If your work is flow-based rather than date-based, our guide to Kanban boards explains how to set up columns in either tool.

Automation and request intake

This is one of the clearest practical differences. Asana includes unlimited automation rules on every paid plan, from Starter upwards, with a library of ready-made rules such as reassigning work when a section changes or posting to Slack when a task is complete.

monday.com meters automation. Standard includes 250 automation actions and 250 integration actions a month; Pro includes 25,000 of each; Enterprise up to 250,000 combined. 250 actions disappears quickly in a busy team, so in practice serious automation on monday.com starts on Pro. In return, monday.com's recipe-style builder is very approachable, and its AI-powered workflows add more capability (capped at 3 active workflows on Standard and 20 on Pro).

For intake, both tools turn form submissions into tracked work. monday.com WorkForms support conditional questions, branding and an AI form builder. Asana forms (from Starter) support required attachments and website embedding, and Advanced adds branching. If your team handles many requests, our free work request template shows the fields worth collecting before you build the form.

AI features

Both vendors now put AI at the centre of their products, and both meter it.

monday.com includes monday sidekick (an AI assistant that summarises, drafts, updates tasks and builds automations, dashboards and forms), prebuilt and custom AI agents, AI blocks on boards, and a meeting Notetaker from Standard. For accounts that joined from May 2026, AI draws on a shared monthly pool of credits: at least 1,000 on Basic, 2,000 on Standard and 3,000 on Pro, with extra credits available to buy.

Asana includes AI Teammates (more than 30 prebuilt agents, plus custom ones), Asana Dash (AI briefs and next steps) and AI Studio (a no-code builder for AI workflows) on paid plans. According to Asana's pricing comparison, AI Teammates and Dash include 5 requests per user per month, capped at 50 per account on Starter and Advanced, and AI Studio includes a monthly credit allowance that rises by plan. Extra usage costs $0.50 per request in prepaid packs or $0.60 pay-as-you-go.

Neither tool's AI is a reason to choose it on its own. Both work within existing permissions, both need a human owner for every agent, and heavy users on either side will pay for extra usage. Test AI on a real workflow during the trial.

Collaboration and external users

Both tools keep discussion attached to the work: monday.com through an updates thread on each item, Asana through comments, collaborators and @mentions on each task, plus project messages and status updates.

For external people, Asana includes unlimited free guests on paid plans, which suits agencies and teams working with freelancers. monday.com includes unlimited free viewers (read-only) on paid plans, and guests who can edit from the Standard plan. Both tools score 9/10 for collaboration in our review.

Users of both report the same downside: notification volume. Capterra reviewers mention notification overload for monday.com, and "excessive notifications" is one of the most discussed topics in Asana's Capterra reviews. Agree notification and @mention habits early, and use automations to notify the right person rather than everyone.

Reporting, goals and portfolios

monday.com reporting is built on dashboards: charts, numbers, progress batteries, Gantt and workload widgets that read live board data. The key limit is how many boards one dashboard can combine: 1 on Free and Basic, 5 on Standard, 20 on Pro and 50 on Enterprise. Full portfolio management is an Enterprise feature.

Asana offers project and team dashboards from Starter. Its standout is on Advanced: portfolios that show the health of many projects on one screen, a status update builder, and goals that can be broken into sub-goals and updated automatically from connected projects. Advanced also adds Tableau and Power BI integrations.

If you manage by OKRs, Asana's goals are a genuine advantage; our free OKR template helps you define them first. If your leaders want a visual, at-a-glance dashboard per department, monday.com is excellent, provided you budget for the plan that combines enough boards. For what a good status report contains, see our guide to project status reports.

Resource management and time tracking

Both tools score 8/10 for planning and resource management in our review, and both reserve the deeper features for higher tiers.

  • monday.com: time tracking and the workload view are on Pro. Portfolio and resource management are on Enterprise.
  • Asana: time tracking (estimated vs actual) and workload within a portfolio are on Advanced. Capacity planning and universal resource management are on Enterprise, and a Timesheets & Budgets add-on is available from Starter.

For small and mid-sized teams, either tool's workload view is enough for basic capacity planning. PMOs and professional services firms that need formal resource allocation, budgets and financial tracking across many projects often find both lighter than they need. That is the main reason Wrike ranks first in our review.

Integrations

monday.com's integrations page advertises more than 850 integrations and apps, counting its marketplace, including Slack, Microsoft Teams, Gmail, Outlook, Zoom, Jira, GitHub, Salesforce, HubSpot, Zapier and Make. Integration actions are metered like automations (250 a month on Standard), and some marketplace apps carry their own cost.

Asana says even its free plan includes more than 100 free integrations, including Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Workspace, Zoom, Jira, GitHub, Figma and Zapier. Salesforce, Tableau and Power BI integrations are listed on Advanced.

Both offer a public API and MCP connectivity, so AI assistants such as Claude, ChatGPT and Copilot can work with your data. For most teams the integration you need exists on both; check the plan it requires and, for monday.com, whether the monthly action limit is enough for a two-way sync.

Security and administration

Both vendors publish extensive trust documentation. monday.com lists SOC 1 Type II, SOC 2 Type II and SOC 3 reports, ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018, 27032 and 27701, CSA STAR and TX-RAMP, with a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement on Enterprise. Asana lists SOC 2 Type 2 and SOC 3 reports, ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018 and 27701, CSA STAR Level 1 and HIPAA support, with data residency in Europe, Australia and Japan and customer-managed encryption keys.

On both, SAML single sign-on and SCIM provisioning are Enterprise features. Both score 9/10 for scalability and enterprise readiness in our review. Confirm current certifications and their scope in each vendor's trust centre during procurement.

Which is better for your team?

Marketing and creative teams

Both are strong. monday.com's visual campaign boards, content calendars and WorkForms suit teams that want a lightweight, colourful tool. Asana's timelines, forms and approvals and proofing on Advanced suit teams that run many campaigns across departments.

Operations and HR teams

monday.com has the edge for repeatable processes such as onboarding, procurement and facilities requests, where flexible boards and automations shine, as long as the action limits fit your volume.

PMOs and cross-functional programmes

Asana has the edge: portfolios, goals and status updates on Advanced give leaders a structured view across many projects at a lower tier than monday.com's Enterprise portfolio features. For deep resource and financial control, compare Wrike as well.

Agencies

Asana's unlimited free guests make client collaboration simple; monday.com's free viewers and client-facing dashboards suit agencies that mainly share progress. Both need Pro or Advanced for time tracking.

Software teams

Neither is a dedicated Agile tool (both score 7/10 here). monday.com sells monday dev for sprints and bugs; Asana connects to Jira and GitHub.

How to switch between monday.com and Asana

Moving from Asana to monday.com is the easier direction. monday.com has a built-in Asana importer: you connect your Asana account, choose a workspace and projects, and each project becomes a board. According to monday.com's help centre, it imports the task name, assignee, completion status, notes, due date and parent name, and creates an automation that keeps new Asana tasks syncing to the board. Comments and attachments are not listed, so plan to move important files separately.

Moving from monday.com to Asana uses Asana's CSV importer: export each board to Excel, save the main sheet as a CSV and map the columns to Asana fields. Specific information needs matching custom fields in Asana before you import.

  1. Pick one live project and rebuild it in the new tool during the trial, including automations and the dashboard you rely on.
  2. Agree the structure first: boards and columns in monday.com, or teams, projects, sections and custom fields in Asana.
  3. Import or recreate active work only; archive completed projects as exports.
  4. Rebuild automations and forms, then test them with real requests.
  5. Run both tools in parallel for one or two weeks, then switch off the old one.

Frequently asked questions

Is monday.com better than Asana?

Neither is better for every team. In our review monday.com scores 9.1/10 and Asana 9.0/10, with monday.com winning only on feature depth. monday.com suits teams that want flexible, visual boards and dashboards; Asana suits teams that want structured cross-team task management linked to goals and portfolios.

Is monday.com cheaper than Asana?

Per seat, usually yes: monday.com costs $9, $12 or $19 per seat/month billed annually, against $10.99 for Asana Starter and $24.99 for Advanced (checked 7 October 2026). But monday.com has a 3-seat minimum and sells seats in buckets, so a team of 6 pays for 10 seats and can end up paying more than on Asana Starter.

Which is easier to use, monday.com or Asana?

Both score 9/10 for ease of use in our review. monday.com is quicker to start with thanks to its visual boards; Asana's fixed structure of projects, sections and single-owner tasks makes it easier to stay consistent across many teams.

Do monday.com and Asana have free plans?

Yes. monday.com Free allows up to 2 seats, 3 boards and 3 docs. Asana Personal allows up to 2 seats per project and team with unlimited tasks and projects. Neither free plan includes timeline or Gantt views or automation, so both suit individuals and pairs rather than teams.

Why does monday.com have a 3-seat minimum?

monday.com sells paid plans in seat groups that start at 3 and then rise in multiples of 5, according to its support documentation. A team of 4 pays for 5 seats and a team of 6 for 10. Free viewers do not count as seats.

Which has better automation, monday.com or Asana?

Asana is more generous: all paid plans include unlimited automation rules. monday.com caps automation actions at 250 a month on Standard and 25,000 on Pro, though its recipe builder is very approachable and Pro's allowance is enough for most teams.

Do monday.com and Asana have Gantt charts?

Yes. monday.com includes timeline and Gantt views from Standard, with milestones and critical path added on Pro according to its plan documentation. Asana includes timeline and Gantt views from Starter, with dependencies and a target schedule to compare progress against.

Which is better for marketing teams?

Both are strong for marketing. monday.com suits teams that want visual campaign boards, content calendars and WorkForms. Asana suits larger marketing functions that run many campaigns across departments and want approvals, proofing and portfolios on Advanced.

Which has better AI, monday.com or Asana?

Both offer AI assistants and AI agents on paid plans, and both meter usage. monday.com uses a monthly pool of AI credits (from 1,000 on Basic); Asana includes AI Teammates, Dash and AI Studio with monthly allowances and paid top-ups. Test each on a real workflow rather than choosing on AI alone.

Can I import Asana projects into monday.com?

Yes. monday.com has a built-in Asana importer that turns each Asana project into a board, bringing across task names, assignees, completion status, notes, due dates and parent names. Going the other way, Asana imports monday.com boards through its CSV importer.

Is Asana or monday.com better for small businesses?

It depends on headcount. Asana Starter is priced per user, which suits teams whose size falls between monday.com's seat buckets. monday.com can be better value for teams of exactly 3, 5 or 10 that want Pro features such as time tracking at a lower per-seat price than Asana Advanced.

What is a good alternative to monday.com and Asana?

Wrike is our top-rated tool and goes further on resource management, portfolios and approvals. ClickUp offers more features at lower prices. See our monday.com alternatives and Asana alternatives pages, or our top 10 project management tools.
