Best Trello Alternatives in 2026

The best Trello alternatives are Wrike for full project and resource management, monday.com for more views and automation with the same ease of use, Asana for more structured project management and ClickUp for far more features at a low price. Trello is still the simplest Kanban board you can buy, so the right alternative depends on what you have outgrown. Below we compare nine tools on scores, pricing, free plans and the features Trello does not include.

Key takeaways

  • Wrike (9.4/10) is the strongest Trello alternative when you need Gantt charts with dependencies, workload views, time tracking and portfolio reporting.
  • monday.com (9.1/10) keeps Trello's visual, easy feel but adds timeline, Gantt, dashboards and stronger automation.
  • ClickUp (8.9/10) gives you the most features for the money, from $7 per user/month (annual), with a Trello importer.
  • Wrike, monday.com, Asana, ClickUp, Notion, Jira and Basecamp all have free plans; Microsoft Planner is included in many Microsoft 365 plans.
  • If you only need more boards, custom fields or timeline views, upgrading Trello to Standard or Premium may be cheaper than switching.

Why teams look for Trello alternatives

Trello is popular for good reasons. It is rated 4.5/5 from 23,628 reviews on Capterra (checked 7 October 2026), anyone can understand a board within minutes, and its Free plan is genuinely useful. Teams usually start looking elsewhere when their work changes shape:

  • The Free plan cap. Free Workspaces are limited to 10 collaborators and 10 boards, so a growing team soon has to pay or move.
  • Advanced views need Premium. Timeline, table, calendar, dashboard and map views, plus Workspace-wide views across boards, require Premium ($10 per user/month, annual).
  • No task dependencies. Trello cannot link cards so that one waits for another, and its timeline has no critical path or baselines. Projects where task dependencies drive the schedule outgrow it quickly.
  • No resource or portfolio management. There is no workload view, capacity planning or portfolio reporting across many projects.
  • Power-Up sprawl. Gaps are filled with Power-Ups, some with their own fees and admin, so a "simple" setup becomes complex.
  • Boards get cluttered at scale. Hundreds of cards and dozens of boards make it hard to see how work connects.

Could upgrading Trello be enough?

Before switching, check whether a higher Trello plan solves your problem. Standard ($5 per user/month, annual) removes the board limit and adds custom fields, advanced checklists and AI features. Premium ($10) adds timeline, table, calendar and dashboard views, Workspace views across boards and unlimited automation runs. If your work still fits on boards, an upgrade is cheaper and less disruptive than a migration. If you need dependencies, workload or portfolio reporting, an alternative is the better route.

How we chose these Trello alternatives

We compared the nine other tools in our top 10 ranking. Each is scored from 1 to 10 on seven categories: ease of use, feature depth, planning and resource management, collaboration, Agile and software support, value for money, and scalability and enterprise readiness. We checked pricing on vendors' pages on 7 October 2026 and considered published G2 and Capterra ratings.

The list is ordered by how well each tool addresses the most common reasons teams outgrow Trello, then by overall score. That is why Notion appears above Smartsheet and Jira here: for many small teams, adding docs to boards is the first thing they miss. Each Trello alternative in brief

1. Wrike: best for full project and resource management

Wrike is our top-ranked tool and the biggest step up from Trello. It keeps board views but adds interactive Gantt charts with dependencies, critical path and baselines on paid plans, workload charts, time tracking, portfolio views, request forms, proofing and approvals, and dashboards. It takes longer to learn than Trello, but it is built for teams running many projects with shared people. Wrike has a free plan; Team costs $10 per user/month (annual, 2 to 15 users) and Business $25 (checked 7 October 2026).

2. monday.com: best for more views and automation

monday.com is the easiest move for teams who love Trello's visual style. Boards switch between table, Kanban, timeline, Gantt and calendar views, with dashboards across boards, recipe-style automations and monday docs. It has a built-in Trello importer. The Free plan covers two seats, and paid plans start at $9 per seat/month annually with a three-seat minimum; time tracking needs Pro (checked 7 October 2026).

3. Asana: best for more structured project management

Asana adds the structure Trello lacks while staying easy to use: list, board, calendar, timeline and Gantt views with dependencies and milestones, rules, forms and dashboards from Starter, and portfolios, goals, workload and time tracking on Advanced. It suits teams coordinating work across several departments. The free Personal plan is limited to two seats; Starter costs $10.99 per user/month annually (checked 7 October 2026).

4. ClickUp: best for far more features at low cost

ClickUp gives Trello users boards plus almost everything else: list, Gantt, calendar, table, timeline, workload and mind map views, native time tracking, docs, whiteboards, goals and sprints. It imports directly from Trello. The trade-off is complexity, and its full AI features are paid add-ons. Unlimited costs $7 per user/month annually and Business $12 (checked 7 October 2026).

5. Notion: best for docs and boards together

If your Trello cards are full of links to documents stored elsewhere, Notion brings docs, wikis and boards into one workspace. Any database can be shown as a board, timeline, calendar or table, and the board view is free on every plan. It lacks native time tracking and resource management, and you design the project structure yourself. Plus costs $10 per member/month annually and Business, which includes Notion AI, $20 (checked 7 October 2026).

Trello alternatives by use case

Best free Trello alternative

ClickUp's Free Forever plan has the most features. Jira is free for up to 10 users, which suits small software teams. Wrike's free plan covers basic task management with board and table views. Asana's and monday.com's free plans are limited to two seats, and Basecamp's to one project.

Cheapest Trello alternative

Trello itself is one of the cheapest tools, at $5 per user/month on Standard. ClickUp Unlimited ($7) is the cheapest alternative per user, and Basecamp's flat pricing can be cheaper for larger teams. For Microsoft 365 customers, basic Planner may cost nothing extra.

Best Trello alternative with Gantt charts

Wrike, Smartsheet and ClickUp offer full Gantt scheduling with dependencies, critical path and baselines. monday.com and Asana include Gantt and timeline views with dependencies on their mid-tier plans.

Best Kanban-first alternative

monday.com and Jira keep the board at the centre while adding more. Jira supports WIP limits on its boards, which plain Trello boards do not. Our guide to Kanban boards explains which board features matter.

Best for small teams

ClickUp, Asana and Basecamp suit small teams that have outgrown Trello. Wrike's Team plan (2 to 15 users) is a good choice if the team needs Gantt charts and dashboards from day one.

Best for enterprises and PMOs

Wrike and Smartsheet score 10/10 for scalability in our review, with portfolio management, governance and advanced reporting. monday.com and Asana also offer strong enterprise plans.

Best for software and Agile teams

Jira is the standard for software teams, and ClickUp is a lower-complexity option that also includes docs and sprints.

Open-source and self-hosted options

Some teams want a self-hosted Kanban board instead of a cloud service. Open-source projects such as Taiga, Wekan and Kanboard are often suggested. They are outside our scored top 10, so check their current features, maintenance and support before you commit.

How to choose the right Trello alternative

  1. Name the limit you have hit. Collaborator cap, views, dependencies, workload, reporting or Power-Up costs each point to different tools.
  2. Check whether a Trello upgrade solves it. If Premium's views and Workspace views would do the job, compare that cost with switching.
  3. Shortlist two or three alternatives. Use the use-case section above and our head-to-head comparisons.
  4. Check the plan you would need. Dependencies, time tracking, workload and portfolios often sit on mid or upper tiers.
  5. Pilot one real project. Import or rebuild a live board, add dates and owners, and ask the team to work in it for a week or two.
  6. Compare total cost. Include seat minimums, guests, AI add-ons and any integrations you would still need.

How to migrate from Trello

Several alternatives have direct Trello importers, which makes moving boards much quicker than rebuilding them:

  • monday.com: a built-in importer connects to your Trello account. Its help centre says it imports task names, labels and dates, but not descriptions, checklists, activity or attachments, and handles up to 10,000 rows at a time.
  • ClickUp: a direct Trello importer is included alongside importers for Asana, Jira, monday.com, Wrike and others.
  • Jira: Jira Cloud can import data directly from Trello; Atlassian says only organisation, site or Jira administrators can run it.
  • Asana: Asana's app directory lists a third-party integration, Migrator.xyz, for importing Trello boards.
  • Other tools: most accept CSV imports. Trello Premium includes simple data export.

Whichever tool you choose, follow the same steps:

  1. Archive finished cards and boards you no longer need.
  2. Decide how Trello lists, labels and custom fields map to statuses, tags and fields in the new tool.
  3. Import one board as a test and check what came across, especially descriptions, checklists and attachments.
  4. Rebuild automations and Power-Up functions with the new tool's own features.
  5. Move one team first, then the rest once the pilot works.
  6. Keep Trello boards read-only for reference until you are sure nothing is missing.
Tip

Importers vary in what they bring across. Check checklists, comments and attachments on a test board before you migrate everything, and keep the originals until the team signs off.

Our verdict

Trello is still the quickest way to make work visible, and for many individuals and small teams it is all they need. When projects outgrow boards, choose Wrike for full project, resource and portfolio management, monday.com to keep the visual simplicity with more views and automation, Asana for structured cross-team work, or ClickUp for the most features at a low price. Software teams should look at Jira, Microsoft 365 organisations at Planner, and agencies that mainly need client communication at Basecamp.

Try your shortlist on a real project before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Trello?

Wrike is our top pick when you need Gantt charts with dependencies, workload views, time tracking and portfolio reporting. monday.com is best if you want Trello's ease of use with more views and automation, and ClickUp offers the most features for the money.

Is there a free alternative to Trello?

Yes. ClickUp, Wrike, Asana, monday.com, Notion, Jira and Basecamp all have free plans with limits. ClickUp's is the most feature-rich, and Jira's covers up to 10 users. Basic Microsoft Planner is included in many Microsoft 365 plans.

What is cheaper than Trello?

Few tools are cheaper per user than Trello Standard at $5 per user/month (annual). ClickUp Unlimited at $7 is the closest, and Basecamp's flat pricing, from $25/month, can be cheaper for larger teams (checked 7 October 2026).

Is there a Trello alternative with Gantt charts?

Yes. Wrike, Smartsheet and ClickUp offer full Gantt charts with dependencies, critical path and baselines. monday.com, Asana and Microsoft Planner's paid plans also include Gantt or timeline views with dependencies.

Is Asana better than Trello?

Asana is better for structured, multi-team projects with dependencies, goals and portfolios. Trello is simpler and cheaper for board-based work. See Trello vs Asana.

Is monday.com better than Trello?

monday.com offers more views, dashboards and automation while staying easy to learn. Trello is simpler and costs less. See Trello vs monday.com.

Is Jira or Trello better?

Both are from Atlassian. Jira is better for software teams that need backlogs, sprints and reports; Trello is better for simple, visual boards for any kind of team. See Trello vs Jira.

Does Microsoft 365 have a Trello equivalent?

Yes. Microsoft Planner provides Trello-style boards inside Teams and Outlook, and basic Planner is included in many Microsoft 365 business plans. Paid Planner plans add timelines, dependencies and resource management.

Is there an open-source Trello alternative?

Yes. Open-source Kanban tools such as Taiga, Wekan and Kanboard can be self-hosted. They are not part of our scored review, so check their current features and support before choosing one.

Why do teams outgrow Trello?

The most common reasons are the Free plan's 10-collaborator limit, advanced views needing Premium, no task dependencies, no resource or portfolio management, reliance on Power-Ups and boards becoming cluttered as work grows.

What is the best Trello alternative for small teams?

ClickUp offers the most features at a low price, Asana adds structure without much complexity, and Basecamp suits small businesses that mainly need communication and client access. Wrike's Team plan suits small teams needing Gantt charts and dashboards.

Can I import my Trello boards into another tool?

Yes. monday.com, ClickUp and Jira Cloud have direct Trello importers, Asana's app directory lists a third-party migration tool, and most other tools accept CSV files. Always test with one board first, because importers differ in what they bring across.
