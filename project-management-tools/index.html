Best Project Management Tools in 2026

The best project management tools in 2026 are Wrike, monday.com, Asana, ClickUp, Smartsheet, Jira, Microsoft Planner, Notion, Trello and Basecamp. We scored each one on seven categories, compared their features, plans and published G2 and Capterra ratings, and ranked Wrike first for teams that want projects, resources, portfolios, approvals and reporting in one platform. The right choice still depends on your team, so this guide also shows which tool wins for ease of use, value, software teams, Microsoft 365 and flat pricing.

  • Best overall: Wrike (9.4/10), the most complete all-in-one platform for planning, resources, portfolios, proofing and reporting.

What is project management software?

Project management software is an application that helps a team plan, organise and track the work needed to deliver a project. It typically combines task lists, schedules such as Gantt charts or timelines, Kanban boards, file sharing, comments, automation and reports, so everyone works from one shared, up-to-date plan instead of separate spreadsheets and email threads.

Modern tools range from simple Kanban boards to full work management platforms that also cover resource planning, request intake, approvals, portfolios and business intelligence. Most now include AI features as well. Our guide to project management software explains the categories and core features in more detail.

Why Wrike ranks first

Wrike scores 10 out of 10 in three of our seven categories: feature depth, planning and resource management, and scalability. No other tool in the review matches that combination. In one platform it brings together interactive Gantt charts with dependencies, board, table and calendar views, dashboards, workload and resource planning, cross-project portfolio views, request forms, automation, built-in proofing and approvals, and AI. That makes it a strong fit for PMOs, marketing and creative teams, professional services firms and any organisation that wants a single system of record rather than separate task, resource, proofing and reporting tools.

The trade-offs are the ones you would expect from a deep platform. Wrike takes longer to set up and learn than Trello or monday.com, and its most advanced resource, budgeting and BI features sit on the quote-based Pinnacle and Apex plans. On Wrike's pricing page (checked 7 October 2026), the Free plan covers basic task management with board and table views, Team costs $10 per user per month billed annually for 2 to 15 users and adds interactive Gantt charts, shareable dashboards and AI, and Business costs $25 per user per month for 5 to 200 users. A 14-day free trial is available with no credit card.

Best tools for your team

Best for small teams and start-ups

Small teams usually value speed of setup and price. Trello is the simplest place to start, ClickUp gives the most features for the money, Basecamp suits client-facing teams that want flat pricing, and Notion works well when documentation matters as much as tasks. If you expect to need Gantt charts and dashboards soon, Wrike's Team plan (2 to 15 users) gives you room to grow into a full platform.

Best for mid-sized organisations and enterprises

Wrike and Smartsheet score highest for scalability and enterprise readiness, with monday.com and Asana close behind. Choose Wrike if you want resource management, portfolios, request intake and approvals in one platform; Smartsheet if your organisation thinks in spreadsheets; and monday.com or Asana if fast adoption across many departments is the priority.

Best free project management tools

Nine of the ten tools in our review offer a free plan or include a free tier, but the limits vary (checked 7 October 2026):

  • Jira: free for up to 10 users, with Scrum and Kanban boards, backlogs and basic automation. One of the most capable free plans for software teams.
  • ClickUp Free Forever: unlimited members and tasks with limited storage and automations; Gantt charts and dashboards are available as trials.
  • Trello Free: up to 10 collaborators and 10 boards per Workspace, with unlimited cards. Ideal for simple Kanban.
  • Wrike Free: basic task and project management with board and table views, plus web, desktop and mobile apps; a good starting point if you expect to grow into Wrike's paid plans.
  • Notion Free: aimed at individuals; block limits apply once a workspace has two or more members.
  • Asana Personal: unlimited tasks and projects for one or two people.
  • monday.com Free: up to 2 seats and 3 boards.
  • Basecamp Free: one project with 1 GB of storage.
  • Microsoft Planner (basic): no stand-alone free plan, but included with many Microsoft 365 business subscriptions.

Smartsheet has no free plan but offers a 30-day trial. Free plans are best for trying a tool or running small projects; most teams that run several projects at once move to a paid plan within months.

How to choose a project management tool

Rankings narrow the field; a short, structured evaluation makes the decision. This is the process we recommend:

  1. Describe the work. List your project types, team size, methods (Waterfall, Scrum, Kanban or hybrid) and who outside the team needs access, such as clients or executives.
  2. Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves. For example: Gantt charts with dependencies, resource and workload planning, sprints, proofing, request forms, time tracking or Microsoft 365 integration. Our guide to project management software features explains each one.
  3. Match the tool to your method. Predictive projects need schedules, dependencies and baselines; Agile teams need backlogs and sprints; continuous work needs Kanban boards and WIP limits. Read about Gantt chart software if scheduling is central.
  4. Decide how much you want in one place. If you need projects, resources, portfolios and approvals together, an all-in-one platform such as Wrike avoids stitching several tools together. If you only need task boards, a simpler tool will be adopted faster.
  5. Check security and administration with IT. Single sign-on, permissions, audit logs, data residency and the integrations you already rely on.
  6. Work out the total cost for a year, including seat minimums, guests, add-ons such as AI, and the plan you are likely to need as you grow.
  7. Trial two or three tools on a real project for two weeks, with the people who will use them daily, and agree who will own templates, training and adoption.

AI in project management tools

All ten tools now offer AI features, from summarising comment threads and drafting updates to creating automations in plain language and running AI agents. How AI is sold differs: Wrike, monday.com, Asana, Jira and Trello include AI allowances in paid plans; Notion includes Notion AI from its Business plan; ClickUp sells its AI as a paid add-on; and Microsoft 365 Copilot is a separate add-on that also works in Planner. Check the allowance on the plan you are considering, as several vendors meter AI with monthly credits.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best project management tool in 2026?

Our top pick is Wrike (9.4/10) for growing teams and enterprises, because it combines projects, resources, portfolios, approvals and reporting in one platform. monday.com (9.1) is best for ease of use, Asana (9.0) for cross-team coordination, ClickUp (8.9) for value and Jira for software teams.

What is the best free project management tool?

It depends on your team. Trello is the simplest free option, ClickUp's Free Forever plan includes the most features, Jira is free for up to 10 users and suits software teams, and Wrike's Free plan is a good start if you expect to grow into a full work management platform.

What is the cheapest project management software?

Among per-user tools, Trello Standard ($5 per user/month billed annually) and ClickUp Unlimited ($7) are the cheapest paid plans in our review. Basecamp's flat pricing, from $25 a month for the whole account, can be cheapest for larger teams with few projects.

Which project management tool is easiest to use?

Trello scores highest for ease of use in our review (10/10), followed by monday.com, Asana and Basecamp (9/10).

Which project management tool is best for small teams?

Trello, ClickUp, Basecamp and Notion suit small teams. Wrike's Team plan, for 2 to 15 users, is a good choice if the team needs Gantt charts and dashboards.

Which project management tool is best for enterprises?

Wrike and Smartsheet score highest for scalability and enterprise readiness (10/10), with monday.com, Asana and Jira close behind. Wrike is particularly strong for PMOs that need portfolio and resource management.

Which project management tool is best for Agile and Scrum?

Jira is the standard for software teams and leads our Agile category. ClickUp also supports sprints with sprint points and reporting, and Wrike supports Agile boards alongside traditional project plans; see our Scrum guide.

What features should project management software have?

Most teams need task management with owners and due dates, at least two views (such as Kanban and Gantt or timeline), file sharing and comments, notifications, basic automation and reporting. Growing teams add resource and workload management, portfolios, request forms, approvals, time tracking and single sign-on.

Do I need Gantt charts in a project management tool?

If your projects have dependencies and fixed dates, yes. Wrike, monday.com, Asana, ClickUp, Smartsheet and Microsoft Planner's premium plans include Gantt or timeline views, usually on paid plans. See our guide to the Gantt chart.

Is Microsoft Project still available?

Microsoft retired Project for the web in 2025 and moved its scheduling features into Microsoft Planner's premium plans. Microsoft's pricing pages also stated that Project Online would be retired on 30 September 2026. The Project desktop app remains available with Planner and Project Plan 3. See our Microsoft Planner review.

Can I use a spreadsheet instead of project management software?

For small projects, yes: our free project management templates cover plans, schedules, RACI matrices and risk registers. Software becomes worthwhile when several people need to update the same plan in real time or you run many projects at once.

Can project management software use AI?

Yes. All ten tools offer AI features, from summaries and writing help to automation and AI agents. Some include AI allowances in their plans; ClickUp and Microsoft 365 Copilot sell AI as add-ons.
