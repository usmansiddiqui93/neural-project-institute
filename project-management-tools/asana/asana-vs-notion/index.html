Asana vs Notion: Which Should Your Team Use in 2026?

Asana vs Notion comes down to one question: do you want a dedicated work management tool, or a flexible workspace where you build your own project system next to your documents? Asana is the stronger choice for structured, cross-team project work with goals, portfolios and workload views; Notion is the better fit for docs-first teams that want wikis, specs and lightweight project tracking in one place. This comparison covers ease of use, planning, docs, AI, pricing, ratings and how to choose.

Quick verdict

Choose Asana if you need a ready-made system for assigning, scheduling and tracking work across several teams, and want to connect that work to goals and portfolio reporting. Choose Notion if your team lives in documents and wants specs, meeting notes, wikis and simple project boards in one flexible workspace, and you are happy to design how it works.

In our scoring, Asana (9.0/10, ranked #3 in our top 10 project management tools) wins on ease of use, planning and resource management, Agile and software teams, and enterprise readiness. Notion (8.0/10, #8) wins on value for money, and the two tie on feature depth and collaboration. The scores reflect our focus on project management: as a knowledge base, Notion is the stronger product.

If neither fits because you need detailed resource planning, budgets, critical path scheduling and portfolio control in one platform, our top-ranked tool, Wrike, goes further than both.

The core difference: work manager vs connected workspace

On the surface, Asana and Notion look alike. Both have boards, timelines, calendars, forms, automations and AI. The difference is in how they are built, and that shapes almost every other comparison on this page.

Asana is purpose-built work management. Tasks, projects, portfolios and goals are fixed building blocks that Asana connects in what it calls the Work Graph, so finishing a task can update a project, a portfolio and a goal. Every team gets the same structure on day one, which makes Asana fast to adopt and easy to report across.

Notion is a set of building blocks. Pages hold documents; databases hold items with properties; views show those items as tables, boards, timelines or calendars. A project tracker in Notion is a database you (or a template) design. That flexibility lets Notion become a wiki, a CRM, a roadmap and a task board in the same workspace, but someone has to design and maintain it.

What are Asana and Notion?

Asana

Asana, Inc. was founded in 2008 by Dustin Moskovitz and Justin Rosenstein and is listed on the New York Stock Exchange. It is used for cross-team projects, campaign planning, request intake, goals and OKRs, and portfolio reporting. Each task has one owner, a due date and optional dependencies, and a task can sit in several projects at once.

Notion

Notion is made by Notion Labs, Inc., a privately held company based in San Francisco that launched Notion 1.0 in 2016. Teams use it for wikis, product specs, meeting notes, roadmaps and project trackers, with companion apps for calendar and mail. Project management features (projects and tasks databases, sprints, timelines, forms and automations) are built on its databases.

Ease of use and setup

Asana gives you a working structure immediately: create a project from a template, add tasks, invite colleagues and switch between list, board and calendar views. Reviewers consistently describe the interface as clean and intuitive, and its Capterra ease-of-use sub-score is 4.4/5 (checked 7 October 2026). The harder part is organisational, not technical: agreeing custom fields, templates, rules and portfolio structures so every team works the same way.

Notion is easy to start and harder to master. Writing pages and ticking off to-dos takes minutes. Building a reliable project system with related databases, rollups, formulas, filtered views and automations takes real design effort, and G2 users list a steep learning curve for advanced setups among its main drawbacks. Capterra reviewers also report that Notion can feel sluggish with very large databases and long pages. Its Capterra ease-of-use sub-score is also 4.4/5, but that reflects its many users who mainly write documents.

In our scoring, Asana takes ease of use 9/10 to Notion's 7/10 because a non-technical team can manage projects in Asana without anyone building the system first.

Tip

If you choose Notion for projects, start from Notion's projects and tasks template rather than a blank page, and appoint one owner for the core databases. Most Notion sprawl comes from several people building slightly different trackers.

Asana's Gantt chart view, from the Starter plan, shows dependency chains, lets you drag tasks to reschedule and compares progress against a target schedule. That covers most campaign plans, launches and cross-team projects. Notion's timeline plots database items on a Gantt-style chart with draggable dates and visible dependencies, which is well suited to roadmaps and high-level plans.

Neither vendor describes critical path analysis, so teams running tightly sequenced, date-driven schedules should test carefully or consider a scheduling-focused tool. Both offer Kanban boards on every plan, including free.

For product teams, Notion's built-in sprints are a genuine plus: switching them on creates a sprint board with current sprint, planning and backlog views, and lets you choose what happens to unfinished tasks. It covers the basics of sprint planning, though neither tool matches Jira for velocity and burndown reporting.

Docs, wikis and knowledge management

This is Notion's clearest win. Pages are fast to write, nest without limit, embed databases, files, code and design frames, and can be organised into a searchable team wiki. On Business, verified pages tell readers which documents are current. For project teams, the big benefit is that the brief, requirements, decisions and meeting notes live next to the tasks they produce. Our meeting notes template shows the kind of page that works well as a Notion database item.

Asana is not a wiki. It has task descriptions, project overviews and briefs, project messages and status updates, which are enough to give context to the work. But company handbooks, long-form specs and knowledge bases usually live in another tool alongside Asana, and Notion is a common choice for that role.

Goals, portfolios and multi-project reporting

This is Asana's clearest win. On the Advanced plan, goals let you set company, team and individual objectives, break them into sub-goals, and update progress automatically from connected projects. Portfolios group projects so leaders see status, progress and owners on one screen and publish written status updates. Dashboards, from Starter, chart tasks by status, overdue work and custom-field totals. If you manage by OKRs, our OKR template is a good way to draft goals before building them in Asana.

Notion has no packaged goals, portfolio or status-reporting features. You can build them: a Projects database with status and owner, a Goals database related to it, rollups for progress, and a dashboard page of charts and filtered views. Free includes one chart and paid plans include unlimited charts. It works, but someone has to build and maintain it. If leaders need consistent reporting across many projects, see our guide to managing multiple projects for what that reporting should cover.

Workload, resources and time tracking

Asana's workload view (Advanced) shows each person's effort across projects in a portfolio against a capacity you set, and lets managers drag work to rebalance. Advanced also adds time tracking with estimated versus actual effort, and Enterprise adds organisation-wide resource management and capacity planning.

Notion has no native time tracking, workload view or resource scheduling. Teams approximate them with number properties for estimated hours, rollups and third-party time-tracking integrations. For a small team that rarely matters; for agencies billing hours or PMOs doing capacity planning, it is usually decisive.

This gap explains most of the difference in our planning and resource score (Asana 8/10, Notion 5/10). Organisations that need deeper resource, budget and financial control than Asana offers usually look at Wrike; see Wrike vs Asana.

Automation and AI

Automation

Asana's rules pair a trigger (a form submission, a task moved, a due date approaching) with actions such as assigning, moving, requesting approval or posting to Slack, and every paid plan includes unlimited automations. Notion's database automations run when a page is added, a property changes or a schedule fires, and can edit properties, add pages, notify people, post to Slack or send webhooks. They require a paid plan, and multi-step logic is simpler in Asana.

AI

Both vendors now put AI at the centre of their products, but they package it differently (checked 7 October 2026):

  • Asana: AI Teammates (prebuilt and custom AI agents), Asana Dash (AI briefs and next steps) and AI Studio (a no-code builder for AI workflows) are included on paid plans, with usage metered by requests and credits and paid top-ups available.
  • Notion: Notion AI (Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes and Enterprise Search) is included on Business and Enterprise; Free and Plus get only a limited trial. Custom Agents, which run on triggers or schedules, use Notion credits sold to Business and Enterprise customers at $10 per 1,000 credits a month.

In practice, Asana's AI is aimed at moving project work along (triage, routing, status reports, capacity checks), while Notion's is strongest at writing, summarising meetings and finding information across your workspace and connected apps. On data, Asana states that its AI partners do not use customer data to train their models, and Notion states that it does not train AI models on customer content.

Collaboration, guests and notifications

Both tools score 9/10 for collaboration in our review, for different reasons. Asana keeps conversation on the task: comments, @mentions, collaborators who follow a task, approvals (on Advanced) and status updates, with unlimited free guests on paid plans for clients and freelancers. Notion excels at real-time co-editing: several people can write on the same page, comment on any block and @mention colleagues, and Plus and above allow unlimited guests.

Each has a familiar downside. Asana users frequently mention notification overload, so agree notification settings and @mention habits early. Notion users more often mention workspace sprawl, so agree a teamspace structure and database owners. For intake, both offer forms that create work automatically; our work request template lists the fields worth collecting in either tool.

Integrations and using Asana and Notion together

Asana says even its free plan includes more than 100 integrations, including Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Workspace, Zoom, Jira, GitHub, Figma and Zapier; Salesforce, Tableau and Power BI are listed on Advanced. Notion's Plus plan includes basic connections such as Slack and Google Drive, and Business adds premium connections, including GitHub and Asana. Both have public APIs.

That Asana connection points to a common pattern: use both. Many teams keep their wiki, specs and meeting notes in Notion and run day-to-day execution in Asana, linking the two so context and tasks stay connected. It costs more than one tool, but it plays to each product's strengths. If you would rather have docs and project management in a single tool, ClickUp combines both; see ClickUp vs Notion.

Security and administration

Both vendors publish strong security credentials. Asana's trust centre lists SOC 2 Type 2 and SOC 3 reports, ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018 and 27701, CSA STAR Level 1, and HIPAA (opt-in) and GDPR support, with data residency options and customer-managed encryption keys. Notion's security page lists a SOC 2 Type 2 attestation and ISO 27001, 27017, 27018 and 27701 certifications, and Notion signs a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement for healthcare customers.

Admin controls scale by plan in both. Asana reserves SAML single sign-on and SCIM for Enterprise; Notion offers SAML SSO from Business and SCIM, audit logs and advanced controls on Enterprise. Confirm current certifications and their scope in each vendor's trust documentation during procurement.

How to switch between Asana and Notion

Moving in either direction is manageable if you migrate active work, not your entire history.

  1. Decide what moves. List active projects, templates and the documents people still use. Archive the rest in the old tool or export it.
  2. Design the target structure first. In Asana, agree teams, projects, sections and custom fields. In Notion, design your Projects and Tasks databases, their properties and relations before importing anything.
  3. Export and import. Asana exports projects to CSV, and Notion databases export to CSV and pages to Markdown or HTML. Notion's importer lists Asana among its supported sources, and Asana can import CSV files into a new project. Check field mapping (assignees, dates, statuses) on a small sample first.
  4. Rebuild automation. Rules, automations and forms do not transfer. Recreate the few that matter most and add others as people ask for them.
  5. Pilot with one team for two to four weeks, then fix structure problems before the wider rollout.
  6. Set a cut-over date after which new work only goes into the new tool, and keep the old one read-only for reference.
Tip

If you are moving from Notion to Asana, you may not need to move your docs at all. Keeping the wiki in Notion and linking it to Asana projects is often less disruptive than recreating long documents as task descriptions.

Frequently asked questions

Is Notion better than Asana?

Not overall for project management, but it is better for documentation. Notion is the stronger choice for wikis, specs and meeting notes connected to lightweight project tracking. Asana is stronger for structured cross-team work, with goals, portfolios, workload and approvals. In our review Asana scores 9.0/10 and Notion 8.0/10.

Can Notion replace Asana for project management?

For small teams and lightweight-to-moderate projects, yes: Notion has task databases, boards, timelines, sprints, forms and automations. It cannot replace Asana's native goals, portfolios, workload view or time tracking, so teams that rely on those usually keep a dedicated tool.

Is Asana or Notion cheaper?

Notion is slightly cheaper at each level: Plus costs $10 per member/month and Business $20, against $10.99 for Asana Starter and $24.99 for Asana Advanced on annual billing (checked 7 October 2026). Add Notion credits for Custom Agents or extra Asana AI usage if you need them.

Are Asana and Notion free?

Both have free plans. Asana Personal is free but limited to 2 seats per project and team. Notion Free is aimed at individuals, with limited blocks once two or more members join, 5 MB uploads and 7-day page history. Growing teams will need a paid plan on either.

Which is easier to use, Asana or Notion?

Asana is easier for project management because the structure is ready-made: projects, tasks, owners and dates work out of the box. Notion is easy for writing and simple lists, but building a full project system with relations, rollups and automations takes more learning. We score ease of use 9/10 for Asana and 7/10 for Notion.

Does Notion have Gantt charts like Asana?

Notion has a timeline view that works like a simple Gantt chart, with draggable dates and visible dependencies. Asana has both timeline and Gantt views from the Starter plan, including comparison against a target schedule. Neither vendor describes critical path analysis.

Which has better AI, Asana or Notion?

They are strong in different areas. Asana's AI Teammates, Dash and AI Studio focus on moving project work along, such as triage, routing and status reports, and are on all paid plans with metered usage. Notion AI excels at writing, meeting notes and search, but is included only from the Business plan.

Does Notion have time tracking or workload management?

No. Notion has no native time tracking, workload view or resource scheduling; teams use number properties, rollups or third-party integrations instead. Asana includes workload and time tracking on Advanced, and capacity planning on Enterprise.

Can you use Asana and Notion together?

Yes, and many teams do. A common set-up keeps the wiki, specs and meeting notes in Notion and runs execution in Asana. Notion's Business plan includes a premium connection with Asana, and both tools have APIs and connect through automation tools such as Zapier.

Which is better for small teams or start-ups?

Notion often suits start-ups that want one affordable workspace for docs and tasks. Asana suits small teams that mainly need clear ownership, deadlines and timelines, and Starter is reasonably priced. Note that Asana's free plan is limited to two seats, so most teams will pay from the start.

Which is better for product and engineering teams?

Notion is popular for specs, PRDs, roadmaps and sprints, while Asana is popular for cross-functional launches. Both integrate with GitHub and Jira, and many engineering teams keep a dedicated issue tracker for execution alongside either tool.

Which is better for large organisations?

Asana, in most cases. It offers goals, portfolios, enterprise identity controls, data residency and capacity planning for coordinating many teams. Large organisations often adopt Notion as a knowledge and collaboration layer rather than as their main delivery system.

How do I move from Notion to Asana, or from Asana to Notion?

Export active projects to CSV, design the target structure first, import a small sample to check field mapping, then rebuild your most important automations and pilot with one team. Notion's importer supports Asana, and Asana imports CSV files into new projects.

What are good alternatives to Asana and Notion?

Wrike suits organisations needing resource, budget and portfolio control; ClickUp combines docs and project management at a lower price; monday.com offers very visual, customisable boards. See our Asana alternatives and Notion alternatives.
