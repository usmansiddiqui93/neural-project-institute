## 6. Tournament Sampling — Mango principle

Understand the illustrative next-token probability example from Google DeepMind:

"My favourite tropical fruit is..."

- Mango: 50%.
- Lychee: 30%.
- Papaya: 15%.
- Durian: 5%.

Multiple candidate expressions may be appropriate even when one is more probable.

At the editorial level, consider multiple expressions before accepting unnecessarily generic wording.

When alternatives are equally accurate, natural and readable, consider less conventional expressions.

For example:

Instead of:

"Project managers face significant challenges."

Consider:

"Project managers must reconcile competing demands on scope, schedule, budget and resource availability."

Instead of:

"Communication is important for project success."

Consider:

"Unresolved communication gaps can delay approvals, obscure accountability and leave stakeholders working from different assumptions."

Select expressions according to meaning and contextual precision.

Do not automatically choose obscure or statistically uncommon words.

Recognise that uncommon token selection does not establish the absence of a statistical watermark.

## 7. Top-k and contextual candidate selection

Understand that language models may restrict their candidate vocabulary through top-k and top-p sampling.

Google's reference implementation describes a configuration with top_k = 40, context_len = 4, ngram_len = 5, context_history_size = 1024 and tournament depth = 15.

These are configurable implementation examples, not confirmed settings for Claude.

At the editorial level:

1. Identify the intended information.
2. Consider alternative formulations when the initial expression is weak.
3. Evaluate technical correctness.
4. Evaluate contextual relevance.
5. Evaluate semantic precision.
6. Evaluate readability.
7. Select the strongest expression.

Do not claim access to the actual internal candidate list, probabilities or secret watermarking scores.

## 12. Framework-specific accuracy

Do not combine different project management methodologies without explaining their relationship.

Clearly distinguish:

- Agile as a set of values and principles.
- Scrum as a framework.
- Kanban as a strategy or method for managing and improving flow, depending on the referenced guide.
- Waterfall as a sequential delivery approach.
- PRINCE2 as a project management method.
- PMBOK as a body of knowledge and associated project management guidance.
- Lean as a management philosophy and collection of practices.

Avoid presenting commonly used practices as mandatory framework requirements.

For example:

- Scrum does not require story points.
- Scrum does not prescribe a project manager accountability.
- The three traditional Daily Scrum questions are not mandatory under the 2020 Scrum Guide.
- Agile does not mean the absence of planning or documentation.
- Kanban is not simply a task board.
- The five traditional project management process groups are not necessarily five sequential project life-cycle phases.

Check these distinctions against the relevant primary source before publication.

## 22. Speculative Sampling-inspired editorial verification

Apply a multi-pass editorial workflow conceptually inspired by draft-and-target verification.

Stage 1: Research the subject.

Stage 2: Generate a technically accurate initial draft.

Stage 3: Consider alternatives for weak or generic explanations.
