### Content gaps we can fill

- Most pages confuse **Scrum events** (which are mandatory) with **complementary techniques** (which are optional). We can make the line explicit using the 2020 Scrum Guide's "purposefully incomplete" wording.
- No page maps techniques to **the problem they solve** and **the Scrum element they support**.
- Few give **step-by-step instructions** (planning poker, story splitting, five whys) or **worked examples** (a story split, a burndown, a five whys chain).
- No page gives a **process for choosing and trialling a technique** through the Retrospective.
- Little coverage of **non-software teams** and of **common mistakes** such as adopting every technique at once.

## Facts verified

- 2020 Scrum Guide: "purposefully incomplete"; "Various processes, techniques and methods can be employed within the framework"; mentions burn-downs, burn-ups and cumulative flows as forecasting practices; does not mention user stories, velocity, planning poker or Definition of Ready (scrumguides.org PDF).
- Planning poker: named by James Grenning (2002), popularised by Mike Cohn (Wikipedia).
- Three Cs: Ron Jeffries, 2001 (Agile Alliance glossary).
- User Story Mapping: Jeff Patton, O'Reilly book 2014.
- INVEST: Bill Wake (attributed without a year).
