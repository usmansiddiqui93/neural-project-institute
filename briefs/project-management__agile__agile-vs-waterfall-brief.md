## Search Intent

Informational with a decision element. The reader wants to understand what actually differs between the two approaches and, usually, to decide which one (or which mix) to use for a specific project. They want a clear comparison table, practical criteria, examples and a way to combine them.

- Team leads and PMO staff designing a hybrid governance model

## Facts to use (verified)

- Royce 1970 paper is usually cited as the first detailed diagram of the model; he called the single-pass version risky and recommended feedback and iteration; the term "waterfall" may first appear in Bell and Thayer (1976) (Wikipedia, Waterfall model). Benington presented phased software development in 1956. US DOD-STD-2167 (1985) required sequential phases.
- Agile Manifesto: 2001, 17 practitioners, four values and twelve principles (consistent with site).
- PMI Agile Practice Guide (2017, with Agile Alliance) includes suitability filters; the filter rates culture, team and project attributes and plots them on a radar chart (Agile, hybrid, predictive zones). Present our scoring as a simplified adaptation, not PMI's exact tool.
- PMBOK Guide 8th edition (November 2025; site fact) covers predictive, adaptive and hybrid development approaches (O'Reilly table of contents: 4.2.1–4.2.3).
- PMI new PMP exam page: from July 2026, "covers predictive and agile methods, and hybrid applications"; People 33%, Process 41%, Business Environment 26%.
- Scrum Guide 2020: Sprints one month or less.
- Tool facts from our review pages: Wrike #1 (9.4) with Gantt, boards and request forms; Jira strongest Agile tool for software but no classic Gantt with critical path; Smartsheet spreadsheet-style with Gantt; monday.com Gantt from Standard.
- No statistics on success rates: widely quoted figures were not verifiable from primary sources, so leave them out.
