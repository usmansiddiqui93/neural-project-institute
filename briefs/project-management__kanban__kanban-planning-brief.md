Pages fetched for structure (wording not reused):

1. **Businessmap (What is Kanban planning)**: resilience benefits (transparency, waste, flexibility); planning on the board (reversed timeline, card position as priority, timeline and Gantt views); accuracy via cycle time, throughput and Monte Carlo; product-led forecasting. Thin on meetings, no worked numbers, no FAQ.
2. **6sigma.us (Kanban planning)**: Kanban principles; setting up a board, WIP limits and swimlanes; seven implementation steps (value streams, system design, WIP limits, backlog prioritisation with cost of delay/WSJF, standups, metrics, improvement); metrics definitions; integrating with Scrum, Lean, SAFe and DevOps. Generic, no forecasting example, no FAQ.
3. **Kanban University (Kanban cadences)**: seven cadences (replenishment/commitment, delivery planning, standup, service delivery review, operations review, risk review, strategy review); replenishment commits to servicing a request while delivery planning commits to a delivery date, deliberately decoupled.
4. **ProKanban.org playbook (forecasting delivery)**: forecasts are only as reliable as the system is stable; you can start Monte Carlo with about 10 completed items; two forecast questions ("how many by a date" and "when will these be done"); confidence levels such as 85% and 95%; wide ranges reflect real variability.

Primary sources checked:
- **The Kanban Guide** (John Coleman and Daniel Vacanti, edition 2025.5, May 2025): four flow measures (WIP, throughput, work item age, cycle time); an SLE has two parts, a period of elapsed time and a probability, is based on historical cycle time and is shown on the Definition of Workflow; when WIP drops below its control, that can signal new work may be selected. The guide does not prescribe meetings.
- **Kanban University glossary**: commitment point ("the point at which the decision is made to start activities to deliver a work item"); class of service archetypes expedite, fixed date, standard and intangible defined by the impact of delay.
- **Open Guide to Kanban** (July 2025): backlog is optional and emergent; start work only when there is a clear capacity signal.
