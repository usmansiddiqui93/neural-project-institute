## Search Intent

Informational with a strong "how to" angle. The reader is a manager (often newly responsible for Scrum or Kanban teams) who wants to know:

- What their job is now that the team is self-managing
- How to split responsibilities with the Scrum Master and Product Owner
- How to delegate decisions without losing control
- How to measure performance and give feedback fairly
- How to handle conflict, remote work and underperformance
- What a typical week looks like and which mistakes to avoid

## Target Audience

- Line managers, engineering and delivery managers, heads of department
- Project managers moving into Agile environments
- Team leads promoted from within an Agile team
- Managers of non-software teams adopting Agile (marketing, operations)

## Recommended Length

3,600–4,000 words (sections + FAQ), with tables, step-by-step processes and illustrative worked examples.

## Facts to use (verified or established on site)

- 2020 Scrum Guide (current): Scrum Team is self-managing and decides who does what, when and how; Daily Scrum is for the Developers; Scrum Master serves the team, Product Owner and organisation.
- Jurgen Appelo, Management 3.0, seven levels of delegation: tell, sell, consult, agree, advise, inquire, delegate (verified on Appelo's own article).
- Google Project Aristotle: psychological safety most important of five dynamics (attribute to Google; same wording as the what-is-an-agile-team page).
- Agile Manifesto principles: build projects around motivated individuals; self-organising teams; regular reflection; sustainable pace.
- Tuckman stages (1965), Daniel Pink's autonomy, mastery and purpose (Drive, 2009), Thomas-Kilmann five conflict modes: attribute, no statistics.
- No invented statistics on manager effectiveness.
