## Search Intent

Informational with a strong "how to" layer. Searchers want a short definition, the types of requirements with examples, and a process they can follow on their own project. A smaller group searches for templates or for a specific term (high-level requirements, traceability matrix, MoSCoW). The page should define requirements in the first sentence, then go further than competitors with one worked example carried through the whole guide (from business need to traced, prioritised requirements), a step-by-step process, a "how to write a good requirement" section with before/after rewrites, and clear Agile and predictive treatments.

# Outline

## H1: Project Requirements: Types, Examples and How to Gather Them

Intro: define project requirements in the first sentence (conditions or capabilities the result must meet to satisfy stakeholders and the business need). Say what the guide covers: types, process, writing, prioritising, tracing, worked example.

## H2: What are project requirements?
- 40–60 word definition paragraph.
- The "what, not how" principle; requirements vs design.
- Product requirements vs project requirements (conditions on how the project runs: milestones, reporting, compliance).
- Where they live: charter (high-level), BRD/requirements document, backlog, traceability matrix.

## H2: Why project requirements matter
- Basis of scope, WBS, estimates, tests and acceptance.
- PMI Pulse of the Profession figure (47% of unsuccessful projects), attributed.
- Cost of late discovery (qualitative, no invented numbers).
