Comparison / informational with a decision layer. The reader wants to:

- Understand the real differences (planning, scope, roles, change, testing, risk, documentation, reporting)
- See both approaches applied to the same project
- Decide which suits their project, or whether to combine them

## Fact-Checking Notes

- 2020 Scrum Guide (checked 4 November 2026 via scrumguides.org PDF): Sprints are fixed length of one month or less; Scrum Team typically 10 or fewer; self-managing; three accountabilities (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers); artifacts with commitments (Product Goal, Sprint Goal, Definition of Done); Sprint Review max four hours and Retrospective max three hours for a one-month Sprint.
- Scrum Guide has no project manager role: state as such, without claiming what every organisation does.
- Waterfall facts kept consistent with our Waterfall guide rewritten today: six phases, phase gates (go, hold, recycle, stop), predictive approach per PMI, Royce 1970 paper, V-model, water-scrum-fall.
- PMI terminology predictive / adaptive / hybrid consistent with site (PMBOK Guide 8th edition, November 2025).
- Tool facts from our review pages (checked 4 November 2026): Wrike #1 (9.4), Gantt with dependencies, critical path and baselines from the Team plan, board view on every plan, Agile/sprint tools marked partial; Jira is the most complete Agile tool for software teams we review but lacks a classic Gantt with critical path and baselines; Smartsheet combines grid, Gantt and dependencies.
- Competitor statistics (Agile vs Waterfall failure rates, hybrid adoption percentages) deliberately not used.
- Worked example labelled illustrative; all figures invented.
