# Outline

## H1: Theory of Constraints (TOC): Five Focusing Steps Explained

Intro: define TOC in the first sentence (every system has at least one constraint that limits output; improve the system by managing the constraint). Say what the guide covers: five steps, worked Kanban example, DBR, project management, throughput accounting.

## H2: What is the theory of constraints?
- 40–60 word definition paragraph.
- Chain analogy: a chain is only as strong as its weakest link.
- Origin: Goldratt, The Goal (1984, with Jeff Cox), five focusing steps formalised in What is this Thing Called Theory of Constraints (1990), thinking processes (1991, popularised in It's Not Luck, 1994), Critical Chain (1997). Goldratt died in 2011.
- Where TOC is used now: manufacturing, supply chains, healthcare, software, projects.

## H2: The core ideas behind TOC
- The goal and the measure (throughput).
- Local optimisation vs system performance; time lost at the constraint is lost to the whole system; time saved elsewhere is not.
- Busy is not the same as productive; non-constraints should have spare capacity.
- Table: common belief vs TOC view.

## H2: Constraint vs bottleneck: types of constraints
- Bottleneck = resource with capacity below demand; constraint is wider (policy, market, supplier).
- Internal vs external.
- Table: type, example in operations, example in knowledge/project work.
- Policy constraints are the most common in knowledge work.

## H2: The five focusing steps
- Step zero: agree the goal and the measure.
- H3 per step (identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, repeat/avoid inertia), each with what to do and project/Kanban examples.
