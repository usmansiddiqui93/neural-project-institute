## Outline

**Intro (lead):** The Sprint Retrospective is the Scrum event in which the Scrum Team inspects how it worked and plans improvements; it concludes the Sprint; three-hour maximum. What this guide covers.

**Key takeaways (6).**

### H2: What is a Sprint Retrospective?
- 40–60 word snippet definition.
- Where it sits (after Sprint Review, before next Sprint Planning); one of five Scrum events.
- Why it matters: empiricism applied to the team's own process; review is about product, retro about process.

## Facts to verify / verified

- 2020 Scrum Guide Sprint Retrospective text (purpose; individuals, interactions, processes, tools and Definition of Done; assumptions that led them astray; most impactful improvements addressed as soon as possible, may be added to Sprint Backlog; three-hour maximum; concludes the Sprint): verified against scrumguides.org 2020 PDF.
- Scrum Master ensures events are positive, productive and kept within the timebox; Scrum Team = one Scrum Master, one Product Owner and Developers: verified.
- 2017 guide: Sprint Backlog included at least one high-priority process improvement from the previous Retrospective (cited on Scrum.org forum, November 2017); 2020 replaced with "may even be added": treat as verified, phrase carefully.
- Five stages: Esther Derby and Diana Larsen, *Agile Retrospectives: Making Good Teams Great* (2006); 2nd edition with David Horowitz, Pragmatic Bookshelf, February 2024: verified (O'Reilly listing).
- Prime directive: Norm Kerth, *Project Retrospectives: A Handbook for Team Reviews* (2001): verified attribution; paraphrase rather than quote in full.
- Typical durations by Sprint length are common practice, not Scrum Guide rules: label them.
