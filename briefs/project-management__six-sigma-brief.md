## Search Intent

Mainly informational. Searchers want a plain definition, what "six sigma" means statistically, the DMAIC steps and the belt levels; a sizeable group (project managers, operations and quality staff, certification seekers) wants to know how to run a Six Sigma project and how it relates to normal project management. ## Writer Notes and Fact Checks

- Bill Smith introduced Six Sigma at Motorola in 1986 (Wikipedia); the old page said "1980s" and "mid-1980s", consistent. Motorola registered the mark in the early 1990s (service mark 1991, trademark 1993): not needed on the page.
- GE: Jack Welch made it central to strategy in 1995 (Wikipedia). Avoid quoting savings figures; they are company claims.
- ISO 13053 was first published in 2011 (part 1 DMAIC, part 2 tools). Revision status not confirmed: say "first published in 2011".
- Sigma table values (66,807 / 6,210 / 233 / 3.4 DPMO) assume the 1.5 sigma shift; checked by calculation (DPMO = 1,000,000 x (1 - Φ(level - 1.5))).
- The 1.5 sigma shift is attributed to Mikel Harry and is criticised (e.g. Donald Wheeler). Present as a convention.
- Anbari (PMI, 2002): Six Sigma projects typically 3–6 months; Black Belts full time. Attribute.
- ASQ roles and certifications (CSSYB, CSSGB, CSSBB, CMBB) from asq.org. Do not quote fees or exact experience rules; they change. No single body governs Six Sigma certification.
- The worked example is invented and must be labelled illustrative.
- Keep consistent with our Lean vs Six Sigma page (Six Sigma from Motorola in the 1980s; Lean from the Toyota Production System).
