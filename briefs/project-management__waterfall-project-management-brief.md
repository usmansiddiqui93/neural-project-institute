Informational, with some practical "how to" intent. Readers want a clear definition, the phases, pros and cons and when to choose waterfall over Agile. A sizeable minority want to actually run a waterfall project: how to plan it, what documents to produce, how phase gates and change control work, and which tools support it. Exam candidates (PMP, CAPM, PMAI) want the vocabulary: predictive approach, baselines, phase gates, V-model, hybrid.

Content gaps we can fill:
- **Accurate history**: Royce's 1970 paper warned that a single-pass sequence is risky and recommended feedback and doing the job twice; he never used the word "waterfall" (the earliest known use is usually traced to Bell and Thayer, 1976). Most pages repeat the myth.
- **A full worked example** (illustrative): a phased office relocation or a software/system rollout with phases, deliverables, gate criteria, durations and a change request handled through change control.
- **Step-by-step "how to run a waterfall project"**: charter, requirements sign-off, WBS, schedule and baseline, phase gates, change control, closure.
- **Deliverables-per-phase table** and **gate checklist** (competitors list phases without outputs).
- **Variants**: V-model, waterfall with feedback/overlap (sashimi), phased/incremental waterfall, hybrid.
- **Decision checklist** for waterfall vs Agile vs hybrid, plus common mistakes.
- Neutral, not Agile-biased; terminology aligned with PMI (predictive) and the PMBOK Guide.
